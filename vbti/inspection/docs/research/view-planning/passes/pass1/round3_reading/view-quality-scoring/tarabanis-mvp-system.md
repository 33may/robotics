## Core method / idea

MVP ("Machine Vision Planner") is a **model-based, task-driven sensor-planning system**: given (1) a CAD/polyhedral geometric model of the scene, (2) an optical/geometric model of the camera+lens, and (3) a specification of which point/edge/face features must be detected, it automatically computes a camera pose and lens settings that guarantee those features are simultaneously **visible, resolvable (magnified enough), in focus, and inside the field of view** (Fig. 1, Sec. I-C). It targets a fixed eye-in-hand robotic camera (IBM CRR manipulator), not view *selection from a discrete menu* — it computes one continuous-space optimum.

**The generalized viewpoint (the 8-D imaging space the framing doc references).** Sec. I-C, p. 73: "a viewpoint is considered to be the viewer location alone" is deemed insufficient; MVP instead defines a *generalized viewpoint*
`V(r_V, ν; d, f, a)`
with:
- **3 positional DOF** `r_V = (x_V, y_V, z_V)`: position of the lens's front nodal point.
- **2 orientational DOF**: unit vector `ν` along the viewing (optical) axis (pan+tilt only; the third rotational DOF — swing/roll about the optical axis — is explicitly *not* planned because the image plane is modeled as circular, diameter = min of the two image-plane dimensions).
- **3 optical/lens DOF**: `d` = back-nodal-point-to-image-plane distance (focus setting), `f` = intrinsic focal length, `a` = entrance pupil diameter (aperture).

Total = 3 + 2 + 3 = **8**, stated explicitly: "planning in MVP is done in eight-dimensional imaging space [21]" (p. 73, right column). This confirms the framing doc's "8‑D imaging space" claim exactly, for *this specific paper*. (A different, later survey paper by the same authors — "A Survey of Sensor Planning in Computer Vision," Tarabanis/Allen/Tsai, same T‑RA issue, pp. 86–105 — reportedly extends to a 12-parameter model with 3 additional sensitivity/illumination parameters; that is a distinct document, not this one, and was not fetched here.)

**Four feature-detectability constraints, each characterized analytically as a *locus* (admissible region) in this 8-D space** (Sec. II):

1. **Visibility** (Sec. II-A): the region of viewpoints from which all target features are unoccluded, computed via a **constructive solid geometry (CSG)** representation of the occluded region as a union of pairwise convex-polygon occlusion terms, built from a boundary-representation solid modeler (TGMS/GDP). Admissibility test = **point-in-polyhedron classification** (ray-cast from the candidate viewpoint, count face crossings) — i.e., visibility is tested as a *discrete inside/outside membership query* against a precomputed 3-D solid, not a continuous formula.
2. **Resolution** (Sec. II-B, Eq. 1): for a linear feature `AB` of length `l` that must image to at least `w` (in image-plane length units) at framebuffer-pixel resolution,
   `g1: | [(r_A − r_V) × u_l] × ν | / [ (r_A−r_V)·ν · (r_B−r_V)·ν ] − w/(d·l) ≥ 0`
   where `u_l` is the unit vector along `AB`, `d` is the back-nodal-to-image-plane distance. This is a magnification/GSD-style constraint (bigger standoff along `ν` shrinks the image), but it is **not phrased as a cosine-of-incidence term** — MVP's "features" are points/edges of polyhedra, not oriented surface patches, so there is no explicit surface-normal-vs-view-ray angle anywhere in this constraint.
3. **Focus / depth of field** (Sec. II-C, Eqs. 2–5): the nearest (`r_c`) and farthest (`r_f`) feature vertices along the viewing direction must lie between near/far depth-of-field limits `D2`, `D1`:
   `g2a: (r_c − r_V)·ν − D2 ≥ 0`, `g2b: D1 − (r_f − r_V)·ν ≥ 0`
   `D1 = a·f·d / (a(d−f) − c·f)`, `D2 = a·f·d / (a(d−f) + c·f)` (`c` = acceptable blur-circle diameter, `a` = entrance pupil diameter, `f` = focal length, `d` = image-plane distance).
4. **Field of view** (Sec. II-D, Eq. 6): `g3: (r_K − r_V)·ν − ||r_K − r_V||·cos(α/2) ≥ 0`, where `α = 2·tan⁻¹(I_min/2d)` is the FOV half-angle (`I_min` = smallest active sensor-area dimension), and `r_K = r_C − R0·ν` with `R0 = R_f/sin(α/2)`, `r_C`/`R_f` = center/radius of the sphere circumscribing all target features. This constrains the viewpoint to lie inside the cone whose apex half-angle is the sensor's FOV.
5. **Unit-vector normalization** (Eq. 11): `g5: ||ν||² − 1 = 0` (equality constraint, kept separate from the four inequality constraints above).

## The optimization formulation (directly answers the branch's "best view within a neighbourhood" question, RQ14)

Sec. III separates **admissibility** (all `gi ≥ 0`, `g5 = 0` — i.e., "does this viewpoint satisfy the hard requirements at all") from **optimality/margin** (Sec. III-C, p. 76): "a solution is considered preferable if it satisfies the constraints with a greater degree of tolerance (i.e., more comfortably)." The scalar value of each `gi` *is itself* the margin — e.g. `g4` (visibility) is defined (Eqs. 12–14) as the **signed Euclidean distance** from the viewpoint to the visibility-region boundary (`+d_V` inside, `−d_V` outside), and an approximate ray-based version `g4 = k·min_i d_{r_i}` is offered for cheaper computation (`k ∈ {1,−1,0}` for inside/outside/on-boundary, `d_{r_i}` = distance along ray `i` from viewpoint to boundary).

**Objective (Eq. 15):** `h = max( α1·g1 + α2a·g2a + α2b·g2b + α3·g3 + α4·g4 )` subject to `gi ≥ 0` (Eq. 16) and `g5 = 0` (Eq. 17) — a single **weighted-sum scalarization** of five heterogeneous constraint-slack terms (mixed units: mm distances for `g2a,g2b,g4`; a dimensionless magnification-margin for `g1`; an angular-cosine difference for `g3`).

- **Weights are hand-tuned, not principled** (p. 77, Sec. III-D): "weights `αi`... are currently chosen so that the contribution of each constraint to the objective function is of the same order of magnitude **at the start of the optimization**... determination of the appropriate weights is a problem of its own [6]." Explicit admission that the resulting solution can be biased and that weights may need per-run "calibration."
- **Explicit negative finding on weighted-sum composition** (p. 77, Sec. III-D, final paragraph): "This constrained optimization formulation constitutes a simple integration scheme based on the assumption that multiple and coupled objectives can be combined into a single global objective... The problem may also be posed as one of multiple objective optimization... For simplicity the problem has currently been formulated as one of global objective optimization. The result of the optimization will be locally optimal only with respect to the chosen objective function. **This result will generally be different than the optimal generated by a multiple objective approach.**" This is a direct, citable, author-acknowledged limitation of exactly the "score = weighted sum of several terms" pattern the branch is evaluating (relevant to RQ6, redundant/correlated terms).
- **Not a bounded/normalized score** (relevant to RQ8): `h` is an unbounded weighted sum of raw physical-unit slack values with a lower admissibility bound of 0 per term but no fixed range or normalization; comparing `h` values across different objects/scenes (different feature sets, different `R_f`, different units per constraint) is not meaningfully done anywhere in the paper.
- **Local, not global, optimization**: solved with **NCONF** (IMSL library), a **sequential quadratic programming (SQP)** nonlinear-constrained solver, seeded from a hand-chosen initial guess. This is literally "argmax of a margin function in a neighborhood of an initial point," matching the branch's characterization of MVP as "the 1995 form of RQ1's best-within-its-neighbouring-region." It is explicitly **not** a global search: "a generalized viewpoint is sought that is central to the admissible domain" *relative to where the optimization started* (Sec. III-A/D).
- **Explicit non-smoothness admission** (p. 77, top of Sec. III-D): "Strictly speaking this assumption [continuous differentiability] is not satisfied by all the constraint relationships... in the case of the focus constraint, as the viewing direction varies, **different feature points become the farthest and nearest points along this viewing direction, thus leading to a discontinuity in the associated constraint.**" This is a precise, citable mechanism for RQ15 ("is the view-quality landscape smooth enough to climb?") — not just visibility but *any* constraint defined as a min/max over a discrete feature/vertex set has combinatorial switching discontinuities. No smoothing trick is proposed for it in this paper (no soft-min, no fuzzy filter) — the SQP solver is simply run anyway, with manual restarts on premature termination (see Experiments below).

## Concrete results and numbers (Sec. IV)

- **Sensor:** Javelin CCD camera, 480 (vertical) × 384 (horizontal) sensor elements; element spacing 23 µm (H) × 13.5 µm (V); horizontal sensor-to-framebuffer pixel scale factor 0.70642 (from calibration [14]). **Robot:** IBM CRR (Cleanroom Robot), two 7-joint manipulators (3 linear XYZ + 3 rotary roll/pitch/yaw + gripper).
- **Object** (Fig. 4–5): a machined block with two nested rectangular through-holes (small hole `SH`, large hole `LH`) exposing two edges of an internal cube — assembled from primitive shapes (cubes, parallelepipeds) specifically so the object geometry could be reconfigured across trials.
- **Numeric worked example (Eq. 18):** resolution spec = 1 framebuffer pixel per 0.1 in of minimum feature size (`l = 0.1 in`); diagonal sensor-plane pixel spacing computed as `w = sqrt((23×0.70642)² + 13.5²) = 21.12 µm`. `I_min ≈ 6.5 mm` (from 480×13.5 µm and 384×21 µm active sensor dimensions — used in the FOV half-angle formula). Circle-of-confusion `c` taken = 13.5 µm (min sensor element spacing). Aperture `a = f/16`, focal length `f = 12.5 mm` fixed a priori (not optimized — only continuous parameters `x_V,y_V,z_V,ν,d` were optimized; `f,a` were pre-set because available lenses only cover a discrete set of focal lengths).
- **Experiment 1 — visibility excluded from the objective (α4 = 0)** (Table I, Fig. 9–10): initial guess `V_i = (165.1, −279.4, 368.3) mm`, `ν = (−0.336, 0.569, −0.750)` → optimized `V_f = (107.58, −231.43, 258.68) mm`, `ν = (−0.389, 0.694, −0.608)`, `d = 13.07 mm`. `V_f` was *checked post hoc* against the visibility region and happened to fall inside it — but **the real captured image (Fig. 12/14) shows the horizontal edge "very close to being occluded by the overhang"** (p. 82, discussion) — i.e. nominally "satisfied" but with near-zero margin, explicitly used by the authors to argue **visibility must be inside the objective**, not just checked as a post-hoc filter.
- **Experiments 2–3 — visibility included (α4 = 1, α1 = 10², α2a = α2b = 10⁻², α3 = 1)** (Tables II–III, Fig. 10): two different, deliberately infeasible initial guesses. `V_i1 = (228.6, 0.0, 368.3) mm`, `ν = (0,0,−1)` — starts *inside* the visibility region but violates FOV and focus. `V_i2 = (2.54, 2.54, 2.54) mm`, `ν = (0,0,−1)` — starts *outside* visibility and violates every constraint. Both converge to feasible, "central" solutions: `V_f1 = (197.10, 100.58, 330.45) mm`, `ν=(−0.513,−0.255,−0.820)`, `d=12.84 mm`; `V_f2 = (112.78, 110.49, 159.77) mm`, `ν=(−0.444,−0.583,−0.680)`, `d=13.25 mm`.
- **Verification**: resolution is quantitatively verified from the captured image — the two feature edges measured **45 and 39 framebuffer pixels** for a 1‑in edge, comfortably exceeding the 1‑pixel/0.1‑in spec (i.e. ~4–4.5 px per 0.1 in achieved vs. 1 px required — large margin). Focus/DOF satisfaction is only verified **qualitatively** by visual inspection of the image ("A quantitative measure of focus will also be employed in future work" — p. 82, an admitted gap). FOV satisfaction is likewise checked visually since the graphical-simulation renderer wasn't configured to match the real camera's FOV limits exactly.
- **No iteration count or wall-clock runtime is reported anywhere in the paper** for the SQP optimization — a gap worth flagging if the branch wants "does gradient-based local refinement stay fast in a small neighbourhood" (RQ16): this paper gives no timing evidence either way.
- **Practical sim-to-real gap**: the optimizer's computed focus parameter `d` (13.07 mm and 13.25 mm in two of the three trials) **lay outside the physical lens's native focusing range** and required a **manual lens extension tube** to realize (p. 81, Sec. IV-C) — a concrete example of a continuous-optimization output that isn't directly actuable by real hardware without an extra physical-compatibility constraint that MVP did not model ("If the sensor operating range is ignored, the computed sensor parameter values are typically not simultaneously achievable by the sensor at hand," p. 78).
- **Optimizer robustness caveat** (p. 80): "Adjustment of the initial guess point or the weights was needed in cases of premature program termination due to errors associated with attaining the maximum number of iterations, not satisfying the optimality condition due to roundoff errors, or not finding a search direction while the current iterate is still infeasible." I.e., the local SQP solver sometimes needed manual restarts — a negative finding on out-of-the-box robustness of gradient-based local refinement for this constraint set.

## Load-bearing assumptions

- **Polyhedral/CAD geometry with known, exact feature locations** (points, line segments, faces) — MVP consumes a solid CAD model with named target features, not a noisy survey point cloud. There is no measurement-uncertainty model anywhere in the constraint formulas.
- **Pinhole/thick-lens camera model** with front/back nodal points, entrance pupil, and a circular image-plane simplification (swing DOF dropped) — a **passive monocular CCD camera**, not a stereo or structured-light depth sensor. No active-stereo or projector physics of any kind.
- **Task = feature detectability of discrete polyhedral primitives (points/edges/faces)**, not surface-normal alignment or "orthogonality to a surface region." Consequently **incidence angle / surface-normal-vs-view-ray alignment is not a term in this model at all** — a directly relevant negative-transfer finding for the branch (see below).
- **All four detectability requirements assumed simultaneously achievable and combinable via a linear weighted sum** — flagged by the authors themselves as a simplification with known consequences (see Sec. III-D quote above).
- **Continuous differentiability assumed by the SQP solver but not actually guaranteed** by the constraint set (focus constraint's vertex-switching discontinuity, explicitly admitted).

## What transfers to our setting

- **The "generalized viewpoint as an 8-D point with a margin-based objective, refined by local constrained optimization from a seed" pattern is a clean, citable precedent for RQ14** ("best view within a neighbourhood"). MVP's answer: optimality = maximize the minimum (or weighted sum) of *constraint slack values themselves*, each slack being a signed distance/margin to an infeasibility boundary, using local SQP from an initial guess. This is directly reusable as *one* formal definition of "best within a neighbourhood" to compare against viewpoint-stability/mutual-information framings and pure gradient-magnitude framings (RQ14 explicitly asks for this three-way comparison — MVP supplies one leg of it with full mathematical detail).
- **Concrete, citable, closed-form constraint functions** (Eqs. 1–6, 11) for resolution, focus/DOF, and FOV give the branch working formulas for three of the term-table rows in RQ5 (resolution/GSD-style term, depth-of-field/standoff term, FOV/framing term) — all in PLY/CAD space, all with explicit inputs (feature points, view position, `ν`, lens params).
- **Visibility-as-signed-distance-to-a-CSG-boundary** (Eqs. 12–14) is a directly relevant, older precedent for RQ11 ("how is visibility/self-occlusion computed without a surface?") — though MVP assumes a *solid* CAD model (a real surface with a well-defined interior/exterior), which our defective survey PLY does not have; this is exactly the gap the branch's hidden-point-removal question is trying to fill for point clouds instead of solids.
- **Explicit, author-acknowledged critique of weighted-sum multi-term scoring** (Sec. III-D final paragraph, quoted above) is directly citable evidence for RQ6 ("is a score summing correlated terms one term with extra steps?") — even the original authors flag that their own weighted-sum result differs from what a proper multi-objective (e.g. Pareto) treatment would produce, and that weight selection is itself an unsolved sub-problem citing a separate optimization-weights reference [6] in their own bibliography.
- **The non-smoothness/discontinuity admission for a feature-set-dependent min/max constraint** (focus constraint, Sec. III-D) is a precise, generalizable mechanism for RQ15's "visibility is discontinuous in camera pose" catalogue — it shows the same failure mode also strikes *any* constraint computed as an argmin/argmax over a discrete point/vertex set (e.g., depth-of-field near/far point, and by direct analogy, "worst point" pooling for defect detectability per RQ9) as the viewing direction sweeps continuously.
- **A concrete, quantified example of a computed-but-physically-unrealizable optical parameter** (the `d` focus-distance values falling outside the lens's native range, requiring hardware lens extensions) is a useful cautionary precedent for RQ13/RQ17-adjacent concerns about scores computed in an idealized space not matching what the physical rig can realize.

## What does NOT transfer, and why

- **No incidence-angle / surface-orthogonality term exists in this paper at all.** MVP's task is feature (point/edge) detectability, not surface-normal alignment; nowhere in Eqs. 1–14 does a surface normal or a cosine-of-incidence appear. This paper **cannot answer** the branch's core RQ1 numeric question (D405 depth error vs. incidence angle), RQ2 (smooth-vs-threshold angle penalty), or RQ3 (specular retroreflection at normal incidence) — it simply does not model surface orientation as a first-class quantity. Its relevance is entirely architectural/formal (how to define and combine viewpoint-quality constraints), not physical/sensor-specific.
- **No depth sensor, no active stereo, no RGB-D error model of any kind.** The camera is a passive monocular CCD with a fixed-focal-length lens; none of the visibility/resolution/focus/FOV math generalizes to stereo-correspondence or structured-light depth noise. Zero transfer to RQ1's core ask.
- **No defect detectability, no raking-light/glancing-angle visibility content, no industrial-inspection framing of "what makes a scratch visible."** Task = geometric feature detectability (can the sensor resolve/see/focus on a known CAD edge), not surface-defect visibility. Zero transfer to RQ4.
- **No viewpoint entropy, saliency, information-gain/NBV utility, or image-space judging of any kind** — this is a pre-NBV, pre-entropy-era (1995) constraint-satisfaction paper predating that literature; it does not appear anywhere in the References ([1]–[37], checked). Zero transfer to RQ5's entropy/saliency/information-gain rows, RQ18–21 (VLM/image-space judging).
- **Assumes an exact, complete CAD solid model with named features and a well-posed visibility solid**, not a fused, defective survey point cloud (mm pose error, dropout holes, no mesh, ~4 cm fused-vs-single-view offset per our framing doc). None of MVP's visibility/CSG machinery, nor its assumption of an exact object pose (obtained here via a dedicated camera-calibration step, Appendix Sec. VI-A, not from noisy survey data), addresses uncertainty propagation from an imperfect input geometry into the view score — this paper offers **no answer to RQ12** (propagating input geometry uncertainty into the score) and implicitly assumes the input is uncertainty-free once the object pose is calibrated.
- **Weighted-sum scoring is unbounded and unnormalized** (see above) — this paper cannot serve as precedent for RQ8's "bounded, normalized, cross-view/cross-object comparable score" requirement; if anything it is a cautionary example of the opposite (hand-tuned, run-specific weights, raw mixed-unit sum).
- **Small, hand-picked test object and no benchmark/ablation protocol.** A single reconfigurable primitive-block object, three optimization trials, no comparison against alternative scoring functions or against an information-gain baseline, no statistics beyond the three (initial, final) viewpoint pairs in Tables I–III. This paper is not usable as a benchmark-protocol precedent for RQ23–25 (Branch 2 handoff) beyond noting that its "object built from primitives, reconfigurable across trials" methodology (p. 78) is conceptually well-aligned with our own primitive-shape regime — closer in spirit to our setting than most later Stanford-model-benchmarked NBV papers, even though it predates that literature and offers no formal benchmark protocol to imitate.

## Answers mapped to specific branch questions

- **RQ14 (formal "best view within a neighbourhood" definitions)** — MVP's answer in full: constrained optimization (SQP/NCONF) over an 8-D generalized-viewpoint space, admissibility = all constraint slacks ≥ 0, optimality = maximize a hand-weighted sum of the slacks themselves (a margin/robustness criterion), started from and confined to the neighborhood of a user-supplied initial guess. This is the literal 1995-era version of "most orthogonal AND within tolerance AND locally optimal," except MVP has no orthogonality term to optimize.
- **RQ6 (redundant terms / weighted-sum critique)** — Direct citable quote (Sec. III-D) that the authors' own weighted-sum result is *not* equivalent to a proper multi-objective optimum, and that weight determination is an open problem.
- **RQ8 (bounded/normalized score)** — Negative precedent: MVP's `h` is explicitly unbounded and unnormalized, requiring manual, run-specific weight tuning; not usable as a "how to normalize" template, only as a cautionary counter-example.
- **RQ15 (smoothness of the view-quality landscape)** — Concrete, author-acknowledged discontinuity mechanism: focus constraint switches which feature vertex is nearest/farthest as viewing direction varies continuously, breaking differentiability; visibility is a discrete point-in-polyhedron test outside of its (continuous but non-differentiable-at-boundary) signed-distance approximation. No smoothing trick offered.
- **RQ16 (sampling vs. gradient for small local refinement)** — MVP uses gradient-based local SQP, not sampling, but reports no runtime/iteration numbers and admits the solver sometimes needs manual restarts (premature termination) — so this paper supports "gradient-based local refinement is the classical choice" but supplies no speed evidence for or against it.
- **RQ5 (term table)** — Contributes rows: *visibility/self-occlusion* (PLY/CAD-space, point-in-polyhedron + signed distance to a CSG boundary, Eqs. 12–14), *resolution/magnification* (PLY-space, Eq. 1), *depth-of-field/focus* (PLY-space, Eqs. 2–5), *field-of-view/framing* (PLY-space, Eq. 6). No incidence/cosine, sampling-density, information-gain, viewpoint-entropy, surface-normal-entropy, or defect-detectability rows.
- **RQ1–4, RQ17–21 (D405 depth-error curve, angle-penalty shape, specular retroreflection, defect detectability, image-space judging, VLM-as-judge)** — Not addressed by this paper at all; must be sourced elsewhere (as already reflected in this branch's other reading notes on Soudarissanane, D405 datasheet, etc.).
