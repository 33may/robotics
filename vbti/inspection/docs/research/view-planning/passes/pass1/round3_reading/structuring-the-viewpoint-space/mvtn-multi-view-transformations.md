arXiv:2212.13462v2 [cs.CV], 6 Jun 2024 (IJCV journal extension; preliminary version at ICCV 2021, Hamdi et al. 2021). KAUST. CC BY 4.0. Full text obtained via arXiv HTML (`arxiv.org/html/2212.13462v2`), read end-to-end including the supplementary IJCV-extension sections (§7–10.4); references list skimmed only for citation resolution.

## Core method

MVTN is a small point-cloud encoder + regressor `G: R^{P×3} → R^τ` that predicts per-shape camera view-points, trained **jointly and end-to-end** with a downstream multi-view task network `C` through a differentiable renderer `R` (PyTorch3D), with **no extra supervision beyond the task loss** (classification cross-entropy or segmentation loss) — §2.2, Eq. (2):

```
argmin_{θ_C, θ_G}  Σ_n L( C( R(S_n, u_n) ), y_n )   s.t.  u_n = u_bound · tanh( G(S_n) )
```

- `S_n` = a 3D shape (mesh or point cloud); `G` = PointNet-style shared-MLP point encoder (2048 sampled points, feature dim `b=40`) + 4–5-layer MLP regressor.
- `u_n` ∈ R^τ, τ=2M, is the set of azimuth+elevation angles for M cameras pointing at the object center; `u_bound` = 180° (azimuth) / 90° (elevation), enforced by a `tanh` squash (§2.2, "View-Points Conditioned on 3D Shape").
- **M (number of view slots) is a fixed architecture hyperparameter, constant across every shape in the dataset** — geometry only moves *where* the M cameras point, never *how many* there are. This is the fixed-cardinality claim the branch selected this paper for. Distance-to-object was also tried as a learnable parameter and found to hurt or not help; it is fixed at 2.2 units in all main experiments (§10.1, "Learning Distance to the Object," Fig. 21).
- Two prediction modes (§3.4 "View-Point Prediction"): **MVTN-direct** regresses absolute `u` from shape features alone; **MVTN-circular/-spherical** regress an *offset* added to a fixed canonical initial configuration `u0` (12-view circular @ 30° elevation, or Deserno-2004 equal-area spherical placement, "dodecahedral" à la ViewGCN) — i.e., `u_n = u0 + u_bound.tanh(G(u0, S_n))`.
- Point encoder input is raw points (no primitive fitting, no CAD features, no part/face labels) — `G` never sees anything but xyz coordinates.
- MVTorch (github.com/ajhamdi/mvtorch, §5.7) is the released library: differentiable multi-view renderer for meshes and point clouds, view-selector module (MVTN/random/circular/spherical), dataloaders for ModelNet/ShapeNet/ScanObjectNN/S3DIS.

## Concrete results and numbers

- **ModelNet40 classification** (Table 1/14): MVTN 93.8% overall / 92.0% per-class at 12 views, vs. matched-renderer ViewGCN* 93.0%/90.7% at 12 views and 93.3%/91.3% at 20 views (ViewGCN with its original non-differentiable OpenGL renderer reaches 97.6%, not a fair comparison — §4.1).
- **ScanObjectNN classification** (Table 2/15, real point-cloud scans w/ background+occlusion): MVTN 92.6% (OBJ_BG) / 92.3% (OBJ_ONLY) / **82.8%** (PB_T50_RS hardest, translation+rotation perturbed) — state of the art on the hardest split by **+2.6%** over BGA-PN++'s 80.2% (§4.1).
- **Retrieval mAP** (Table 3/16): ModelNet40 92.9%, ShapeNet Core55 82.9%, both SOTA using only 12 views (§4.2).
- **Rotation robustness** (Table 4, random test-time Y-axis rotation): MVTN 91.7% / 90.8% / 91.2% at 0°/±90°/±180° perturbation, vs. PointNet 88.7/42.5/38.6 — MVTN degrades far less under viewpoint-unaligned test rotation than point-based baselines (§4.3).
- **Occlusion robustness** (Table 5, object cropped 0–75% along axes): MVTN 92.3/90.3/89.9/88.3/**67.1**/9.5 vs. PointNet 89.1/88.2/86.1/81.6/53.5/4.7 — **+13% accuracy over PointNet at 50% occlusion** (§4.4).
- **Direct geometry-conditioned vs. uniform-sphere comparison, same renderer/backbone** (Table 1, matched setup): 12 views, fixed spherical ViewGCN* 93.0% vs. learned MVTN 93.8% (Δ+0.8%); 20 views, 93.3% vs. 93.5% (Δ+0.2%) — **benefit shrinks as view count grows** (consistent with Fig. 7's reported "~2% improvement... across a variety of views," though the matched-table deltas at higher M are smaller than 2%).
- **Optimizing scene parameters instead of learning a network** (§4.5, Table 6, IJCV-extension-only experiment): directly SGD-optimizing azimuth/elevation per batch (maximizing/minimizing CE, coverage, or adversarial loss) on ScanObjectNN gives, e.g. on the hardest split: baseline-no-noise 72.83, baseline-with-random-noise 78.52, maximize-CE 74.95, minimize-CE 75.19, maximize-coverage 75.75, maximize-adversarial 76.89 — **none of the optimization variants beats the naive noise baseline**, and all are well below the learned-MVTN 82.8% from Table 2. Direct online optimization of view parameters (no learned regressor) is a documented failure mode, not a viable substitute for the learned network in this pipeline.
- **Late-fusion ablation** (§5.1): naively max-pooling PointNet shape features together with MVCNN features (rather than using them to steer the camera) gets 88.4% vs. MVTN's 91.8% on the same setup — having shape features available is not sufficient; using them to *place the camera* is what earns the gain.
- **MVTN module cost is negligible relative to the backbone** (Table 12): MVTN regressor alone 18.52K FLOPs / 0.9ms; full MVTN incl. PointNet encoder 1.78G FLOPs / 3.5ms — vs. MVCNN 43.72G FLOPs/39.89ms and ViewGCN 44.19G FLOPs/26.06ms. Per-shape *inference* is cheap; this cost figure says nothing about training cost (see below).
- **Transferability across downstream networks** (§10.2): a 12-view MVTN-spherical trained jointly with MVCNN, then frozen and its predicted views handed to a separately-trained ViewGCN, reaches 93.1% (vs. vanilla fixed-view ViewGCN's 92.4%, +0.7%; vs. fully joint MVTN+ViewGCN's higher number, −0.3%). Learned views generalize somewhat across which classifier consumes them, but the paper does not claim or test transfer across *object categories* not seen in training, nor across datasets.
- **Per-instance vs. per-class-averaged views** (§10.3): a 4-view MVTN gets 91.0% with genuinely per-instance views, 90.6% using the *class-averaged* fixed view for every instance of that class, and 89% using the original fixed generic (class-agnostic) views. Per-instance conditioning beats per-class conditioning beats no conditioning, but the gap between per-instance and per-class-averaged is small (0.4%) — most of MVTN's benefit over a fixed sphere comes from *category-level* geometry adaptation, not fine per-instance placement.
- **MVTN-direct instability** (§5.2, §10.1): visualized as azimuth/elevation histograms per class (Fig. 8/22), MVTN-direct with unconstrained absolute-angle prediction "suffers from over-fitting when the number of views is larger than four... the predicted view-points tend to be similar to each other" — i.e., without an anchor to a fixed base configuration, the learned views collapse toward each other at higher M and hurt generalization. Only the circular/spherical offset variants (anchored to a pre-spread canonical base) avoid this collapse.
- **Part segmentation** (§5.4, Table 11): MVTN improvement over fixed views is only "marginal," and shrinks further as view count grows (Fig. 9) — the classification-task gains do not transfer cleanly to a per-pixel/per-point task.
- **Large-scale scenes** (§5.5): explicitly reported as *not solved* — "it is tricky to place the camera viewpoints... because... there is no clear 'object of interest'"; the paper only speculates MVTN "can in theory mitigate this," with no experiment given.

## Load-bearing assumptions

- **Requires a large labeled dataset of many similar shapes to train against.** MVTN is trained via task loss (classification cross-entropy on ModelNet40's 9,843 train shapes/40 classes, or retrieval/segmentation losses) — the point encoder `G` learns to predict good views only by seeing gradients backpropagated through the renderer and the downstream classifier across thousands of labeled examples (§3.4 Training Setup: 100 epochs for MVCNN, a 50+35-epoch two-stage schedule for ViewGCN). There is no mechanism to produce a view prediction for a *single novel object with no labels and no comparable training set* — which is exactly our situation (one surveyed PLY per inspection session, no class labels, no dataset of similar tabletop parts).
- **Requires a differentiable renderer over the actual shape representation** (mesh rasterizer+shader, or point-cloud alpha-blending, both via PyTorch3D) — the gradient path is image→scene-params, so the shape must be renderable and the loss must flow through pixels. ScanObjectNN point clouds are rendered directly with no fitted primitives.
- **Presupposes canonical dataset-level pose alignment.** §2.1 "Canonical Views": "the fixed camera view-points are usually selected based on the alignment of the 3D models in the dataset," and MVTN's `u_bound`/offset design is defined relative to that pre-existing canonical frame (ModelNet40/ShapeNet ship pre-aligned/upright-normalized, as is standard for these benchmarks). The rotation-robustness experiment (§4.3, Table 4) tests robustness to *test-time* perturbation away from this canonical alignment — it is not evidence that canonical alignment is unnecessary at *training* time; MVTN never learns to discover its own up-axis or front/back convention from scratch.
- **The optimization objective is downstream-task accuracy, never a geometric or interpretability criterion.** The string "orthogonal" does not appear anywhere in the paper (confirmed by full-text search); no loss term, ablation, or discussion targets surface-orthogonality, framing, or any human-interpretable view-quality criterion. Views are whatever the gradient of the classification/retrieval loss says to pick — e.g., the paper's own illustrative example is avoiding "looking at a bed from the bottom," which is a classification-*failure-avoidance* argument, not a view-quality argument.

## What transfers to our setting

- **The fixed-cardinality existence proof itself is real and directly evidenced**: M is chosen once at design time (the paper reports results for M = 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 20 across ablations) and stays constant per trained model while the *placement* of those M views is geometry-conditioned per shape (Fig. 3, Fig. 8/22 histograms show per-instance, per-class-varying azimuth/elevation distributions). This is solid evidence that "N addressable slots, geometry decides where they point" is an achievable, trained pattern — useful as a data point for the branch's "bounded-cardinality guarantee" question, at the level of *architecture design*, not as a technique we could lift as-is.
- **Matched-condition uniform-sphere-vs-geometry-conditioned comparison, on the same task/backbone/renderer, exists and favors the geometry-conditioned space** (Table 1: 93.0→93.8% at 12 views; Table 2: on the harder/noisier ScanObjectNN split the margin is larger, +2.6% over the next best method). This is directly responsive to the branch's benchmark-tag question: **yes, a matched geometry-conditioned-vs-uniform-sphere comparison with a metric does exist in the literature** — it just measures classification accuracy, not orthogonality/inspection quality, and the margin shrinks as view count grows, suggesting diminishing returns from adaptive placement once a uniform set is already dense.
- **The negative optimization-vs-learning result is directly relevant to our "analytical vs. optimization track" framing.** §4.5/Table 6 is a controlled internal experiment (same architecture, same task, same dataset) comparing *learning a regressor end-to-end* against *directly optimizing scene parameters* — and directly optimizing loses even to a noise baseline. This is evidence (from a closely analogous problem, not ours) that naive direct optimization of view parameters against a task loss is fragile and can underperform doing nothing; if our optimization track ends up computing gradients of a similar surrogate objective directly rather than learning any structure, this result is a caution flag, not a proof either way for our PLY-space objective.
- **The per-instance-vs-per-class result (§10.3) is a useful calibration point for RQ2/RQ3's "does geometry conditioning need to be per-object or is per-primitive-class enough" question**: MVTN shows most of the benefit of geometry-conditioning is already captured by *category-level* geometry (per-class-averaged views get 90.6% vs. per-instance 91.0%, vs. 89% generic), consistent with our own primitive-level (box/cylinder) deterministic-rule intuition — you likely don't need per-instance-fitted continuous optimization to capture most of the win; a primitive-class-conditioned deterministic scheme (box → face-normal views, cylinder → orbit) may already capture the bulk of what fine per-instance optimization would add.
- **MVTN's negative result on `MVTN-direct` collapsing views at higher M (§5.2/§10.1) is a concrete warning for any scheme that lets a model freely place several views with no anchor/ordering constraint**: without something tying each address/slot to a distinct canonical role, the learned placements can converge onto each other and lose the intended coverage — an argument, from evidence (not just intuition), for anchoring each address to a fixed canonical role (a named face, a named orbit angle) rather than free per-slot regression, which is precisely what a primitive-face/orbit addressing scheme already does structurally.

## What does NOT transfer, and why (negative findings)

- **MVTN is not a discrete-addressing scheme at all — it is pure continuous regression, with no analog anywhere in the paper to a symbolic address `k` resolved to a pose `x_k`.** There is no discrete "menu," no node ID, no face/part label; the only thing resembling an "address" is the array index `i ∈ {1..M}` of a regression output, and nothing in the architecture or training constrains that index to carry a stable geometric meaning across shapes — no experiment tests or claims that "slot 3" points at a consistent semantic direction for different object categories. This directly answers the branch's classification question (a/b/c) for MVTN: **it fits none of the three cleanly**; it is closer to "none," since even the node-ID category (c) presumes *some* discoverable relational meaning, which MVTN neither claims nor demonstrates.
- **No stable ordering, adjacency, or seam semantics of any kind are discussed.** The word "adjacency" does not appear in the paper; there is no ring/nested numbering, no next/previous relation between slots, and no treatment of what happens at tessellation-like defects — this document is silent on essentially all of the branch's adjacency/ordering/seam questions (Q4, Q5, Q7). Whatever ordering exists in the circular/spherical variants is entirely inherited from the fixed canonical base configuration they offset from, not learned or guaranteed by MVTN itself.
- **Requires per-task, per-dataset end-to-end training over many labeled shapes before it can predict anything for a new object — there is no per-object, session-time "compile" mode.** This is the single largest mismatch with our setting: we have one surveyed PLY per inspection session, real (not CAD) defects, no class labels, and no large corpus of similar tabletop parts to jointly train a point encoder + classifier against. MVTN gives no evidence about (and provides no mechanism for) predicting anything useful from a single novel noisy point cloud with no training distribution behind it.
- **The training signal is classification/retrieval accuracy, never surface-orthogonality, framing, or any inspection-relevant geometric criterion** — confirmed by full-text search finding zero occurrences of "orthogonal" anywhere in the paper. A view MVTN learns to prefer is whatever helps a downstream softmax distinguish object categories (its own illustrative example, avoiding a confusing bottom-up view of a bed, is a classification-confusion argument, not an orthogonality argument); nothing here transfers as evidence toward RQ1's "how orthogonal is this view" scoring problem.
- **No primitive/part structure of any kind is used or produced.** The point encoder operates on raw, unlabeled points with no face/segment/primitive decomposition — this paper offers zero evidence, positive or negative, about whether the technique would work on primitive-fitted noisy survey clouds (the branch's "does it tolerate primitives fitted to a noisy cloud" question is simply unaddressed, not answered).
- **Canonical-frame alignment is presupposed, not solved.** MVTN never discovers an object's up-axis or front/side convention; it operates entirely relative to a dataset-level canonical alignment that ModelNet40/ShapeNet provide for free. The rotation-robustness experiment (Table 4) is evidence of robustness to a specific, narrow perturbation (test-time random rotation about the known gravity/Y axis only) applied after training on canonically-aligned data — it is not evidence that the front/side disambiguation problem the branch asks about is solved or unnecessary.
- **"Bounded cardinality" is a designer choice, not a geometric guarantee, and slot semantics are not shown to be shape-invariant.** The paper never varies M *by* object complexity (a chair and an airplane get the same M in a given trained model); nothing in the method adapts cardinality to how many "natural" views an object's geometry has (our box-vs-cylinder-vs-assembly variable-arity question, branch Q12/Q13, gets no support from this paper beyond the general architectural pattern of "M is fixed at design time").

## Assessment for the branch

MVTN earns its selection as the fixed-cardinality existence proof — it is real, evidenced across multiple M values, and does show geometry-conditioned placement beating a matched uniform sphere on a real task with real numbers (Table 1/2), which is also useful as one of the few located instances of the exact geometry-conditioned-vs-uniform-sphere benchmark comparison the branch was asked to check for. But it is an existence proof for the *narrowest* possible reading of "geometry-conditioned view set": continuous, per-shape, task-loss-trained camera-angle regression with no discrete addressing layer, no adjacency, no ordering, no interpretability criterion, and no primitive structure whatsoever — it does not, and structurally cannot, answer any of the branch's address-classification, adjacency, seam, or cross-shape-meaning-stability questions, because it has no notion of address beyond an unconstrained array index. Its most transferable content for us is negative/cautionary: naive direct optimization of view parameters underperformed even a noise baseline in-paper (§4.5), and unconstrained per-slot regression collapsed views together at higher counts (§10.1) — both are evidence-based arguments for anchoring any view-parameterization scheme to fixed canonical roles (named faces, named orbit angles) rather than letting placement float freely, which is exactly the direction the primitive-level deterministic-rule branch of our own design already points.
