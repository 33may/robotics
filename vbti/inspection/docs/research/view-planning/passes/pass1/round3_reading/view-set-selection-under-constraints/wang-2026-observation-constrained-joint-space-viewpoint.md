# Observation-Constrained Joint-Space Viewpoint Optimization for Robotic Inspection of Cylindrical Cavities

- **arXiv**: 2608.16442v1 [cs.RO]
- **Authors**: Yuezhong Wang, Rongshen Yin, Bichi Zhang, Sören Schwertfeger
- **Source used**: full HTML (`arxiv.org/html/2608.16442v1`), fetched and parsed directly, multiple targeted passes (abstract, full method, related work II-B verbatim, baseline definitions, limitations verbatim). Section/table/equation numbers below are as in the paper.
- **Domain**: RoboCup Rescue Robot League-style inspection of a **cylindrical cavity's interior** (per ASTM E2853/E2853M-22): a 6-DoF arm must position an eye-in-hand camera to see through a narrow circular opening and expose a target fraction of the cavity's bottom. This is **axial look-into-a-hole inspection**, not orbital look-at-an-exterior-surface inspection — see "What does NOT transfer" below, this distinction matters a lot for how this paper should be cited.

## Core idea (Abstract, III-A, III-B)

Standard manipulation pipelines "first choose a Cartesian target and then invoke an inverse-kinematics (IK) solver" (Introduction). The paper's objection: this decomposition throws away reachable, high-quality views whenever the nominal/idealized Cartesian pose isn't the one that turns out to be kinematically best — "a configuration that is not closest to a nominal camera pose may provide a better executable view after joint limits and collision-free connectivity are considered."

Their fix: don't pick a Cartesian pose at all. Represent the inspection objective as **"a set of valid viewing geometries"** (a region, not a point) and search directly over robot joint configurations `q`, evaluating observation feasibility straight from forward kinematics: "Forward kinematics maps each joint configuration to an eye-in-hand camera pose, from which observation feasibility can be evaluated without first constructing a Cartesian goal" (III-A). This is explicitly framed as avoiding "the rejection of reachable viewpoints and configurations with poor joint-limit margins."

**Important nuance on what "holds by construction" actually means (correcting the task brief's framing):** only **observation constraints (visibility) and joint-limit constraints** are baked into the optimizer's hard-cost term and therefore hold by construction for the winning configuration. **Collision geometry and trajectory connectivity are explicitly delegated to MoveIt** as a separate downstream stage (III-B: "The optimizer handles observation geometry and joint limits; collision geometry and trajectory connectivity are delegated to MoveIt"). So collision-feasibility is *not* guaranteed by the joint-space search itself — it's a post-hoc filter, handled by trying the ranked candidate list in order until one plans successfully (III-F). The paper's real contribution is narrower than "visibility+kinematics+collision all hold by construction": it's "visibility+joint-limits hold by construction; collision is handled by a ranked-candidate fallback ladder, not eliminated as a failure mode." Evidence this matters: even the proposed method only reaches 92/100 end-to-end success (see Results) — 8% still fail somewhere in the collision-planning-or-visibility-verification chain despite the joint-space formulation.

## Method — pipeline (III-A, Fig. 2)

Perception (RGB) → observation-region definition → joint-space search returns **ranked list of up to 16 feasible configurations** → MoveIt tries them in rank order for a collision-free plan (advance to next candidate on planning failure) → post-execution visibility verification confirms/rejects success.

## Method — perception (III-C)

RGB-only front end (no depth used for the geometry estimate) fits an ellipse to the cavity rim via a restricted "rim band" mask (`dilate(M̂,k_b) − erode(M̂,k_b)`), recovers depth and inclination from the ellipse's major/minor axis ratio (`Z_e = f_x D / A_e`, `τ = arccos(B_e/A_e)`), and resolves the minor-axis sign ambiguity (which of two symmetric cavity-tilt hypotheses is correct) using a body-mask-based disambiguation score (`q_axis`, `q_body`, `q_center`, optionally a "side-generator-line" score `S_side`). This is a self-contained sub-contribution (arc-supported ellipse fitting under weak texture) — not something our PLY-based pipeline needs, we already have 3D geometry, not a monocular RGB inverse problem.

## Method — observation model and constraints (III-D)

Task representation: `M^C = {p_o^C, a^C, L, r_in}` (opening center, directed axis into cavity, cavity length, inner radius). Bottom center `p_b = p_o + L·a`.

Three observation error terms, all computed directly from forward kinematics `q → (R_c(q), p_c(q))`:
- **Axis error** `e_axis(q) = arccos(clip(z_c(q)·a, -1, 1))` — angle between camera boresight and cavity axis. This is the paper's orthogonality-type term: since the cavity bottom is (implicitly) a disk perpendicular to the axis, `e_axis` small ⇔ camera looks straight down the axis ⇔ camera is orthogonal to the bottom surface. **Directly analogous to our "deviation from surface-orthogonal" quantity, just for an axial rather than a lateral face.**
- **Lateral offset** `e_lat(q)` — perpendicular distance from the camera center to the cavity axis line.
- **Axial standoff** `d_axial(q)` — signed distance along `−a` from the opening plane to the camera.

**Valid observation region** (Eq., III-D): `C_obs = {q | e_axis(q) ≤ θ_max, e_lat(q) ≤ e_lat,max, d_min ≤ d_axial(q) ≤ d_max}`. Camera roll about the optical axis is explicitly left unconstrained ("camera roll can vary without changing whether the cavity bottom is visible") — a one-DoF slack the optimizer is free to use, relevant to our own "how much of the pose is actually constrained by the orthogonality requirement" question.

**Numeric thresholds used in experiments (IV-A):**
- `θ_max = 0.15 rad ≈ 8.6°` — tight axial/orthogonality tolerance.
- `e_lat,max = 0.005 m` (5 mm) — tight lateral tolerance.
- `d_axial ∈ [0.08 m, 0.38 m]`, with soft-preferred standoff `d_desired = 0.20 m`.
- `r_clear = 0.002 m` — inward clearance margin subtracted from the inner radius when testing bottom-point visibility.
- Target cavity: inner diameter 5 cm, outer diameter 6 cm, length 5.5 cm (L/D ≈ 1.1, a fairly deep, narrow aperture — this geometry, not defect size or FOV/overlap reasoning, is presumably what drives such a tight `θ_max`: a shallow rim + narrow bore occludes the bottom quickly under axis misalignment. The paper does not spell out this causal claim explicitly; it's the geometrically obvious reason but I did not find a sentence deriving `θ_max` from `L`, `D`, and FOV in closed form.)

## Method — feasibility-first joint-space search (III-E)

Two-tier lexicographic objective, evaluated purely in joint space via forward kinematics — **no IK inversion anywhere in the optimizer**:

- `J_hard(q)`: squared-hinge penalties on axis/lateral/axial constraint violation plus joint-bound violation `E_bound(q) = Σ_i[(v_i^-)² + (v_i^+)²]`. Zero exactly when `q ∈ C_obs` and within joint limits.
- `J_quality(q)` (evaluated only for hard-feasible `q`): motion economy `E_motion = Σ_i δ_i²` (deviation from current joint state, normalized by joint range), wrist-subset cost, joint-limit-margin cost `E_limit(q) = Σ_i[m̄_0 − m̄_i(q)]_+²`, plus squared `e_axis`, `e_lat`, `(d_axial − d_desired)`, and a soft "gaze" term `e_gaze` (angle to a virtual boresight proxy point placed slightly beyond the bottom, encouraging the optical axis to point through center even though roll is unconstrained and translation alone can satisfy the hard axis/lateral bounds off-center).

Optimizer: multistart derivative-free **coordinate search** (not gradient-based — no differentiable collision/visibility model needed), 48 seeds within 0.35 rad of the current joint state, up to 300 coordinate updates per seed, step size starts at 0.14 rad and halves down to 5×10⁻⁴ rad. Output: **top-N distinct feasible configurations ranked lexicographically by `(J_hard, J_quality)`**, capped at N=16 in the experiments. This ranked list — not a single pose — is what gets handed to the executor.

## Method — execution and verification (III-F)

MoveIt attempts motion planning for each candidate in rank order; on planning failure it advances to the next candidate (this is the paper's actual reachability fallback mechanism — a **next-best-candidate ladder**, not a re-optimization-in-place). After execution, a ray-cast visibility test samples points on the cavity bottom, checks each ray from camera to sample crosses the opening plane within the inner radius (minus `r_clear`), and computes bottom-visible ratio `V = N_visible / N_total`. **Success criterion: achieved pose satisfies `C_obs` AND `V ≥ V_min = 0.85`.** This is a concrete instance of "per-region view quality subject to a coverage floor" rather than either minimum-set-cover or max-k-coverage — but note it is a **single-view, single-region** instance (one cavity, one viewpoint budget of 1), not a multi-view/multi-region set-selection problem at all.

## Results (IV-A, IV-B, Table I)

Setup: 6-DoF AgileX Piper arm, eye-in-hand Intel RealSense D435i, Isaac Sim + MoveIt. 100 target configurations, cavity depth 0.40–0.70 m from robot base, axis tilt up to ~50°. **All methods received identical ground-truth opening center/axis** (perception error isolated out — the 92/100 number is a viewpoint-generation-and-planning number, not an end-to-end-with-real-perception number; the paper says this explicitly in IV-D).

| Method | Position err (mm) | Angular err (deg) | Bottom visible (%) | Success (/100) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed Pose + IK (one Cartesian pose from geometry, then IK) | 140.9 | 33.60 | 44.58 | 34 |
| View Sampling + IK (sample multiple Cartesian views, test IK) | 49.2 | 15.51 | 80.2 | 65 |
| Multi-Start CD (multistart coordinate search, but on a "conventional single-view objective" — i.e. same optimizer family, no observation-region/lexicographic/ranked-candidate structure) | 37.9 | 28.41 | 84.3 | 76 |
| **Proposed** (observation region + feasibility-first + ranked candidates + post-exec check) | 42.9 | 21.68 | **91.65** | **92** |

Headline: **+16 points success over Multi-Start CD, +27 over View Sampling+IK, +58 over Fixed Pose+IK**. Notable: **the proposed method does not have the lowest position or angular error** — "the lowest position and angular errors are achieved by different baselines, while neither baseline attains the highest end-to-end success... minimizing one deviation from a nominal view does not ensure that the executed camera simultaneously remains inside the axial and lateral bounds, faces through the opening, and exposes enough of the finite bottom surface" (IV-B). This is a clean empirical argument for **task-native constraint satisfaction over pose-distance-to-nominal as the optimization target** — directly supports our framing-doc instinct that "orthogonal-ish and close to a canonical pose" is the wrong proxy for "actually produces a usable evidence view."

**"View Sampling + IK" is the closest published analog to our current viewsphere+IK-filter architecture**, and it is beaten by the joint-space method by 27 success points and 11.45 visibility points on this task — direct quantitative evidence (on a different but structurally comparable problem) that generate-Cartesian-candidates-then-filter-by-IK is a weaker strategy than searching in joint space directly, at least for this narrow-aperture task.

## Hardware demonstration (IV-C)

Tabletop fixed-base Piper and a Piper mounted on a Unitree A2 quadruped, both running the full perception→plan→verify pipeline. **No trial count given** — explicitly a feasibility demo, not a statistical study (confirmed verbatim in IV-D, see below).

## Limitations (IV-D, quoted in full)

"The simulation experiment intentionally uses ground-truth target geometry; therefore, the 92% result characterizes viewpoint generation, motion planning, and executed-pose verification rather than RGB perception accuracy. The hardware evidence establishes system feasibility but is not a statistical success-rate study. Repeated trials, perception error against measured target geometry, runtime distributions, and stage-wise failure counts are needed for a complete robustness assessment. The formulation also assumes known cavity dimensions, a static visible target, and fixed camera-to-end-effector calibration. Near-frontal views remain weakly conditioned for monocular axis recovery, and **coordinate search may become costly as the number of seeds or manipulator degrees of freedom increases**. These limitations motivate future work on uncertainty-aware target geometry, online visual correction, and data-driven seed proposals, while retaining the explicit geometric acceptance test."

The bolded scalability caveat is self-reported and un-quantified — no wall-clock numbers, no scaling curve vs. DoF or seed count anywhere in the paper. This is a real gap if we wanted to cite this as "joint-space search scales fine to more DoF/more constraints" — it doesn't demonstrate that; it only demonstrates 6-DoF, one cavity, one visibility objective, single-arm.

## Related work II-B, verbatim (positioning against the wider NBV/active-perception field)

"Classical view planning selects sensor poses for reconstruction or inspection coverage. Recent systems extend this idea to online next-best-view selection for mobile manipulators, joint viewpoint planning and depth completion in clutter, and hand-eye active perception for optically difficult objects. Such methods commonly optimize scene coverage, reconstruction uncertainty, or task perception quality over a sequence of views. **Our problem is narrower but more geometrically explicit: a single inspection action must see through a finite circular aperture and retain a prescribed fraction of the internal bottom.**" — i.e., the authors themselves flag that they are solving a single-view feasibility problem, not a multi-view coverage-planning problem. This self-positioning confirms the paper is off-target for most of our multi-view set-selection branch questions (Q1–Q3, Q5, Q6, Q13, Q14) and on-target specifically for the reachability/fallback questions (Q4 partial, Q8, Q9 partial, Q11).

## What transfers to our setting

- **Q8 (fallback ladder for unreachable ideal view) — headline evidence.** This paper is a real, quantified instance of ladder rung **(iii) "never leave the feasible set by optimizing in joint space"**, combined for the collision term specifically with rung **(i)/(iv)-like "try the next-best candidate, discard/defer on planning failure"**. It should be cited for *both* pieces, not just (iii) — the pure "(iii) only" reading overstates what the paper actually does (see nuance above). Concretely: visibility+joint-limits are constraint-satisfied by construction inside the optimizer; collision-feasibility is a ranked-list-with-fallback search over up to 16 candidates via MoveIt, and even then 8/100 cases still fail end-to-end. **Useful honest number for our own architecture: "search in joint space" reduces but does not eliminate the ideal-view-unreachable failure mode.**
- **Q4 (does candidate-generate-then-select earn its keep) — negative data point for naive enumeration, but not a clean answer for us.** The paper's own ablation is direct evidence against "sample Cartesian candidates, then IK-filter" (View Sampling+IK: 65/100, 80.2% visible) relative to searching directly (92/100, 91.65%). But note the paper's ranked-candidate list (≤16 configs) is **never exposed to any higher-level chooser** — it's internal to the motion-planning stack, consumed automatically by MoveIt's rank-order retry loop. So this paper demonstrates "derive-then-rank-internally beats enumerate-then-filter" for the *motion-planning* layer, but says nothing about whether a small ranked/enumerated set is presentable to a VLM (our RQ4) — that requirement doesn't exist in this paper at all.
- **Q7 (orthogonality tolerance, concrete number).** `θ_max = 0.15 rad ≈ 8.6°` is a genuinely tight, task-validated (not hand-picked-looking, tied to a narrow-aperture occlusion geometry) number for "how far can the boresight deviate from the surface normal before the view stops counting as valid." Usable as one data point in the cross-paper table for Q7, with the caveat below about why it's this tight.
- **"Per-region quality subject to a coverage floor" as a formalization pattern (Q1).** `V ≥ V_min = 0.85` combined with hard geometric constraints is a clean, reusable instance of exactly the pattern Albert's requirement implies for us (view must be good enough on this region, not "cover more of the object"). Directly citable precedent for answering "coverage floor, not set-cover" for Q1, even though it's single-region here.
- **Nominal-pose-distance is not a good proxy for task success (Q7/Q9 adjacent).** The result that the lowest-error baselines are not the highest-success ones is a strong argument for scoring against the *actual* observation constraints (visibility ray-cast, angle-to-surface-normal) rather than distance to some canonical/idealized pose — relevant to how we should build our own view-quality judge (RQ1) rather than to view-*set* selection per se.
- **Roll-DoF-is-free as a modeling move.** Leaving camera roll unconstrained in `C_obs` is a small but useful reminder: our "orthogonal to surface" requirement similarly constrains only 2 rotational DoF (boresight direction) out of the camera's 3, plus position — worth checking our own action-space parameterization doesn't over-constrain roll unnecessarily.

## What does NOT transfer, and why

- **Wrong cylinder geometry for our "ring around a cylinder" primitive (Q5).** This paper's "cylinder" is a **cavity/hole viewed axially from outside-looking-in** (borescope-style), not an **external cylindrical surface viewed radially from an orbiting ring of cameras**. There is no "ring of views," no azimuthal sweep, no orbit concept anywhere in this paper — the entire observation model is built around a single axis-aligned viewing cone through one aperture. **It supplies zero precedent for "a ring per cylinder."** Citing it for that recipe would be a category error; it should be cited only for the reachability/fallback-ladder and orthogonality-tolerance points above.
- **Not a multi-view coverage/selection problem at all (Q1–Q3, Q6, Q13, Q14).** Confirmed by the authors' own scoping sentence (II-B, quoted above): one inspection *action*, one region (the bottom), one visibility floor. No set cover, no k-coverage, no submodularity question arises because there is no set being chosen — only a ranked list of *alternative attempts at the same single view*, consumed automatically without ever being exposed as a discrete choice. Cannot be used as evidence for view *count* per primitive (Q6), minimality/stopping criteria (Q13), or set-size-vs-redundancy findings (Q13/RQ3-RQ4 pressure) — none of that machinery is present.
- **No VLM, no discrete action-space presentation layer, no learned selection (Q4 fully, Q15).** The ranked-candidate list is purely an internal motion-planning artifact; nothing here informs RQ3/RQ4 (how to present a geometry-conditioned action space to a blind orchestrator) — the paper has no analogous "present choices to an agent" step to draw from.
- **No capability map, no pre-filtering by reachability statistics (Q9).** Reachability is tested per-candidate via MoveIt's actual planner in the ranked-list loop — there is no capability-map-style precomputed reachable-volume approximation, and therefore no false-positive-rate number to reuse for Q9's "what does a capability-map false positive cost us" question.
- **No coverage-loss-under-reachability number analogous to ObjView-Bench's 45/128 (Q10).** This paper doesn't report an unreachable-fraction statistic at all; its "success" metric already folds reachability, visibility, and collision together into one pass/fail number (92/100), so it cannot be decomposed into "how much of the object is geometrically unreachable" the way we need for Q10.
- **No IK-multiplicity discussion (Q11).** The paper's entire point is to avoid IK inversion by searching in joint space directly — so it has nothing to say about how many IK solutions a given Cartesian pose admits, or how that multiplicity should be reconciled with a discrete Cartesian-pose action space. It is evidence *for* treating "a view" as a joint configuration rather than a Cartesian pose (sidesteps the multiplicity question entirely by never posing it), but it doesn't discuss the tradeoff explicitly — that's an inference we're making, not a claim the paper makes.
- **Different platform, different camera, different DoF regime.** 6-DoF AgileX Piper (not UR5e), Intel RealSense D435i eye-in-hand (longer minimum range, active-stereo + IMU, not our D405 macro/short-range depth camera), single hardware demo with no repeated-trial statistics. The `d_axial ∈ [0.08, 0.38] m` working-distance band happens to sit in a plausibly similar close-range regime to our D405's minimum shell, but this is coincidental to their task geometry (a 5–6 cm cavity), not a transferable number for our tabletop object scale.
- **Self-reported, unquantified scalability caveat (IV-D).** "Coordinate search may become costly as the number of seeds or manipulator degrees of freedom increases" is asserted, not measured — no scaling curve exists in the paper. We should not cite this paper as evidence that joint-space multistart search scales to a UR5e (6-DoF, same as Piper, so plausibly fine) combined with a larger/more complex constraint set (multiple regions per object, orthogonality tolerance, table/gripper collision hull) — that combination is untested here.

## Relevance summary

Most useful as **quantified precedent for the reachability-fallback-ladder question (Q8)** and as a concrete counter-example to "sample Cartesian candidates, then IK-filter" (Q4, Q9) — plus one hard number for orthogonality tolerance (Q7: ≤8.6°) and a clean formalization of "quality subject to a coverage floor" (Q1). It is **not** evidence for the "ring per cylinder" recipe (Q5) — its cylinder is an axial cavity, not an orbited exterior surface — and it says nothing at all about multi-view set selection, minimality, learned selection, or presenting a discrete action space to a VLM (Q1–Q3, Q6, Q13–Q15). Cite narrowly, for the reachability-architecture point and the tight orthogonality number, not as a general view-set-selection reference.
