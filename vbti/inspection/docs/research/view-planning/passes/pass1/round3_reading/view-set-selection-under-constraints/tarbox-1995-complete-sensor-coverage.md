## Core method / idea

Tarbox & Gottschlich built **IVIS** (Integrated Volumetric Inspection System), a CAD-based inspection planner that finds a set of sensor poses guaranteeing complete measurement coverage of an object's exposed surface. The pipeline, as reconstructed from Scott et al. 2003 §5.1 and §9.2.3, and Gospodnetić et al. 2021 §2.1:

1. **Discretize viewpoint space** to a *view sphere*: recursive subdivision of an icosahedron, viewpoints constrained to lie on a sphere fully enclosing the object, viewing direction always pointed at the sphere's center (Scott et al. 2003 §5.1, p.74). This is the standard "reduce 6D+ pose to 2D" move (Scott et al. 2003 §9.2.3, p.89) — position and orientation are coupled by construction, not independently searched. Gospodnetić et al. (2021, §2.1) adds: Tarbox & Gottschlich state **no a priori preference on viewpoint placement**, use a **densely sampled** sphere, fix the camera viewing distance to an arbitrary constant, and distribute viewpoints uniformly, explicitly to raise the odds of catching hard-to-sense regions "by introducing redundancy" at the candidate stage (over-generate now, let the selection algorithm discard later).
2. **A distinctive spacing rule**: unlike other view-sphere users, Tarbox & Gottschlich space icosahedron vertices by the sensor's **optical baseline distance** (the camera–light-source separation of their bistatic triangulation sensor), giving 5–6 orientations about the sensor boresight per viewing axis, and — uniquely among the methods Scott et al. survey — **do vary orientation about the boresight** rather than leaving twist angle fixed (Scott et al. 2003 §9.2.3, p.90: "Orientation about the sensor boresight is fixed (excepting the approach by Tarbox)"). Ray tracing is done once per view-sphere vertex (used alternately as source or receiver location) rather than twice per viewpoint, an explicit efficiency trick.
3. **Discretize the surface** into points (from the CAD/rough model) and build a **measurability matrix M(i,j)**: row i = discretized surface point, column j = discretized admissible viewpoint, entry = the result of a full bistatic visibility analysis — a point is measurable only if it is seen by **both** the camera and the light source (Scott et al. 2003 §5.1, p.74; consistent with the "bistatic visibility, not monostatic" framing Scott et al. flag as a general field weakness elsewhere, §9.2.2). Entries encode estimated *measurement quality*, not raw binary visibility — grazing-angle admissibility is baked in, with **separate grazing-angle thresholds for camera and light source**.
4. **Cull inadmissible viewpoints before building the matrix**, using an octree-encoded voxel occupancy grid to remove viewpoints occluded by a mounting fixture and supporting plane (Scott et al. 2003 §5.1, p.74, via the companion file `scott-2003-view-planning-survey.md`'s "what transfers" section) — a hard-filter-and-discard step applied to fixture occlusion, upstream of selection.
5. **Select a view set covering the matrix**, using one of three algorithms Tarbox & Gottschlich developed and compared (Scott et al. 2003 §5.1, p.74–75):
   - **Algorithms 1 and 2**: an **irrevocable (greedy) selection strategy** — the two differ only in how grazing-angle constraints are enforced during selection. Scott et al.'s separate summary of Tarbox's behavior (§7, "Comparison of Existing Methods," p.85) describes the mechanism concretely: "Tarbox and Gottschlich computed a measurability matrix... and selected a viewpoint maximizing the void surface visible." This is the textbook greedy max-marginal-coverage heuristic for set cover — at each step, add whichever candidate viewpoint covers the most currently-uncovered ("void") surface, repeat until the surface is covered (or no further coverage gain is possible).
   - **Algorithm 3**: a **revocable selection strategy** — a randomized search using **simulated annealing** to further minimize the size of the view set, i.e., a post-hoc refinement pass that can swap out previously chosen views (unlike 1/2, which never reconsider a choice).
6. **Robustness to pose error**: rather than modeling positioning-system error probabilistically, Tarbox & Gottschlich apply **morphological erosion** to the viewpoint set associated with each surface point — peripheral viewpoints (those near the edge of their admissible region) are stripped out, so a small pose perturbation can't push the executed view outside the region that was verified admissible (Scott et al. 2003 §8.4.3, p.87 and §9.3, p.91 — both explicitly name this as the *only* pose-error treatment among all methods Scott et al. survey: "Excepting Tarbox, no author addressed the impact of pose error on view planning").

## Concrete results and numbers

Everything numeric I could recover, all via the secondary sources — **no output view-plan cardinality, computation time, or per-test-object result was recoverable** (see negative findings):

- **~15,000 candidate viewpoints** generated for meshes of **1,000–2,500 vertices**, used to evaluate their selection algorithms (Gospodnetić et al. 2021, §2.1, p.3, directly attributed to Tarbox & Gottschlich [9]) — roughly 6–15 candidate views per surface vertex at the discretization stage. This is a candidate-*generation* density, not a final selected-set size.
- **View-sphere vertex spacing = sensor optical baseline distance**, yielding **5–6 orientations about the boresight per viewing axis** for a tessellated icosahedron (Scott et al. 2003 §9.2.3, p.90).
- **The View Planning Problem (as a covering problem) is NP-complete** — Tarbox & Gottschlich are the originating citation for this claim in the field (Scott et al. 2003 §2.3, p.68: "the View Planning Problem has been shown to be NP-Complete ([Tarbox and Gottschlich 1995]; [Scott 2002])"; independently confirmed in the companion file's read of Scott, Roth & Rivest 2001a, which attributes VPP≅SCP and the NP-completeness citation to Garey & Johnson, via Tarbox & Gottschlich [ref. 15 in that document]). Given this, the paper's own stated conclusion (per Scott et al. 2003, §5.1, p.75) is that finding the **shortest possible view plan is impractical**, so a satisfactory-but-not-necessarily-optimal algorithm is the deliberate design choice — i.e., Tarbox & Gottschlich themselves chose the greedy/heuristic route *because* of the NP-completeness result they established, not despite ignoring it.
- **"Given the span of the discretized variables, the computational complexity of the approach was prohibitive"** (Scott et al. 2003, §5.1, p.75) — Scott et al. flag this as "a fundamental limitation regarding direct applicability of the work to object reconstruction." Read this claim carefully: Scott et al. are evaluating Tarbox & Gottschlich's technique against **reconstruction-scale** discretization (their own companion paper reports typical reconstruction-scale figures of s≈10⁵ surface points and v≈10¹¹ unreduced viewpoint-space levels, per `scott-2001-view-planning-as-set-covering.md`), not necessarily reporting that Tarbox & Gottschlich's own **inspection**-scale experiments (mesh sizes of 1,000–2,500 vertices, per the Gospodnetić figure above) were themselves intractable. This nuance matters directly for branch Q3: the "prohibitive" verdict is a *scale* statement, and the scale in question (reconstruction of complex freeform objects) is far above both Tarbox's own tested inspection instances and our own (a handful of primitives, tens of views, tens of regions).

## Load-bearing assumptions

1. **A prior geometric model (CAD, "tolerance reference model of the ideal object") exists and is trusted** — the whole measurability-matrix construction is model-based: surface points, viewpoint admissibility, and grazing-angle thresholds are all computed against a known-correct geometry, not a noisy acquired point cloud. This is the single biggest assumption mismatch with our setting (see negative findings).
2. **The sensor is bistatic (separate camera and light source)**, and measurability requires *both* to see the point — this is why the view-sphere spacing rule (optical baseline) and the "shadow effect" language exist. Our D405 is a monostatic-style eye-in-hand RGB-D device (structured-light/stereo baseline internal to the housing, not a separately-positioned light source under planner control) — the bistatic visibility machinery does not map onto our sensor directly; only the general "some points need more than raw line-of-sight to be usable" idea transfers.
3. **View sphere fully encloses the object at a fixed stand-off distance** — position and orientation are coupled (boresight toward sphere center), so the technique cannot express "close-up orthogonal face shot with the camera *not* aimed at the object centroid," which is exactly what our face-orthogonal / cylinder-ring rules need for a non-spherical primitive (a box face's outward normal does not pass through the box's centroid except for the face directly opposite, and even then the view sphere's fixed radius may not match the primitive's actual working distance).
4. **Object lies entirely within the sensor's frustum from every admissible viewpoint** — assumes a single-shot, whole-object framing, not a part-of-object framing. This does not match "one orthogonal view per face" (which frames a *region*, not the whole object).
5. **Reachability = fixture/self-occlusion only, not manipulator kinematics** — the octree-based culling step removes viewpoints blocked by a mounting fixture or table, not viewpoints that are geometrically clear but kinematically unreachable by a 6-DOF arm. There is no IK, no joint-space reasoning, no capability map anywhere in this reconstruction of the method (consistent with the sibling files' confirmed finding that this entire literature generation predates such concepts).
6. **A full cover is implicitly assumed achievable** — the greedy algorithm's stopping behavior ("maximizing void surface visible" until void is exhausted) presumes there exists a combination of admissible viewpoints that eventually covers everything; nothing in the reconstructed method discusses what happens when some surface points have **no** admissible viewpoint at all (an empty matrix row) — directly relevant to our reachable-shell problem.

## What transfers to our setting

- **This is the historical origin of exactly the object our branch keeps re-deriving**: discretize the surface, discretize the viewpoint space, build a measurability/coverage relation, select a covering subset. Every later formulation in this branch's reading list (Scott, Roth & Rivest's ILP; SCVP; PRVNet; ObjView-Bench) is a variant of this same skeleton — this paper is the root of the citation chain, confirmed independently by both secondary sources treating it as the field's originating reference for NP-completeness and the measurability-matrix formalism.
- **The greedy algorithms (1 and 2) are literally the classic max-marginal-coverage greedy heuristic for set cover** — "select the viewpoint maximizing the void surface visible" at each step, repeated. This is worth noting explicitly for branch Q2 (submodularity): unweighted coverage (a point is covered or not) is a textbook submodular set function, and greedy max-marginal-gain selection over a submodular function carries the classical (1 − 1/e) [max-coverage] / ln(n)+1 [set-cover] approximation guarantee — **Tarbox & Gottschlich appear to have implemented this algorithm without invoking or proving the guarantee** (neither secondary source mentions them citing Nemhauser/Wolsey or discussing an approximation ratio at all). This is useful ammunition for Q2: the guarantee would hold for their *unweighted* coverage objective by construction, even though the original authors never say so — but the moment an orthogonality/quality *weighting* is added on top (as our RQ2 branch question asks), whether the resulting weighted coverage function is still submodular is a genuinely separate question this paper does not answer or even pose.
- **Direct precedent for RQ8's fallback-ladder rung (i) hard-filter-and-discard**: fixture/self-occlusion culling via an octree occupancy grid, done *before* the measurability matrix is built — structurally identical in spirit (if not in constraint source) to filtering unreachable candidates before offering them to a selector.
- **Direct precedent for RQ8's rung (ii) "shrink/re-center within the admissible region"**: morphological erosion of peripheral viewpoints for pose-error robustness is a real instance of "don't just discard a marginal candidate, contract the admissible region and re-derive membership" — motivated by pose error, not kinematic reachability, but the mechanism (topological erosion of an admissible set) is directly reusable for reachability margins too.
- **A concrete, citable instance of "over-generate candidates deliberately, then let selection do the work"** (Gospodnetić et al.'s framing of the 15,000-candidates-for-1,000–2,500-vertex-mesh number) — directly relevant to RQ4: enumeration-then-selection isn't merely a VLM-legibility convenience, it is literally how the field's originating method operates, motivated by nothing more than "increase the odds of catching hard-to-sense regions."
- **The NP-completeness result, taken at face value, is not an argument against exact solving at our scale** — Tarbox & Gottschlich established NP-completeness for the *general* problem and then, by their own account (via Scott et al. 2003), concluded exact solving was impractical **for their own tested instances** (mesh sizes of 1,000–2,500 vertices, ~15,000 candidates) — several orders of magnitude above our stated instance size (a handful of primitives, tens of candidates, tens of regions). This paper is therefore evidence *for*, not against, treating our smaller instance as exactly solvable (directly relevant to RQ3, and consistent with the same conclusion the sibling `scott-2001-view-planning-as-set-covering.md` file reaches from a different angle via that paper's own complexity figures).

## What does NOT transfer, and why (negative findings — first class)

- **No primitive-decomposition or shape-specific view rule of any kind.** The view sphere treats the object as an undifferentiated whole enclosed by a bounding sphere; there is no "one orthogonal view per face" or "ring per cylinder" reasoning anywhere in the reconstructed method — viewpoints are generated by uniform geometric subdivision of a sphere around the *whole object*, with all shape-specificity deferred entirely to the visibility/measurability computation against the surface mesh. This paper is dense-then-filter, not shape-rule-then-place; it supports the branch's suspicion (RQ5, RQ19) that this specific recipe has no clean precedent in the classical CAD-inspection literature, Tarbox included.
- **No handling of a noisy, self-acquired survey point cloud as the input model.** Everything here is built against a trusted CAD/tolerance-reference model. Our setting's core wrinkle — a fused PLY with real defects (pose error of a few mm, depth dropout, a measured ~4 cm cloud-vs-live-depth offset) — has no analog: there is no discussion of *input*-model uncertainty anywhere in the reconstructed method, only output/positioning error (the pose-error-robustness erosion step is about the **robot's** pose error at execution time, not the **model's** geometric error at planning time). This is the same gap flagged independently in the sibling `scott-2003-view-planning-survey.md` (its negative finding on branch Q18/Q16) — two independent readings converge on the same conclusion.
- **No manipulator kinematics, IK, or capability map.** "Reachability" in this paper is exclusively fixture/self-occlusion (an octree occupancy test), never joint-limit or collision-with-arm reasoning. Nothing here bears on RQ9, RQ10, or RQ11.
- **No numeric grazing-angle/orthogonality-deviation threshold was recoverable.** Both secondary sources confirm Tarbox & Gottschlich used separate grazing-angle thresholds for camera and light source as an admissibility cutoff baked into the measurability matrix, but neither reports the actual angular values. RQ7's specific number cannot be sourced from this reconstruction — would require the primary text.
- **No reported output view-plan size, computation time, or specific test-object results.** I could recover the *candidate*-generation count (15,000 for 1,000–2,500-vertex meshes) but not the *selected* set size their algorithms actually produced, nor what objects (turbine blades, automotive parts, etc. — genre unknown to me) they tested on, nor a comparison of algorithm 1 vs. 2 vs. 3's resulting set sizes or runtimes. This is a genuine information gap in this reconstruction, not a "the paper doesn't report it" finding — I simply could not access text that would answer RQ13 for this specific paper. Flag for a follow-up primary-source attempt (e.g., via an institutional library proxy) if RQ13's numbers end up load-bearing for the branch's argument.
- **No stopping/minimality criterion beyond "greedy until void is exhausted, then optionally anneal for further reduction."** There is no reported bound, no comparison against a known-optimal solution, no e_v-style efficiency metric (that metric is Scott et al.'s own later contribution, not Tarbox & Gottschlich's) — RQ14 gets only the existence of a two-stage greedy-then-anneal pattern from this paper, not a defensible minimality *guarantee*.
- **No treatment of the case where full coverage is geometrically unattainable.** Consistent with the sibling files' findings on the same underlying set-cover formalism: nothing in the reconstructed method addresses what happens if some surface region has zero admissible viewpoints (an all-zero matrix row) — RQ12's "does reachability reshape the objective" question is unaddressed here, same conclusion as `scott-2001-view-planning-as-set-covering.md` reaches for the general set-cover formulation this paper originates.

## Assessment for the branch

This is the branch's root citation, and the reconstruction confirms it deserves that status: discretize-viewpoints / discretize-surface / build-a-measurability-relation / greedily-cover-it is exactly the skeleton every later paper in this reading list refines. The two most load-bearing things this document adds beyond what the sibling files (`scott-2003-view-planning-survey.md`, `scott-2001-view-planning-as-set-covering.md`) already established are (1) the concrete mechanics of the greedy algorithm itself — "maximize void surface visible," the classic submodular-greedy move, run without ever invoking the guarantee — which sharpens Q2 into "the guarantee already holds unweighted, the open question is only what a quality weighting does to it," and (2) the ~15,000-candidates-for-1,000–2,500-vertices density figure, the only concrete instance-size number attributable to Tarbox & Gottschlich's own reported experiments, which is exactly the kind of "field's own instance sizes were this small, and even that needed heuristics, but ours is smaller still" evidence Q3 needs. Its biggest weakness as a source for this branch's live questions (RQ7's numeric threshold, RQ13's actual reported set sizes) is not the paper's fault but mine: I could not obtain the primary text, and the two secondary sources — while unusually thorough — were themselves surveys, not verbatim reproductions, and neither one reports Tarbox & Gottschlich's raw output numbers. **If RQ7 or RQ13 becomes load-bearing for a written claim, this paper needs a genuine primary-source read (institutional access) before that claim ships** — everything here should be treated as strong circumstantial evidence, not a verified primary citation, for anything beyond what is explicitly quoted from Scott et al. 2003 or Gospodnetić et al. 2021 above.
