## Core method / idea

The paper is a **framework + head-to-head benchmark** for scoring candidate views during active volumetric 3D object reconstruction (Next-Best-View / NBV loop), not a single new algorithm. It:

1. Defines **volumetric information (VI)**, `I(x)`, as a per-voxel scalar quantifying expected information content of a probabilistic occupancy voxel (OctoMap). A view's information gain (IG) is the sum of VI over every voxel touched by every ray cast from that view (**Eq. 1**): `Gv = Σ_{r∈Rv} Σ_{x∈X} I` — i.e., **sum/integral pooling** of a per-voxel score to a per-view score (contrast with Soudarissanane's RMSE pooling of per-point noise to per-patch quality — two different aggregation families for two different quantities, gain vs. error).
2. Defines voxel occupancy entropy (**Eq. 2**): `H(x) = -Po(x)ln Po(x) - P̄o(x)ln P̄o(x)`, max 1.0 Shannon at `Po=0.5` (total ignorance). Pure-entropy VI (**Eq. 3**): `Ie(x)=H(x)`.
3. Reproduces the state-of-the-art baseline, **Average Entropy VI (Kriegel et al. 2015)** (**Eq. 4**): `Gv,Kriegel = (1/n) Σ H(x)` — entropy sum normalized by traversed-voxel count `n`.
4. Proposes **Occlusion Aware VI**: a visibility likelihood `Pv(xn) = Π_{i=1}^{n-1} P̄o(xi)` (**Eq. 5**, product of free-space probabilities along the ray up to voxel `xn` — a *soft*, probabilistic relaxation of hard ray-occlusion visibility), then `Iv(x)=Pv(x)·H(x)` (**Eq. 6**) — visibility-weighted entropy.
5. Proposes **Unobserved Voxel VI**: gate Eq. 6 by an unobserved-indicator `Iu(x)` (**Eq. 7**, 1 if unobserved else 0): `Ik(x)=Iu(x)·Iv(x)` (**Eq. 8**).
6. Proposes **Rear Side Voxel VI**: binary indicator `Ib(x)` (**Eq. 9**) for voxels in the "rear side set" `So` — unobserved voxels whose next voxel along the ray is estimated occupied (i.e., just behind an already-seen surface, likely still-unseen object). This is a pure count of such rays hit per view.
7. Proposes **Rear Side Entropy VI**: `In(x)=Ib(x)·Iv(x)` (**Eq. 10**) — rear-side gating combined with visibility-weighted entropy.
8. Proposes **Proximity Count VI** (**Eq. 11**): `Ip(x) = dmax - d(x)` for unobserved voxels (0 if observed), where `d(x)` is the pre-computed distance from voxel `x` back to the nearest already-observed surface point (registered once per new point cloud, up to a cap `dmax`, not recomputed per candidate view) — a continuously-weighted version of Rear Side Voxel that favors voxels *closer* to known surface, motivated by "continuous objects are more likely occupied near an already-seen surface."
9. Reproduces the other state-of-the-art baseline, **Area Factor VI (Vasquez-Gomez et al. 2014)** (**Eqs. 17–20**): targets *desired percentages* of occupied (`αdes,oc=0.2`) and occlusion-plane/"occplane" (`αdes,op=0.8`) voxels in the view, via a **piecewise-cubic, unimodal, bounded scoring function** `f(α,αdes)` that peaks (≈1) exactly at `α=αdes` and falls off on both sides (Eq. 18: `h1` below the target, `h2` above it; both cubic-Hermite-style polynomials in Eqs. 19–20). `Gv,Vasquez = f(αoc,αdes,oc) + f(αop,αdes,op)` (Eq. 17). **This is a directly citable example of a bounded/normalized, target-band (not monotonic) per-view score** — relevant to branch Q8 and Q14.
10. **Selection loop** (explicitly out of this branch's scope per the task framing, but needed to understand what the score numbers mean): utility `Uv = (1-γ)·Gv/VG - γ·Cv/VC` (**Eq. 12**, normalized IG minus normalized movement cost, `γ` a tunable weight), NBV `v* = argmax Uv` (**Eq. 13**), stopping rule `Gv < gthresh ∀v∈V` (**Eq. 14**).

## Concrete results and numbers

**Setup (§4.1):** Idealized, noise-free, free-flying 6-DoF RGB-D sensor (no sensor uncertainty, no motion constraints, no IK/collision) in Gazebo. 11 object models: Stanford bunny, Stanford dragon, TUM Armadillo, and 8 free-form models generated from the DTU MVS dataset — **all organic/complex scanned shapes, none are box/cylinder/sphere primitives**. Each model scaled to ~0.5 m along its largest dimension, bounded in a 1.0 m cube. **Static** candidate viewspace: 48 views distributed uniformly over a cylinder-with-hemisphere-cap around the object. Map: OctoMap voxel grid, 1 cm resolution, occupied threshold 0.97, free threshold 0.12. Each (model × VI) combination run 20 times with random start view, 20 iterations/trial (no early termination — full budget always used), results averaged over the 20 trials.

**Evaluation metrics (§4.2):**
- **Surface coverage** (**Eq. 15**): `observed surface points / total surface points in ground-truth model`, where a ground-truth surface point counts as "observed" if the nearest reconstructed point is within a registration threshold `d_reg = 0.5 cm` (footnote 6). Ground truth here is the original watertight mesh the model was generated from — **a trusted, complete, non-defective ground truth**, unlike our fused-PLY setting.
- **Total map entropy** (**Eq. 16**): sum of per-voxel entropy over a 1.28 m bounding cube (= `0.01 m × 2^7` voxels/side, the smallest octree level fully containing all models). Max possible = `-(2^7)^3 · log2(0.5) = 2.097×10^6` Shannon (every voxel at `Po=0.5`).
- **AUC** of both curves, normalized to `[0,1]` by max-iteration-count (20) and max metric value: higher-is-better for surface-coverage AUC, lower-is-better for entropy AUC.
- **Per-view computation time**, averaged over all 20 trials × 11 models, on an 8-core i7-4770K @ 3.5 GHz, 8 parallel threads for ray casting.

**Table 1 caveat (citability):** the PDF's Table 1 packs results for 6 objects into an extraction that dropped the per-block model-name labels (a text-extraction artifact of a complex multi-column table, not a content gap in the paper) — I can *not* reliably say "model X's AUC was Y" from the extracted text. What **is** reliably readable across every block (the VI-row labels OA/UV/RSV/RSE/PC/VG/Kr/Rand are intact, and the pattern holds in all 6 visible blocks):
- **Entropy AUC is compressed into a narrow band (~0.94–0.95) across all VI formulations and all models** — directly visible in the raw numbers, and it's the quantitative substrate for the paper's own claim (see below) that entropy AUC barely discriminates between methods.
- **Surface-coverage AUC has real spread** (roughly 0.75–0.93 depending on model/VI in the visible blocks) — coverage AUC *does* discriminate methods, unlike entropy AUC.
- Row-by-row, **OA and UV (Occlusion Aware vs. Unobserved Voxel) track each other very closely** (e.g. one visible block: OA `.732/.904/.960/.963`, UV `.741/.910/.959/.963`) — a directly-observable near-redundancy between these two formulations in this table, though the paper reports no formal correlation coefficient for this (my own read of the raw numbers, not an authors' claim).

**Reliable, unambiguous claims (from body text, not the garbled table):**
- **"Area Factor VI (Vasquez-Gomez) and Proximity Count VI exhibit superior performance during the first few views"** and, by full-trial AUC, **each is best on 4 of the 11 models** (§5) — i.e. together they win 8/11 models by AUC, the clearest quantitative head-to-head result in the paper.
- **"Other VIs typically achieve comparable or superior surface coverage after 4–6 views, and beyond 6–10 views... surface completion is asymptotic"** (§5) regardless of model — i.e., **the choice of IG formulation matters mainly for few-view efficiency, not for eventual completeness**.
- **Random view selection ("Rand" baseline) performs "reasonably well" on average but with "much larger" variance than any IG-driven method** (§5) — and since per-view compute time is "small enough (<<10s) that taking additional random views would not be more efficient" than IG evaluation given typical robot move times, cheap-random is not recommended despite being nearly as good on average.
- **Timing (Table 2, clean/unambiguous):** all formulations cost essentially the same, 3.7618–3.7889 s/view average — Occlusion Aware 3.7798 s, Unobserved Voxel 3.7786 s, Rear Side Voxel 3.7709 s, Rear Side Entropy 3.7889 s, Proximity Count 3.7756 s, Area Factor 3.7618 s, Average Entropy 3.7714 s. **Spread across all 7 formulations is <0.03 s (<1%)** — computational cost of the score formula itself is negligible next to the shared ray-casting cost; the paper's own conclusion (§5) is that "computational cost is negligibly different between all of the proposed and state of the art VI formulations."
- **Central negative finding (§5, explicitly flagged as such by the authors):** Average Entropy (Kriegel) VI achieves the best entropy-AUC for most models, **but "the ability of a VI formulation to effectively reduce the entropy in the map is not well correlated with its effectiveness in surface reconstruction."** Reason given: a voxel must be observed *multiple times* to reduce its entropy toward certainty, so an entropy-maximizing view-choice policy re-visits already-partially-seen regions rather than exploring new surface, directly trading off against coverage. Authors' own hedge: "for scenarios in which certainty about occupancy... is more important than completeness, Average Entropy is more effective... but the results are not very conclusive."
- **Conclusion (§6):** "the reconstruction is able to achieve most of its model completion within a small number (<10) of well-chosen views, regardless of the choice of VI formulation" — the overall takeaway is that scoring-function choice has diminishing returns once the loop runs long enough; the practical win from a better score is front-loaded (fewer views needed), not asymptotic completeness.

## Load-bearing assumptions

- **Idealized, noise-free, unconstrained sensor and platform** (§4.1): "free-moving sensor as an idealized 'robot' without movement constraints or sensor pose uncertainty... the only independent variable... is the next best view chosen by each VI formulation." Explicitly designed to *isolate* the scoring function from every real-world confound — the opposite of our setting (defective fused PLY, IK/collision-constrained reachable shell, D405 depth noise).
- **Ground truth is a trusted, complete, watertight mesh** used both to build the simulated sensor's exact returns and to compute surface-coverage AUC (Eq. 15) — no analog of our ~4 cm fused-vs-single-view offset, no pose error, no dropout holes. Coverage-against-ground-truth is circular by construction in exactly the way the framing doc's Q22/Q24 flag as a risk for metric-based "best view" ground truth, but the paper is explicit that this is a controlled-isolation choice, not a claim about real-world scoring.
- **Static, uniform 48-view cylinder+hemisphere viewspace**, known a priori "since we know a priori the volume in which the unknown object is contained" (§4.1) — same class of geometry-agnostic action space our framing doc is trying to move away from, just for a different (reconstruction, not orthogonal-inspection) purpose.
- **Occupancy-grid ("visibility" = not-occluded) is the only geometric notion of view quality present.** Nowhere does the paper use incidence angle / surface-normal alignment / orthogonality — "visibility" here means "unobstructed line of sight through the voxel grid" (Eq. 5's product of free-space probabilities), not "camera facing the surface normal." This is an important terminology trap for our term table (Q5): their "Occlusion Aware VI" is not a competitor to our orthogonality term, it answers a completely different question (can the ray reach the voxel at all) than incidence angle does (at what angle does the ray strike the surface once it arrives).
- **Task framing is exploration/coverage-maximization for an as-yet-unknown object**, not "judge whether a specific already-selected view of an already-known surface region is good" — the entire VI apparatus answers "which view will reveal the most new, uncertain volume," which is the **NBV information-gain family that RQ1 explicitly wants scored but not looped** (task scope: "score only, not the selection loop"). The score formulas (Eqs. 2–11) are reusable as static per-voxel/per-region quality terms once stripped of the `argmax`/utility/termination machinery (Eqs. 12–14) built around them.

## What transfers to our setting

- **A clean, citable example of sum/integral pooling from voxel-level score to view-level score** (Eq. 1) — a third aggregation family for Q9 alongside RMSE-pooling (Soudarissanane) and whatever the industrial-inspection literature uses; useful to contrast against "worst-point pooling" when the branch asks whether worst-point pooling is defensible for inspection (Q9) — this paper's answer for *information content* is explicitly additive/summed, not min-pooled, because gain is meant to be cumulative across a ray, not bottlenecked by one bad point. Not directly the same aggregation problem as inspection defect-scoring (that's plausibly closer to min/worst-region pooling), but it's the clearest documented alternative to keep on the table.
- **Occlusion Aware VI's visibility-likelihood relaxation (Eq. 5)** is a directly reusable answer to branch Q16 ("catalogue the smoothing tricks for a pose-discontinuous visibility landscape"): replace a hard binary "is voxel occluded" test with a **product of per-voxel free-space probabilities along the ray**, turning a discontinuous 0/1 visibility function into a smooth `[0,1]` likelihood that a gradient-based or sampling-based local optimizer could actually climb. Table 2 shows this relaxation is essentially free computationally (3.78 s vs. 3.77–3.79 s for the other formulations, ray-casting-dominated either way) — directly answers the "computational cost" half of Q16.
- **Vasquez-Gomez's Area Factor (Eqs. 17–20) is a concrete, bounded, unimodal (peak-at-target, not monotonic) scoring function** — a genuine precedent for Q8 ("bounded, normalized per-view score comparable across views") and a genuine precedent for Q14 ("formal definitions of best view within a neighbourhood" — here "best" is not "most" of anything, it's "closest to a hand-tuned target percentage," a qualitatively different local-optimality shape than gradient-ascent-to-maximum). Worth flagging to whoever writes the term table that a target-band score (peaks at a desired value, penalizes both under- and over-shoot) is a validated alternative to a monotonic "more is better" score — structurally similar to how our orthogonality term might behave if off-normal is sometimes preferred (branch Q3).
- **This paper is close kin to the ablation/protocol template the framing doc explicitly wants** (branch Q25, "the head-to-head comparison of score functions under one protocol" — this is literally that paper, selected for exactly this reason). Reusable protocol elements: (a) fix everything except the score formula (idealized sensor, fixed viewspace, fixed map resolution) to isolate the scoring function as the only independent variable; (b) report *both* a task-relevant outcome metric (surface coverage) *and* a proxy metric the score is nominally optimizing (map entropy), specifically so a mismatch between them (their central negative finding) is visible; (c) always include a **random-baseline control**; (d) report per-formulation compute cost as a first-class result, not a footnote; (e) run enough random-seed trials (20) to average out start-view variance, and report variance, not just means, because the paper's own headline caveat about "Rand" is entirely about variance, not mean performance.
- **Negative-transfer methodological warning, itself transferable as a design principle:** the paper's strongest empirical result is that **a metric can look like it's working (best entropy-AUC) while being decoupled from the outcome that actually matters (surface completeness)** — directly relevant to this branch's Q6 ("is a score summing five correlated terms one term with extra steps") and Q7 (score degradation under noisy inputs): before combining view-quality terms into one composite score, this paper is citable evidence that you must validate each *proxy* term against the *actual downstream task metric* (for us: defect detectability / evidence sufficiency), not just check that the proxy is internally well-behaved.

## What does NOT transfer, and why

- **Zero content on incidence angle, orthogonality, or depth-noise-vs-angle** (branch Q1–Q4). The paper's "visibility" is occlusion, not surface-facing-angle; there is no sensor-noise model at all (idealized noise-free sensor by explicit design, §4.1). This document contributes **nothing** to the D405 sigma(range,θ) curve, the 60° knee question, the specular-reflection-off-normal question, or the defect-detectability-vs-angle question. Must be sourced elsewhere (as the Soudarissanane note and industrial-inspection literature already partially do).
- **Ground-truth model is a trusted complete mesh, not a defective fused PLY.** The entire surface-coverage metric (Eq. 15) and the underlying simulated sensor returns assume the ground truth is exact. Nothing here addresses judging a view against a cloud that is *itself* wrong (branch Q12), the ~4 cm fused-vs-single-view offset (branch Q13), or hidden-point-removal / dropout-hole visibility artifacts (branch Q11) — the paper's occlusion reasoning happens inside a probabilistic voxel grid built from a noise-free simulated sensor, which sidesteps rather than solves the "visibility without a trustworthy surface" problem our branch cares about.
- **No image-space content whatsoever** (branch Q17–Q21). The reconstruction, scoring, and evaluation all happen in voxel/point-cloud space; there is no rendered image, no VLM, no defect-contrast or exposure/blur consideration anywhere in the paper. Zero transfer to the image-space judging questions.
- **Object set is exclusively organic/free-form scanned shapes** (Stanford bunny/dragon, Armadillo, 8 MVS models) — **none are primitives** (box, cylinder, sphere). Per branch Q24 (Branch 2 handoff), this means the reported coverage %, AUC numbers, and "8/11 models won by these two VIs" tallies are not directly informative magnitudes for our primitive-shape regime; only the *methodology* (isolate the score, use AUC + entropy + timing + random control) transfers, not the numeric results.
- **The task objective (maximize new-surface discovery / minimize map entropy for an unknown object) is a different objective than RQ1's** (judge whether *this specific, already-designated* view of an *already-known* surface region is the best available evidence view). IG/NBV metrics here are optimized for exploration efficiency of an unknown shape, not for confirming/inspecting a known region for defects. The task instructions' own scope note ("score only, not the selection loop") is doing real work: the *scores* (entropy, visibility-likelihood, proximity weighting) are borrowable as raw ingredients, but the *loop* they're embedded in (Eqs. 12–14, utility/argmax/termination) answers a next-best-view exploration question we are not asking in this branch.
- **No sensitivity analysis to input-geometry/normal-estimation error** (branch Q7, Q12). The occupancy grid's "ground truth" is exact by simulation construction; there is no experiment perturbing the input point cloud or measuring `d(score)/d(input error)`. This paper cannot answer the branch's top technical risk question.
- **Idealized platform removes IK/reachability/collision constraints entirely** — contradicts our "not every desired view is reachable" given. The static 48-view cylinder+hemisphere viewspace here is a convenience for isolating the scoring function, not a claim about what real hardware can reach.

## Answers mapped to specific branch questions

- **Q5 (term table)** — contributes several PLY/occupancy-grid-space rows, all under the "information gain" family: entropy `H(x)` (Eq. 2–3, inputs: voxel occupancy probability), visibility-weighted entropy / Occlusion Aware (Eq. 5–6, inputs: ray-traversed free-space probabilities + entropy), unobserved-gated entropy (Eq. 7–8), rear-side-voxel count (Eq. 9), rear-side entropy (Eq. 10), proximity-weighted rear-side (Eq. 11, inputs: precomputed distance-to-nearest-known-surface), and Area Factor (Eqs. 17–20, inputs: fraction of occupied/occplane voxels in view vs. two hand-tuned target percentages). All of these are occupancy-grid/voxel-space, a middle ground between pure PLY-space and mesh-space — worth a distinct tag from "PLY-space" in the table if the branch's table distinguishes representations that finely.
- **Q6 (redundancy/correlation clusters)** — no formal correlation statistics reported, but two usable signals: (1) my own read of Table 1 shows Occlusion Aware and Unobserved Voxel VI producing near-identical numbers row after row (plausible near-redundancy, unconfirmed by the authors); (2) the paper's central negative finding is a *de-correlation*, not a redundancy — entropy-AUC and coverage-AUC rank formulations differently, so "maximize entropy reduction" and "maximize coverage" are demonstrably *not* the same objective, a caution against assuming two intuitively-related terms measure the same thing.
- **Q8 (bounded, normalized per-view score precedent)** — yes: Vasquez-Gomez's Area Factor `f(α,αdes)∈` a bounded, unimodal cubic (Eqs. 18–20) is a directly citable precedent, and the paper's own AUC metric is likewise normalized to `[0,1]` by construction (§4.2) — though AUC is a whole-trajectory summary, not a single-view score.
- **Q9 (unit of scoring / aggregation)** — sum/integral pooling of per-voxel VI to per-view IG (Eq. 1), contrasted with Soudarissanane's RMSE point→patch pooling; a data point that different quality *types* (information gain vs. measurement noise) plausibly warrant different aggregation operators, relevant when the branch's table has to pick an aggregation rule per term rather than one rule for everything.
- **Q14 (formal "best view within a neighbourhood")** — contributes a formal utility-maximization definition (`argmax` of a linear combination of normalized gain and normalized cost, Eqs. 12–13) as one family, and Vasquez-Gomez's target-band definition (peak at a desired occupancy percentage, not a maximum) as a structurally different family — useful contrast when the branch compares this against MVP's constraint-satisfaction-with-margin and mutual-information viewpoint stability.
- **Q16 (smoothing discontinuous visibility)** — Occlusion Aware VI's visibility likelihood (Eq. 5, product of per-voxel free-space probabilities along a cast ray) is a concrete, near-zero-extra-cost (Table 2) smoothing trick, filed under "probabilistic-occupancy relaxation," alongside whatever fuzzy-filter/alpha-compositing tricks other sources contribute.
- **Q25 (Branch 2 handoff, ablation protocols worth imitating)** — this paper *is* the requested "head-to-head comparison of score functions under one protocol" and the reusable protocol elements are listed under "What transfers" above; recommend Branch 2 read this note's protocol bullet list directly rather than re-deriving it.
- **Q24 (Branch 2 handoff, replicable on primitives)** — negative: object set is 11 organic/free-form models, zero primitives; flag that published coverage/AUC magnitudes are not informative baselines for our regime, only the evaluation methodology is.
