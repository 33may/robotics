## Core method / idea

A survey + benchmark, not a new method. Reviews **22 viewpoint-quality measures for polygonal (triangle) meshes**, gives every measure a common information-theoretic notation, groups them into 5 families (area, silhouette, depth, stability, surface curvature — Table 2, Section 3), implements 21 of the 22 in one public framework (Section 4, framework released at ref [54]), and benchmarks all 22 against the same human-preference ground truth: the Dutagaci et al. 2010 benchmark (68 models, 26 human subjects picking "the most informative view" via a web page).

The unifying formalism (Section 3.1, adopted from Feixas et al. [35]) is an **information channel V→Z** between a set of viewpoints 𝒱 and a set of polygons 𝒵, defined by:
- conditional probability `p(z|v) = a_z(v)/a_t(v)` (share of the model's projected area from v that belongs to polygon z),
- input distribution `p(v) = a_t(v) / Σ_v a_t(v)` (viewpoint "importance" ∝ its own projected area),
- output distribution `p(z) = Σ_v p(v)p(z|v)` (average projected area of polygon z across all viewpoints).

Most of the 22 measures are then just different statistics of this channel (entropy, mutual information, KL/Jensen–Shannon divergence) or of simpler per-polygon/per-vertex geometric quantities (area, silhouette curvature, depth, discrete Gaussian curvature, mesh saliency).

Selected for this branch specifically because it is the highest formula-density source found: every measure has a numbered, closed-form equation, all computed under one identical rendering/benchmark protocol.

## Concrete results and numbers

**Rendering / action-space setup (Section 4, exact):**
- Viewpoint sphere: **642 viewpoints**, from recursive subdivision of an icosahedron.
- Sphere radius: **6× the radius of the model's smallest bounding sphere** ("perspective distortion being acceptable").
- Camera: looks at sphere center; FOV **19.2°**, sized to show "only the model and the minimum background."
- Projected-area rendering resolution: **640×640 px**; **no back-face culling** — polygons rendered from both sides.
- VQ18 (depth-based visual stability) uses a reduced **128×128 px** projection "to reduce computation time," similarity threshold **0.87** on normalized compression distance (NCD).
- 21 of 22 measures implemented in the authors' shared framework; VQ20 (visible saliency) uses Dutagaci's own implementation instead — "the only measure not included in the framework" (Section 4).

**Benchmark:** Dutagaci et al. 2010 — 68 3D models, 26 human subjects picked "the most informative view" per model via a web interface; error metric is bounded **0–1**, averaged per model, corrected for model symmetry (Section 2, citing [14]). Dutagaci's own original benchmark paper tested only 7 methods on a 258-viewpoint sphere; this survey re-runs all **22** measures on the same ground truth with the 642-viewpoint sphere above.

**Top-5 ranked measures — identical set for both median and mean±SD ranking (Figure 2, Section 4):**
1. Projected saliency (VQ21)
2. # visible triangles (VQ1)
3. Viewpoint entropy / I₂ (VQ5/VQ6 — tied, see below)
4. Curvature entropy (VQ19)
5. Plemenos and Benayada (VQ3)

Quoted finding: *"the five best measures belong to two categories: area attributes and surface curvature attributes. In contrast, the measures from the silhouette attributes category perform poorly."* (Section 4). Authors' own hypothesized explanation: object recognition relies on volumetric-primitive-like representations, not 2D outline shape (citing Biederman-style part decomposition from Section 1) — i.e., silhouette info alone under-performs richer 3D-derived attributes for the recognition task this benchmark measures.

**No numeric error values are tabulated anywhere.** Figure 2 is two box plots (ordered by median; ordered by mean±SD) with no printed numbers — I confirmed this by reading the actual results text, not just an AI summary of it; there is no results table, and Section 4's only quantitative content is the ranked-list-of-5 statement above. **This paper cannot supply exact citable numeric scores**, only a qualitative ranking.

**Three demo models** (used for the illustrative Figure 1/Figure 3 renderings, not the full 68-model benchmark run): Stanford Armadillo (17,296 triangles), a cow (23,216 triangles), Stanford dragon (26,142 triangles). Quoted: *"Most of the models used in this benchmark are common objects highly familiar to humans"* (Section 2) — i.e. everyday recognizable objects, not primitive shapes (box/cylinder/cone).

**No correlation matrix or redundancy analysis is reported anywhere in the paper** (confirmed by full-text search: the string "correlat" appears only in prose describing what VMI/I₁ measures — degree of correlation between a viewpoint and the polygon set — never as a cross-measure statistical result). The Conclusion (Section 6) explicitly defers this: *"it is worth investigating a convex linear combination of the information-theoretic measures I₁, I₂, I₃"* is listed as **future work**, not something the paper itself did.

**A documented internal inconsistency worth flagging for citability:** Table 2 marks Visibility Ratio (VQ4, row 4) with best-direction = **H** (highest is best), but the running prose under "Visibility ratio" (Section 3.2) states verbatim: *"The best viewpoint corresponds to the minimum value of the measure."* I verified this directly against the raw PMC HTML (not a transcription artifact) — it is a genuine contradiction in the source. Given VQ4 = (visible-area-weighted sum)/(total area), the geometrically sensible reading is that **higher** is better (more of the object's real surface visible), matching every sibling area-attribute measure and Table 2's "H"; the prose sentence appears to be an editing error, possibly copy-pasted from the adjacent Viewpoint Kullback–Leibler paragraph, where "minimum" genuinely is correct. Use Table 2's H, but do not cite this row's direction without this caveat.

## Load-bearing assumptions

- **A proper polygonal mesh with connectivity, not a raw point cloud.** Every formula needs one of: per-polygon area `A_z`/`a_z(v)` (needs faces), per-vertex discrete Gaussian curvature `K_i = 2π − Σφ_j` (Eq. 24 — needs the 1-ring of triangles around a vertex), or mesh-saliency curvature via Taubin's estimator (needs vertex normals from mesh connectivity). None of this is defined on an unstructured point set.
- **Visibility/occlusion computed by rendering the mesh from a synthetic camera** (rasterization/item-buffer style — "polygons are rendered from both sides," Section 4), not by any point-cloud hidden-point-removal technique. `vis_z(v)` is a binary "was this triangle's ID buffer non-empty," which presupposes closed, oriented polygons.
- **Ground truth is subjective recognizability/informativeness for familiar objects**, not measurement-quality or defect-detectability. The 26 human raters were asked for "the most informative view" for object recognition (Section 2), a canonical-view task, not an inspection task.
- **The camera/viewpoint sphere is fixed and object-independent per model** (always 642 points at 6× bounding-sphere radius) — action-space construction itself is not part of what's being studied; only scoring of a pre-fixed discrete candidate set is.
- **No lighting/BRDF/material model at all.** All measures are purely geometric/topological (area, curvature, depth, silhouette shape) computed from an orthographic-ish synthetic render; there is no shading, specularity, or reflectance term anywhere in the formalism.
- **Mesh resolution is assumed stable enough that per-polygon/per-vertex statistics are meaningful** — explicitly flagged as a known failure mode for viewpoint entropy itself (see below), not merely an implicit assumption.

## What transfers to our setting

- **A large, precisely-specified term table for the branch's Q5 deliverable.** See table below — 22 rows, each with an exact equation number, direction (H/L), discretization-sensitivity flag, and a defensible space/modality tag. This is the paper's real contribution to this branch.
- **Curvature entropy (VQ19, Eq. 24–25) is effectively a "surface-normal-entropy" term already in the branch's target list** — discrete Gaussian curvature from angle-deficit `K_i = 2π − Σφ_j`, histogrammed and Shannon-entropy'd over the visible vertex set. This is a ready-made, literature-precedented formalization for the branch's requested "surface-normal entropy" row, and it independently placed 4th of 22 on the benchmark.
- **Mesh saliency (VQ20, Eq. 26–27, `S(x) = |G(C(x),σ) − G(C(x),2σ)|`, a difference-of-Gaussians on mean curvature)** is a multi-scale geometric-anomaly detector — conceptually adjacent to (not the same as) defect detectability: it is built to flag geometric regions that stand out at one curvature scale vs. another. Not validated for scratches/dents/coating flaws by this paper, but the DoG-on-curvature construction is a reusable primitive for a geometry-side "does this patch look anomalous" signal, worth testing against real defects rather than assuming it transfers.
- **A second, distinct point→view aggregation family, beyond Soudarissanane's RMSE-of-residuals precedent** (see that pass's notes): this paper's measures aggregate per-primitive quantities into a per-view scalar via (a) plain sum/count (VQ1, VQ20), (b) area-weighted sum/ratio (VQ2, VQ4), (c) **Shannon entropy of a normalized histogram** (VQ5, VQ11, VQ19; Gini–Simpson variant in VQ16), (d) **KL or Jensen–Shannon divergence** to a reference or neighbor distribution (VQ7, VQ17, VQ21, VQ22), and (e) mutual-information decompositions (VQ6, VQ8, VQ9). Directly answers the branch's Q9 ("what is the unit of scoring... how do papers aggregate per-point quality to per-region/per-view") with concrete alternatives to mean/min/RMSE: **entropy-of-distribution and divergence-to-neighbor are both established aggregation patterns**, not novel to propose.
- **A concrete, literature-grounded negative result for the "bounded, normalized, cross-comparable score" question (branch Q8):** the paper states plainly that plain viewpoint entropy (VQ5) is *not* usable as a bounded/comparable score — quoted: *"An important drawback of viewpoint entropy is that it goes to infinity for finer and finer resolutions of the mesh... while I₂ presents a more stable behavior due to the normalizing effect of H(Z)"* (Section 3.2, after Eq. 8). I₂ was invented specifically to patch this pathology by subtracting a per-mesh constant `H(Z)`. Takeaway for the branch: raw entropy scores are **not resolution-invariant or cross-object-comparable by default**; achieving that requires an explicit normalization/subtraction step, and even I₂ is not literally bounded to a fixed interval, only "more stable." Visibility ratio (VQ4) is the one measure here that is genuinely bounded in [0,1] by construction (ratio of areas) and resolution-insensitive (Table 2, column D=N) — the strongest bounded/normalized precedent in this table.
- **A formalized "neighbourhood stability" notion of local viewpoint quality (VQ17, Eq. 21; VQ18, Eq. 22–23)**, directly relevant to branch Q ("formal definitions of best view within a neighbourhood"): instability is the mean Jensen–Shannon divergence between a viewpoint's visibility distribution p(Z|v) and its immediate graph-neighbors' on the 642-point sphere; depth-based visual stability instead counts neighbors below an NCD similarity threshold. Both formalize "is this view a locally stable representative of its neighbourhood," a third family alongside MVP's constraint-satisfaction-margin and mutual-information viewpoint stability, worth adding to the branch's comparison list.
- **Reusable head-to-head benchmark protocol** (Section 4, exact numeric settings above — 642-viewpoint icosahedral sphere, 6× bounding-sphere radius, 640×640 render, no back-face culling, one shared codebase for 21/22 measures) is precisely the kind of "same protocol, many score functions" comparison the branch's Branch-2-handoff question asks to imitate verbatim. **[Branch 2 handoff]**
- **Ground-truth protocol precedent for "human-labelled best view"** (Section 2): 26 subjects, 68 models, web-based single-best-view pick, symmetry-corrected 0–1 error metric (citing Dutagaci et al. 2010). Directly answers part of the branch's "what is ground truth for the best view" question with the human-labelled family — but flagged below as task-mismatched. **[Branch 2 handoff]**

## What does NOT transfer, and why

- **Nothing here is computable on a raw fused point cloud.** Every one of the 22 formulas needs either polygon connectivity (area attributes, silhouette attributes via rendered silhouette, depth attributes) or per-vertex mesh curvature from a triangle fan (VQ19, VQ20). Against the branch's Q10 partition ("computable-today / needs-Branch-3-structure / needs-a-mesh-we-do-not-have"): **the entire term set as literally formulated falls in "needs-a-mesh-we-do-not-have,"** with a caveat — the *image-based* subfamily (silhouette length/entropy/curvature/curvature-extrema, VQ10–13, and the depth attributes VQ14–16) only need a **rendered/splatted 2D image** of whatever geometry is available, so they could plausibly be recomputed from a point-splat render of the raw PLY without first meshing it; the area/entropy/mutual-information/curvature subfamily (VQ1–9, VQ17, VQ19–22) genuinely needs per-face area or per-vertex curvature and cannot be approximated without meshing or at least a robust local-neighborhood normal/curvature estimate.
- **Visibility/self-occlusion is computed by mesh rasterization (item/z-buffer), never by point-cloud hidden-point-removal.** No transfer of evidence to the branch's HPR-parameter-sensitivity question (Q11) — this paper is silent on point-based visibility entirely.
- **Zero incidence-angle/orthogonality formalization**, despite the intro narratively crediting Kamada & Kawai [18] as foundational (*"a measure to select a good view based on the angle between the view direction and the normal of the planes of the model... to avoid degenerative views"*, Section 2, Background). **Kamada & Kawai's own angle-based measure is never given an equation and is not among the 22 tested/benchmarked measures in Section 3.** This is a real gap worth being explicit about: the paper most historically adjacent to the branch's core "orthogonality" question is cited only in one narrative sentence, not formalized or benchmarked here — do not expect this survey to answer the D405/incidence-angle branch questions at all, and do not conflate the narrative citation with an actual tested formula.
- **No GSD/standoff/resolution term, no depth-of-field term, no defect-detectability content, and no image-quality-in-the-photographic-sense (exposure, motion blur, specular blowout) anywhere.** "Depth" here means synthetic z-buffer max-depth/depth-diversity within a rendered frame (VQ14–16), not standoff distance to a physical sensor with a working range.
- **No RGB-D/active-stereo sensor physics, no incidence-vs-noise curve, no glossy/specular retroreflection discussion, no industrial-inspection framing anywhere** (confirmed by full-text search: zero hits for "RGB-D," "point cloud," "depth sensor," "defect," or "inspection" outside of an unrelated psychology citation about "inspection" of novel 3D objects in the perception background). This document cannot serve the branch's RQs about D405 incidence-vs-depth-error, orthogonality-vs-glossy-specular tradeoffs, or defect detectability under raking light — those need the metrology/industrial-inspection literature, not this one.
- **No gradient-based or differentiable-rendering local optimization of any kind.** All 22 measures are evaluated by brute-force enumeration over the fixed 642-point icosahedral sphere; there is no continuous refinement, hill-climbing, or gradient step on any VQ(v) anywhere in the paper (confirmed by full-text search for "gradient," "differentiable," "hill-climb," "local search," "refine" — zero relevant hits). No evidence either way for the branch's sampling-vs-gradient question (Q "Sampling vs gradient"); this paper is 100% on the sampling side by construction, without ever discussing the tradeoff.
- **No sequential next-best-view / information-gain selection loop.** "Next best view" appears exactly once, as a one-line citation to Massios & Fisher [62] in the Table 3 applications list (3D reconstruction with a laser range scanner), never analyzed. This is a single-static-best-view survey for recognition/rendering, not a coverage-planning or NBV-loop paper — consistent with the branch's explicit scoping to "score only, not the selection loop," but worth noting there is no NBV *utility function* content here beyond the same entropy/mutual-information family already tabulated.
- **No correlation/redundancy analysis between the 22 measures**, despite this being the single most obviously answerable branch question (Q6) for a paper that benchmarks 22 measures against one ground truth. The gap is explicit and self-acknowledged: the authors propose looking at combinations of I₁/I₂/I₃ only as **future work** in the Conclusion, meaning even the paper's own authors had not, at time of publication, established which of their 22 measures are redundant with each other. **This is a real gap, not a nuance to work around** — branch Q6 cannot be answered from this source; a correlation study across VQ1–VQ22 does not exist in the published literature as far as this paper shows.
- **No propagation of upstream geometry uncertainty into any score** — meshes are treated as ground truth once loaded; no discussion of noisy/uncertain input geometry anywhere. No transfer to branch Q12 or Q13 (the 4 cm fused-vs-single-view offset question).
- **Test objects are recognizable everyday things (armadillo, cow, dragon, and presumably similar free-form objects across the other 65 Dutagaci models — explicitly "common objects highly familiar to humans"), not primitive shapes.** Flag for Branch 2's primitive-shape-replicability question: this benchmark's difficulty and its "which family wins" result (area + curvature attributes beat silhouette) may not generalize to boxes/cylinders, where curvature entropy is near-degenerate (flat faces have ~zero curvature almost everywhere except edges) and silhouette could behave very differently (a box's silhouette is trivially informative about orientation in a way an armadillo's isn't). **[Branch 2 handoff]**
- **No sensitivity analysis of normal/curvature-estimation error on any score** (branch Q7) — curvature-dependent measures (VQ19, VQ20) implicitly assume clean, dense, manifold mesh connectivity for the angle-deficit and Taubin curvature estimators; nothing here indicates how these degrade under noisy point-derived normals, sparse sampling, or dropout holes, all of which are stated properties of our fused PLY.
