## Core method / idea

A **pure taxonomy/survey paper, not a planner or a benchmark** — no original algorithm, no original experiment, no original numbers. Screened >150 publications (Scopus, Web of Science, IEEE Xplore, Google Scholar; keywords "Next Best View," "NBV planning," "viewpoint selection," "active vision," "3D reconstruction"; window 2010–present), applied three inclusion criteria (explicit NBV/viewpoint-planning focus; applied to 3D scene understanding/reconstruction/exploration; implementation or evaluation detail described), and selected **100 papers** for in-depth analysis (Introduction, p.2).

Sorts every paper into **five non-exclusive strategy classes** (§2.1–§2.5, Figs. 2–3):
- **Rule-based** (§2.1): predefined heuristics over visibility/area/distance; scoring is additive: `v* = argmax_v G(v)`, `G(v) = Σ_{c∈C} G_c(v)` (Eq. 2–3, Algorithm 1) — a weighted/summed multi-criterion score, not a set-cover or submodular formulation.
- **Uncertainty-based** (§2.2): entropy-reduction framing, `v* = argmax ΔU(v)`, `ΔU(v) = max(0, H_before − H_after(v))` (Eq. 4–5, Algorithm 2); often paired with NeRF/radiance-field uncertainty.
- **Sampling-based** (§2.3): `v* = argmax_{v∈A} G(v)` over randomly/directedly sampled candidates from action space A (Eq. 6, Algorithm 3).
- **Learning-based** (§2.4): RL policy `π* = argmax_π E[Σ γ^t r_t]` (Eq. 7, Algorithm 4); six sub-buckets given — supervised, RL, deep-regression, "scene coverage and view optimization" (SCVP is filed here), zero-shot/domain-specific, semantic-driven, hybrid-with-uncertainty.
- **Prediction-based** (§2.5): `v* = argmax G(v)`, `G(v) = Coverage(U(v;P))` where P is a prediction model estimating unseen regions from priors (Eq. 8–9, Algorithm 5); distinguished from learning-based by **not** continuously adapting online — deterministic inference from a pretrained/regression model on priors.

A generic top-level NBV objective is given once (Fig. 1, Eq. 1): `x*_{t+1} = argmax(G_t(x) − C_t(x))`, coverage gain minus a movement/feasibility cost term `C_t(x)` — this is the paper's only formalized acknowledgment that reachability/cost belongs in the objective, and it is never expanded anywhere in the paper's 21 pages (no IK, no collision, no capability map, no joint-space term).

Two **cross-cutting axes**, orthogonal to the five strategies, are also defined and are directly relevant to our framing:
- **Model-based vs. free-model-based NBV** (Table 1, p.3): model-based methods "assume that at least partial or complete knowledge about the environment's geometry is available," used for "inspecting targeted objects, recognizing objects, or mapping structured environments," optimizing by "prioritizing regions with the greatest informative gain" given known model properties — but "often lack flexibility in unpredictable or dynamic environments." Free-model-based methods impose no prior model, are adaptive to unknowns/dynamic scenes, at the cost of higher compute. **This is precisely our regime's name in the field's own vocabulary: known-geometry, targeted-object, structured-scene NBV = "model-based NBV."** The paper does not cross-tabulate which specific rule-/uncertainty-/sampling-/learning-/prediction-based papers fall on which side of this axis (Table 1 lists ~10 example refs per column without explaining the assignment logic), so it can't be used to pull out "the model-based rule-based subset" directly.
- **Surface-based vs. volume-based NBV** (Table 2): surface-based (meshes/surfels) suits grasping/texture-mapping tasks "where internal structure is less relevant" (closer to our object-inspection setting); volume-based (voxels/point clouds, full 3D volume) suits medical imaging/SLAM at higher compute/storage cost.

§3 catalogs **open-source libraries per strategy class** with GitHub links: 1 rule-based (fkie-nbv-planner, from Naazare et al. 2022), 3 uncertainty-based, 5 sampling-based (incl. `nbvplanner`/Bircher et al. 2016, `aeplanner`, `GradientNBV`, `mav_active_3d_planning`, `FUEL`), 4 learning-based (`PC-NBV`, `NBV-Net`, `ScanRL`, `6D_NBV`), 4 prediction-based (`GenNBV`, `SCONE`, `MACARONS`, `MAP-NBV`).

§4 Discussion answers the paper's own per-strategy research questions narratively and closes with Table 3, a **qualitative** pros/cons/example-application matrix (no numbers) — rule-based's example application is literally "Surface inspection, structured environments."

## Concrete results / numbers

There are **no experimental results or benchmark numbers in this paper at all** — confirmed by full read. Every quantity is either a citation-count statistic about the review itself or an equation:
- >150 candidate papers screened → **100 selected** for in-depth analysis (Introduction).
- Search window: 2010–present, across 4 databases.
- Table 3 (p.18): qualitative pros/cons/example-application per strategy, e.g. rule-based → "Efficient, simple, task-specific" / "Inflexible, limited in unstructured environments" / "Surface inspection, structured environments"; learning-based → "Adaptive to complex tasks, capable of continuous improvement" / "High data requirements, training complexity" / "Object tracking, autonomous navigation."
- No view-count numbers, no coverage percentages, no accuracy figures, no timing numbers anywhere — the paper never runs or reruns anything; it only summarizes what cited papers report qualitatively.

## Load-bearing assumptions

- The paper is descriptive/taxonomic, not experimental — treat every specific claim about a cited method (e.g., "SCVP achieves near-optimal reconstruction... under constrained resources") as this paper's *paraphrase* of that method's own claims, not as independently verified.
- Category boundaries are **explicitly fuzzy and admitted as such by the authors**: Dhami et al. (2023b, Pred-NBV) is listed under both uncertainty-based (Fig. 5) and prediction-based (Fig. 8) "due to its focus on neural prediction models"; Vasquez-Gomez et al. (2021) is discussed as learning-based (3D CNN regression, §2.4) but tabulated as a prediction-based *library* (regression-model-based, §3); GenNBV is a prediction-based library entry but the text itself says "For some readers, GenNBV might be more fit for learning-based NBV because it applies reinforcement learning." The five-way split is a soft, author-judgment-call taxonomy, not a formal partition.
- "Rule-based" as used here is a broad bucket spanning weighted-sum multi-criterion heuristics, MOO-formulated exploration-inspection (Naazare et al. 2022), and topological-relation methods (Dierenbach et al. 2016) — it is not reserved for, or specifically pointing at, deterministic per-primitive-shape recipes (face-orthogonal grid / cylinder orbit). The paper's rule-based section never descends to shape-primitive granularity.

## What transfers to our setting

- **Validates "model-based NBV" as our regime's recognized name in the field** (Table 1, §1): known/partial geometry + targeted-object inspection + structured environment is a first-class, named NBV subproblem in the field's own self-understanding — not a niche or superseded framing, and explicitly distinguished from the free-model-based exploration NBV the branch scope excludes. Directly usable framing sentence for our own writeup.
- **Rule-based NBV is textually anchored to exactly our application** — Table 3 names "Surface inspection, structured environments" as rule-based's example application, and the narrative (§2.1, Discussion) repeatedly ties rule-based methods to "industrial settings where the scene is predictable and well-defined." This is direct textual support (independent of ObjView-Bench and independent of any CAD-inspection-specific literature) that deterministic/heuristic per-object view rules are a recognized, still-current NBV strategy class for our exact regime — not a regression to something superseded, countering any implicit pressure toward learned selection by default.
- **SCVP (Pan, Hu & Wei, 2022) is independently corroborated as literal minimal-set-cover view planning**: "neural networks help determine a minimal, yet efficient, subset of views for object reconstruction. With low redundancy and maximum surface coverage, SCVP realizes near-optimal object reconstruction when critical resources are constrained" (§2.4). This is a second, independent citation trail (beyond ObjView-Bench's own citation of SCVP) for Q1/Q15's minimality-criterion question, and confirms SCVP's title claim ("via set covering") is the paper's own framing of the problem, not a benchmark-paper reinterpretation.
- **The additive rule-based scoring template `G(v) = Σ_c G_c(v)` (Eq. 3) is a directly citable generic form to extend** — if we add an orthogonality/incidence term as one more `G_c`, we are doing exactly what the field's own generic rule-based formalization anticipates (a multi-criterion sum), even though this paper itself never instantiates an orthogonality criterion. Useful as the "here is the field's own template, and here is where our quality term slots in" citation for RQ1/Q7.
- **Eq. 1's cost term `C_t(x)`** is the field's only generic, top-level acknowledgment that movement/feasibility cost belongs inside the NBV objective (alongside coverage gain) — a precedent that "cost/reachability belongs in the objective function, in principle" exists at the survey level, even though (see below) it is never operationalized into anything IK/collision-like anywhere in the paper. Consistent with, and slightly more abstract than, ObjView-Bench's still-coarse quarter-sphere treatment.
- **Curated short-list of the closest rule-based/inspection-adjacent citations for Q5** (published precedent for per-primitive view rules), ranked by proximity: Zhou et al. (2009) — view planning "based on the visual region of the vision system," explicitly optimized so "the camera's field of view is to be aligned precisely with the object or scene," found application "in inspections" (the single closest FOV-alignment/inspection sentence in the whole paper); Karaszewski et al. (2016) — NBV algorithm benchmarking across multiple 3D scanners/manipulators, highlighting "sensor-specific constraints"; Naazare et al. (2022) — exploration-inspection as a weighted-sum multi-objective-optimization problem for an RGB-D-camera-on-robot-arm mobile manipulator, open-sourced as `fkie-nbv-planner`; Dierenbach et al. (2016) — topological-relation-driven NBV; Pito (1999) and Low & Lastra (2006) — classic hierarchical/constraint-evaluation rule-based methods. None of these, per this survey's own description, name or formalize a box-face/cylinder-orbit primitive-decomposition recipe — worth chasing Zhou (2009) and Naazare (2022) directly in a later pass since they are the two closest textual hits to "camera aligned to surface" and "arm-mounted RGB-D inspection," respectively.

## What does NOT transfer, and why

- **Zero mentions of orthogonality or incidence-angle anywhere** (confirmed by full-text grep) — same fundamental gap as ObjView-Bench. This paper cannot inform Q7 (max admissible deviation from orthogonality) or the quality-floor half of Q1 at all; "view quality" beyond raw visibility/coverage does not appear as a concept in any of the 100 reviewed papers as summarized here.
- **Zero mentions of "cylinder," "CAD," "capability map," "reachab-," "kinemat-," "inverse kinematic"/"IK," "ILP"/"integer program," "submodular," or "set-cover"/"covering" outside the two SCVP citation-string occurrences** (confirmed by full-text grep); the one "collision" hit is incidental (an unrelated sentence about a "collision-free graph" in a different paper's RL exploration setup) and "primitive" appears exactly once, used dismissively ("rule-based methods... seem somewhat 'primitive' to modern machine-learning-driven methods," i.e. old-fashioned — not shape-primitive). Consequently this paper is **silent on**: Q2 (submodularity), Q3 (ILP/FPT tractability at our instance size), Q9 (reachability fallback ladder — the four-rung taxonomy), Q10 (capability map vs. post-filter, false-positive cost), Q11 (reachable-fraction numbers), Q12 (IK multiplicity / Cartesian-vs-joint-space action definition), and Q20 (primitive-decomposition-conditioned planning). It is a survey of **selection mechanism** (how a candidate view's score is computed), not of **deployment constraints** (what happens when the ideal view can't be reached).
- **"Greedy" never appears in the Chvátal/set-cover-termination sense** — its only two occurrences are "ε-greedy" (RL exploration policy) and generic "greedy algorithm and upper confidence bound techniques" for exploration-exploitation balance in the uncertainty-based Discussion (§4) — unrelated to greedy-set-cover's approximation guarantee. Q2 and Q15's Chvátal-termination candidate are unaddressed here.
- **No quantitative results anywhere** — the paper cannot supply numbers for Q6 ("how many views does a primitive deserve" — published thresholds) or Q14 ("what set sizes do methods actually emit"); it can only point at candidate source papers (SCVP, GenNBV, PC-NBV, etc.) to chase individually for their own numbers, most of which are presumably already covered elsewhere in this research pass.
- **Bircher et al. (2016) is cited only as a sampling-based frontier/receding-horizon exploration planner** (§2.3, and listed in the free-model-based column of Table 1) — the paper never discusses its redundancy/view-count behavior, so it offers no corroboration or contradiction of the branch's Bircher-et-al. redundancy concern (Q14); that critique must come from elsewhere.
- **No discussion of primitive-decomposition-conditioned planning, and no distinct CAD/industrial-inspection subfield separated from general "structured environment" rule-based NBV** — the paper never raises shape-primitive decomposition as a topic at all, which is itself weak supporting (not confirming) evidence for Q20's claim that this fusion is largely absent from the field, given that the paper's explicit purpose is exhaustive sub-topic categorization.
- **No discussion of VLM/LLM orchestration, discrete action-space presentation to a language-model agent, or any agent-facing action-space design** — entirely outside scope; every strategy here assumes a planner directly outputs/executes the next view itself, with no intermediary agent selecting from a pre-enumerated menu. Irrelevant to RQ4.
- **Learning-vs-analytical comparison (Q16) is not empirically settled** — Table 3 gives only generic, non-quantitative tradeoffs (learning-based: "Adaptive... High data requirements"; rule-based: "Efficient, simple... Inflexible... in unstructured environments") with no head-to-head numbers on any object class, let alone primitive-shaped ones. Adds no evidence beyond textbook-level tradeoffs already known from other sources.
