# Scott, Roth & Rivest — "View Planning for Automated Three-Dimensional Object Reconstruction and Inspection" (ACM Computing Surveys 35(1):64–96, March 2003)

**PDF**: NRC Publications Archive accepted-manuscript copy, https://nrc-publications.canada.ca/eng/view/accepted/?id=c9a6149f-27d0-4269-8122-80dd6104c0a1 (NRC 45866; 35 pp., identical pagination to the published ACM version, pp. 64–96; read in full, all figures/tables/references reviewed)
**Branch**: Scoring a single view: metrics and local optimality
**Read**: 2026-08-27

## Core method / idea

A **taxonomy/critique survey**, not an empirical or algorithmic paper. It formalizes the view planning problem (VPP) for active triangulation-based range cameras used in high-precision 3D reconstruction/inspection, then classifies and critiques ~25 prior methods against a requirements checklist the authors themselves construct. Structure:

1. **Problem formalization** (§2): imaging environment (range camera + positioning system + fixtures + object, Fig. 1), the four-phase reconstruction cycle (plan→scan→register→integrate, Fig. 3), and a formal VPP statement (§2.3): *"For a given imaging environment and target object, find a suitably short view plan N satisfying the specified reconstruction goals and achieve this within an acceptable computation time."* VPP is stated to be NP-complete, citing Tarbox and Gottschlich [1995] and the authors' own Scott [2002] thesis.
2. **A requirements taxonomy for view planning systems** (§3, Table I): 15 named requirements grouped as General (model quality spec, generalizable algorithm, generalized viewpoints, view overlap, robust, efficient, self-terminating), Object (minimal a priori knowledge, shape constraints, material constraints), Sensor (frustum, shadow effect, measurement performance), Positioning System (6D pose, pose constraints, positioning performance) — each given a one-paragraph operational definition (§3.2.1–3.2.16).
3. **Two proposed performance measures** (§3.3): *view plan quality* m_v and *view plan efficiency* e_v (formulas below).
4. **A method taxonomy**: model-based (§5, Fig. 4: set theory / graph theory / computational geometry) vs. non-model-based (§6, Fig. 5: volumetric / surface-based / global), walking through ~20 named systems (Tarbox & Gottschlich, Connolly, Banta & Abidi, Massios & Fisher, Maver & Bajcsy, Pito, Reed, Yuan, Whaite & Ferrie, Papadopoulos-Orfanos, etc.).
5. **A cross-method comparison and critique** (§7–8): Table III grades 10 representative algorithms Y/N/P/? against the 15 Table-I requirements; §8 explains, requirement by requirement, why almost every cell is N or P.
6. **Cross-cutting issues** (§9): theoretical framework (VPP = set covering, NP-complete), the concept of "viewpoint" vs. "view swath," generalized viewpoints, the view-sphere strategy and its limits, intermediate-space representations, and pose error (Fig. 9).
7. **Conclusion** (§10): the field has no general-purpose, performance-oriented view planning system in production use; open problems are efficiency, accuracy, and robustness; four future directions proposed (multi-phase pipelines, multi-technique combination, expert systems, multi-sensor fusion).

## Concrete results and numbers

This is a survey — it contains no new experiments. The quantitative content that exists is entirely definitional/taxonomic:

- **View plan quality**, m_v (§3.3, p.72): *"the overall verified measurability m_v of the reconstruction with respect to the model specification... The goal is m_v = 1.0."* Footnote 3 gives the only closed-form definition offered in this document: *"Verified measurability of a reconstruction can be seen as the ratio of reconstructed surface area compliant with the specified model criteria to the overall object surface area."* I.e. m_v = (compliant surface area) / (total surface area) ∈ [0,1] — **area-weighted, binary-compliance pooling**, not a mean of a continuous score and not min-pooling. Precise mathematical definition is deferred to the companion papers Scott et al. [2001b] and Scott [2002] (not in this document).
- **View plan efficiency**, e_v = n_Opt/n (§3.3, p.72), where n = |N| is the length of the generated plan and n_Opt the (generally unknown) optimal plan length, surrogated by n_Best, the shortest plan found by any method to date on that task. Goal e_v = 1.0. This is a *plan-level* (multi-view), not per-view, efficiency measure — out of this branch's direct scope but useful context for Branch 4.
- **Eq. 1–2** (p.66, Fig. 2): the classical active-triangulation range equation z = d·f₀/(p + f₀·tanθ), x = z·tanθ, where θ is the *laser scan/deflection angle* inside the sensor (not surface incidence angle) — this is sensor-internal optics, not the incidence-angle-vs-error relationship the branch needs; flagged so it isn't mistaken for that.
- **Eq. 3** (p.79, Banta & Abidi / Massios & Fisher): f_total(v̄) = w_v·f_visibility(v̄) + w_q·f_quality(v̄) — the canonical linear-combination NBV objective. f_visibility = count of previously-unseen occlusion-plane voxels visible by ray tracing; f_quality is described only qualitatively ("maximize the number of low-quality voxels visible from a given viewpoint"), formulated as |dot product of estimated local surface normal and viewing-direction vector| for occupied voxels (this is the **cosine-incidence term**, Connolly's "normal" algorithm, p.79) — a directly reusable PLY-space formula: quality(v, x) = |n̂(x) · v̂|.
- **Optical-baseline subtend angle** (§9.2.3, p.90): *"For mid- to high-performance range cameras, the optical baseline typically subtends an angle in the range (50°–35°) at the sensor's optimal stand-off range. Consequently, the shadow effect is sensitive to rotation about the sensor boresight."* This is the only numeric angle given anywhere in the document, and it is about **bistatic shadow/occlusion geometry** (source vs. receiver shadow zones), not about incidence-angle-vs-depth-noise. No numeric noise-vs-angle curve, no knee angle, and no D405-relevant numbers exist anywhere in this document.
- **Table III** (p.86): 10 algorithms × 15 requirements, graded Y/N/P/?/–. The "Measurement performance," "Shadow effect," and "Positioning performance" rows are overwhelmingly N or P across all 10 methods — a quantifiable (if qualitative) statement of how underdeveloped rigorous per-view quality scoring was as of 2003.

## Load-bearing assumptions

1. **Active, triangulation-based range camera with a physically separate source and receiver (bistatic sensor)**, calibrated, with a positioning system moving sensor and/or object (§2.1, assumptions §3.1). This *does* match the D405 (IR projector + stereo imagers, effectively bistatic/multi-baseline), so the taxonomy's sensor-frustum/shadow-effect vocabulary transfers, but the paper's laser-deflection scanner examples (Fig. 2) do not map 1:1 onto active-stereo block-matching depth.
2. **Object model is either absent (non-model-based methods) or a partial/prior surface model built up incrementally during scanning (model-based methods)** — none of the surveyed methods assumes a complete accurate CAD model as a *planning input* the way Tarabanis's MVP system does (that is explicitly the companion paper's territory, referenced but not surveyed here). Closest to our setting: **model-based reconstruction planning that builds on the model acquired so far**, which is the paradigm, though our "model so far" is a defective one-shot survey PLY rather than an incrementally-refined scan.
3. **6D generalized viewpoint space is the theoretical ideal, but almost no surveyed method uses it** — §8.4.1 and §9.2.5: nearly all constrain viewpoint space to a 1D or 2D view-sphere/view-circle with camera boresight fixed on the object centroid. The paper treats this as a *known limitation of the field*, not a requirement — directly relevant to our own uniform-viewsphere critique.
4. **View planning targets full-surface coverage to a specification, not defect detection under variable illumination.** "Inspection" in the title and throughout means *dimensional/geometric measurability against a model spec* (does this patch meet the required sampling density and precision), never visual/optical defect detectability. This shapes what the paper can and cannot answer for this branch (see negative findings below).
5. **Deterministic, single-technique, "monolithic" methods dominate the surveyed literature** (§8.5.3) — the paper's own critique is that no reviewed method combines techniques, and that random/non-deterministic selection was essentially unexplored as of 2003.

## What transfers to our setting

- **Table I's requirements taxonomy is a reusable checklist for scoring our own action-space design**, independent of any specific algorithm: model quality spec, generalized viewpoints, view overlap, robustness to pose error, shape/material constraints, frustum/shadow modeling, positioning performance (§3, Table I, pp.69–72). Useful as an audit template when we specify what our view-quality score must account for.
- **m_v as area-weighted compliance-ratio pooling is a directly relevant precedent for branch question 9 (unit of scoring / aggregation)**: rather than mean- or min-pooling a continuous per-point score, the field's own proposed top-level metric is *fraction of surface area meeting a pass/fail threshold* — i.e., threshold first, then area-weight, don't average raw scores. Worth weighing against min-pooling for our own "worst-point defensible?" question (footnote 3, p.72).
- **m_v and e_v are both bounded, normalized, goal=1.0 quantities** — this is genuine (if thin) precedent for branch question 8 ("is there precedent for a bounded, normalized per-view score comparable across views/objects"). Caveat: m_v is a *whole-plan* reconstruction-quality measure aggregated over many views, not a *single-view* score; it is evidence that the field values boundedness as a design goal, not a ready-made per-view formula.
- **The cosine-incidence "normal algorithm" (Connolly, p.79) is a clean, directly implementable PLY-space term**: quality(v,x) = |n̂(x)·v̂| per occupied voxel/point — exactly the "incidence/cosine" row the branch's term table (Q5) needs, with an explicit citation trail (Connolly 1985, formalized in Banta & Abidi's Eq. 3 combination).
- **The view-sphere critique (§9.2.3, pp.89–91) is a direct, citable indictment of our current uniform-{h,v} action space**, from the field's own canonical survey: *"To the degree that object shape is not spherical, viewpoint position is nonoptimal. Measurement performance is further degraded if the view sphere radius must be set to view the entire object... the view sphere approach... fails to take into account the important dependence of sensor performance on stand-off distance... It is not possible to optimize sensor performance with such limitations"* (§8.4.1, p.87; §9.2.3, p.90). This is a strong, checkable citation for the framing doc's premise that the geometry-agnostic sphere is a known-inadequate design, not a strawman.
- **Precedent for surface-anchored (not sphere-anchored) viewpoint generation**: the paper references the authors' own companion work, Scott et al. [2000, 2001b], as generating an "optimal scan zone" by *offsetting from the sampled surface patch* rather than from a view sphere (§2.4, p.69) — direct precedent for a per-primitive/per-face standoff-offset construction rather than uniform spherical binning. (This companion paper — `scott-model-based-view-planning`, MVA 2009 — is flagged HIGH priority in the round-2 map and should be read next for the actual formula.)
- **Grazing-angle-as-quality-factor precedent, with an explicit critique of how it was implemented (branch Q2)**: Tarbox and Gottschlich [1995] used *"different grazing angle thresholds... for the light source and the camera"* as **admissibility cutoffs** in their measurability matrix (§5.1, p.74) — i.e., angle was treated as a hard binary feasibility gate in the most rigorous set-theoretic treatment surveyed, not a smooth weight. Scott et al.'s own critique of this (same paragraph): *"The only grazing angle effects considered were the impact on dilution of surface sampling density and viewability at acute angles in the presence of surface microstructure. This masked the most important impact of grazing angle, which is on measurement error."* This is a first-class, checkable finding for Q2: **the field's most careful early treatment of angle used a threshold, and the paper's own authors call that a mistake because it ignores the (presumably smoother) effect on measurement error.** It argues for smoothness in the *true* underlying quantity (error), even where implementations use thresholds for tractability.
- **Pose-error framing directly matches our fused-cloud-vs-live-depth problem (branch Q12/Q13)**: §9.3 (p.91, Fig. 9) states the exact failure mode we face — *"When the NBV list is sent to a positioning system whose position and orientation accuracy is inferior to that of the sensor, the coverage of individual viewpoints and of the NBV list as a whole is compromised... We can recover a refined pose estimate post facto by employing suitable registration techniques and subsequently reestimate measurement quality within the acquired image. However, we are still left with data acquisition differing from that which had been planned."* This is precisely our situation: a view is planned in fused-PLY coordinates, executed via IK, and the camera's own single-view depth may disagree with the planning geometry — the paper frames this as an open, largely unsolved robustness problem (§8.1.5: *"None of the methods incorporated both sensor and positioning system error models"*; only Tarbox and Gottschlich's morphological erosion of per-point viewpoint sets is cited as a mitigation, §9.3). This supports treating the offset as a **modelling requirement that must enter the score** (build in margin / erode the admissible-view set), not merely a calibration nuisance to fix away — consistent with the branch's own framing of the question.
- **Multi-sensor / multi-phase future-direction (§10.2, p.92)**: *"a multi-phase approach such as scene exploration, rough modeling, fine modeling, problem resolution"* and *"multisensor fusion approaches — perhaps using a fast, wide field-of-view, low-precision sensor for scene exploration and collision avoidance in combination with a high-quality range sensor for precise surface measurements"* — a structural precedent relevant to branch Q19 (does refinement belong offline or online): the paper's own prescription is staged coarse-then-fine, which maps onto "refine each candidate once at action-space construction time" rather than continuous online refinement.
- **Aspect graphs are explicitly disqualified as a "best view in a neighbourhood" formalism (branch Q14)**: §5.2 (p.75) — an aspect graph partitions viewpoint space into regions of *qualitatively equivalent visible topology* (same visible vertices/edges/faces), but *"viewpoints with the same visibility (i.e., the same aspect) are by no means equivalent for sensing purposes."* Directly rules out aspect-graph-style topological equivalence as a proxy for view *quality* equivalence — useful negative precedent when enumerating candidate "best-view-in-neighbourhood" definitions for Q14.

## What does NOT transfer, and why (negative findings — first class)

- **No σ(range, θ) curve or any sensor noise formula for any sensor, D405 or otherwise (branch Q1).** This survey states, as a *requirement* (§3.2.13, p.71), that "sensor measurement performance should be modeled, including variation of measurement precision and sampling density within the frustum and surface inclination effects," and separately notes (§2.1, p.66) that measurement "is subject to random nonisotropic geometric noise [Beraldin et al. 1993]" — but it never supplies the formula itself, and its own Table III / §8.3.3 critique confirms that almost none of the 25 surveyed methods modeled measurement performance quantitatively either ("the view planning literature generally assumes idealized, error-free sensors... No author addressed the nonisotropic and nonstationary nature of residual geometric noise"). **This document is a taxonomy/vocabulary source for Q1, not a numeric source** — the actual curve must come from dedicated sensor-characterization papers (Nguyen/Izadi, Soudarissanane, or the companion Scott [2009] MVA paper, all separately flagged in the round-2 map).
- **No numeric knee/threshold angle for any sensor.** The only angle number in the whole document (50°–35° optical-baseline subtend, §9.2.3) describes *bistatic shadow-zone* geometry, not a measurement-error knee. Branch Q2's "is there a knee near ~60°" cannot be answered from this document with any number; only the qualitative threshold-vs-smooth framing point above (from Tarbox & Gottschlich's critique) transfers.
- **No treatment of specular reflection driving the optimum off-normal (branch Q3).** The paper discusses "shiny surfaces" and "multiple reflections" only as failure/artifact sources to be modeled or avoided (§3.1, p.70: *"Depending on illumination and observation geometry, specular surfaces may result in drop outs... Multiple reflections on a shiny surface in corner regions can also produce wild measurements"*; §10.1, p.92 flags reflectance-step-edges and multiple reflections as unsolved). It never proposes or surveys a method that **deliberately** moves the camera off-normal to avoid retroreflection into the imager. This is a genuine, explicit gap: the survey names the failure mode's *cause* (specular reflectance) but no surveyed method treats it as an argument against pure orthogonality — the "is orthogonality ever bad" question is open in this literature, not answered either way.
- **No defect-detectability content whatsoever (branch Q4).** Despite "Inspection" in the title, "inspection" here means conformance to a dimensional/geometric model specification (precision, sampling density) — never visual defect (scratch/dent/coating-flaw) detectability, raking light, or foreshortening-vs-visibility trade-offs for surface flaws. Zero mentions of "defect," "scratch," "flaw," or "raking." **This paper answers the reconstruction-geometry question, explicitly not the industrial-inspection-defect-detectability question** — confirming the branch brief's own framing that these are different literatures requiring different sources.
- **No hidden-point-removal or bare-point-cloud visibility algorithm (branch Q11).** Predates HPR-style methods (Katz et al. 2007 postdates this survey). Every surveyed visibility mechanism needs *some* intermediate structured representation — voxel occupancy grid, octree, mesh, occlusion-edge extraction from a range image, or a solid-geometry CAD model (§5, §6, Fig. 4–5) — never raw point-cloud ray visibility. This is a structural gap: nothing here validates or critiques computing visibility directly on an unstructured PLY without first building a surface/volume proxy.
- **No propagation of input-geometry uncertainty into a per-view quality score (branch Q12).** Pose error is discussed (§9.3) only as corrupting the *execution* of a planned view (planned pose ≠ actual pose), never as *uncertainty in the object-model geometry itself* feeding forward into how a candidate view is scored. Tarbox and Gottschlich's morphological erosion (removing viewpoints "not robust to pose variation," §5.1, p.75) is the closest precedent, and it operates on the *viewpoint* set, not on propagating *object-geometry* uncertainty into a measurement-quality number. Confirms the branch brief's suspicion that this is a real gap in the literature, not something already solved and merely uncited.
- **No VLM/LLM, learned, or gradient/differentiable-rendering content at all** — this is a 2003 survey; branch Qs on gradient-based local optimization, differentiable rendering, and VLM-as-judge are entirely out of scope for this document (its own "future directions," §10.2, don't anticipate any of this).
- **No ground-truth-for-best-view methodology, no benchmark protocol, no ablation of quality terms** (branch Q2/Q23–25, handoff to Branch 2). §3.3's m_v/e_v are *proposed* performance measures, not measures anyone had actually used in a comparative benchmark at time of writing — §8.1.6 states explicitly: *"Given the lack of standardized performance benchmarks, it is difficult to make quantitative performance comparisons between proposed view planning methods... Few authors explicitly evaluated the computational complexity of their algorithms."* This is the canonical citation for "no benchmarks exist," useful for Branch 2, but there is no protocol here to imitate.
- **No smoothing-trick catalogue for a discontinuous visibility landscape (branch Q15).** Visibility discontinuity under viewpoint perturbation is not discussed as a *problem to be smoothed*; the surveyed methods handle it via discretization (view-sphere sampling) rather than any continuous relaxation (fuzzy visibility, occupancy fields, alpha compositing). This branch question needs a different, more recent source (the round-2 map's differentiable-rendering/NBV-gradient cluster).

## Assessment for the branch

High value as the **taxonomy/vocabulary spine** the round-2 map predicted it to be, and as a source of two genuinely load-bearing citations: (1) the field's own canonical critique of the view-sphere strategy (directly validates our project's departure from the uniform {h,v} action space), and (2) the exact framing of the pose-error / "planned view ≠ what was actually measured" problem that maps onto our fused-PLY-vs-single-view offset. It is explicitly **not** a source for any numeric curve, threshold, benchmark protocol, or defect-detectability claim — those gaps are first-class findings in their own right (the paper's own §8 critique documents, table-by-table, that the field it surveys never solved measurement-performance modeling either). The highest-value follow-up reads it points to are Scott's own companion papers (2001a set-covering formulation, 2001b multi-stage/optimal-scan-zone method, 2002 pose-error-effects paper, and the 2009 MVA "Model-Based View Planning" already flagged HIGH in the round-2 map) — those, not this survey, are where the actual formulas for measurability, incidence-angle-weighted quality, and pose-error propagation would be found.
