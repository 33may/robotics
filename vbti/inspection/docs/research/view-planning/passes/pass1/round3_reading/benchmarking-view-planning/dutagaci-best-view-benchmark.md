## Core method / idea

A benchmark, not an algorithm. Three components:

1. **Ground truth from humans, not geometry.** Web interface (§4.2, Fig. 2b) shows 68 triangular meshes one at a time, each starting from a random pose; the subject drags/rotates the model to what they judge the "most informative" view (explicitly *not* "most aesthetic" — the instructions steer subjects toward a recognition-maximizing view) and submits. 26 subjects × 68 models = up to 1768 labeled viewpoints, each a continuous point on the view sphere.
2. **Discretization + symmetry-aware ground truth (§4.3.1).** Viewpoints are sampled with a geodesic sphere built by iteratively subdividing an octahedron; they use **258 vertices**. Every raw subject click is snapped to the nearest geodesic-sphere vertex via geodesic distance. For each vertex `v` of a model `m`, they compute a **symmetry set** Σ_v = {w ∈ V : d(I_w, I_v) ≤ t}, where I_v is the rendered depth image from viewpoint v and d is a rotation-invariant image dissimilarity (Fourier–Mellin-based image matching, Casasent & Psaltis 1976). Threshold t is set low ("almost-identical views only"). The ground truth for subject s on model m is *the whole symmetry set* Σ_{v^s_m}, not a single point — so a box or cylinder's 4-fold/∞-fold equivalent views are all counted correct.
3. **Scoring metric — View Selection Error (VSE), Eq. 1–2.** For an algorithm's chosen viewpoint v^a_m and one subject's ground-truth symmetry set Σ_{v^s_m}:
   W_s(v^a_m) = (1/π) · min_{v ∈ Σ_{v^s_m}} GD(v, v^a_m)
   (GD = geodesic distance on the unit sphere, normalized by π so W ∈ [0,1]). VSE averages W_s over all S subjects for a model (Eq. 2), then results are further averaged over the 68-model set (Table 2). Lower VSE = better (0 = exact human match, on average).
4. **Inconsistency metric (§5.1, Eq. 3–4)** — same machinery applied leave-one-subject-out: for every pair of subjects (p, r), compute W_p(v^r_m), average over all S² pairs → Inconsistency_m. This quantifies how much humans agree with each other on a model, using the *same* symmetry-aware distance as the algorithm-scoring metric — i.e., human inter-rater disagreement is measured on a scale directly comparable to VSE.

## Concrete results / numbers

- Dataset: 68 triangular meshes, mix of Stanford 3D Scanning Repository + SHREC2007 watertight database; mostly familiar objects (bunny, teapot, armadillo, David's head, chair, cat, cow, camel, ant, dog, dragon, helicopter, cup, octopus, rockerarm, hand, vase, human, teddy...); only 2/68 are "unfamiliar."
- Geodesic sphere: 258 viewpoints.
- 7 algorithms benchmarked (§3): view area, ratio of visible area (Polonsky et al.), surface area entropy (Vázquez et al.), silhouette length (Polonsky et al.), silhouette entropy (Page et al.), curvature entropy (Page et al.), mesh saliency (Lee/Varshney/Jacobs). All are single-shot "maximize a scalar geometric-complexity descriptor over the 258 candidate viewpoints" methods — no view clustering, no learning.
- **Table 2 — VSE averaged over 68 models, and n = number of models where that algorithm was the single best (lowest VSE) among the 7:**
  - View area: VSE 0.517, n=9
  - Ratio of visible area: VSE 0.473, n=7
  - Surface area entropy: VSE **0.396** (best average), n=**15** (most frequent winner)
  - Silhouette length: VSE 0.446, n=12
  - Silhouette entropy: VSE 0.484, n=7
  - Curvature entropy: VSE 0.474, n=9
  - Mesh saliency: VSE 0.430 (2nd best average), n=9
- Key finding stated explicitly: **no single algorithm dominates** — "None of the methods is consistently the best (or worst) over all the objects; they are rather complementary" — echoing Polonsky et al. 2005. n-values sum to 68 (9+7+15+12+7+9+9=68), i.e. every model has exactly one "winner," consistent with per-model comparison across all 7 methods.
- Table 1 — per-model human Inconsistency (symmetry-aware, Eq. 4): low end = bunny 0.107, teddy 0.113, cat 0.114, cow 0.126, David's head 0.127, vase 0.140, human 0.149, Utah teapot 0.198, armadillo 0.212; high end = camel 0.319, ant 0.329, dog 0.330, dragon 0.342, helicopter 0.391, cup 0.392, octopus 0.395, rockerarm 0.444, hand 0.456. Range is roughly 0.11–0.46 on the same [0,1] scale as VSE — i.e., **best algorithms (VSE ≈ 0.40) are still worse than or comparable to human-human disagreement on hard models (Inconsistency up to 0.46), but much worse than human agreement on easy models (~0.11–0.15)**. This is the load-bearing number for judging whether an algorithm is "doing well": VSE should be read relative to the model's own Inconsistency floor, not as an absolute score.
- Qualitative failure notes (§5.1 end, §5.2): geometric-complexity maximization is *context-blind* and fails on cues humans use — e.g., bunny's ears/tail/base holes and chair's legs mislead mesh saliency into a wrong view; humans never pick a view showing the object's base/bottom when the object "stands on the ground" (motivates automatic base detection to prune the search space, citing Theetten et al. and Fu et al.); humans prefer three-quarter views generally but frontal views for faces/bodies; the vase has very low inconsistency despite geometrically diverse ground-truth points because of its rotational symmetry (this is the paper's own worked example of why symmetry-set handling matters).

## Load-bearing assumptions

- **"Best view" ≡ most informative/recognition-maximizing view of a whole object**, explicitly *not* aesthetic and *not* tied to any downstream task (no recognition system, no defect, no measurement objective is in the loop) — human judgment is used as a direct proxy for information content, calibrated by explicit instruction text to subjects.
- Ground truth is **continuous-in-principle, discretized only for algorithm comparison** — the human click is a real 3D point on the sphere; only the algorithm side is inherently constrained to 258 candidates. This means the discretization/snap-to-nearest-vertex step is a modeling choice made purely for evaluation, not a fundamental limit of the ground truth.
- Symmetry detection is **image-based and threshold-gated (t "set low")** — a heuristic dissimilarity on rendered depth images, not a formal symmetry-group computation. It is model-specific and per-vertex, not a global object symmetry statement (e.g., a box would need its symmetry set computed the same empirical way, not assumed from a CAD symmetry group).
- The entire evaluation is a **single-view** task: one best view per object, no notion of a view sequence, multi-view coverage, or view budget. There is no fused point cloud, no injected sensor defect, no simulated scan — ground truth comes directly from the (presumably clean) reference mesh being rendered for human viewing and for algorithm rendering. Both GT and algorithm candidates see the *same* clean mesh.
- Evaluation is symmetric/algorithm-agnostic: all 7 candidate methods are deterministic, single-shot, closed-form functions over the same 258-vertex candidate set — no learned or VLM-based method is evaluated, no notion of an "action space" or sequential selection exists in this paper.

## What transfers to our setting

- **The symmetry-set construct is the single most directly reusable idea for Q8.** It gives us a concrete, previously-validated mechanism for "correct view is a set, not a point": Σ_v defined by rendered-image similarity under a low threshold, built once per object/pose. For our primitives (box faces, cylinder rotational symmetry), this is directly applicable — build Σ_v on our candidate action-space viewpoints (their equivalent of the 12×30°×3-vertical sphere) using the same recipe (render depth/silhouette image from each candidate pose, cluster near-identical ones). It also gives a template for the *inconsistency* metric (Eq. 3–4) as a way to establish a noise floor for any human-labeled GT we collect — directly relevant to Q2's "quantify GT angular error relative to differences between methods."
- **The VSE formula structure (Eq. 1–2) is a directly adoptable template for Q7's "metric family for orthogonality-to-surface as a whole-run score with a per-primitive analytical ceiling."** Swap "geodesic distance to nearest ground-truth vertex on unit sphere" for "angular deviation from the analytic surface normal through the region centroid," keep the min-over-symmetry-set and the [0,1] normalization and the average-over-instances structure. This is a ready-made scoring skeleton.
- **The "no single method dominates, they are complementary" finding (Table 2) is relevant to Q13 (negative-result framing) and Q5 (baseline strength).** It's direct historical precedent that purely geometric/heuristic view-selection criteria plateau around VSE≈0.40–0.52 on a human-informativeness task and none is a clear winner — a caution against expecting our geometry-conditioned method (or the VLM) to dominate cleanly across all primitive/compound shapes; per-object-class breakdowns (as Table 2's n column does) should be part of our reporting, not just a pooled average.
- **The base/bottom-avoidance and face-vs-context findings (§5.1) are directly relevant to Q16 (named VLM failure modes) as a *human baseline* for what "good" view selection looks like** — humans systematically avoid certain views and prefer others for structural reasons (support surface, canonical face) that a purely local-geometry-maximizing algorithm misses. This is a template for pre-registering what a *rule-based/geometric* baseline in our benchmark will systematically get wrong, useful for the "hand-programmed per-object recipes are a regression" framing in our own doc — this paper is evidence that hand-picked geometric heuristics (mesh saliency etc.) reliably fail on specific, nameable structural cues (protrusions, thin appendages, holes) even after being tuned by domain experts.
- **Released assets model for Q20**: the paper explicitly released the 68 3D models, the 26-subject viewpoint ground truth, and (implicitly) the scoring code/website (§4.2, ref [21]: NIST SHREC "bestview" benchmark page — note: check if still live, it's a legacy 2010 NIST URL). This is a template for "what to release": models + raw human labels + evaluation script, not just headline numbers.

## What does NOT transfer, and why

- **No sequential/multi-view structure at all.** The benchmark scores exactly one view per object; it has no concept of a view *budget*, a *stopping decision*, view *sets*, coverage, or fusion across views. Every one of our sequential-planning questions (Q6 view-budget regimes, Q10 fused-vs-single-view offset, Q15 view-correct×answer-correct grid, Q14 oracle-selector-vs-action-space separation) has **zero direct precedent in this paper** — it answers "which single viewpoint is best for object recognition/thumbnailing," not "which sequence of views best supports an inspection task." Any reuse of VSE-style scoring for us requires re-deriving the multi-view generalization from scratch.
- **Ground truth mesh is clean and un-defective; there is no injected sensor noise, no fused-cloud-vs-single-view offset, no scanning artifact anywhere in the pipeline.** Both human subjects and algorithms view the *same reference mesh* rendered directly — this sidesteps Q2's "does the analytical answer survive contact with a survey cloud?" entirely. The paper cannot answer Q2, Q4, Q9, or Q10; it has no scanning-defect model and no notion of "fit a primitive to a defective point cloud." Any GT-angular-error quantification for our setting has to come from elsewhere (Branch 2/6 sources on scan noise / primitive fitting), not from this paper.
- **The GT criterion is "most informative for recognition/thumbnailing," not "orthogonal to an inspected surface for defect visibility."** These are different constructs. Orthogonality-to-surface (our GT target per Q2/Q7) is not what the 26 subjects were optimizing for — subjects were told to avoid aesthetic bias and pick an *informative* view, not a *surface-normal-aligned* view. A bunny's "best view" (three-quarter, face-on) is not necessarily surface-orthogonal to any particular patch. This paper's human-label GT strategy is a *different GT paradigm* from the analytical (face-normal-through-centroid) GT paradigm central to our Q2/Q7 — it should be read as a case study in *how* to construct and validate human-label GT (methodology transfers) but its *specific labels/criterion* do not transfer as ground truth for our task.
- **No VLM, no learned method, no scoring by another AI system anywhere.** Completely irrelevant to Q3 (circularity with an RQ1 judge) and Q17 (LLM-judge controls) as a source of *content* — it predates VLM-as-scorer concerns entirely. It's only useful here as a distant methodological analogy (independent human judges as the anti-circularity move — see below).
- **Object set is arbitrary common objects (bunny, teapot, animals), not primitives or compounds.** Not usable as-is for Q11's stratified primitive/compound/near-primitive object set — no boxes, cylinders, or simple compounds are in the 68-model set; the geometric-complexity-maximizing algorithms they test are also not primitive-fitting methods, so there's no baseline-algorithm code to reuse for our primitive-level deterministic rules.
- **Statistics are minimal — no confidence intervals, no significance testing, no per-trial variance reported (Q12).** Table 2 reports only mean VSE and win-count n over 68 models with no error bars, no std, no bootstrap CI. With S=26 subjects this is a moderately large N by our standards, but the paper does not model or report subject-count-driven uncertainty at all — not a template for Q12's statistical-power derivation.

## Answering the specific "circularity" angle (Q3)

Worth flagging explicitly since Q3 is central to our branch: this paper's anti-circularity move is **independent human labeling as GT, with algorithms scored against it by a fixed closed-form geometric distance metric (Eq. 1) that has no learned/judged component**. This is the "different construct" flavor of separation (GT from human perceptual judgment collected once and frozen; algorithm evaluated by geometry, not by another algorithm or by re-asking humans per-algorithm). It is *not* directly a template for our Q3 (VLM-judge circularity), since there is no judge model here at all — but it is useful negative evidence for "reimplementation is not separation": all 7 candidate algorithms share the same underlying assumption (geometric-complexity maximization over a sampled sphere) and the paper's own conclusion ("none dominates, they're complementary," §5.2) can be read as those methods being correlated/non-independent by construction — a cautionary data point that superficially different metrics sharing one paradigm still cluster in performance, i.e., diversity of implementation is not diversity of construct.
