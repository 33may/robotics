## Full abstract (verbatim, the only primary text available)

> "The aspect graph, a graphical representation of an object's characteristic views has been widely developed by several researchers. However, researchers have stated that aspect graphs are limited due to their high complexity and computational cost. A simple non-convex object, such as a step, has 71 distinct characteristic views (nodes in the aspect graph); more complicated objects could have thousands of characteristic views (nodes). Many characteristic views of an aspect graph are not necessary for many applications. In this paper, a new entity-based aspect graph, EAG, is proposed based on the observation that, for most applications, the visibility of only some of the entities on the object is important. The objects of interest are polyhedral solids. We present algorithms for constructing new entity-based aspect graphs based on the faces, edges and vertices of the object, and for combining and contracting previously constructed EAGs in a database to generate EAGs for new objects. The computation time for construction is reduced, yet sufficient information is provided to the application."

## Core method/idea (abstract-level only)

- **The pruning move, stated as the paper's own thesis**: classical aspect graphs enumerate visibility changes for the *whole object*; EAG restricts computation to **entities of interest** ("some of the entities on the object") selected as relevant to the downstream application, not the full face/edge/vertex set. This is the exact "pruning escape from the explosion" this document was selected for — confirmed directly in the paper's own framing, not an inference from context.
- **Object class**: polyhedral solids only, stated explicitly ("The objects of interest are polyhedral solids"). No curved-surface or fitted-primitive treatment is mentioned anywhere in the abstract.
- **Two algorithms are named**, both only at the level of a one-clause description:
  1. Construct a new EAG "from" the faces, edges and vertices of an object (i.e., the entity set is drawn from the polyhedron's own topological elements — a face-graph-adjacent structure, consistent with entity-based addressing rather than a global tessellation).
  2. **Combine and contract previously constructed EAGs stored in a database to generate EAGs for new objects.** This is the single most load-bearing sentence in the abstract for our branch: it states, as a design feature, that the method supports building new objects' aspect structures out of a **library of precomputed per-entity/per-part EAGs**, combined and contracted rather than recomputed whole-cloth. This is a direct, if abstract-only, precedent for a compositional box+cylinder assembly built from independently precomputed per-primitive view structures — but I cannot confirm from the abstract whether "combine/contract" operates at the granularity of whole objects, individual faces, or something else; the mechanism itself is inaccessible.
- **Claimed benefit**: "computation time for construction is reduced, yet sufficient information is provided to the application" — a qualitative claim only; no percentage, no absolute time, no comparison table is present in the abstract.
- **Stated downstream use**: none given explicitly in the abstract (no "sensor placement," "inspection," or "recognition" keyword appears in the abstract text itself). Given the lab's surrounding publication record on the same page (Yang, Marefat & Kashyap, "Active Visual Inspection Based on CAD Models," ICRA 1994; several Yang/Marefat/Ciarallo journal papers on active-vision dimensional inspection and sensor placement, 1998–2000), this EAG paper almost certainly sits inside the same active-vision-inspection research programme — but this is **inferred from bibliographic context, not confirmed by reading the paper's own application section**, and should be labeled as such wherever cited.

## Load-bearing assumptions (as far as can be told from the abstract)

- **Someone/something must designate the "entities of interest" before EAG construction can begin.** The abstract's framing ("the visibility of only some of the entities... is important... for most applications") presupposes an externally supplied relevance criterion — EAG is a computational strategy for a *given* entity set, not a saliency-detection or automatic-entity-selection method. This matters directly for RQ11: the pruning mechanism is "entity-based restriction to entities of interest," and this paper's version of that restriction takes the entity set as an input, not an output.
- **The database/combination mechanism presupposes that EAGs for constituent parts already exist and are reusable across objects** — i.e., a compositional library model, precomputed once and amortized. This is asserted as a capability, not demonstrated with a build-time number.
- **Polyhedral solids only** is stated as the object class, not argued for or defended in the abstract — no discussion of whether/how curved surfaces (our cylinder) could be handled, e.g. via faceted approximation.

## What transfers to our setting

- **The central pruning principle (RQ11) is directly confirmed, not inferred**: restrict aspect-graph computation to entities of interest rather than the whole object's visibility structure. This maps cleanly onto "the agent inspects one surface region at a time" — the selection task doesn't need the whole-object aspect graph, only the aspect structure of the currently-relevant face/region. This is the one finding from this paper I can cite with real confidence, because it is the paper's stated thesis, not a detail buried in a section I couldn't reach.
- **The database combine/contract mechanism is a named precedent for compositional, per-primitive structure reuse** (RQ9, RQ12, RQ17): build box-EAG and cylinder-EAG once, each independently, then combine/contract them for a box+cylinder assembly rather than recomputing a whole-object structure from scratch. This is exactly the shape of our primitive-decomposition approach (box face-by-face, cylinder orbit), and it is reassuring that this compositional move has 1998-era precedent in the aspect-graph literature itself — but the abstract gives no detail on *how* combination/contraction actually preserves per-entity addressing across the seam where two primitives meet, which is precisely what RQ13/RQ7 (seams) would need.
- **Restriction to polyhedral solids is a partial, not full, fit for our box+cylinder object class** — it covers the box natively (faces/edges/vertices are exactly a box's structure) but requires treating the cylinder's curved lateral surface as an approximation or an extension the paper itself does not claim to make. This is a genuine scope mismatch to flag, not something to paper over.
