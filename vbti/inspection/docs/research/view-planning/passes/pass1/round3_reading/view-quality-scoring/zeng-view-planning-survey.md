# Zeng, Wen, Zhao, Liu — "View Planning in Robot Active Vision: A Survey of Systems, Algorithms, and Applications"

*Computational Visual Media* 6(3):225–245, 2020. Open access (CC-BY 4.0). Full text obtained (PDF via `sciopen.com/local/article_pdf/10.1007/s41095-020-0179-3.pdf`, 21 pages including references), read in full with `pdftotext -layout`. Authors: BNRist, Dept. of Computer Science and Technology, Tsinghua University.

This is a **survey of systems and applications, not a survey of scoring-function mathematics**. Its organizing axis is the *task* (object reconstruction / scene reconstruction / object recognition / pose estimation), not the *criterion*. Only two numbered equations in the whole paper are actual per-view quality formulas usable as-is; the rest of the "utility function" content is a one-line qualitative tag per cited paper (Table 4) plus prose description. This matters for the branch: this document is a **map of where to look**, not a source of citable closed-form scoring math beyond two equations.

## Core content: the taxonomy (Table 4, pp. 240–241)

Table 4 ("Work using search-based approaches") is the paper's actual taxonomy artifact — for 25 cited systems it records: task, data type (voxel / triangle mesh / point cloud / lines), sampling method (sphere sample / cylinder sample / voxels-on-ground / random sample / circularly sample / boundary search / neighbour sample / no sample), and **utility function**, drawn from a closed vocabulary of five labels: *Information gain*, *quality*, *moving cost*, *ambiguity*, *entropy*. Every one of the 25 rows uses one or a pairing of two of these five labels (e.g. "Information gain, quality" for [38],[40],[18],[75]; "Information gain, moving cost" for [14],[29],[34],[66]; "Entropy, moving cost" for [16],[89]). No row combines three or more terms, and no row uses a bespoke sixth label — i.e., across the entire surveyed literature (as of 2020) the paper finds **exactly one additive quality term ever gets paired with information gain**, never two or more (p. 240–241, Table 4). This is direct evidence for RQ6 (redundancy): the field itself has not found value in combining more than two criteria in one utility function.

The two load-bearing formulas:

**1. Massios & Fisher 1998 [38], the only explicit incidence/cosine term in the paper (Eq. 1, p. 230):**
```
f_total(v) = ω_v · f_visibility(v) + ω_q · f_quality(v)
```
`f_visibility` = amount of information gain, computed by counting unknown boundary voxels visible from `v`. `f_quality` = reconstruction quality **defined as `n̂ · v̂`, the dot product of the local surface normal and the view direction** — i.e. exactly `cos(θ)` between surface normal and viewing ray. This is the paper's only appearance of an incidence-angle-based scoring term, and it is stated with zero elaboration: no functional form beyond the raw dot product, no clamping/normalization discussion beyond that a dot product is already bounded in `[-1, 1]`, no sensitivity analysis, no mention of what "quality" empirically correlates with (defect detectability, depth noise, etc. — none of that is discussed). The two weights `ω_v, ω_q` are stated as free parameters with no value given or tuning procedure described.

**2. Delmerico et al. 2018 [25], NBV information-gain formalism (Eqs. 2–3, p. 231):**
```
G_v = Σ_{r∈Rv} Σ_{x∈X} I                              (2)
I = −P_o(x)·ln P_o(x) − P̄_o(x)·ln P̄_o(x)              (3)
```
`R_v` = simulated projection rays from viewpoint `v` (pinhole/perspective camera model over a voxel grid), `X` = the set of voxels each ray passes through before being blocked by known-occupied voxels or spatial boundaries, `P_o(x)` = probability voxel `x` is occupied, `P̄_o(x) = 1 − P_o(x)`. This is Shannon binary entropy of occupancy, summed over every unknown voxel along every ray — a double sum, i.e. **information gain here is an unbounded, extensive (grows with voxel count and object size) quantity**, in contrast to the bounded `n̂·v̂` quality term above. The paper states Delmerico et al. derive **five** variant VI formulations from Eq. 3 by reweighting voxels by spatial position/Markov-chain structure — *occlusion aware VI*, *unobserved voxel VI*, *rear side voxel VI*, *rear side entropy VI*, *proximity count VI* (Fig. 9 caption, p. 231) — and that these five were benchmarked **together with two more from other papers**, *area factor VI* [47, Vasquez-Gomez et al. 2014] and *average entropy VI* [11, Kriegel et al. 2015], via simulation experiments, with the reported outcome: **"proximity count VI and area factor VI are the best choices"** (p. 231). This is a genuine head-to-head, same-protocol comparison of seven named information-gain formulations reduced to two winners — directly relevant to RQ6/Branch-2-handoff "ablation shows a quality term earns its place" — but the survey reports only the verdict, not the comparison table, ranking metric, or numbers; that data lives in Delmerico et al. 2018 (*Autonomous Robots* 42(2):197–208) itself, not here.

**Other quantitative terms found (weaker, listed for completeness):**
- **Eq. 4 (Kaba et al. 2017, RL-based view planning, p. 232):** `f_λ(X) = A(X)/L(X)`, area of the reconstructed submesh `X` over its boundary length; `λ` is a learned regularization weight (`λ=0` reduces to a pure-area greedy criterion). This is a compactness/shape-quality metric on the *accumulated reconstruction*, not on a single candidate view's surface framing — it scores what a chosen sequence of views has produced, not a standalone view.
- **Eq. 5 (Wu et al., ShapeNet-based NBV, p. 237–238):** `H = −Σ_k p(y=k|x_o) log p(y=k|x_o)`, Shannon entropy of the current classification posterior over object categories `k`.
- **Eq. 6 (same context, p. 237):** `H_i = H(p(y|x_in, x_o)) = Σ_{x_in} p(x_in|x_o) H(y|x_in, x_o)`, the *expected* posterior entropy after rendering the hypothesized new observation `x_in` from candidate view `V_i`; the NBV is the view maximizing `H − H_i` (entropy reduction). Both Eq. 5 and Eq. 6 are classification-uncertainty terms for object-category recognition, unrelated to surface/incidence quality.
- **Sock et al. 2017 [37] (p. 238, prose only, no equation given):** for 6D pose estimation of objects in clutter, the paper reports that pure voxel-coverage entropy is *"not suitable... since it only considers coverage,"* so Sock et al. propose **"a viewpoint entropy that also considers saliency which can potentially reduce pose estimation uncertainty."** This is the survey's one explicit intersection with the branch's "graphics: viewpoint entropy, saliency" scope-item — but it is reported as a two-sentence paraphrase with zero formula. The graphics-literature *viewpoint entropy* (Vázquez et al., defined over projected-face solid angles) is never mentioned or cited anywhere in this paper; Sock et al.'s "viewpoint entropy" is a same-name, different-definition construct (posterior-distribution entropy over pose hypotheses, not solid-angle-weighted face visibility). **Term-collision warning for RQ6/RQ5**: "entropy" in this survey always means Shannon entropy of a *probability distribution over occupancy or object hypotheses*, never the graphics-style geometric viewpoint entropy.
- **Movement/registration cost** appears in six Table-4 rows (paired with info gain or entropy) but is never given a formula anywhere in the body text — only named.

## Load-bearing assumptions

- The organizing decomposition (object reconstruction / scene reconstruction / object recognition / pose estimation) assumes these are the only four active-vision goals worth taxonomizing (§4, explicit, p. 229). Inspection/defect-detection is not one of the four — it is never named as a task category anywhere in the paper.
- The formal view-planning definition (§3.3, p. 228): view space `V = R³ × SO(3)`, task is to find a sequence `{v_i | i=1,...,n} ⊂ V`; the paper explicitly states that finding the *globally* optimal `V* ⊂ V` is impractical, citing Tarbox & Gottschlich 1995 [32] for the result that minimal-view-set coverage under prior geometric knowledge is **NP-complete** — this is stated as a blanket claim about *view-set selection*, not single-view scoring, and is explicitly out of this branch's scope (coverage/set-selection is branch 4's territory) but is worth flagging as the reason the field settled on greedy/heuristic per-view utility functions rather than global optimization.
- Search-based (generate-and-test) is assumed to be the dominant paradigm and is treated as the default worth tabulating; synthesis-based (direct analytical computation of NBV, no candidate sampling) is described in three sentences (p. 228–229) as faster but *"its performance in terms of accuracy and reliability is worse... in the presence of complex occlusion and spatial uncertainty"* — an unsupported claim attributed to [41] with no number given.
- Every utility function in the paper is defined over a **structured intermediate representation already in hand** — a voxel occupancy grid (most common), a triangle mesh, or a point cloud with normals — never over the raw image alone. The paper assumes this 3D structure exists and is trustworthy; there is no treatment anywhere of what happens to any of these formulas when that structure is itself noisy or wrong (see Negative Findings).

## What transfers to our setting

- **The `n̂·v̂` cosine-quality term (Eq. 1, Massios & Fisher) is a directly reusable, minimal closed form for "how orthogonal is this view to this surface"** — it is exactly `cos θ` between the estimated surface normal at a region and the camera's viewing-ray direction, requires only a normal estimate (which we will have from primitive fitting) and the candidate camera pose, is trivially differentiable (`d(cos θ)/dθ = −sin θ`), and is naturally bounded in `[−1, 1]` (or `[0,1]` if both vectors are oriented to face each other) — directly answering RQ8 (bounded, comparable score precedent) for the orthogonality component specifically, and giving a ready analytic form for RQ7's normal-error sensitivity analysis (small-angle: score error ≈ `sin θ · Δθ_normal`, worst at grazing incidence, near-zero at exact normal incidence — meaning normal-estimation error matters *least* exactly where we want views to be, and *most* right where the view would otherwise be borderline/off-axis).
- **The additive-weighted-sum pattern (`f_total = ω_v·f_visibility + ω_q·f_quality`) is validated field precedent for combining an information/coverage term with a geometric-quality term** — this is the shape our own composite score (orthogonality + framing + visibility, etc.) would take if we follow the dominant pattern in this literature; Table 4 shows this is the *only* combination pattern ever used (at most two terms, additive, weighted) across 25 surveyed systems — a real constraint on how much term-stacking is precedented (RQ6).
- **The search-based generate-and-test paradigm (sample discrete candidates → score each → pick best) is the dominant paradigm in the entire surveyed literature** (all 25 Table-4 rows use an explicit sampling method: sphere/cylinder/voxel/random/circular sampling) — this is direct structural precedent for our own discrete-action-space design being the *normal* way this field operates, not a simplification we're imposing; nobody in this survey does continuous gradient-based online view refinement as the primary mechanism (see Negative Findings — this also bounds RQ-sampling-vs-gradient).
- **Delmerico et al.'s seven-formulation, one-protocol comparison (p. 231) is a citable precedent for exactly the kind of ablation the branch wants** (RQ "what ablation shows a quality term earns its place") — even though the survey only reports the verdict, it identifies the primary source (Delmerico, Isler, Sabzevari, Scaramuzza, *Autonomous Robots* 42(2):197–208, 2018) as the paper to pull for Branch 2's protocol-imitation task.
- **Wu et al. 2014's per-point confidence → viewing-vector-field (VVF) → best-view pipeline** (p. 230) is a named precedent for the point→region→view aggregation RQ9 asks about: per-point confidence scores (from completeness + smoothness of a Poisson-reconstructed surface) are aggregated into a field over the whole surface, which is then used to select best views — an aggregate-then-select pattern, not worst-point pooling, though the survey gives no formula for the aggregation step itself (would need Wu et al. 2014, *ACM TOG* 33(6) directly).

## What does NOT transfer, and why (negative findings — first-class per task brief)

- **No incidence-angle-vs-depth-error curve, no sensor named, no D405, no active-stereo discussion anywhere.** Table 1's sensor comparison (p. 227) is purely qualitative (High/Low labels) and has no incidence-angle column at all. This document cannot answer the branch's top RQ (sigma(range, θ) for D405 at 10–40 cm) — that must come from photogrammetry/metrology sources or manufacturer/independent RealSense characterization papers, not this survey.
- **No threshold/knee-vs-smooth discussion of the angle penalty, no specular-reflection discussion, no discussion of glossy/dark-surface failure modes.** The word "specular" never appears; "glossy" never appears; the only quality-vs-angle content in the entire paper is the single `n̂·v̂` linear cosine term (Eq. 1), which is *by construction* smooth everywhere and has no knee, threshold, or non-monotonicity built in — the paper offers no evidence either for or against a real sensor-failure knee near grazing angles, it simply never engages with the question.
- **Zero industrial-inspection content.** The paper's four task categories (reconstruction ×2, recognition, pose estimation) do not include inspection or defect detection; "defect," "flaw," "scratch," "crack," "inspection" never appear anywhere in the text. This is a genuinely empty result for the branch's defect-detectability-vs-angle RQ — the survey is reconstruction/recognition-centric, and the reconstruction-quality term it does have (`n̂·v̂`) is justified purely by *surface-fitting accuracy*, never by *flaw visibility*. The industrial-inspection answer (raking light, grazing-angle scratch visibility) must come from elsewhere entirely.
- **The Tarabanis-era generalized-viewpoint sensor-constraint tradition, despite being named in this branch's scope description as something this survey should map, is not actually discussed in the body text.** Tarabanis appears only twice, both in the reference list ([5] Tarabanis, Tsai, Allen 1991; [6] Tarabanis, Allen, Tsai 1995, *IEEE TRA* 11(1):86–104 — the classic sensor-planning survey). The only body-text reference to constraint-based synthesis view planning is one sentence (p. 228–229): *"the sensor viewpoints are directly determined from specific constraints modeled by analytical functions [6]"* — attributed generically to the Tarabanis survey with zero reproduction of the actual resolution/focus/field-of-view/visibility constraint formulas. **Field of view, depth of field, and GSD/resolution as explicit per-view scoring criteria never appear anywhere in this paper** (confirmed by direct text search — "field of view," "FOV," "depth of field," and "GSD" have zero occurrences; "resolution" appears exactly once, as a column header in the generic sensor-type comparison table, not as a per-view formula). **This means roughly half of RQ5's minimum-coverage list (FOV framing, depth of field, GSD/standoff) has zero source material in this document** — those terms must be pulled directly from Tarabanis 1995 / Scott, Roth & Rivest 2003 (*ACM Computing Surveys* 35(1):64–96, cited here as [2] but likewise never elaborated in-body), not from this survey.
- **No local viewpoint optimization content whatsoever.** No differentiable rendering, no gradient-based pose refinement, no local search, no continuous optimization of a candidate pose within a neighborhood is mentioned anywhere. Every "synthesis" (non-search) approach described (p. 228–229, Connolly/Wong) computes the NBV via a **closed-form/analytical calculation done once**, not iterative gradient refinement of a candidate — this is a real absence, not a reframing: the entire sampling-vs-gradient RQ has no data point in this document beyond the negative one (100% of surveyed systems use discrete sampling; 0% use gradient-based local refinement as their primary mechanism).
- **No visibility/self-occlusion computation without a surface, no hidden-point-removal discussion.** All occlusion handling in the survey is via ray-casting through a voxel occupancy grid (Eq. 2's `R_v`/`X` construction) — this requires a structured 3D representation with an occupied/free/unknown label per voxel, not a bare point cloud. The paper never discusses computing visibility from a raw, unstructured point cloud (no mesh, no voxelization) — the specific "hidden point removal on a raw cloud" question (RQ11) has no answer here; the survey's whole occlusion apparatus assumes voxelization has already happened.
- **No propagation of input-geometry uncertainty into any view-quality score.** Every probabilistic term in the paper (`P_o(x)` in Eq. 3, the classification posteriors in Eqs. 5–6) is uncertainty *about what the scene contains* (occupancy, object identity), never uncertainty *in the input geometry itself* (point positions, registration/pose error, per-view depth noise propagated from calibration or triangulation). Confirmed by full-text read: "uncertainty" appears in the paper (§1, p. 226, listed among four "key factors that hinder accurate perception for view planning": *"imprecision of motion, and unreliability of visual perception"*) but only as a **stated open challenge in the introduction**, never operationalized into any of the six equations or 25 tabulated systems. This is real, if weak, secondary support for the branch's suspected contribution gap (RQ: "does any method score against a cloud that is itself wrong") — the paper names the class of problem in its intro and then never returns to it.
- **No calibration-vs-modelling-requirement discussion of a fused-cloud-vs-live-depth coordinate offset.** Never discussed; not in scope of any surveyed system as described.
- **No formal "best view within a neighbourhood" definitions.** No constraint-satisfaction-with-margin framework (à la MVP, Scott 2009 — cited as [4] but never elaborated in-body), no mutual-information viewpoint-stability framework, no gradient-magnitude-of-utility-field definition of local optimality. The paper's only formal object is the *global* view-sequence definition in §3.3 (`V = R³ × SO(3)`, find `{v_i}`), which the paper itself immediately declares practically unsolvable and moves past — no local/neighbourhood notion of optimality is ever defined.
- **No smoothing-trick catalogue for discontinuous visibility.** No fuzzy filters, occupancy-field softening, differential ray sampling, or alpha-compositing discussion anywhere; visibility is always the hard binary ray-blocked/unblocked test.
- **No image-space-only view judging for surface/defect quality.** The closest thing is Johns et al. 2016 [76] (p. 236–237): a CNN takes pairs of RGB images and outputs an implicit confidence/entropy measure to pick the next-best-view *for object-category recognition* — this is genuinely image-space (raw RGB in, no 3D geometry used for that step), but it is scoring *recognition-hypothesis disambiguation value*, not surface quality, framing, exposure, or defect contrast — not transferable to the branch's image-space-defect-detectability question without a large reinterpretation.
- **No benchmark of any kind for view-quality scoring itself.** The paper is organized by application, not by controlled comparison; the one exception (Delmerico et al.'s 7-VI-formulation comparison, reported secondhand, p. 231) is the single ablation-style result in the entire document, and even that is reported without the underlying numbers.
- **No object-class/primitive-shape breakdown of test sets.** Datasets are only named in passing (ShapeNet, ModelNet, NYU, SUNCG, ScanNet, plus ad hoc physical objects) with no consolidated table of which algorithm was tested on which object class — Branch 2's "primitive vs Stanford/free-form" tagging cannot be done from this document; would require going to each primary source.

## Assessment for the branch

This survey earns its place as the "spine" for locating *where* view-quality scoring work sits by task and by data representation, and it hands the branch exactly two directly usable equations: the `n̂·v̂` cosine-quality term (Massios & Fisher, Eq. 1) — a ready, bounded, differentiable orthogonality score with a clean normal-error-sensitivity story — and the Shannon-entropy information-gain formalism (Delmerico et al., Eqs. 2–3) with a named, if secondhand, seven-way ablation verdict. Table 4's five-label taxonomy (information gain / quality / moving cost / ambiguity / entropy) and its "never more than two terms combined" pattern are useful field-wide constraints on how much term-stacking is precedented. But the survey is thin exactly where several of the branch's highest-priority RQs need depth: it does not discuss incidence-angle-vs-depth-error curves, sensor-specific behavior (no D405, no active stereo), specular/glossy failure modes, industrial defect detectability, FOV/depth-of-field/GSD as explicit criteria (despite being named in the branch's scope as within the Tarabanis tradition — that tradition is cited, not reproduced), local/gradient-based viewpoint optimization, hidden-point-removal visibility on raw clouds, or propagation of input-geometry uncertainty into a score. Every one of those is a first-class negative finding, not a search failure — confirmed present-or-absent by full-text reading and direct term search. The practical read: use this document for the taxonomy skeleton and the two equations, then go straight to its own primary sources (Tarabanis 1995, Scott/Roth/Rivest 2003, Delmerico et al. 2018, Massios & Fisher 1998, Wu et al. 2014) for the load-bearing math the branch actually needs.
