## Core method / idea

A **taxonomy/classification survey**, not an empirical paper — the modern (2021), industrial-quality-control-side successor to Scott/Roth/Rivest 2003 that the paper itself names as its predecessor and explicitly positions itself as covering "the methods developed *a posteriori*" (§1, p.2). Structure:

1. **Problem statement and three-way classification** (§2.1, pp.2–3): the VPP is formalized as covering a part's outer skin with the minimum number of scans; the SCP reduction (patches = elements, viewpoints = candidate subsets) is stated as Scott et al.'s 2001 contribution, and Karp 1972's NP-completeness result is cited directly (not just via Scott 2003). From this, the paper draws a **three-way top-level split** that is the paper's central contribution for this branch:
   - **SCP transposition** (§3.1): methods that actually solve the set-covering problem — minimize the number of views subject to full coverage.
   - **Covering optimization** (§3.2): methods that do **not** seek to minimize scan count "at all costs" — they optimize coverage first, and reduce/order scans only afterward; the SCP is explicitly *not* invoked.
   - **Search-based** (§4): no input model; iterative NBV-style exploration (out of this branch's scope per the branch's own charter, but read for the stop-criteria catalogue below).
2. **A criteria/scoring grid** (§2.2–2.4, pp.2–6, Table 1 for symbols, Tables 2–3 for the final grid): 16 lettered algorithmic criteria (a–p) grouped as General / Algorithm performance / Object constraints / Sensor constraints / Validation, plus 5 lettered discretization types (q–u: mesh, voxel grid, NURBS, point cloud, B-Rep) and a separate technological-criteria table (sensor type × robot/support type × application type). Each of ~35 surveyed methods is scored ✓/∼/empty/?/NA against every criterion in Table 3 (algorithmic) and Table 2 (technological).
3. **Per-category walkthroughs** (§3.1, §3.2, §4) each formalized around a fixed number of pipeline steps (5 steps for SCP transposition — segment, sample viewpoints, evaluate visibility, solve the minimization, done via Fig. 9; 4 steps for coverage optimization — segment, generate-and-optimize one viewpoint per patch directly, evaluate visibility, compute required scan count via Fig. 13; 4 steps for search-based — represent observed/unknown space, sample viewpoints, evaluate against a cost function, check a stop criterion via Fig. 23), walking through ~35 named methods (Scott, Tarbox, Martins, Loriot, Krause, Jing, Hepp, Mahmud, Lee, Son, Souzani, Shi, Germani, Raffaeli, Lartigue, Bircher, Wu, Koutecky, Ding, Phan, Sadaoui, Pito, Vasquez-Gomez, Kriegel, and others).
4. **Conclusion and future work** (§5, pp.22–24): summarizes Table 2/3 findings, explicitly notes zero surveyed methods use machine learning, flags fair-evaluation-metric absence, registration-overlap-quality, measurement-uncertainty, and TSP-postprocessing-vs-integration as open problems.

## Concrete results and numbers

This survey, like Scott 2003, contains almost no new experiments; its numeric content is either (a) definitional/taxonomic or (b) numbers quoted from the surveyed primary sources. The load-bearing numbers for this branch:

- **Orthogonality/incidence-angle threshold, with a citation trail (branch Q7)**: "In 1999, Prieto [37] demonstrated that the angle between the surface normal and sensor orientation should not exceed 35°, because beyond this angle, the resulting point cloud is extremely noisy and cannot obtain accurate measurements" (§3.1.4, p.8, discussing objective-function criteria for SCP-transposition methods). Reference [37] = F. Prieto, *Métrologie assistée par ordinateur. Apport des capteurs 3D sans contact*, PhD thesis, École de technologie supérieure, 2000 (p.26 of the reference list). This is a **concrete, sourced numeric answer** to "what is the maximum admissible deviation from orthogonality" — a hard 35° cutoff, explicitly tied to measurement noise, not an arbitrary modeling choice.
- **A second, independent incidence-angle threshold (branch Q7, different sensor/method)**: Raffaeli et al.'s combined-visibility-map method (§3.2.1, p.13; Fig. 17 caption references "the maximum glancing angle"): "Only the rays having an angle of less than 50° with the surface normal were projected onto the sphere to preserve the high point cloud quality at the time of scanning (n ✓)." A second, independently-set cutoff (50° vs. Prieto's 35°) for a *different* purpose (visibility-map construction rather than raw noise) — evidence that the field's threshold is method/sensor-dependent, not universal, which matters if we intend to defend a single number for the D405.
- **Adaptive-voxelization normal-deviation threshold (branch Q5, precedent for per-primitive patch construction)**: Sadaoui et al., Lartigue et al., and Koutecky et al.'s adaptive voxelization (§3.2.1, p.11): "a grid of voxels is created, with each voxel having the size of the sensor field. It is ensured that the angle between all the surface normals contained in each voxel is not greater than a certain threshold. Subsequently, the voxel is divided in two, and so on, iteratively." No single numeric value is given in *this* survey (the underlying papers presumably specify it — worth a follow-up read of Sadaoui 2018 / Koutecky 2016 directly), but the **mechanism** — recursively subdivide until per-cell normal spread is bounded — is exactly the generalization of "one view per box face" the branch needs, with an explicit citation chain independent of Mosbach 2021 (already in the round-2 map).
- **One concrete view-count number**: Fig. 12 caption (p.10): a real point cloud "scanned by applying 30 acquisitions optimized through the greedy planning realized using the method of Mohammadikaji et al. [31]" on a mechanical part. This is the only in-text reported view count in the whole document — the survey does **not** tabulate view counts across methods (contrary to what the round-2 map hoped for branch Q13); Tables 2/3 grid criteria, not counts.
- **Karp 1972 cited directly for VPP-as-SCP NP-completeness** (§2.1, p.2) — a second, independent citation trail for the NP-completeness claim beyond Scott 2003/Tarbox & Gottschlich 1995.
- **Zero surveyed methods use machine learning** (§5, p.24, explicit): *"It can be noted that although machine learning is widely used in the field of computer graphics, none of the studied methods use this type of paradigm, likely because these methods require a large amount of data."* Three then-recent (2017–2020) learned works are named only as forward pointers, not analyzed: Mendoza et al. 2020 (supervised NBV regression), Hepp et al. 2018 ("Learn-to-score"), Devrim Kaba et al. 2017 (RL view planning) — none of which are integrated into the taxonomy or Tables 2/3. This dates the field's uptake of learning fairly precisely: essentially **post-2021** by this survey's own account, complementing Alsadik 2025's later, learning-inclusive taxonomy already in the round-2 map.

## Load-bearing assumptions

1. **Every method needs *some* discretized geometric proxy of the surface as an input to the view-derivation step** — one of mesh (q), voxel grid (r), NURBS/parametric surface (s), point cloud (t), or B-Rep (u) (§2.3.6, pp.3–4, Fig. 1 shows a torus in all five representations). Point cloud (t) as a *segmentation substrate* appears (Lee et al., Son et al. sample the surface as a point cloud and cluster by normal/distance), but always as an intermediate structure derived from a clean model, never as a noisy self-acquired survey artifact with dropout or registration offset.
2. **Object model is either a complete, trusted prior model (a priori-knowledge methods, §3) or completely absent (search-based, §4)** — the same absent-middle-ground finding as Scott 2003. Our regime (a defective, self-acquired, fused survey PLY that is neither a clean CAD prior nor "nothing") sits in the gap between §3 and §4 in this taxonomy too; the survey offers no citation for a middle category.
3. **A viewpoint is fundamentally a (position, orientation) pair, sometimes degraded to a scan-path/direction for laser scanners** (criterion a, §2.3.1, p.3; discussed again at §3.1.2, p.7 and §3.2.2, p.12) — laser-scanner methods (Martins, Krause) define only a distance/direction, not a full 6D pose, because the sensor scans along a path rather than capturing a single frame. Our D405 is a frame sensor (like the structured-light methods in this survey), so the full-pose formulation applies; the reduced laser-path formulation does not transfer.
4. **Collision detection (criterion o) and occlusion treatment (criterion m) are two of the 15 named algorithmic criteria, i.e., first-class, near-universally addressed design axes** — in Table 3 (pp.23–24), the (o) column is ✓ for a strong majority of methods across all three macrocategories, confirming that "does the algorithm avoid collisions when generating the scan plan" is treated by this field as intrinsic to view planning, not a bolt-on post-filter. This is direct precedent for treating our IK/collision stack as part of the planning problem's definition, not an afterthought.
5. **Sensor technology is limited to laser scanners, structured-light sensors, and CMM/contact probes** (§2.4.1, pp.4–5, Fig. 2–3) plus a residual "others" category (cameras, wireless sensors). **No RGB-D / active-stereo depth camera (D405-class sensor) appears anywhere in the survey.** Structured-light sensors are the closest analog (fixed during acquisition, single-shot area capture, §2.4.1, p.4) but are described as calibrated, high-precision devices (e.g., GOM-brand, Fig. 3), not consumer stereo cameras with per-pixel confidence and dropout on glossy/dark surfaces.

## What transfers to our setting

- **The three-way macrocategory split (SCP-transposition / coverage-optimization / search-based, §2.1, pp.2–3) is a directly usable, checkable answer to branch Q1** ("is our problem minimum set cover or maximum k-coverage, or is coverage even the objective?"). The survey's own vocabulary distinguishes exactly this: SCP-transposition = classical minimum-set-cover (Chvátal's ln-n regime); coverage-optimization = "optimize coverage first, and later, the number of scans" — i.e., *not* driven by a cardinality-minimizing set-cover objective at all. Crucially, **coverage-optimization methods construct one viewpoint per patch directly** (§3.2, 4-step pipeline, Fig. 13) rather than sampling a candidate superset and culling it — structurally, this is "per-region view quality, with per-patch minimality baked into the segmentation rather than imposed by a covering solver." This is the closest existing formal category to what Albert's requirement (evidence images must be the most orthogonal view of *the inspected surface*, not "cover the object with as few views as possible") actually implies, and it gives the branch a citable name (rather than having to invent one) for the analytical track.
- **The criteria grid (Table 1 symbols + Tables 2–3, §2.2–2.4, pp.2–6, pp.23–24) is the transcribable skeleton the branch plan asked for.** The 16 lettered algorithmic criteria, verbatim (letter, one-line operational definition, section):
  - General: (a) independence to sensor type, §2.3.1 p.3; (b) invariance to object size, §2.3.1 p.3; (c) type of support/installation constraints, §2.3.1 p.3; (d) generic treatment of constraints (easy add/remove), §2.3.1 p.3.
  - Algorithm performance: (e) self-terminating, §2.3.2 p.3; (f) scan-number minimization, §2.3.2 p.3; (g) cover maximization, §2.3.2 p.3; (h) time-inspection minimization, §2.3.2 p.3.
  - Object constraints: (i) type of entity selection (CAD-region-driven scan targeting), §2.3.3 p.3; (j) a priori knowledge of the object, §2.3.3 p.3; (k) overlap between scans, §2.3.3 p.3; (l) analysis of DOF for registration, §2.3.3 p.3.
  - Sensor constraints: (m) occlusion treatment, §2.3.4 p.3; (n) sensor quality measure (inclination/distance-aware), §2.3.4 p.3; (o) collision detection, §2.3.4 p.3.
  - Validation: (p) real-industrial-part validation, §2.3.5 p.3.
  - Discretization types (q–u): mesh, voxel grid, parametric surface/NURBS, point cloud, B-Rep — §2.3.6, pp.3–4.
  This grid is directly reusable as the round-3 synthesis's comparison-table skeleton, exactly as the branch plan anticipated, and (f) vs. (g) being **separate, independently-scored criteria** is itself evidence for the Q1 answer above: the field already treats "minimize scan count" and "maximize coverage" as two different, sometimes-competing axes, not one objective.
- **Two independently-sourced, numeric orthogonality thresholds with citation trails (branch Q7)**: Prieto 2000's 35° noise-driven cutoff (§3.1.4, p.8) and the 50°-glancing-angle cutoff used in Raffaeli et al.'s visibility-map construction (§3.2.1, p.13). Two numbers from two independent methodological lineages, both well under 90°, both framed as *point-cloud-quality* cutoffs rather than arbitrary — useful anchors when the branch has to defend a specific D405 number, and evidence that ~35–50° (not some smaller/tighter number) is the field's working range for "how far off-normal before quality degrades unacceptably."
- **A second, independent precedent chain for per-primitive/per-patch normal-deviation-bounded segmentation (branch Q5)**: Sadaoui 2018, Lartigue 2014, Koutecky 2016's adaptive voxelization — "the angle between all the surface normals contained in each voxel is not greater than a certain threshold" (§3.2.1, p.11) — recursively subdivides until the normal-spread bound holds. This is mechanistically identical to Mosbach 2021's feature-functional subdivision (already flagged HIGH in the round-2 map) but from an independent lineage (French CAD-inspection-planning community rather than German MVA), strengthening the citation chain the branch needs to defend "primitive-level deterministic rules" as standing on precedent.
- **Reachability/collision entering generation directly, not just as a post-filter (branch Q8/Q9)**: Jing et al. 2018 [15] (§3.1.2, p.7): viewpoints are "sampled on an ellipsoidal hemisphere containing the object, and for each viewpoint, a robot configuration was defined in the robot kinematic space (c ✓). The viewpoints for which no configuration existed, or which involved a collision with the environment, were directly removed from the set." The survey's own critique of this: "the method is constrained to a specific workspace for a robot arm whose accessibility can be directly tested" (p.7) — flagged as a *disadvantage* in the survey's framing (loses generality across supports), but for us, where the robot and workspace are fixed by design (UR5e, RG2, D405, tabletop), that "disadvantage" is simply the correct scope, not a limitation to work around. This is a second, independent citation (beyond Yu & Gupta 2000 and Wang 2026 already in the round-2 map) for fallback-ladder rung (iii) — "never leave the feasible set, sample/optimize directly in robot configuration space" — and the first one that frames it as hard up-front filtering rather than post-hoc joint-space optimization, i.e., a citation for fallback rung (i) *implemented via* configuration-space sampling rather than Cartesian-pose IK-checking.
- **A second configuration-space-sampling precedent, this time in the search-based family**: Vasquez-Gomez et al. 2014/2017 [54, 57] (§4.2, p.19 and §4.4, p.20): "sampled viewpoints directly in the robot's configuration (kinematics) space (c ✓)... the viewpoints were sampled randomly with a uniform distribution at each new iteration. Subsequently, the viewpoints were evaluated to ensure that they do not collide with the environment (o ✓) and that the orientation radius of the sensor intersects the box surrounding the object." A second stop criterion tied to reachability: "the process was terminated if the robot did not identify any path between its current position and the positions of the candidate viewpoints" (§4.3, p.20) — i.e., **exhaustion of the reachable set is itself treated as a valid termination condition**, not an error state. Relevant precedent for what our architecture should do when the reachable shell excludes further useful views (branch Q12): stop and report, rather than force a degraded view.
- **A broader catalogue of stop/termination criteria for branch Q14**, beyond what the round-2 map already listed (SCVP, NeRF-PRV, Chvátal): percentage-of-new-surface "redundancy" threshold (Pito, Loriot, §4.3 p.20); a "surface factor" = rate of newly-observed unknown voxels, thresholded (Vasquez-Gomez, §4.3 p.20); reachability exhaustion (no path found to any candidate — Vasquez-Gomez, same page); a coverage index over holes plus average point density (Kriegel, §4.3 p.20); and, when nothing else fires, a hard maximum-iteration cap as the universal fallback (§4.3, p.20: "the process terminated when a predefined maximum number of scans was attained"). None of these are "principled minimality" in the ILP/optimal sense the branch wants, but they are the field's actual, deployed stopping rules and worth listing as the empirical baseline against which any minimality criterion we adopt should be compared.
- **Independent confirmation that generate-then-select (SCP transposition) and derive-directly (coverage optimization) are both mature, parallel traditions, not "one is primitive/obsolete"** (branch Q4). The survey's own §3.1 vs. §3.2 split is exactly the dichotomy the branch asks about: sample-a-superset-then-cull (Scott, Martins, Loriot, Mahmud, Jing, Hepp, Krause, Mohammadikaji) vs. compute-one-viewpoint-per-patch-directly-from-segmentation (Lee, Germani, Phan, Souzani, Bircher, Wu, and the visibility-map family). This gives an independent, citable confirmation (alongside Magaña 2023 and Wang 2026 already flagged) that direct derivation is an established, not a fringe, tradition — but also flags its cost, described explicitly at §3.2.5 (p.17): coverage-optimization methods are "not optimized to easily add or remove constraints... the implementation of the constraints was split between the segmentation and optimization of the viewpoint position," unlike SCP-transposition methods where "constraints are mainly included in the cost function" (d ✓ is more often satisfied by SCP-transposition methods in Table 3). **This is the concrete cost of skipping enumeration**: constraint modularity is harder to achieve when views are derived directly rather than filtered from an enumerated, uniformly-scored candidate set — a genuine trade-off argument for branch Q4, not just a citation.

## What does NOT transfer, and why (negative findings — first class)

- **No submodularity discussion anywhere (branch Q2).** "Greedy" is discussed purely as a practical heuristic for the SCP (§3.1.5, p.9: *"one of the most widely used algorithms is the greedy algorithm [32], which is an iterative algorithm that locally chooses the optimal solution at each iteration. A disadvantage of this method is its tendency to fall into local optimums. Nevertheless, this approach is easy to implement and, under a relevant cost function, the approach is sufficiently efficient..."*) — no mention of submodularity, the 1−1/e maximum-coverage bound, or the ln-n minimum-set-cover bound, and no discussion of whether adding a quality/orthogonality weighting to the coverage function preserves any theoretical guarantee. This branch question needs Chvátal 1979 / Nemhauser–Wolsey–Fisher 1978 directly (already flagged in the round-2 map) — this survey does not help.
- **No fixed-parameter-tractability or exact-solving discussion (branch Q3).** NP-completeness is stated as a blanket fact (Karp 1972, §2.1 p.2) with no discussion of instance-size-dependent tractability, no mention of ILP formulations being solvable exactly at small scale, and no acknowledgment that the SCP's practical hardness depends on instance structure. The survey's own framing implicitly assumes "NP-complete ⇒ heuristics are necessary," which is exactly the assumption the branch's Q3 wants to interrogate and reject at our scale. Mizutani 2024 (already in the round-2 map) remains the needed source, not this survey.
- **No numeric reachable-view-count or coverage-loss-under-reachability-constraint figures (branch Q10).** Collision detection (o) and support type (c) are tracked qualitatively as criteria, but no method's Table-3 entry is accompanied by a quantified reachability cost (e.g., "X% of ideal views were unreachable"). ObjView-Bench (already flagged HIGH in the round-2 map) remains the only source with that number.
- **No IK-multiplicity discussion (branch Q11).** "Robot kinematic space" sampling (Jing 2018, Vasquez-Gomez) treats a robot configuration as if it were in 1:1 correspondence with a Cartesian viewpoint; the possibility that one Cartesian pose admits multiple, differently-feasible joint solutions is never raised. This is a genuine silence, not a partial answer — the branch's Q11 needs Wang 2026 or a UR-specific IK source.
- **No defect-detectability content (only conformance-to-model measurement quality, like Scott 2003).** "Inspection" throughout means dimensional/geometric conformance to a model specification (criterion n: "sensor quality measure," concerned with inclination/distance-driven measurement error), never visual defect (scratch/dent/corrosion) detectability. Zero mentions of "defect," "flaw," "scratch," or "crack." Confirms, independently of Scott 2003, that the CAD-inspection-planning literature and the visual-defect-inspection literature are different bodies of work.
- **No treatment of a self-acquired, defective survey cloud as planning input**, as already found in Scott 2003 and the round-2 map's own gap list. Every method here needs a clean discretized proxy (mesh/voxel/NURBS/point-cloud/B-Rep) of a *trusted* model; point clouds appear only as intermediate segmentation substrates derived from that trusted model, never as the noisy end product of a prior scanning pass with pose error, dropout, or a fused-vs-live-depth offset. Confirms the branch's "almost nothing plans views from a defective self-acquired survey cloud" gap finding (round-2 map) from a second, independent survey.
- **No VLM/LLM content, and no discrete-action-space-for-a-controller framing whatsoever** — a 2021, pre-LLM-robotics-era survey; branch Q4's "how does enumeration serve RQ4 (presenting to a VLM)" question cannot be answered from this document at all; it is entirely absent from the paper's concerns.
- **No RGB-D / active-stereo sensor coverage.** §2.4.1's three sensor categories (laser scanner, structured-light, "other") do not include a D405-class consumer depth camera; the closest analog (structured-light, fixed during acquisition) is a calibrated high-precision industrial device, not a stereo camera with the D405's per-pixel confidence/dropout behavior on glossy or dark surfaces. Any "sensor quality measure" (n) formula transplanted from this literature will need re-derivation for active-stereo noise characteristics, not just re-parameterization.
- **Learned selection is explicitly absent from the taxonomy (2021 cutoff)** — confirmed above under Concrete results; the survey names three learned works only as unanalyzed forward pointers. For branch Q15 ("does learned selection beat analytical for primitive-shaped, known-geometry objects"), this survey supplies zero comparative evidence; BENBV and GenNBV (already flagged in the round-2 map) remain the needed sources.
- **No overlap between the "orthogonality" quality axis and Albert's requirement framing.** Even where incidence angle is a named criterion (n), it always appears as one weighted term among several in a multi-criterion objective function (coverage, overlap, collision avoidance, travel time) — never as a hard qualitative bar an evidence image must clear ("the most orthogonal view of the inspected surface"). This is a structural mismatch worth naming: the field's convention is "orthogonality as one more term to trade off," ours (per Albert) is closer to "orthogonality as close to a first-class deliverable requirement." No surveyed method treats it that way.

## Assessment for the branch

High value, comparable to Scott 2003, but complementary rather than redundant: where Scott 2003 gives the field's taxonomy vocabulary and its view-sphere critique, this paper gives (1) a **directly transcribable criteria grid** that becomes the round-3 synthesis's comparison-table skeleton, (2) the clearest **existing formal name** for "coverage-optimization" as a category distinct from set-cover-driven minimization — which is the closest published match to what Albert's per-region-orthogonality requirement actually implies, resolving branch Q1's biggest ambiguity, and (3) **two independently-sourced, numeric orthogonality thresholds** (35°, 50°) that did not appear in the round-2 map's other spine papers and directly answer branch Q7. It also strengthens (rather than newly establishes) three findings the round-2 map already suspected: no submodularity/FPT treatment in the mainstream taxonomic literature (Q2/Q3 must come from Chvátal/Mizutani, not surveys), no defective-survey-cloud precedent (Q16, now confirmed by two independent surveys), and reachability/configuration-space filtering as a real but under-quantified practice (Q8–Q11, still needing ObjView-Bench and Wang 2026 for actual numbers). The paper's own open-issues list (§5) — no ML in the field as of 2021, no fair evaluation metrics, no measurement-uncertainty propagation, TSP left as post-processing — reads as an independent second data point for what Branch 2 (benchmarking) and Branch 4's own gap list should treat as still-open, not solved-but-uncited. Recommended immediate follow-ups surfaced here and not yet in the round-2 map: Prieto 2000 (PhD thesis, the 35° source, unread), Sadaoui et al. 2018 / Lartigue et al. 2014 / Koutecky et al. 2016 (adaptive-voxelization normal-threshold originals, unread — the actual numeric threshold value is not given in this survey), and Vasquez-Gomez's 2014/2017 configuration-space NBV papers (reachability-exhaustion-as-stop-criterion originals, unread).
