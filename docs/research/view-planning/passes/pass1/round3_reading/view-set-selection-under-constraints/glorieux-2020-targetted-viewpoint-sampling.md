# Glorieux, Franciosa, Ceglarek — "Coverage Path Planning with Targeted Viewpoint Sampling for Robotic Free-Form Surface Inspection"

*Robotics and Computer-Integrated Manufacturing* 61:101843, 2020. doi:10.1016/j.rcim.2019.101843. Author's Accepted Manuscript obtained in full (15 pages incl. references) via University of Warwick WRAP repository (`wrap.warwick.ac.uk/123765/`, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0), read in full — abstract, all 8 numbered sections, both tables of results (Tables 2–3), the computation-time table (Table 4), the feasibility table (Table 1), and the reference list. Authors: Digital Lifecycle Management (DLM) research group, WMG, University of Warwick, UK. Funded by UK EPSRC project EP/K019368/1.

Industrial single-sided dimensional inspection of free-form sheet-metal automotive body panels using a surface-metrology gauge (CogniTens WLS400a white-light stereo-vision system) mounted as an end-effector on a 6-DoF ABB IRB6620-150 arm (Fig. 2, p. 7). This is a **model-based, known-CAD-geometry** planning paper — no self-acquired/defective point cloud, no primitive decomposition, no learned selection. Its real contribution to the branch is *not* primitive-level view recipes (it has none) but a clean, fully-formalized, numerically-benchmarked treatment of exactly the "generate admissible candidates → minimum-set-cover select → sequence" pipeline our branch questions interrogate, including hard evidence on ILP tractability, a documented redundancy failure mode, and an explicit (if numerically undisclosed) incidence-angle admissibility gate.

## Core method

**Problem decomposition (§2, p. 3, citing Almadhoun et al. [7]).** Coverage path planning splits into two independent sub-problems: (1) *coverage sampling* — find the smallest set of viewpoints giving full coverage; (2) *multi-goal path planning* — find the optimal sequence and collision-free paths connecting the selected viewpoints (a TSP-with-obstacles problem). The paper works almost entirely on sub-problem (1).

**Coverage sampling is formalized explicitly as a set-cover problem (§2.1, p. 3):** a set system `(S, Q)` where `S` is the finite set of geometric primitives (small elements — nodes/triangles/rectangles — discretizing the to-inspect surface) and `Q` is the robot configuration space; a viewpoint is a feasible configuration `q_j ∈ Q` with an associated subset of *observed* primitives; solving means finding the **minimum number of viewpoints** such that every primitive in `S` is in at least one observed subset. This is unambiguous, stated minimum-set-cover, not max-k-coverage — there is no "given k views, cover as much as possible" framing anywhere in the paper.

**Three-subproblem pipeline actually used (§4.2 workflow, Fig. 1, p. 6; confirmed as the paper's own recommended architecture in §6, p. 9 and §8, p. 13):**
1. **Targeted viewpoint sampling (TarSamp)** — the paper's contribution — iteratively generates a redundant *admissible* set of viewpoints via constrained continuous optimization (not from a fixed candidate grid).
2. **Set-covering** — solve minimum-set-cover over the admissible set (ILP if <1000 admissible viewpoints, else greedy approximation — the 1000 threshold is stated explicitly, §4.2 p.6 and §7.1 p.10) to get the smallest coverage-achieving subset.
3. **Multi-goal path planning** — iterative TSP (integer-programming formulation, subtour elimination added iteratively) + RRT for collision-free point-to-point paths, adopted unmodified from Englot & Hover [8] (§7.1, p. 9–10).

**Targeted viewpoint sampling objective (§4.1, pp. 5–6).** For a candidate viewpoint `Q_i` (defined as a robot *joint* pose `{q_i^1,...,q_i^J}`, though the search itself runs in the 6-DOF **Cartesian** end-effector pose space `W_p ⊆ ℝ⁶ = (x,y,z,α,β,γ)` — joint values are only derived via IK as a feasibility check, not searched directly):

- **Objective 1 — marginal coverage gain (Eq. 1, p. 5):**
  `f_obs(Q_i, S\{s_1...s_{i-1}}) = Σ_{k=1}^{|S\{s1...si-1}|} Pr(Q_i, s_k)`, with `Pr_k = 1` if primitive `s_k` is covered from `Q_i`, else 0. This maximizes the count of **not-yet-covered** primitives newly covered by `Q_i` — the textbook greedy weighted-set-cover marginal-gain criterion, here evaluated over a continuous search space rather than a discrete candidate list.
- **Objective 2 — travel-cost proxy (Eq. 2, p. 5):** `f_trav(Q_i, Q_{1...i-1}) = Σ_{j=1}^{i-1} f_dur(Q_i, Q_j)`, the sum of point-to-point motion durations from `Q_i` to **all** previously sampled viewpoints (a cheap, sequence-agnostic proxy; in the actual implementation, §7.1 p. 9, this sum is further approximated by distance-to-the-average-pose of all prior viewpoints, to avoid recomputing pairwise travel cost at every iteration).
- **Combined via linear scalarization / weighted sum (Eq. 3, p. 6):** `f_obj = c1·f_obs + c2·f_trav`, tuned so coverage gain always dominates travel cost (§7.1, p. 9).
- **Hard feasibility constraints, not soft penalties (Eqs. 4–5, p. 6):** `g_kin(Q_i) ≥ 1` (at least one IK solution exists — the function literally *counts* feasible robot poses and requires ≥1) and `g_col(Q_i) ≥ 1` (at least one of those IK solutions is collision-free, checked against robot links, end-effector, part, fixture, obstacles, floor). A candidate viewpoint that fails either is not a degraded/penalized option — it is infeasible and excluded from consideration during the search.

**What "covered" actually means (Eq. 6, §7.1, p. 9) — the orthogonality/quality gate is a hard AND of three tests, not a continuous score:**
`f_obs(Q_i,{Q_1...i-1},S) = f_scat(f_ray(f_fov(Q_i,{Q_1...i-1},S)))`
- `f_fov` — is the primitive inside the gauge's truncated-pyramid field of view;
- `f_ray` — ray-tracing test: is the line of sight to the primitive unoccluded by other primitives;
- `f_scat` — is the angle between the line of view and the primitive's surface normal **within the limitations of the gauge** (light-scatter/incidence-angle tolerance).
A primitive counts as "covered" (`Pr_k=1`) only if it passes all three. **This is the paper's answer to "is coverage the objective, or per-region quality subject to a coverage floor": neither in our sense — quality (incidence-angle tolerance) is folded into the binary coverage predicate itself as a pass/fail gate, not carried forward as a continuous score to be maximized or floored separately.** The optimization objective that follows (Eq. 1) only ever counts *how many* primitives pass this gate, never *how well*.

**Iterative sampling loop (Fig. 1, p. 6; §4.2).** Maintain `S_p` = primitives not yet covered `k` times (`k ∈ ℕ⁺`, the **"coverage-redundancy"** parameter — the paper's explicit, tunable stopping criterion). Each iteration: (1) shrink the search space `W_p` adaptively to the region relevant to `S_p`; (2) run Self-Adaptive Differential Evolution (SADE [24]) to find the `Q_i` maximizing Eq. 3 subject to Eqs. 4–5, tuned to converge to "good enough" (90%) solutions at ~1000 viewpoint evaluations; (3) update `S_p` to the currently least-covered primitives. Repeat until every primitive in `S` is covered ≥`k` times (or, for controlled comparison against baselines, until a fixed max number of viewpoint evaluations is reached). Output: a redundant admissible set `{Q_1...Q_i}`, then downstream set-cover selects the minimal subset.

## Concrete results and numbers

**Case study 1 — inner door panel, 2283 point primitives (Fig. 3; Table 2, p. 11).** Across three test configurations (250k / 125k / 60,250 max viewpoint evaluations):
- TarSamp (greedy set-cover): 60.87–64.57 mean viewpoints. TarSamp (ILP set-cover): 53.80–64.77.
- RedunSamp (Englot & Hover [8], random *m*-per-primitive, *m*=10/5/3): 66.63–70.13 viewpoints.
- DualSamp (Danner & Kavraki [14], best-of-*m* random per primitive): 75.63–76.40 viewpoints.
- **TarSamp beats RedunSamp by avg 8.1% fewer viewpoints, DualSamp by avg 17.2% fewer** (greedy set-cover comparison, both sides).
- Verified travel cost (VTC): TarSamp ≈12.24–12.75 s vs RedunSamp 15.86–16.30 s vs DualSamp (worse still) — **TarSamp is 20.1% shorter than RedunSamp and 22.7% shorter than DualSamp** (verified travel cost, averaged across the three configs).
- ILP vs greedy set-covering for TarSamp: ILP gives **5.5% fewer viewpoints on average**; no significant travel-cost difference; at the highest sampling redundancy, ILP is ~11.6% better than greedy (§7.3, p. 10–11).
- Because RedunSamp/DualSamp always generate ≥2283 admissible viewpoints (one per primitive minimum), ILP set-covering was **infeasible for them** in this case study — only greedy approximation could be used; TarSamp generated only 60–250 admissible viewpoints depending on config, which *does* allow ILP.

**Case study 2 — window reinforcement frame, 3287 point primitives (Fig. 4; Table 3, p. 12).** Six configurations (38,000 up to 660,000 max viewpoint evaluations):
- TarSamp+ILP: as low as **14.00–17.38 mean viewpoints**. TarSamp+greedy: 18.12–18.96.
- RedunSamp: 18.56–20.18. DualSamp: 19.52–20.18 (worst).
- **TarSamp performs 3.7% better than RedunSamp and 6.9% better than DualSamp** in viewpoint count (greedy-vs-greedy comparison, §7.3 p. 11).
- Travel cost: TarSamp is **9.2% (0.6 s) / 11.4% (0.8 s) shorter (approximated)** and **9.6% (0.7 s) / 11.5% (0.8 s) shorter (verified)** than RedunSamp/DualSamp respectively.
- **ILP set-covering gives 23.8% (3.5) fewer viewpoints than greedy approximation, on average across the six test configurations, for TarSamp specifically** (§7.3, p. 11) — see caution below.

**Important caveat on the "23.8%" headline number.** The abstract states "up to 23.8% reduction in cycle-time... when using targeted viewpoints sampling," and the Conclusion (§8, p. 13) writes: *"The proposed targeted viewpoint sampling strategy performs 3.7% up to 23.8% better in minimising the number of viewpoints... compared to state-of-the-art methods."* Read literally this attributes the full 3.7–23.8% range to *TarSamp vs. RedunSamp/DualSamp*. But tracing the number back to its actual source in §7.3 (p. 11), **23.8% is the gain from switching the set-covering solver from greedy approximation to ILP, holding TarSamp's own sampling fixed** — it is not a targeted-vs-random-sampling comparison at all. The genuine TarSamp-vs-SOTA viewpoint-count gains reported are 3.7–17.2% (both case studies, greedy-vs-greedy); the travel-cost range (9.6–22.7%) *is* consistently TarSamp-vs-SOTA across both case studies. **This is worth flagging explicitly if this paper's headline number is cited in our own writing: the 23.8% figure is about exact (ILP) vs. approximate (greedy) set-cover solving, not about targeted vs. random candidate sampling** — a distinction the paper's own conclusion blurs.

**Set-covering computation time (Table 4, §7.4, p. 12, case study 2):** greedy approximation is a near-constant ~1.0 s regardless of the number of admissible viewpoints (0.98 s at 38,000 evals-config, 1.06 s at 660,000). **ILP scales from 15.6 s (lowest-redundancy config) up to 1062 s (~17.7 min) at the highest-redundancy config** — and the paper explicitly states ILP "becomes impractical when the number of admissible viewpoints increases (>1000)" (§4.2 p. 6, restated §7.1 p. 10). Multi-goal path planning (TSP+RRT) computation was not separately compared across methods but "never took longer than 150 seconds" for case study 2 (§7.4, p. 13).

**Redundancy-vs-travel-cost independence (§7.5 Discussion, p. 13) — explicit, surprising, negative-ish result:** *"it was found that for both case studies the viewpoint sampling redundancy only contributes to minimising the number of viewpoints, and not to minimise the travel cost. Even for the cases where there was a significant reduction in the number of viewpoints, the travel cost remains similar with an increase of the viewpoint sampling redundancy."* Table 3 also shows **no significant improvement across the three highest-redundancy configs (660k/495k/330k viewpoint evaluations)** — i.e., there is an empirical saturation point above which more admissible-sampling redundancy buys nothing further in viewpoint-count minimization either (§7.3, p. 11–12: *"there is an upper limit for the admissible viewpoint sampling redundancy above which there is no further improvement in terms of minimising the number of viewpoints"*).

**Documented redundancy failure of Bircher et al. (§6, p. 8) — direct primary-source citation, not secondhand:** *"The methodologies proposed by Bircher et al. [12] manages to provide full coverage however the number of selected viewpoints was 300 times larger compared to other successful methodologies [14,15]. The resulting total time for the inspection task will always be much longer, even when optimising the path using the proposed iterative resampling. The assumption that there is no need to minimise the number of viewpoints is thus unrealistic for dimensional inspection of sheet metal parts."* This is the paper's own feasibility screen rejecting a real, named published method purely for gross redundancy — directly citable evidence for our branch's redundancy-disqualification RQ.

**Feasibility table (Table 1, p. 8)** screens 9 existing methods [6,8–14,17] plus this paper's own approach against four criteria (`f_obs` optimized, `f_trav` optimized, `g_kin`&`g_col` guaranteed, full coverage `FC` guaranteed). Only **Danner & Kavraki [14]**, **Englot & Hover [8]**, **Jing et al. [17]**, and this paper's TarSamp satisfy all four; Jing et al. is rejected anyway on computation cost (**1 hour vs. 5 seconds** for an equivalent-quality solution using the decoupled Englot & Hover approach, §6 p. 8) — a concrete, quantified data point that joint (non-decoupled) set-cover + path-sequencing formulations cost roughly **720× more compute** for no quality gain in this instance.

## Load-bearing assumptions

- **Known CAD geometry, model-based.** Explicit: "it is assumed that reference geometries of the to-inspect surfaces, workspace and obstacles are available... and that the workpiece and robot placement are predefined" (§2, p. 3). No point-cloud noise, registration error, or as-acquired-vs-as-designed discrepancy is ever modeled.
- **Constant part thickness** ("sheet metal thickness is constant across the geometry... all dimensions are known" — Introduction, p. 2) — this single-sided-known-thickness assumption is why full 3D reconstruction/occlusion reasoning from multiple unknown angles is unnecessary; it does not transfer to arbitrary free-form solids of unknown internal structure.
- **Gauge model with disclosed FOV/ray-trace/incidence-tolerance mechanism but undisclosed numeric thresholds.** `f_fov`, `f_ray`, `f_scat` are named and formalized (Eq. 6) but no angle-of-incidence tolerance value, FOV angle, standoff distance, or overlap fraction is ever given anywhere in the paper — these are treated as proprietary CogniTens WLS400a gauge properties, supplied as opaque model inputs, not derived or reported.
- **The `<1000` admissible-viewpoint threshold for ILP tractability is empirical to this problem's ILP formulation** (uni-cost set cover as integer programming, citing Bradley/Hax/Magnanti [23]), not a universal claim — it depends on solver, primitive count (2283–3287 here), and viewpoint-primitive incidence density; it should be read as "our instance size, our formulation" evidence, not a hard law.
- **Weighted-sum (linear scalarization) is used to trade off coverage gain vs. travel cost (Eq. 3)**, with weights tuned once, manually, to prioritize coverage; no Pareto-front exploration or sensitivity analysis on the weights is reported.
- **A single robot/single gauge, static scene, no dynamic re-planning.** The whole pipeline is offline, planned once before execution; no discussion of failure recovery, re-scanning, or adaptive replanning based on execution feedback.

## What transfers to our setting

- **Minimum-set-cover is the unambiguous framing this paper uses for "cover the surface with as few views as possible," with an explicit formal definition (§2.1) directly citable for our branch's RQ1** — and it demonstrates the field's standard move of decoupling "quality" into a binary admissibility gate (Eq. 6: FOV ∧ unoccluded ∧ within-incidence-tolerance) rather than treating it as a separate continuous objective. This is a clean, checkable precedent for framing our own "coverage at acceptable quality" as a gated predicate rather than a weighted continuous term, if we want the simplest defensible design.
- **Direct, numeric precedent that exact ILP set-cover is tractable at instance sizes far above ours.** Table 4's timing curve (15.6 s at the smallest config up to 1062 s at >1000 candidates) combined with the explicit "<1000 admissible viewpoints" tractability threshold is strong, load-bearing support for our branch's RQ3 — our instance (tens of candidate views, tens of regions) is 1–2 orders of magnitude below where this paper's own ILP approach still ran in seconds. This paper is a citable existence proof that exact set-cover is the right tool, not a heuristic, at our scale.
- **A concrete, quantified argument that decoupling (generate admissible candidates → select) beats joint reformulation.** Jing et al.'s combined set-cover+sequencing formulation took **1 hour vs. 5 seconds** for equivalent quality (§6, p. 8) — directly usable evidence for RQ4's "does generate-then-select earn its keep" question, at least for the specific alternative of jointly optimizing selection and sequencing together (not for Wang-style joint pose optimization, which this paper doesn't test).
- **A working, implementable "coverage-redundancy k" stopping criterion (§4.2)** with reported behavior: increasing k reduces final selected viewpoint count (with a documented saturation point — Table 3, three highest configs show no further gain) but does *not* reduce travel cost. This is directly citable for RQ14 (minimality/stopping criteria) as a simple, understood, already-implemented mechanism, plus an empirical caution that redundancy tuning only buys count-minimality, not path efficiency.
- **The Bircher et al. redundancy-failure citation (§6, p. 8, 300× more viewpoints) is a primary-source hit for RQ13's redundancy-disqualification screen** — this paper is not secondhand testimony but the actual comparative source making the "unrealistic" judgment, with a specific multiplier.
- **The `g_kin(Q_i) ≥ 1` / `g_col(Q_i) ≥ 1` hard-constraint formulation (Eqs. 4–5) is a clean, minimal, directly reusable answer for RQ8's fallback ladder: this paper's answer is unambiguously "(i) hard filter and discard."** Infeasible poses are excluded from the search outright; there is no sliding, no joint-space-only optimization, no deferral mechanism. Useful as one clear data point on the ladder even though the paper never discusses what happens if a primitive has *no* feasible covering viewpoint at all (implicitly assumed not to occur, given full coverage was always achieved in both case studies).
- **"A view" is Cartesian end-effector pose, with joint configuration only checked for existence via IK, not searched or represented directly** — the optimization variable is `W_p ⊆ ℝ⁶` (x,y,z,α,β,γ), and `g_kin` just counts how many IK solutions exist (≥1 required) without discussing which of possibly-multiple solutions gets used or whether they differ in collision status. This is a direct, citable data point for RQ11 ("is a view a Cartesian pose or a joint configuration") — this paper picks Cartesian pose as the represented action, with IK-existence as a gate — but it is silent on IK multiplicity/redundancy handling (no discussion of choosing among multiple IK branches), so it doesn't resolve the collision-inconsistency risk our RQ raises.

## What does NOT transfer, and why (negative findings)

- **No primitive decomposition, no per-primitive view recipes.** The surface is a flat discretization (points/triangles) of a single free-form sheet-metal panel, never segmented into boxes/cylinders/etc. "one orthogonal view per face, a ring per cylinder" (branch RQ5) gets **zero support** from this paper — it contributes nothing to that citation chain beyond the generic FOV+ray-trace+incidence-angle admissibility machinery (Eq. 6), which is a *general* visibility-and-quality-gate pattern, not a primitive-specific recipe.
- **No numeric incidence-angle tolerance, FOV angle, overlap fraction, or defect-size-driven view count anywhere in the text.** `f_scat`'s threshold is named as "within the limitations of the gauge" and never quantified — a genuine, confirmed absence (full-text read, no numeric angle value appears). This is a direct, explicit negative result for RQ6/RQ7 ("what is the maximum admissible deviation from orthogonality — extract the actual published thresholds"): **the mechanism exists in this paper, but the number is treated as a proprietary sensor specification and withheld.** Anyone wanting the actual number must go to CogniTens/Hexagon Manufacturing Intelligence gauge documentation, not this paper.
- **No capability map / precomputed reachability structure.** Feasibility (`g_kin`, `g_col`) is checked live, per-candidate, during the continuous optimization search — there is no offline-precomputed reachability map, no discussion of a capability-map false-positive rate, and no comparison of "filter up front" vs. "post-filter per-candidate" strategies (branch RQ9 gets no data from this paper beyond confirming that per-candidate IK checking, done live, is one workable design point).
- **No tabletop / small-object / eye-in-hand-on-small-arm geometry, no reachable-shell-radius discussion.** The robot is a large industrial arm (ABB IRB6620-150) inspecting large automotive body panels from an open cell (Fig. 2) — there is no minimum-standoff constraint, no "hard minimum shell radius," and no quantification of what fraction of a part's surface becomes unreachable. Branch RQs 10 and 12 (reachable-shell coverage loss, does the reachable set change the shape of the answer) get **no transferable numbers** from this paper — the geometry and scale are too different from our UR5e-tabletop setting.
- **No IK-multiplicity discussion.** `g_kin(Q_i) ≥ 1` only checks *existence* of a feasible solution; the paper never discusses the up-to-8-solutions-per-pose behavior typical of 6-DOF arms, never discusses selecting among multiple IK branches, and never discusses collision-status differences between IK solutions for the same Cartesian pose. RQ11's deeper concern (execution failure risk after a VLM commits to a pose that has infeasible/colliding IK branches even though ≥1 feasible branch exists) is **not engaged with at all**.
- **No self-acquired, defective, or noisy point-cloud planning.** Explicitly model-based with assumed-available reference geometry (§2, p. 3) — this paper offers **zero evidence either way** on branch RQ16 ("does anyone plan views from a defective self-acquired survey cloud rather than a model") — it is squarely in the "plans from a model" camp, reinforcing (by absence, alongside everything else read for this branch) that the self-acquired-defective-cloud case remains an open gap.
- **No submodularity discussion, no orthogonality weighting inside the coverage objective.** The word "submodular" never appears; Eq. 1's marginal-gain criterion is the textbook submodular set-cover form but is never proven or even named as such, and no continuous quality/orthogonality weighting is added on top of the binary coverage predicate — branch RQ2 (does adding a quality weighting break submodularity) gets **no engagement** from this paper; it sidesteps the question entirely by keeping quality purely gate-based rather than score-based.
- **No learned selection, no NBV-style online single-step decision-making.** Selection is exact/approximate combinatorial optimization (ILP or greedy) over a batch-generated candidate set, and candidate generation is metaheuristic (differential evolution), not learned. Branch RQ15 (learned vs. analytical selection) gets no direct evidence — this paper is neither a learned-NBV baseline nor a hand-coded closed-form recipe; it's a third category, formal black-box optimization with a hard-coded objective.
- **No maximum-k-coverage framing, no per-region quality-vs-coverage-floor separation as our branch defines it.** As discussed above, quality is fused into the coverage predicate, not treated as a separate axis with its own floor — so while the paper is unambiguous on "minimum set cover, not max-k-coverage" (a positive answer for RQ1's first half), it gives **no direct answer to RQ1's second half** ("does Albert's requirement even make coverage the objective, or is it per-region view quality subject to a coverage floor") because it never separates the two in the way our RQ poses the question.

## Assessment for the branch

This paper's core value is as a **fully-formalized, numerically-grounded existence proof for the "generate admissible candidates → exact/approximate set-cover select → sequence" architecture at industrial scale**, with genuinely load-bearing numbers for RQ3 (ILP tractability curve, explicit <1000-candidate threshold), RQ4 (720× compute cost of joint vs. decoupled formulation), RQ13 (primary-source Bircher-et-al. 300× redundancy citation), RQ14 (a working, simple coverage-redundancy stopping parameter with an honest null result on travel cost), and RQ8 (a clean "hard filter and discard" data point plus explicit Cartesian-pose-not-joint-config framing for RQ11). It is comparatively weak — deliberately out of its scope, not a search failure — on everything primitive-shape-specific (RQ5–RQ7: no primitive decomposition, no disclosed incidence-angle number, no per-primitive view-count derivation), everything reachability/tabletop-scale-specific (RQ9–RQ12: no capability map, no small-object geometry, no IK-multiplicity treatment), and everything about self-acquired/defective geometry or learned selection (RQ15–RQ16: purely model-based, purely optimization-based). The one number most likely to get repeated uncritically — "23.8% cycle-time reduction from targeted sampling" — should be cited carefully: in the paper's own body text it is actually the gain from exact (ILP) vs. approximate (greedy) set-cover solving, not from targeted vs. random candidate sampling, even though the abstract and conclusion phrase it ambiguously enough to invite the stronger reading.
