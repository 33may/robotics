## Core method / idea

A taxonomy/critique survey of ~25 prior view-planning methods, not an algorithmic paper. The two structural pieces this branch cares about:

1. **The problem is formally posed as covering, and named NP-complete** (§2.3, p.68): *"For a given imaging environment and target object, find a suitably short view plan N satisfying the specified reconstruction goals... N will be a subset (preferably a very small subset) of viewpoint space V, that is, N ⊂ V. As the View Planning Problem has been shown to be NP-Complete ([Tarbox and Gottschlich 1995]; [Scott 2002]), in most realistic reconstruction or inspection tasks it is impractical to seek an absolute minimal length view plan."*
2. **Set-theoretic formalization via a measurability matrix, reduced explicitly to set covering** (§5.1, p.74; §9.1, p.88): Tarbox and Gottschlich [1995] built a *measurability matrix* M(i,j) — rows = discretized surface points, columns = discretized admissible viewpoints, entries = an estimated measurement-quality value (not just binary visibility) from a full visibility/grazing-angle analysis. The paper's own follow-on work, **Scott, Roth & Rivest [2001a] "View Planning as a Set Covering Problem" (NRC Tech Rep. NRC-44892)**, is cited as having *"showed that the view planning problem can be expressed as the problem of covering the rows of a binary measurability matrix by a minimal subset of its columns and provided an integer programming formulation of the problem with a registration constraint"* (§9.1, p.88). This is the exact citation chain the branch brief references as "the source of the measurability-matrix/set-cover reduction every later formulation inherits" — confirmed, with the precise formulation being **minimum set cover via ILP**, not max-k-coverage (see Q1 below).
3. **A method taxonomy**: model-based (§5, Fig.4: set theory / graph theory / computational geometry) vs. non-model-based (§6, Fig.5: volumetric / surface-based / global) — this is the canonical split the branch selection note references.
4. **Viewpoint generation is explicitly framed as a two-paradigm choice** (§9.2, p.88): *synthesis* (solve directly for the viewpoint via optimization/constraint-satisfaction over continuous viewpoint space) vs. *generate-and-test* (discretize viewpoint space first, then select a subset by some optimization/covering algorithm). The paper states synthesis is in the minority because *"in practice, computational difficulties of nonlinearity and convergence usually arise"* and that *"the great majority of view planning methods follow the generate-and-test paradigm."* This is a direct, citable answer to branch Q4 (does "generate candidates then select" earn its keep, or should views come from constraint intersection / joint optimization).

## Concrete results and numbers

This is a survey; there are no new experiments. Numeric/formal content relevant to this branch:

- **NP-completeness of VPP**, restated three times (pp.68, 74, 88), always citing Tarbox and Gottschlich [1995] and Scott [2002] — no proof sketch given in this document, just the citation and the practical conclusion that exact minimum-length solutions are considered impractical *"in most realistic reconstruction or inspection tasks."* No claim is made about instance size thresholds — NP-completeness is a worst-case statement over unconstrained discretizations of V and S, not a statement that *every* instance (including a handful of primitives / tens of candidates / tens of regions) is intractable. The paper does not make this distinction — it is left for us to draw (relevant to branch Q3, ILP/FPT feasibility at our scale).
- **View plan efficiency**, e_v = n_Opt/n (§3.3, p.72), where n_Opt is surrogated by n_Best, the shortest plan found by any method to date on a given task, because *"it may be impractical to determine with certainty the optimum view plan length n_Opt for complex reconstruction tasks."* This is the paper's own redundancy/minimality metric — directly relevant to branch Q13/Q14 (set-size discipline, stopping criteria) — but Table III and §8.1.6 confirm it was essentially never rigorously measured by the surveyed methods (see negative findings).
- **Table I** (§3, p.70; §3.2.1–16, pp.70–72): 15 named requirements, including **"Self-Terminating" (§3.2.7)**: *"The view planning algorithm should recognize when the goal has been achieved or when progress toward it has stalled"* — precedent for a named, decades-old stopping-criterion requirement (branch Q14).
- **Table III** (§8, p.86): 10 algorithms × 15 requirements graded Y/N/P/?/–. The **"Efficient"** row is N/?/uncertain for 9 of 10 methods (only Connolly gets "?"), and **"Self-Terminating"** is Y for most (methods do stop) but §8.1.7 clarifies the criteria used were ad hoc, not tied to explicit model requirements. **"Pose constraints"** and **"6D pose"** rows: only Pito scores Y on 6D pose; most score N on both — quantified evidence that reachability/pose-space richness was almost universally under-modeled circa 2003.
- **View-sphere critique** (§9.2.3, pp.89–90; §8.4.1, p.87) — quantified partially: *"For mid- to high-performance range cameras, the optical baseline typically subtends an angle in the range (50°–35°) at the sensor's optimal stand-off range"* (this is a bistatic-shadow-geometry number, not a coverage or reachability number, flagged so it isn't over-read).
- **Table II** (§4, p.73): a list of 28 prior PhD/Master's theses and survey papers on view planning, 1990–2002 — useful as a citation-trail index if any branch question needs a specific era's primary source, but not read here.

## Load-bearing assumptions

1. **Viewpoint space V, in the paper's own idealization, is 6D+ (three position, three rotation, plus configurable sensor parameters like laser power/scan length), but almost no surveyed method actually uses more than 1–2 dimensions of it** (§2.3, p.68; §9.2.2, p.89; §9.2.5, p.90–91). Reachability/kinematics in this paper means "positioning-system degrees of freedom and range of motion" (§3.2.15–16) — an abstract DOF-and-range model, never an IK-solved or joint-space-aware model. This is the single most important scope boundary for this branch: **the paper's entire "positioning system" vocabulary predates 6-DOF serial-arm eye-in-hand systems with multiple IK solutions per pose.** The reviewed positioning systems are turntables (Maver & Bajcsy: "a single rotational degree of freedom"; Banta et al.: "a single degree of freedom"), translation stages (Papadopoulos-Orfanos: "only translations were employed"), or abstract view-spheres with no kinematic model at all.
2. **The measurability matrix assumes every surface row has at least one admissible column** (implicit in the set-cover reduction, §9.1) — the paper never discusses the case where a reachability/kinematic constraint could make some surface elements *uncoverable by any candidate viewpoint*, i.e., an empty row. This is a structural gap directly relevant to our reachable-shell problem (branch Q11): if the reachable shell systematically excludes viewpoints for certain regions, standard set-cover's implicit "a full cover exists" assumption breaks, and the paper gives no guidance on what replaces the objective in that case.
3. **Model-based methods assume a prior object model "at some level of fidelity"** (§5, p.73) — either exact (CAD-driven, referenced only in passing, e.g. Prieto et al.'s CAD-based inspection criteria, §2.4 p.70) or a partial model incrementally built during scanning. Neither matches "a single fused survey-cloud with real defects, planned against once" — see negative findings.
4. **All surveyed methods are deterministic, single-technique ("monolithic")** (§8.5.3, p.87) — random/heuristic-search selection (e.g., Tarbox and Gottschlich's third algorithm, simulated annealing over a *revocable* selection strategy, §5.1 p.75) is the sole exception noted, and even that operates over the same discretized measurability matrix, not a learned model.

## What transfers to our setting

- **The measurability-matrix → minimum-set-cover → ILP reduction is the direct precedent for branch Q1 and Q3.** Confirmed citation chain: Tarbox and Gottschlich [1995] (measurability matrix, §5.1) → Scott, Roth & Rivest [2001a] "View Planning as a Set Covering Problem," NRC-44892 (binary measurability matrix, minimal column subset covering all rows, **integer-programming formulation with a registration constraint**, §9.1 p.88). This is exactly the base formulation to check against our own instance size (a handful of primitives, tens of candidate views, tens of regions) — the paper doesn't run the numbers for us, but it hands us the formulation and its own author's own instinct that ILP is the *right* exact-solution tool once you accept the set-cover framing, not a heuristic escape hatch. **This is a strong argument that at our scale, "is this just an ILP" is not a naive question — it's literally what the field's own theoretical formalization proposes**, and the field only reached for heuristics because the *general* problem (large V, large S, continuous viewpoint space) is NP-complete, not because ILP is inherently wrong at any scale.
- **Because the matrix entries are *measurability* (a quality value), not raw binary visibility, the field's own canonical reduction already answers part of branch Q1's second half**: "coverage" in this formulation was never purely visibility — it was already "coverage at acceptable measurement quality" by construction (a view "covers" a surface row only if the measurability entry crosses whatever threshold the matrix encodes). This directly supports treating our own problem as coverage-with-a-quality-floor rather than needing a separate quality layer bolted onto plain visibility coverage.
- **§9.2's synthesis-vs-generate-and-test framing is a direct, citable justification for the "enumerate then select" architecture** (branch Q4): the field's own survey states synthesis methods (direct constraint intersection / joint optimization, closer to what Magaña/Wang do) are in the minority precisely because of *nonlinearity and convergence* difficulties, while discretize-then-select dominates because it sidesteps them — independent of any VLM-legibility argument, this is the field's pre-existing, non-VLM-motivated reason for choosing our architecture. Worth citing as convergent evidence: two unrelated reasons (numerical tractability *and* VLM action-space legibility) both favor enumeration.
- **Tarabanis et al.'s "centralize viewpoints in the admissible domain" and Tarbox and Gottschlich's morphological erosion of viewpoint sets are direct precedent for fallback-ladder rung (ii)** (branch Q8): §9.3 (p.91): *"Tarabanis et al. [1995b] used a synthesis approach for generalized viewpoints which seeks to centralize viewpoints in the admissible domain. Tarbox and Gottschlich [1995] used morphological erosion of viewpoints on the periphery of viewpoint sets to reduce vulnerability to pose error."* Both are instances of "don't discard a marginal/infeasible view outright — shrink or re-center the candidate within its admissible region" — precisely fallback-ladder option (ii), *"slide the viewpoint inside its admissible region and re-optimize,"* with a clean two-source citation (though both were built for *pose-error robustness*, not *kinematic reachability* — the mechanism transfers, the motivating constraint differs; flag this distinction when citing).
- **Culling inadmissible viewpoints up front is precedent for fallback-ladder rung (i)** (branch Q8): Tarbox and Gottschlich's octree occupancy grid was used to cull viewpoints "occluded by a mounting fixture and supporting plane" (§5.1, p.74) before the measurability matrix was even built — i.e., hard-filter-and-discard applied to *fixture occlusion*, structurally identical to hard-filtering on *reachability* even though the constraint source differs.
- **§9.1's closing statement that "most other work in the field has relied on a variety of heuristic techniques without a theoretical framework"** (p.88) is a direct, citable precedent supporting branch Q15's premise (redundancy is disqualifying at small set sizes): if the theoretical framework (set cover) was rare and heuristics dominate, then reported view counts in the literature likely reflect heuristic slack rather than a principled minimum — worth citing when screening candidate methods for redundancy per branch Q13.
- **§3.2.7's "Self-Terminating" requirement plus §8.1.7's critique** ("various termination criteria were used, but none related to explicit requirements to be met by the reconstructed object model") is precedent that a principled, requirement-tied stopping rule (branch Q14) was a *named, unmet* goal of the field from at least 2003 — supports treating a coverage-floor-tied stopping rule (e.g., stop once every region's measurability crosses threshold, or ILP's own optimality certificate) as closing a decades-old, explicitly-flagged gap rather than reinventing something already solved elsewhere.
- **§3.1's one-line acknowledgment of shape-primitive-restricted methods** (p.70): *"Some view planning techniques are applicable to restricted shape classes, such as manufactured objects constructed of a limited set of shape primitives."* No citation is attached to this sentence in the text (it is a general aside, not a pointer to a specific paper) — weak, uncitable-by-itself precedent, but it does confirm the *concept* of primitive-restricted view planning existed as a recognized category in the field's own 2003 taxonomy, even though this survey names no specific primitive-decomposition-conditioned method (see negative findings, relevant to branch Q17).

## What does NOT transfer, and why (negative findings — first class)

- **No submodularity discussion anywhere in the document** (branch Q2). Grep-verified: the term "submodular" does not appear. The set-cover formulation (§9.1) is the closest structural anchor — classical unweighted set cover has a submodular coverage function and greedy gives the ln(n)+1 bound (this is textbook knowledge, not stated in this paper) — but the paper never raises the question of whether *adding an orthogonality/quality weighting* preserves submodularity, nor does it discuss greedy approximation guarantees at all. This branch question needs a different, more theoretical source (submodular-optimization literature, e.g. Krause/Golovin or the NBV-specific submodularity papers flagged elsewhere in the map).
- **No maximum-k-coverage formulation anywhere** (branch Q1, second half). Grep-verified: "k-coverage" does not appear. Every formal statement in this paper is a minimization over N ("find a suitably short view plan," §2.3; "covering... by a minimal subset of its columns," §9.1) — the paper only ever poses the problem as *minimum set cover given a coverage target*, never as *maximum coverage given a fixed budget k*. If Albert's requirement is closer to "the VLM gets k views, cover as much as possible," this paper gives **no precedent at all** for that dual formulation — it must be sourced elsewhere (this is itself a useful negative finding: the field's canonical formalization assumes the budget is a *consequence* of the coverage target, not a *given constraint*, which may not match our VLM-driven small-discrete-set framing).
- **No capability-map concept, no IK false-positive-rate discussion, no pre-filter-vs-post-filter framing** (branch Q9). Grep-verified: "capability map" does not appear. The closest analog is generic "positioning system pose constraints" (§3.2.15, Table I) treated as an abstract DOF/range-of-motion envelope, never as a precomputed reachability data structure with an associated error rate. This branch question is anachronistic to this survey by roughly two decades (capability maps as a named robotics concept postdate this paper) — must be sourced from modern manipulation-planning literature.
- **No IK-multiplicity or joint-space-vs-Cartesian-pose treatment anywhere** (branch Q10). Grep-verified: "inverse kinematic," "joint space," "joint-space" all return zero hits. Every positioning system reviewed is a turntable, translation stage, or abstract sphere/circle — none involve a redundant or non-redundant serial arm with multiple IK branches. **This is a clean, confirmed gap**, not an oversight in our search: the survey's positioning-system vocabulary (§3.2.14–16) has no place to even express "which of 8 IK solutions" because none of its examples have more than ~2 controllable DOF.
- **No reachable-shell coverage-loss numbers, no ObjView-Bench-style benchmark** (branch Q11, Q13). Confirmed absent — the paper itself states (§8.1.6, p.86) that *"given the lack of standardized performance benchmarks, it is difficult to make quantitative performance comparisons between proposed view planning methods."* Any numeric denominator for "how much of the surface is unreachable" must come from modern sources.
- **No explicit treatment of "reachability changes the shape of the answer" (objective redefinition when some regions become structurally uncoverable)** (branch Q12). As noted under load-bearing assumptions, the set-cover formulation implicitly assumes a full cover is achievable; the paper never discusses degrading gracefully to "best-available view per region plus an explicit unreachable-region report" when that assumption fails. This is a genuine, first-class gap the paper leaves open — it doesn't even flag it as an open problem in §10.1 (whose named open problems are efficiency, accuracy, robustness to *sensor/pose* error, and material/reflectance handling — not structural uncoverability).
- **No learned NBV, no learned budget predictor, no auto-stop regime** (branch Q16, Q17). Confirmed: §6.3.3 ("Expert System") is the only AI-adjacent subsection, and it states such approaches *"have been few and limited mainly to illumination issues"* — zero learned selection-strategy content of any kind. SCVP, PRVNet, BENBV, GenNBV are all ~20 years post this survey's methods; nothing here compares learned vs. analytical selection.
- **No treatment of planning from a defective, self-acquired survey cloud as a static a priori artifact** (branch Q18). The paper's model-based/non-model-based split assumes either a genuine prior model (some fidelity level, built independently of the scanning-to-be-planned, e.g. CAD) or a model built up *live, incrementally, view-by-view during the same reconstruction session* (§2.2, Fig.3's plan→scan→register→integrate loop). Neither matches "survey once into a fused, defective PLY (pose error, dropout, ~4cm cloud-vs-live-depth offset), then plan a *separate, later* set of inspection views against that static artifact." The closest conceptual neighbor in this paper is §9.3's pose-error discussion, but that is about *execution* error corrupting a *planned* view, not about *input model* uncertainty at planning time (this exact distinction is also flagged in the sibling `view-quality-scoring` notes file, Q12 — consistent finding across both branch reads). **Confirms the branch brief's suspicion that "planning from a defective self-acquired survey cloud" is essentially unaddressed in this canonical survey** — supports treating it as a genuine gap/contribution claim per branch Q18, though this one document from 2003 cannot rule out that later (post-2003) work fills it; that determination belongs to the round-3 recency check.
- **No primitive-decomposition-conditioned view-planning method is named or cited** (branch Q19). Beyond the single unattributed sentence in §3.1 (quoted above), no method in the entire survey — set theory, graph theory, computational geometry, volumetric, surface-based, or global — is described as operating by first decomposing the object into primitives (box/cylinder/etc.) and then applying primitive-specific rules. Every method treats the object as a single mesh/point cloud/voxel grid/solid, planned over holistically. This is consistent with (though does not by itself prove, since the survey is 20+ years old) the branch's hypothesis that primitive-decomposition-conditioned view planning is a real gap.
- **No per-primitive view-count formula or closed-form budget** (branch Q6, Q7). §3.2.4 names "View Overlap" as a requirement (*"the algorithm should provide the degree of image overlap necessary for integration and registration"*) but gives no numeric overlap fraction, FOV-driven count, or defect-size-driven sampling-density formula anywhere in the document. The only numeric angle in the whole paper (50°–35° optical-baseline subtend, §9.2.3, p.90) is about bistatic shadow geometry, not incidence tolerance or view count. Grazing-angle *thresholds* are mentioned as used by Tarbox and Gottschlich (§5.1, p.74: *"different grazing angle thresholds were set for the light source and the camera"*) but the actual numeric values are not reproduced in this survey — they exist only in the original Tarbox and Gottschlich [1995] paper. **Confirmed: this document names the existence of orthogonality-deviation thresholds as a field practice but supplies no number** — branch Q7's specific number must come from a primary source, not this survey.

## Assessment for the branch

High value as the **formal spine** for this branch: it is the confirmed, checkable source of (1) the measurability-matrix → minimum-set-cover → ILP reduction (Scott, Roth & Rivest 2001a is the actual formulation to read next), (2) the field's own justification for generate-and-test over direct synthesis, and (3) two citable precedents for the "slide inside the admissible region" fallback rung (Tarabanis, Tarbox & Gottschlich). Equally valuable as a **negative-findings source**: it cleanly and checkably confirms that submodularity, max-k-coverage, capability maps, IK multiplicity, joint-space action definitions, reachable-shell benchmarks, learned selection, defective-survey-cloud planning, and primitive-decomposition-conditioned planning are *all* absent from the field's own 2003 canonical accounting — which is exactly the kind of first-class negative evidence this branch's RQs are built to surface. Every one of those gaps needs a separate, more recent source; this document's job is done once it establishes that the gap isn't hiding somewhere in the classical literature this survey already covers. The single highest-priority follow-up read it points to is **Scott, Roth & Rivest 2001a, "View Planning as a Set Covering Problem" (NRC-44892)** — the actual ILP formulation, needed to answer Q1/Q3 with the real math rather than this survey's one-paragraph summary of it.
