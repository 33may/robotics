# Pan, Hu, Huang, Wingender & Bennewitz — "ObjView-Bench: Rethinking Difficulty and Deployment for Object-Centric View Planning" (arXiv:2605.10707, 2026)

Source: arXiv HTML full text (https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10707v1), read in full via multi-pass fetch (methodology sections re-queried separately for robot/IK/quarter-sphere/orthogonality details), not abstract-only. Authors are the GS-NBV / active-vision group (Bennewitz lab, Bonn) — same lineage as the GS-NBV paper already cited in the framing doc's "1D circle" precedent.

## Core method / idea

A **benchmark and diagnostic framework** for object-centric next-best-view (NBV) planning, not a planner. Three deliberately disentangled quantities, computed per object at a fixed voxel resolution `r = 0.02` (normalized object units):

1. **Omnidirectional self-occlusion `r_vis^(r)`** (§3.3, Eq. 2): the ceiling on observable surface fraction, `r_vis^(r) = Y_sat^(r) / |S_gt^(r)|`, where `Y_sat^(r)` is the surface cardinality reached by a dense saturating Tammes-sphere sampling. This is a property of the *object's shape*, independent of any planner.
2. **Observation saturation difficulty `d_sat^(r)`** (§3.3): the view count `N_⋆^(r)` at which a monotone-fit observable-surface curve enters a "stable low-gain regime" — i.e., how many *ideal, unconstrained* views it takes before more views stop helping.
3. **Planning difficulty `d_plan^(r)`** (§3.4, Eq. 3): formalized as an **explicit minimum Set Cover problem**:
   `d_plan^(r) = min Σ x_i  s.t.  ∀u ∈ U^(r), Σ_{i: u ∈ 𝒪(v_i,r)} x_i ≥ 1`
   where `U^(r) = ⋃_{v_i ∈ V_plan} 𝒪(v_i, r)` is the coverable target under the protocol's candidate set, and `𝒪(v_i,r)` is the (binary, visibility-only) set of surface voxels observed from candidate view `v_i`. This is exact minimum-set-cover, not maximum-k-coverage, and not weighted/quality-conditioned in any way — coverage predicate `u ∈ 𝒪(v_i,r)` is pure point-visibility at resolution `r`.

Around these three axes the paper builds ObjView-Bench (5,691 Objaverse++-derived objects, §4), evaluates 7 planners (classical/learned/hybrid, §6.1) under three **budget/stopping regimes** — fixed K=5, fixed K=30, and Automatic (stop on a Map-Stabilization criterion) — and separately quantifies **reachability-constrained deployment** by re-running the whole-sphere vs. a reachability-restricted "quarter" view space, including one **real tabletop-arm reachability measurement** (§6.3).

## Concrete results / numbers

- **Real-robot reachability (§6.3, our target pathology):** "a physical reachability check in the real-world tabletop setup of Figure 1 shows that only 45.4/128 candidate views are reachable on average, with counts ranging from 26/128 to 74/128 across five objects and five placements." Candidates are the canonical Tammes-128 protocol set, filtered by IK + collision. **No robot make/model, IK solver, collision-checking method, or workspace geometry is given anywhere in the paper** — Figure 1 is the only documentation of the physical setup, and the text does not name the arm, camera, or checker. This number is a precedent/anchor, not a reproducible pipeline.
- **Coverage cost of reachability restriction (Table 4, §6.3):** comparing whole-sphere vs. quarter-sphere view space: classical planners (RSE, RSE-Mov, Random+TSP) and hybrid (PoinTr-C+*) lose **ΔSC ≈ 0.128–0.131** absolute surface coverage while ΔNSC stays ≈0 (because NSC is renormalized by the *quarter-space's own* observable ceiling — i.e., NSC hides the loss, SC exposes it). Learned NBV-Net collapses far worse: ΔNSC = 0.516, ΔSC = 0.545. Other learned methods (BENBV-Net, MA-SCVP): ΔNSC 0.016–0.023, ΔSC 0.157–0.165. The paper's own headline number, "ΔSC around 0.13–0.17 for most robust methods," is exactly the number cited in our branch's selection rationale.
- **Feasibility-handling protocols actually used (Table 1):** score-based iterative planners → "select best feasible view" (post-filter + pick); set-prediction planners → "remove infeasible views" (hard filter/discard); classical/hybrid planners → "optimize directly over feasible subset"; NBV-Net is the outlier — it outputs a single discrete index and **terminates the whole run if that index is infeasible** (a total-failure mode, not a fallback).
- **Budget/stopping regimes (§5.2):** K=5 → 6 total views executed (5 + 1 initial); K=30 → 31 total; Automatic uses **Map-Stabilization (MS@δ, δ=0.02)**: "stops when relative count gain is below 1% for three consecutive steps," with a hard cap of 128 views if never triggered.
- **Rankings flip with budget (Table 2, §6.2):** at K=5 the top-3 by NSC are RSE (0.980), NBV-Net (0.978), PoinTr-C+MCP (0.977); at K=30 the ordering is PoinTr-C+MCP (0.998) > Random+TSP (0.996) > PoinTr-C+NBV (0.996); under Automatic, PoinTr-C+SCP wins at only 15.5 views (NSC 0.993) while MA-SCVP needs 49.5 views for a comparable 0.990 — a >3x view-count spread for near-identical coverage. This directly evidences "restricting/changing the view regime reorders method rankings," the second half of our selection rationale.
- **Difficulty stratification (Table 5, §6.4):** main-hidden-test (528 objects) mean `d_sat`=32.2, mean `d_plan`=18.5; slow-saturation subset (168 objects) mean `d_sat`=161.7, mean `d_plan`=45.9. Auto-stop (MS) triggers at 9.0 views (main) / 12.7 views (slow-sat) but leaves a residual coverage gap of 0.0026 vs. 0.0081 respectively — MS's stopping point means something different on hard objects (undershoots more).
- **Difficulty-aware training (§6.5, Fig. 3):** rebalancing MA-SCVP's training distribution toward medium/high-`d_plan` objects (no architecture change) improves its coverage specifically on medium/high-`d_plan` test objects — evidence that `d_plan` is a real, actionable axis of object hardness, not just a post-hoc benchmark statistic.
- **Dataset composition (§4):** 5,691 objects total from Objaverse++ (large-scale generic 3D asset corpus) after manual filtering; 5,523 "main" pool (`d_sat ≤ 128`) split into 4,995 train / 528 hidden-test, plus 168 "slow-saturation" (`d_sat > 128`) diagnostic objects. Objects are characterized qualitatively as having "deep cavities, aperture-mediated internal surfaces, and inter-part occlusions" — no category taxonomy, no primitive/CAD subset identified.

## Load-bearing assumptions

- **Coverage predicate is pure visibility, never quality-weighted.** `𝒪(v_i,r)` in Eq. 3 and the SC/NSC metrics are binary "is this surface voxel seen from this viewpoint at threshold τ," with no incidence-angle, distance, or resolution term. The paper never mentions surface normals, orthogonality, or view "quality" beyond visibility — confirmed by targeted re-query of the full text (no hits for orthogonality/incidence anywhere).
- **Objects are generic assets, not primitives.** Objaverse++-derived, described by occlusion topology (cavities, apertures) not shape class; no box/cylinder/primitive taxonomy, no CAD-inspection framing, no defect/inspection task at all — this is a reconstruction-coverage benchmark, not an inspection benchmark.
- **Reachability constraint is a coarse binary proxy ("quarter" vs. "whole" sphere), not the object's real reachable shell.** The one real-robot number (45.4/128) is an isolated empirical measurement layered on top of the abstract quarter/whole simulated proxy used for the main experiments; the two are not the same protocol, and the paper is explicit that the quarter-sphere abstraction is "a strongly constrained reachable-view region" defined only qualitatively — no spherical-wedge angle, axis, or orientation is given. This means the ΔSC 0.13–0.17 numbers mostly come from the *simulated* quarter-sphere proxy, with the real 45.4/128 figure serving as separate motivating evidence rather than the source of the 0.13–0.17 statistic itself. (Re-read §6.3 to confirm this before citing the 0.13-0.17 figure as directly tied to the real robot — the text interleaves both but the table itself is whole-vs-quarter, an abstraction.)
- **Idealized sensing (§8):** no depth noise, no calibration error — object geometry is Objaverse ground-truth meshes, not fused survey point clouds with real defects.
- **`d_plan` (Eq. 3) is computed as an exact minimum-set-cover objective for benchmarking/difficulty-labeling purposes**, at the scale of thousands of objects — the paper does not state which solver (exact ILP vs. approximate/greedy) is used to obtain `d_plan^(r)`, which is a gap worth flagging if we want to cite this as precedent for "ILP is tractable at benchmark scale."

## What transfers to our setting

- **Direct precedent for Q1's first half:** ObjView-Bench formalizes planning difficulty as literal minimum Set Cover (Eq. 3), not maximum-k-coverage — citable confirmation that the field's default framing for "how many views to cover a shape" is min-set-cover. But it is used only as a *difficulty diagnostic*, computed once per object to stratify a benchmark — none of the 7 evaluated planners actually solves set cover as their deployed policy (they're iterative NBV / learned-selection / TSP-budgeted). This is useful nuance: the ILP formulation exists and is solved at scale in the literature, but as an offline oracle/difficulty label, not as an online planner — relevant caution for Q3 ("if ILP is tractable, is anyone actually deploying it that way, or does everyone still use heuristics operationally").
- **Table 1's feasibility-handling taxonomy is one more concrete "what does the field do when the ideal view is unreachable" data point for Q9's fallback ladder** — three observed behaviors here: (a) "select best feasible view" ≈ our ladder rung (i)/(ii) blend — filter then pick nearest surviving candidate, no re-optimization in a continuous admissible region; (b) "remove infeasible views" = hard filter/discard, our rung (i) exactly; (c) "optimize directly over feasible subset" = closest to rung (iii), constrained optimization that never proposes an infeasible view, though this is at the *candidate-set* level (classical/hybrid planners re-run their heuristic over whatever candidates survive), not true joint-space optimization. None of the 7 planners implements rung (iv), "defer and re-test later" — worth noting as an observed gap, not just an omission on our part.
- **Real-robot 45.4/128 (range 26–74/128) reachability number is the direct anchor for Q11** — the framing doc's rationale literally cites this figure. Treat it as an order-of-magnitude precedent (roughly 35% average reachable fraction, wide per-object/per-placement variance) to sanity-check our own UR5e+RG2+D405 measurement against, not as a transferable exact number (different, unspecified robot/workspace).
- **Table 2/3's view-count spread under Automatic budget is usable raw data for Q14** ("what set sizes do methods actually emit"): observed range 9.6 (RSE) to 49.5 (MA-SCVP) views for comparable NSC (~0.99), a >3x spread — concrete cross-method evidence that view-count minimality is not correlated with coverage quality, i.e. redundancy is a real, measured failure mode in exactly the sense our Bircher-et-al. concern (Q14) describes.
- **Map-Stabilization (MS@δ) is a directly citable cautionary example for Q15** — an analytical, threshold-based auto-stop criterion (relative gain <1% for 3 consecutive steps) that the paper's own ablation (Table 5) shows means something different across difficulty regimes (residual gap 0.0026 vs 0.0081, a 3x difference) — i.e., a naive "gain has plateaued" stopping rule is not resolution/difficulty-invariant. Any minimality criterion we adopt should be checked against an object-hardness axis the way this paper checks MS against `d_plan`/`d_sat` stratification.
- **§6.5's difficulty-aware resampling result is modest positive evidence for Q16** — for a learned set-prediction method (MA-SCVP), simply rebalancing training-distribution difficulty (no architecture change) recovers coverage on hard objects, suggesting learned selection's weaknesses under distribution shift are addressable by data curation, not fundamental to the paradigm — tempers a purely "learned methods are brittle" conclusion.
- **The classical-vs-learned degradation asymmetry under reachability restriction (Table 4) is relevant supporting evidence for Q16**: classical/hybrid planners (which re-optimize over whatever candidate set they're given) degrade gracefully (ΔSC ~0.13, ΔNSC ~0) under the quarter-sphere restriction, while at least one learned method (NBV-Net, single-index output with hard termination on infeasibility) collapses (ΔNSC 0.516). This is a concrete failure-mode precedent for "learned selection over a fixed discrete action space is fragile to that action space shrinking underneath it" — directly relevant to our VLM-over-discrete-action-space design, independent of the learned-vs-analytical framing per se.

## What does NOT transfer, and why

- **No orthogonality/incidence-quality dimension anywhere.** This is the single biggest gap relative to our branch's core concern. SC/NSC/d_plan are all binary-visibility coverage constructs; the paper never asks whether a covering view is a *good* view of the surface it covers. If we adopt the Eq. 3 set-cover skeleton, the coverage predicate `𝒪(v_i,r)` must be redefined by us (e.g., gated by an incidence-angle/orthogonality threshold — the branch's own Q7) before it says anything about Albert's requirement. As-is, this paper cannot answer Q1's second half ("does Albert's requirement make coverage the objective, or per-region quality subject to a coverage floor") — it simply doesn't model quality at all, so it silently assumes the former and cannot be cited for the latter.
- **Real reachability data is aggregate-only; no spatial/directional structure reported.** Re-queried explicitly: the paper gives only pooled counts (45.4/128 average, 26–74 range) and does not report *which* views are systematically excluded (e.g., top-down views for tall objects, or grazing/near-table views). It does not verify whether the quarter-sphere abstraction's shape matches real exclusion patterns. This means it cannot answer Q13 ("does the reachable set change the shape of the answer or only prune it") — that has to be measured directly on our own platform; ObjView-Bench only tells us the aggregate magnitude of loss, not its structure.
- **No IK-solver detail, no discussion of IK multiplicity or Cartesian-pose-vs-joint-configuration.** Q12 is entirely unaddressed — the paper treats "reachable" as a black-box binary per candidate view, with no mention of how many IK solutions were tried, whether joint-limit or self-collision or table-collision dominated infeasibility, or whether a UR5e-style wrist has multiple valid configurations per Cartesian target. Nothing here informs whether we should discretize the action space in Cartesian or joint space.
- **Objects are generic Objaverse assets, not primitives.** No box/cylinder/CAD-primitive category exists in the dataset (§4's qualitative description is cavities/apertures/inter-part occlusion, i.e. complex organic/mechanical shapes) — so Q16's "does learned beat analytical *for primitive-shaped objects specifically*" cannot be answered from this paper's object pool; its classical-vs-learned comparison is on generic-shape reconstruction, one level more general than our primitive-decomposition setting.
- **No fused/defective survey-cloud pipeline; idealized sensing only (§8).** Ground truth is clean Objaverse meshes with simulated dense depth, explicitly "no depth noise or calibration error." This paper cannot speak to Q18/Q20 (planning from a self-acquired, defective survey cloud) at all — it reinforces rather than fills that gap: even a 2026 benchmark explicitly built to stress-test "difficulty and deployment" realism stops short of noisy real point clouds, treating idealized sensing as an acceptable scope boundary (§8).
- **No primitive-decomposition-conditioned planning.** Consistent with the object pool gap above — there is no notion of decomposing a shape into primitives and applying per-primitive rules; every method plans over the whole object's point cloud/mesh directly. Confirms (does not itself settle, but adds one more negative data point to) Q20's claim that primitive-decomposition-conditioned view planning is essentially unaddressed in the current literature.
- **"Quarter sphere" is not a capability map, and no false-positive-rate analysis exists.** Their reachability constraint is applied as a geometric proxy (restrict candidate generation to a quarter of the sphere) for most experiments, and as a direct per-candidate IK+collision post-filter only for the one real-robot measurement — no offline reachability/capability map is built or evaluated, and no false-positive/false-negative rate for any feasibility-prediction shortcut is reported. This paper gives us zero evidence either way on Q10's capability-map-vs-post-filter question; it simply doesn't use the capability-map approach at all.
