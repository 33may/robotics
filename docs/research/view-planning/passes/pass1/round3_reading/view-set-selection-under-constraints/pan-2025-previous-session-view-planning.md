# Pan, Lobefaro, Taherkhani, Huang, Menon, Stachniss & Bennewitz — "Efficient View Planning Guided by Previous-Session Reconstruction for Repeated Plant Monitoring" (arXiv:2510.07028)

Source: arXiv HTML full text (https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07028v2), read via multi-pass fetch (method/results extraction, then a targeted re-query for reachability/IK/orthogonality/primitive/submodularity terms with quoted context), not abstract-only. v1: 2025-10-08, v2: 2026-03-23. cs.RO. Authors: Sicong Pan, Luca Lobefaro, Moein Taherkhani, Xuying Huang, Rohit Menon, Cyrill Stachniss, Maren Bennewitz — Bennewitz/Stachniss lab, Bonn. Sicong Pan is also first/co-author on `pan-2024-ma-scvp` (MA-SCVP, arXiv:2304.00910, T-RO 2024) — which appears here as a baseline comparator (Table I) — and on the GS-NBV lineage cited elsewhere in this branch's map; this paper is best read as that group's next step: replace their own iterative/one-shot NBV work with a version that starts from a *previous-session* reconstruction instead of a fresh scan.

## Core method / idea

A **one-shot view-planning pipeline for repeated (multi-session) monitoring**, not single-session NBV. The problem: re-scanning a plant/greenhouse row from scratch every session is wasteful when a reconstruction from the *previous* session already exists — degraded (plant grew, moved slightly, was re-registered under noisy pose) but not worthless. The pipeline (Fig. 2; six stages, §III):

1. **Coarse current-session warm-start**: acquire a rough reconstruction 𝒬 of the current scene — either a short NBV burst (individual plants, §IV-B) or a fixed passive multi-camera scan (greenhouse rows, §IV-C).
2. **Non-rigid registration** (§III-C1): warp the previous-session point cloud 𝒫 onto the current coarse 𝒬 via an embedded-deformation (ED) graph with per-node rigid transforms, `p̃ᵢ = Σⱼ wᵢⱼ(Rⱼ(pᵢ−nⱼ)+nⱼ+tⱼ)`, optimizing `ℒ = λ_arap·ℒ_arap + λ_cd·ℒ_cd + λ_lap·ℒ_lap` (Eq. 3: as-rigid-as-possible + symmetric Chamfer + Laplacian smoothness) to get the aligned cloud 𝒫̃.
3. **Conservative inflation** (§III-C2, Eq. 4): `𝒫̃⁺ = 𝒫̃ ∪ 𝒬 ∪ ℐ`, where ℐ adds points near the current coarse scan but far from the aligned prior (1-NN to 𝒬 < γ_near AND 1-NN to 𝒫̃ > γ_far) — this is how the method captures inter-session growth/change without trusting the transferred geometry unconditionally.
4. **Voxelize 𝒫̃⁺** at resolution `r_cover`, compute per-voxel visibility from each candidate view via OctoMap ray casting → binary predicate `I(p,v) ∈ {0,1}`.
5. **Solve minimum set cover to optimality with Gurobi** (§III-C3, Eq. 6): `minimize Σ_v x_v s.t. Σ_v I(p,v)·x_v ≥ 1 ∀p ∈ 𝒫_surf, x_v ∈ {0,1}`. Already-executed views are excluded before optimization. This is the whole "which views" decision — a single ILP call, solved exactly, no greedy approximation and no learned selector.
6. **Solve the shortest path connecting the selected views** as an open TSP (§III-B1, Eq. 1: `minimize Σ d(vᵢ,vᵢ₊₁)`, collision-free local paths, following [8]'s bounding-sphere collision approximation), also with Gurobi. For greenhouse rows, hierarchical path planning splits views into upper/lower height bands due to limited arm workspace.

Candidate generation is decoupled from selection and differs by target: individual plants discretize camera *position* on a hemisphere (32 candidates) or full sphere (63 candidates) with look-at fixed to the plant centroid, collapsing 6-DoF planning to a 3-DoF position choice; greenhouse rows use Poisson-disk sampling of ~1,000 positions in an oriented bounding box, paired via k-NN (k=8) with 100 look-at targets to yield 800 6-DoF candidates. Three look-at sampling strategies are compared for rows: PriorOBB (plant bounding box), PriorPassive (frontiers of the passive coarse scan), PriorTransfer (their proposed inflated transferred surface, excluding already-observed regions).

## Concrete results / numbers

**Individual plants (Table I):** Sphere candidates — proposed method 9.22±1.66 views, 98.44±0.68% coverage, 3.35±0.41 m path, vs. MA-SCVP baseline 12.72±1.09 views, 92.92±4.29% coverage, 2.99±0.22 m path. Hemisphere candidates — proposed 11.06±3.38 views / 98.77±0.91% / 3.10±0.51 m, essentially tied with MA-SCVP (12.61±1.20 views / 98.80±0.43% / 3.05±0.30 m) and both below GMC-20 (20 views, 99.11±0.53%) but far above GMC-5 (5 views, 91.12±3.12%).

**Plant-complexity effect (Table II):** tomato (more self-occluded) needs 14.17±1.34 views vs. maize 7.94±1.11 views on hemisphere — view budget is not fixed, it tracks per-instance geometric complexity even within one "species" category.

**Inflation ablation (Table III):** removing the conservative-inflation step drops coverage from 98.06±0.75% to 96.99±1.16% (maize) and 99.15±0.40% to 98.98±0.50% (tomato) — a small but consistent, measured contribution from the "don't fully trust the prior" mechanism.

**NBV warm-start quality (Table IV):** one additional NBV after the initial view reduces Chamfer distance from 2.764±1.195 mm to 1.743±0.444 mm — quantifies how much the coarse current-session scan needs before registration is reliable.

**Greenhouse rows (Table VI):** their method at 4 cm resolution reaches 93.26±1.30% coverage with 345.8±18.9 views and 64.54±2.36 m path; at 3 cm, 95.46±1.01% with 476.0±17.1 views / 80.28±2.19 m. Compare Block-Wise iterative NBV: 500 views → 94.70±1.60% coverage but 279.02±7.68 m path (>3x their path length for comparable coverage). Passive-only coarse baseline: 15 views, only 44.48±1.09% coverage.

**Look-at-strategy ceiling (Table V):** PriorOBB 94.48%, PriorPassive 97.21%, PriorTransfer 97.34% max reachable coverage — the paper's own reading: "the primary role of previous-session transfer here is not to expand the theoretically observable surface, but to provide a more reliable planning geometry" (i.e., the transferred prior mainly helps registration robustness / avoids wasted views, not raw achievable coverage ceiling).

**Pose-noise robustness (Table VII, greenhouse, 4 cm):** without ICP refinement at (σ_t, σ_r)=(0.01 m, 2°): Chamfer 13.87±0.47 mm, F-score 42.1% precision / 27.3% recall. With lightweight ICP: Chamfer 6.08±0.16 mm, F-score 99.3% precision / 98.7% recall — registration noise is catastrophic unless corrected, and a simple ICP pass recovers almost all of it.

**Planning cost:** individual plants 10–15 s total; greenhouse rows 3–8 s (registration) + 1 s (set cover) + 2–10 s (path planning) + 250–2000 s (visibility computation, the dominant cost) — vs. sequential NBV at ~1.5 s/iteration (~30 s for 20 iterations). Set cover itself is cheap (1 s); ray-cast visibility computation, not the ILP, is the bottleneck.

## Load-bearing assumptions

- **Coarse cross-session alignment is assumed pre-existing** — plant at a fixed location, consistent global frame (§III-C1, §IV-B3). The method does not solve loop closure or re-localization across sessions; it assumes you already know roughly where things are.
- **A warm-start reconstruction is required before registration is attempted** — either a short NBV burst or a passive multi-camera pass. The pipeline is not "one-shot from nothing"; it is "one-shot given a coarse current scan plus a previous-session prior."
- **Visibility predicate `I(p,v)` is pure ray-cast occlusion, no angle/distance/quality term stated in the equation itself.** Confirmed by targeted re-query: no mention of "orthogonal," "incidence angle," or "surface normal" anywhere in the full text. "Informative" is operationalized purely as coverage maximization ("high reconstruction quality by maximizing plant surface coverage"), not view quality.
- **Set cover is solved exactly (Gurobi ILP), not greedily, and the paper never claims or needs a submodularity argument.** "Submodular" and "NP-hard" do not appear anywhere in the text — the paper simply calls the ILP solver and reports it completes in ~1 s at their problem scale (hundreds of candidate views, thousands of surface voxels), treating tractability as an empirical non-issue rather than a theoretical one worth discussing.
- **No reachability/IK modeling whatsoever.** Confirmed absent by targeted re-query: "reachability," "inverse kinematics," "IK" do not occur in the text. Robot motion is abstracted to a Euclidean TSP over camera poses with a bounding-sphere collision check (following an external reference [8]) — the paper explicitly flags this as future work: "Incorporate robot-specific motion via motion planning stack (MoveIt) rather than Euclidean distance approximation" (§V-3). Feasibility of a selected view against a real arm's workspace is simply not part of this system.
- **No primitive/shape-class structure of any kind.** "Primitive," "cylinder," "box shape," "CAD model" all absent. Plants are treated as generic deformable point clouds; candidate generation is a fixed hemisphere/sphere/OBB sampling, not shape-conditioned.
- **Simulation-only evaluation with a virtual sensor**, explicitly stated as a limitation (§V-1): "our current evaluation is simulation-based with a virtual sensor and assumes that a coarse warm start is available... Due to seasonal constraints, we have not yet validated the full end-to-end closed-loop system on live crops in an operating greenhouse."
- **Target objects deform between sessions** (plant growth) — the entire registration/inflation machinery (Eqs. 3–4) exists specifically to handle change between the prior geometry and current reality. Our object does not grow or deform; the "trust but verify the prior" problem this paper solves is real for them and does not exist in the same form for us (see below).

## What transfers to our setting

- **Direct structural precedent for "derive the view set from a self-acquired, imperfect prior geometry rather than a CAD model."** This is the closest thing in the whole corpus to our actual setup: a fused/registered point cloud (theirs: cross-session and non-rigidly deformed; ours: single-session multi-view fusion with pose error and depth dropout) stands in for ground truth, and view selection runs against it directly. The framing-doc phrase "treating prior geometry as evidence rather than truth" is earned — the conservative-inflation step (Eq. 4) is a concrete, measured (Table III) mechanism for "don't just trust what the model says is there; also look for the current reality diverging from it," which is directly analogous to how we'd want to treat our fused survey PLY (trust it as a strong prior, but be aware fused geometry can be wrong locally).
- **Direct, clean precedent for Q1's first half and Q3.** The paper's whole selection stage answers "minimum set cover, solved exactly with an off-the-shelf ILP solver, at ~1 s runtime" for candidate counts in the tens-to-thousands range (32–63 for individual plants; 800 for greenhouse rows) and thousands of surface-voxel constraints. This is concrete evidence (not just ObjView-Bench's offline-difficulty-label usage) that **exact ILP set cover is a real, deployed, fast online planning step at problem sizes larger than ours** — directly supports Q3's claim that our instance size (a handful of primitives, tens of candidates, tens of regions) needs no heuristic apparatus at all. Also directly answers Q2's "does greedy's guarantee matter" — this paper doesn't use greedy, doesn't discuss submodularity, and doesn't need to, because ILP at this scale is just fast.
- **Reinforces Q14's redundancy concern with a fresh, uncontested number.** Their own method uses fewer views for equal-or-better coverage than both classical iterative NBV (their own group's prior work, MA-SCVP) and block-wise NBV — 9.22 vs 12.72 views for +5.5pp coverage on sphere candidates; 476 vs 500 views for comparable coverage but 3.5x shorter path in greenhouse rows. This is one more concrete case where an iterative/greedy baseline is measurably more redundant than an exact one-shot set-cover solve, on the *same* candidate set — good corroborating evidence, though note it's cross-method (theirs vs. MA-SCVP), not the identical Bircher pathology.
- **Table IV's NBV-warm-start-improves-registration result is transferable reasoning (not number) to our fusion pipeline**: it operationalizes "how much do you need to see before you can trust alignment" as a measurable Chamfer-distance curve, which is a useful pattern if we ever want to argue our own survey pass has "enough" views before deriving a view set from it — though our survey isn't cross-session registration, so the specific mechanism doesn't transfer, only the "quantify prior reliability before committing" instinct.
- **The candidate-generation/selection split (hemisphere/sphere or OBB sampling → separate ILP selection stage) is a light structural precedent for the "generate then select" pipeline question (Q4)**: this paper treats candidate generation and selection as cleanly separable stages, with selection being the only place any optimization happens; candidate generation itself is dumb (fixed geometric sampling), and all the intelligence is in the coverage/visibility computation and the ILP. This slightly favors "enumeration is cheap and not where the real work is" for Q4 — the interesting problem is selection over a well-chosen candidate set, not smarter enumeration.

## What does NOT transfer, and why

- **No orthogonality/incidence-angle/view-quality notion anywhere — same gap as ObjView-Bench, confirmed independently.** `I(p,v)` is pure ray-cast visibility; "informative" = surface coverage, full stop. This paper cannot be cited for Q1's second half, Q6, or Q7 (the orthogonality-deviation threshold) — it simply has no quality axis to extract a number from. Two independent papers in this branch (ObjView-Bench and this one) now confirm that the mainstream model-based/prior-guided view-planning literature treats coverage as pure visibility and leaves quality-weighting as unaddressed territory we have to define ourselves.
- **No reachability/IK/kinematic-feasibility treatment at all — a genuine, explicit gap, not just an unstressed assumption.** This is stronger than ObjView-Bench's "coarse binary quarter-sphere proxy" — here there is *nothing*: robot motion is Euclidean-distance TSP with a bounding-sphere collision check borrowed from another paper, and the authors themselves flag "robot-specific motion via MoveIt" as unaddressed future work (§V-3). This paper is silent on Q8, Q9, Q10, Q11, Q12 — it offers no fallback-ladder data point at all (not even a hard-filter baseline), because infeasibility is never modeled. Do not cite this paper for anything reachability-related.
- **Plants deform between sessions; our object does not — the paper's central technical contribution (non-rigid registration + conservative inflation, Eqs. 3–4) solves a problem we don't have.** Our survey PLY is single-session, and the "prior" and "current reality" are the same acquisition, not two acquisitions separated by growth. This means the ED-graph deformation machinery, the γ_near/γ_far inflation-threshold tuning (0.003/0.005 m for plants, 0.01/0.01 m for rows), and the whole cross-session-trust framing do not transfer as mechanisms — only the *pattern* ("treat self-acquired geometry as evidence, augment for regions it might be wrong about") transfers, not the implementation.
- **No primitive-shape structure or CAD-like decomposition anywhere** — confirmed by targeted re-query (cylinder/box/primitive/CAD all absent). This paper adds one more independent negative data point for Q20 ("is primitive-decomposition-conditioned view planning real") — a third group (after ObjView-Bench's Objaverse-generic pool) with mature view-planning machinery that never touches primitive decomposition, from a lab (Bennewitz/Bonn) that has published extensively across NBV, one-shot planning, and now prior-guided planning without ever introducing shape-primitive conditioning.
- **Simulation-only, virtual sensor, explicitly unvalidated end-to-end on real hardware (§V-1).** Chamfer-distance and coverage numbers are simulation metrics against ground-truth plant meshes/scans, not measured against a real degraded point cloud acquired by a real depth camera with the noise profile our D405 survey has. The pose-noise robustness experiment (Table VII) is the closest they get to "what if the prior is wrong" — and even that is synthetic Gaussian noise injection (σ_t=0.01 m, σ_r=2°) with an ICP correction step, not the depth-dropout/glossy-surface/4cm-fusion-offset defect profile our framing doc specifies. Treat their noise-robustness numbers as a lower bound on difficulty, not evidence our defect profile is survivable the same way.
- **Objects are whole plants/rows, not object-with-defects inspection targets** — there's no notion of "defect," "surface region needing its own view," or per-region quality floor. This paper cannot inform Q6/Q7's per-primitive view-count/tolerance thresholds at all; its "how many views" driver (Table II) is self-occlusion complexity per plant instance, not FOV/incidence-tolerance/overlap-fraction/defect-size as our RQs need.
