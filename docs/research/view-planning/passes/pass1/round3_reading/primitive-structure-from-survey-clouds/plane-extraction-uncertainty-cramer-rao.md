## Core method

The paper derives, from a range-and-incidence-angle-dependent sensor noise model, closed-form maximum-likelihood/least-squares plane fits **and** their parameter covariance matrices — no iteration required for the recommended variant.

**Noise model (Sec. 2, Eq. 5).** Each range sample `ρ_j` along known direction `m̂_j` is Gaussian, `ρ_j ~ N(ρ̄_j, σ²{ρ̄_j, n̂·m̂_j})`, with
`σ{ρ̄_j, n̂·m̂_j} = σ̂{ρ̄_j} / |n̂·m̂_j|`, `σ̂{ρ̄_j} := κ ρ̄_j²` (κ a sensor-specific constant, ρ̄_j the true range = `d/(n̂·m̂_j)`).
Substituting ρ̄_j gives `σ ∝ d² / |n̂·m̂_j|³` — i.e. **range-noise std-dev grows as range² and as the inverse-cube of cos(incidence angle)**. This single equation is the paper's central deliverable for propagation work: it is an explicit, closed-form range-error(range, incidence-angle) model, not a vague "orthogonality helps" claim.

**Four plane-fit/covariance methods compared (Secs. 2–5):**
1. **EMLP** (Exact Maximum-Likelihood Problem, β=1 in Eq. 6/7) — iterative nonlinear optimization, most accurate, Cramér-Rao-bound-tight covariance from the Hessian (Eq. 11, 15). Expensive.
2. **Renormalization** (Kanazawa & Kanatani 1995, ref [5]) — iterative, but built on an *older, incidence-angle-independent* noise model (`σ = κ₁ρ̄`, linear in range only).
3. **ALSP** (Approximate Least-Squares Problem, Sec. 3) — the paper's recommended method. Closed-form: solve is the eigenvector of minimum eigenvalue of a weighted scatter matrix `S` (Eq. 20), `d* = n̂*·r_G` where `r_G` is the weighted centroid (Eq. 19); covariance is `C_ALSP = -H_ALSP^+` (Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse of the closed-form Hessian, Eq. 21).
4. **EVPP** (Eigenvector Perturbation Problem, Sec. 4, adapting Weng/Huang/Ahuja 1989) — reformulates the fit in a unit 4-vector `ν̂ ∝ [n̂;d]` parameterization and perturbs the eigenvector; comparable accuracy to ALSP but its covariance terms don't decompose into interpretable geometric quantities.

**Key structural property (Eq. 22):** for ALSP, the optimal solution `[n̂*; d*]` lies **exactly in the null-space of its own covariance matrix** `C_ALSP`. This is stated as "remarkable" and is exploited directly for plane fusion (Sec. 6): because the covariance's large eigenvalues point in directions *orthogonal* to the plane's own parameter vector, two plane estimates of the same physical surface can be fused with a Kalman-style update (Eqs. 40–42) using the covariance-weighted average, with a Frobenius-norm correction (Eq. 42) to keep the fused covariance singular in the correct (solution) direction.

**Bias warning (Sec. 2.2, text before Eq. 9a; explicit citable claim):**
> "we can show that unless β = 0, the solution is biased" (line ~181 of extracted text, immediately preceding Eq. 9a).

I.e. the theoretically "more correct" full maximum-likelihood objective (β=1, EMLP, which includes the `log σ` normalization term) yields a **biased** plane estimator under this noise model; only the β=0 (least-squares) variant is unbiased. This is reinforced later (Sec. 5 Discussion, Fig. 4 caption/discussion): EMLP's estimated relative-uncertainty ratio ε3 sometimes falls *below* the theoretical minimum of 1, which the authors explicitly read as evidence of small residual bias, "since the Cramér-Rao bound is only applicable to unbiased estimators."

## Concrete results and numbers

**Simulation setup (Sec. 5):** simulated Swiss-ranger (CSEM SR, PMD-based time-of-flight camera), κ = 0.0018 (all lengths in meters), experimentally calibrated by Anderson, Herman & Kelly 2005 [ref 1] for a *different* commercial flash-ladar, not the Swiss-ranger itself (the paper reuses that calibration). Measuring directions `m̂_j`, j = 176×144 (= sensor's native resolution), matched to the real device. Infinite planes at constant distance d = 4 m from origin, normals swept over incidence angles θ, φ ∈ [-30°, 30°] (four plot corners truncated — those combinations exceed the device's 7.5 m max range).

**Error metrics defined (Eqs. 34–36), each computed against the true Cramér-Rao bound `C̄_EMLP`:**
- ε1 — squared Mahalanobis distance of estimated to true principal covariance eigenvector (ideal 0).
- ε2 — weighted angular error of all four principal uncertainty directions (ideal 0).
- ε3 — "Relative Total Uncertainty," ratio of estimated to CR-bound-optimal generalized variance (product of nonzero eigenvalues); **theoretically bounded below by 1** (Eq. 36).

**Readable colorbar ranges from Fig. 4 (ε3, relative total uncertainty over the ±30°×±30° incidence sweep):**
- Renormalization: colorbar spans ≈0.9–1.7 — i.e. estimated uncertainty can be inflated up to 70% relative to the CR bound, *and* can also read as low as 0.9 (below the theoretical floor — flagged in the caption as untrustworthy, an artifact of the wrong noise model).
- ALSP: colorbar spans ≈1.0–1.012 — at most ~1.2% inflation over the CR bound across the whole tested angle range.
- EVPP: colorbar spans ≈1.0–1.012 — essentially identical to ALSP.
- EMLP (Exact): colorbar spans ≈0.997–1.003 — closest to unity, consistent with near-optimality (and with the small-bias reading above).

I'm reading these off the OCR'd colorbar tick labels next to each subplot, not the heatmap pixels themselves, so I cannot say *which* (θ,φ) combination produces the worst-case value — only that ALSP/EVPP stay within ~1% of CR-optimal and renormalization can be off by tens of percent, **and** that (per the Sec. 5 Discussion text, not just the plot) "the ratio of the estimated uncertainty to the least possible uncertainty steadily worsens as the incidence angle to the plane increases" for renormalization, ALSP, and EVPP alike — EMLP is the only method that resists this degradation. No single numeric "angular error budget" threshold (e.g., "beyond N° expect Xσ") is given; the relationship is shown graphically, qualitatively monotonic in incidence angle, not tabulated.

**Runtime (Sec. 5, MATLAB, 840 runs, one full 176×144-point plane fit per run):**
- ALSP: 0.0052 s/sample — closed-form, no iteration.
- Renormalization: 0.4393 s/sample (iterative).
- EVPP: 0.3332 s/sample.
- EMLP: ≈15 s/sample (iterative nonlinear optimization) — "recommended only for offline optimization."

**Real-data application (Sec. 6, Figs. 5–7):** Swiss-ranger mounted on a robot rotating in place, ~10 successive views, plane-patches extracted via region-growing (a *different*, earlier paper, ref [13]) + ALSP fit, matched/registered across views (ref [11]), then fused via Eqs. 40–42. Reported pipeline runtime (region-growing + registration + fusion, C++ on a 2009-era Pentium 4 HT 3GHz/1GB RAM): ≈0.25 s per view-pair, ~10 planar patches per view.

## Load-bearing assumptions

1. **Noise model form is sensor-specific and externally calibrated.** κ = 0.0018 was taken from a different flash-ladar's calibration (Anderson et al. 2005), applied to the Swiss-ranger by assumption, not fitted to the Swiss-ranger directly. The quadratic-in-range / inverse-cube-in-cos(incidence) functional form itself is asserted as generally applicable to "commonly available 3D sensors like the Swiss-ranger and Laser-range-finder (LRF)" citing three prior characterization studies (refs [14,1,8]), but no direct empirical validation against real Swiss-ranger ground truth is shown in this paper (validation is against the CR bound *within the assumed model*, i.e. self-consistency, not against an independent ground-truth check of the noise model itself).
2. **Points are treated as i.i.d. radial-Gaussian, valid returns only.** No dropout, no outliers, no multi-path/specular effects modeled. All noise is "how far off is a returned point," never "did a point fail to return at all."
3. **Registration between views is assumed exact.** In the plane-fusion application (Sec. 6, Eq. 38), the relative pose `R, t` between the two sensor frames is stated as "both assumed known" — its own uncertainty is never propagated into the fused plane covariance. The fusion equations (40–42) only rotate/translate the *per-view* plane covariance into a common frame via `U C_ℓ U^T`; they add zero uncertainty for the registration step itself.
4. **Infinite-plane parameterization throughout.** The quantity being fit and its covariance is always the 4-parameter `(n̂, d)` pair of an *unbounded* plane — segmentation into a bounded patch (extent, boundary, centroid) is delegated entirely to a separate, earlier paper (ref [13], Poppinga/Vaskevicius/Birk/Pathak, IROS 2008, "Fast plane detection and polygonalization in noisy 3D range images") that is cited but not re-derived here.
5. **The Cramér-Rao bound is only a valid reference for unbiased estimators** (stated explicitly, Sec. 2.2.1) — and the paper's own analysis suggests EMLP (β=1) may carry small residual bias, which muddies its use as ground truth for evaluating the other three methods, though the authors judge the bias "quite small."

## What transfers to our setting

- **The propagation *mechanism*.** Given any per-point range-noise model σ(range, incidence-angle) for the D405, the ALSP machinery (Eqs. 19–21) gives a **closed-form, non-iterative** plane-normal/offset covariance built from nothing but the weighted centroid and weighted scatter matrix of the inlier points — exactly the "orthogonal ± σ" deliverable RQ1 needs, and cheap enough (5 ms/plane on 2009 MATLAB for 25k points) to run per-face at inspection time with no retraining, satisfying the "off-the-shelf, no retraining" framing-doc constraint.
- **The null-space property (Eq. 22)** is a useful sanity check/diagnostic if we implement our own covariance: the fitted `[n̂; d]` should lie in `C_ALSP`'s null-space; if it doesn't (numerically), the fit code has a bug — cheap to unit-test.
- **The benchmark template (Eqs. 34–36).** ε1/ε2/ε3 (Mahalanobis distance to ground truth, principal-direction angular error, relative total uncertainty vs. CR bound) is a ready-made metric set for a synthetic validation harness of any face-normal-uncertainty estimator we build or evaluate on our objects — directly answers part of the branch's "what protocol can seed our benchmark" question, independent of Fit4CAD/SHREC/ABC.
- **The explicit bias warning** is a transferable methodological caution: if we build our own incidence-angle-weighted plane/primitive fitter, using the "textbook" full-likelihood objective (their β=1) is not automatically the safest choice — the least-squares variant that drops the `log σ` term can be the numerically *safer, unbiased* choice. Directly relevant to the branch's "systematic bias is the dangerous case" concern (though this paper's bias finding is about the *fitting objective*, not about dropout — see below).
- **Registration/alignment covariance is explicitly NOT handled** — see next section; this is itself a useful negative data point steering us away from expecting this literature to answer RQ9 for free.

## What does NOT transfer, and why

- **Sensor mismatch.** The noise model and its calibration constant κ are for a PMD/ToF flash-ladar (Swiss-ranger), not an active-stereo sensor (D405). Active stereo's dominant noise sources (baseline-limited triangulation error growing with range², IR-pattern/correspondence-matching noise, texture-dependence) are structurally different from ToF range noise; the quadratic-in-range term is plausible by analogy (all range sensors get worse with range²-ish scaling due to triangulation/SNR geometry), but the incidence-angle exponent (inverse-cube) and the coefficient κ cannot be assumed to transfer — they would need to be re-derived or re-calibrated for the D405, which this paper gives no guidance on doing (it cites, but doesn't reproduce, the calibration methodology of Anderson et al. 2005).
- **No answer to RQ9 (registration covariance propagation).** As noted, `R, t` are assumed exactly known in the one place (Sec. 6) where multi-view fusion is discussed. There is no M3C2-EP-style treatment here; this paper simply doesn't address how pose/registration uncertainty (our "few mm of registration error") propagates into a fitted plane's covariance — only how *per-point range noise*, given a known pose, propagates. This is a genuine gap the paper leaves open, not a place to expect a ready answer.
- **No dropout modeling, hence no answer to the glossy/dark-dropout branch question.** The entire analysis assumes every ray returns a valid, Gaussian-noisy range sample. Missing returns (specular/dark dropout) are outside scope; whether dropout *biases* (vs. merely reduces N for) the fitted plane is not addressed at all here — that question needs a different source.
- **Planes only — and this is confirmed to be a hard boundary, not just unaddressed scope.** No cylinder, sphere, cone, or superquadric treatment in this paper. The same authors' direct follow-up (Vaskevicius, Pathak, Pascanu, Birk, "Extraction of quadrics from noisy point-clouds using a sensor noise model," ICRA 2010, reproduced as Ch. 3 of Vaskevicius's 2017 PhD thesis) extends the identical range-and-incidence-angle noise model (their Eq. 3.16 is this paper's Eq. 5 verbatim) to general quadrics and states explicitly: *"Unlike the planar case in [9], analytical expressions for the Hessian cannot be obtained for the quadric case"* — refinement there requires numerical MLE (MATLAB nonlinear optimizer) and only a *numerically*-evaluated Hessian at convergence, no closed form, no ALSP-style non-iterative solution, and no proven null-space property. So the branch's "cylinder axis ± σ, closed form" ask has a documented negative answer from this exact research lineage: closed form for planes only; cylinders/cones need iterative ML refinement with a numerical (not analytical) covariance, which is slower and has no guaranteed real-time budget the way ALSP does (5 ms/plane).
- **No bounded-face output.** The fitted object is always the infinite-plane 4-vector; centroid/extent/boundary recovery is outsourced to a separate paper (ref [13]) not fetched here. So this document alone does not answer "which methods recover bounded faces" — it answers "given a bounded patch's inlier points (from elsewhere), how do you get the plane parameters and their covariance."
- **No fit-reliability/failure-detection score beyond the covariance itself.** The paper doesn't propose an inlier-ratio/residual-RMS/MDL-style scalar "is this fit trustworthy" signal for triggering a fallback — the covariance matrix *is* the uncertainty output, not a scalar go/no-go score. Relevant to the branch's two-tier fallback question: this paper would let us threshold on, e.g., the largest covariance eigenvalue or ε3-like ratio, but doesn't validate such a threshold against downstream task failure — that mapping (score-below-X ⇒ derived pose is wrong) is not attempted here and would be "the minimal experiment" the branch questions flag as still needed.
- **2009-era hardware and a 2009-era registration/plane-matching pipeline** (refs [11], [13]) are cited as black boxes for the parts of the pipeline (segmentation, correspondence, registration) that would matter most for reproducing the real-data section; this paper alone is not a recipe for a modern Open3D/CGAL/PCL plane-extraction-with-uncertainty implementation, just the math for the covariance once you have inlier points.
