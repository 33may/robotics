## Core method / idea

Not a view-planning or NBV paper at all — it is a **2D image-corruption robustness study for embodied manipulation**, structured as a four-stage pipeline argument:

1. **Perception→Cognition→Decision→Execution pipeline (§2.1, citing "Mertonian" systems theory).** The paper's central theoretical move: Human Visual Systems (HVS) and Machine Visual Systems (MVS, e.g. detectors) are "Newtonian" — their downstream processing is deterministic/robust, so a single perceptual-quality score suffices. Robots are "Mertonian" — Decision and Execution do **not** reliably follow from Cognition ("a deviation of one character in Cognition may greatly change the pose in Decision; a one-centimeter path offset in Decision may also cause Execution to hit obstacles," §2.1) — so a Robot Visual System (RVS) needs quality scored **separately at each of the three downstream stages**, not once at the perception stage.
2. **Corruption injection is single-image, reference-relative, not multi-view.** 1,230 clean reference images (7 existing robotics databases, filtered via Q-Align to remove pre-existing distortion, balanced across Sim2Real × first-/third-person perspective × 5 subject/background categories, §3.1) each get **one randomly-composited application of all 30 distortion types** (7 categories: Blur, Luminance, Chrominance, Noise, Compression, Spatial, Other; 5 intensity levels each, calibrated so "quality degradation perceived by the HVS is aligned at the same level," §3.1) → 36,900 distorted images, each paired 1:1 with its clean reference (Table 1; abstract text itself is inconsistent, saying "over 30k" in the Abstract section body but "over 36k" / 36.9k in the contributions bullet and Table 1 — the precise, checkable number is 36,900, §3.1).
3. **Ground truth is not human-labelled and not geometric — it is self-referential model-consistency.** For each reference image, 5 manipulation tasks are hand-annotated in natural language (drawn from a fixed 10-verb vocabulary: Cover, Insert, Move, Pick, Place, Pour, Press, Pull, Push, Twist, §3.2). The "quality score" of a distorted image is **not** an absolute label — it is how much a downstream model's output *changes* between the clean reference and the distorted version of the same image, summed over the 5 tasks:
   - **Cognition (§3.3):** 15 VLMs (≤8B params) each produce a ~10-word task solution for reference and distorted image; BLEU/ROUGE/CIDEr (weighted 1:1:0.1) measure divergence.
   - **Decision (§3.4):** 15 VLAs (≤8B params) each output a 7-DoF pose; divergence = normalized combination of position (mm, Euclidean), rotation (rad, cosine similarity), gripper state (0–1, absolute difference).
   - **Execution (§3.5):** the only stage with *real* ground truth — ~1,500 real UR5 + Robotiq 2F-140 trials, executing only the easiest of the 5 tasks per image (to isolate distortion as the failure cause): success=100, failure=100 minus Euclidean deviation in cm, collision/emergency-stop=0.
4. **Experiment (§5):** 15 off-the-shelf IQA methods (5 zero-shot: PSNR, SSIM, Brisque, Q-Align, Q-Align+; 5 full-reference (FR) fine-tuned: AHIQ, CKDN, DISTS, LPIPS, TOPIQ-FR; 5 no-reference (NR) fine-tuned: CLIPIQA, CNNIQA, DBCNN, QualiClip, TOPIQ-NR) are trained/evaluated (train/val 29,520/7,380, 8:2 split, §5.1) to predict the **Decision** score (VLA-consistency) from the image pair, and separately (Appendix F) the **Cognition** score. This is the paper's actual "benchmark": can standard, human-tuned IQA metrics predict embodied-task-relevant image quality?

## Concrete results / numbers (all grep-verified against raw HTML text)

- **The headline anti-circularity number (§5.2, verbatim):** *"The SRCC of FR IQA methods with subjective labels is less than 0.65, while NR is even less than 0.6. Note that these methods have a correlation close to 0.9 with HVS in traditional human-oriented IQA tasks... but they cannot adapt to the database we proposed."* This is stated as an aggregate across the three Decision dimensions (Position/Rotation/State) — I recomputed the average across the 5 FR methods over these three dimensions from Table 2's raw cells and got **0.582** (FR) and **0.487** (NR), consistent with the "<0.65 / <0.6" claim. It is **not** a per-cell universal bound: the best individual cells clear it easily — TOPIQ-FR Position SRCC = **0.7748**, AHIQ Position SRCC = **0.7481** (Table 2) — so the ceiling result is dimension- and method-dependent, and the honest summary is "aggregate degrades from ~0.9 to ~0.5–0.65, best-case single dimension/method reaches ~0.75–0.83," not a flat number.
- **Best achievable (TOPIQ family), stated directly (§5.2):** *"TOPIQ maintaining the leading performance in most cases, with an SRCC of about 0.75."* Table 2 shows TOPIQ-FR SRCC = 0.7748 (Position), 0.6428 (Rotation), 0.6684 (State), 0.8307 (First-person subset), 0.8322 (Third-person subset). Interesting secondary finding I checked directly in Table 2: **within-subgroup SRCC (conditioned on perspective) is higher (~0.80–0.83) than across-dimension SRCC (~0.6–0.77)** — i.e. splitting by a nuisance variable (perspective) and scoring within each split is an easier prediction problem than scoring across the whole heterogeneous set. Worth remembering for our Q12 (stratified reporting can look better than pooled reporting for reasons that have nothing to do with method quality).
- **Rotation is the hardest Decision dimension, Position the easiest (§5.2):** consistent across nearly every method in Table 2 (e.g. AHIQ: Position 0.748 > State 0.647 > Rotation 0.646; TOPIQ-FR: Position 0.775 > State 0.668 > Rotation 0.643). Directly relevant to our Q7/Q16: rotational/orientation-sensitive scores are the hardest for a proxy metric to predict, which is exactly the axis our "orthogonality" metric lives on.
- **Metrics literally frozen to HVS get *worse* after fine-tuning (§5.2, verbatim):** *"the main parameters of some methods have been frozen based on HVS, such as LPIPS, DISTS, and CLIPIQA. Thus, although after training, they are even worse than the zero-shot baseline."* LPIPS Position SRCC = 0.392, below Q-Align zero-shot Position SRCC = 0.533 (Table 2). This is a clean, checkable demonstration that **architectural commitments baked in for human-perceptual alignment can actively hurt** when the target construct changes — relevant to Q3/RQ1: if our RQ1 judge borrows a human-vision-tuned visual-quality backbone, its frozen assumptions could work against an orthogonality/coverage objective the same way.
- **Inter-annotator agreement among the "ground truth" models themselves is low (§4, Figure 4, verbatim):** *"a correlation slightly higher than 0.3"* for VLM pairwise SRCC (Cognition), and *"the correlation of VLA is even lower than that of VLM, at around 0.25"* (Decision) — average pairwise SRCC across 15 diverse ≤8B models. Figure 3 gives finer per-dimension pairwise SRCC: Precision 0.31, Recall 0.31, Semantic 0.30 (VLM); Position 0.25, Rotation 0.23, State 0.28 (VLA). **This is a hard number for Q17**: even with 15 independent judge models, their mutual agreement tops out around 0.25–0.31 SRCC — i.e., the "ground truth" ensemble used to train/evaluate all 15 IQA metrics is itself a noisy, low-agreement pool, not a clean oracle. The paper's own conclusion from this: *"using only one model as the subject is far from sufficient, especially for VLA. It is necessary to collect their general preference"* (§4) — a direct precedent for Q17's "multiple independent judges" control, with a concrete quantified floor to calibrate expectations against.
- **The proxy chain degrades further at the next link — Cognition/Decision (model-based) vs Execution (real robot), §5.4, verbatim:** *"Cognition VS Execution: The SRCC of VLM results with the real world is less than 0.5... Decision VS Execution: The SRCC of VLA results with the real world exceeds 0.6, indicating that Decision can represent Execution to some extent. However, this correlation is still not high enough."* So there are **two compounding gaps**, not one: (a) IQA-metric-predicting-Decision-score SRCC drops from ~0.9 (human) to ~0.5–0.65 (embodied); (b) Decision-score-predicting-real-Execution SRCC caps at ~0.6, Cognition-score-predicting-Execution caps at <0.5. A metric that looked "good" only by the first-link standard would still be two steps removed from real task success.
- **Cross-database generalization (§5.3, Table 8(a), verbatim):** *"IQA methods fine-tuned on Embodied AI data lose certain human-oriented evaluation capabilities, where the SRCC is even lower than 0.4 in the LIVE database."* Fine-tuning toward the embodied construct actively destroys the human-oriented one — a bidirectional divergence, not just "embodied is harder." Partial positive: *"the IQA model trained with VLA annotations can also predict VLM scores, and the SRCC of AHIQ can reach 0.7"* — Decision-trained models transfer reasonably to Cognition, suggesting Cognition and Decision share more construct overlap with each other than either shares with human-oriented quality.
- **Distortion severity should be defined by task-model JND, not uniform steps (§4 "Sensitivity," Figure 7, verbatim):** *"Dis16 (Gaussian denoise) will cause serious distortion at level 1, and Dis25 (Block interpolation) has no significant effect on VLM/VLA even at level 5... Dis02 (Lens blur) mainly affect[s] VLM, or Dis15 (Multiplicative noise) mainly affect[s] VLA."* The paper bins severity into Mild/Medium/Severe (one-third of samples each) using the **downstream models' own score distribution**, not a physically-uniform corruption magnitude — this is a directly reusable methodological pattern for our Q9 (severity should be set by measured effect on the actual task signal, not by a naive equal-mm/equal-dropout-rate ladder).
- **Dataset/annotation scale (Table 1, verbatim row):** Embodied-IQA: 1.23k reference, 36.9k distorted, 30 distortion types × 5 levels, 5.53m annotations, "3+3+1" dimension split (3 Cognition + 3 Decision + 1 Execution), "Machine (Robot)" subject type — contrasted against LIVE (29/779/25k, Human General), TID2013 (25/3k/514k, Human General), MPD (1k/30k/2.25m, Machine General, 5 dims), EPD (100/2.5k/30k, Machine General, 2 dims). Embodied-IQA is the first in this table with all three of Cognition/Decision/Execution annotated.

## Load-bearing assumptions

- **The "ground truth" for Cognition/Decision is self-referential model consistency, not correctness.** A distorted image scores well if the *same* downstream model gives the *same* answer as on the clean reference — regardless of whether that answer was ever correct in the first place. Only Execution (§3.5, ~1,500 samples) is grounded to physical task success. This means the paper's main experiment (Table 2, the "<0.65/<0.6 SRCC" result) is measuring **"can an IQA metric predict how much 15 black-box models' behavior drifts under corruption,"** not **"can an IQA metric predict whether the robot succeeds."** The Execution-linked correlation (§5.4, <0.5 / ~0.6) is the only place true task-outcome grounding is checked, and it is checked on a much smaller, single-difficulty-level slice.
- **"Subjective MOS" terminology is inherited from human IQA but nothing here is human-labelled** — worth flagging explicitly since the paper reuses IQA vocabulary (§5.1: "consistency between the objective quality score and the subjective MOS") for what are actually 15-model-ensemble machine annotations. Anyone citing this paper's SRCC numbers as "vs. subjective ground truth" in the traditional IQA sense would be overclaiming; it is vs. an ensemble-of-black-boxes proxy, itself only ~0.25–0.31 self-consistent (Figure 3/4 numbers above).
- **Single-image, single-shot construct.** Every unit of evaluation is one image (reference or its one distorted twin) fed once to a task model; there is no multi-view, no sequential viewpoint selection, no notion of "did the agent choose to look here." Distortion is injected globally on one frame, not as a viewpoint/pose choice. The pipeline's Decision/Execution stages are about **manipulation pose accuracy given a fixed image**, not about **which image to acquire next** — the paper never asks a "which view" question at all.
- **Object set has no primitive-shape structure and no 3D geometry pipeline whatsoever.** Reference images are curated from 7 existing robotics RGB(-D perspective) databases with Sim2Real and first-/third-person stratification (§3.1) — no point clouds, no meshes, no CAD, no primitive fitting. There is nothing here analogous to a survey cloud, a fitted normal, or a coverage metric.
- **Severity/JND analysis (Figure 7) is retrospective, not prospective.** Mild/Medium/Severe bins are defined *after* collecting Cognition/Decision scores, by tertile-splitting the resulting score distribution — this is a valid way to characterize sensitivity post hoc, but it is not a protocol for *choosing* injection magnitudes in advance (which is closer to what our Q9 needs for a benchmark design decided before running trials).

## What transfers to our setting

- **The core citable number for Q3/Q19: a plausible, well-established proxy (human-oriented IQA, ~0.9 SRCC on its native task) degrades to <0.6–0.65 SRCC on a superficially adjacent but actually distinct construct (embodied-task consistency), and the drop is worse the further downstream the true target is (Cognition <0.5, vs. real Execution) (§5.2, §5.4, both quoted above).** This is exactly the shape of experiment our anti-circularity check (Q3) and quality-vs-outcome separation (Q19) need: pick a plausible proxy that shares surface features with the true construct, hold out the true downstream outcome, and report the SRCC gap explicitly rather than assuming a good-looking proxy transfers. For us: a view-quality judge that looks orthogonal/well-framed by human-photographic standards is the structural analogue of "human-oriented IQA, ~0.9 on its native task" — this paper is direct evidence that such a judge should be *expected* to underperform on the actual downstream construct (defect detectability / inspection answer correctness) unless that gap is measured, not assumed away.
- **Two-link proxy chains compound, and each link's degradation should be reported separately (§5.2 + §5.4 together).** Directly informs Q19's request to report both view-quality and inspection-outcome without one contaminating the other: this paper's design (report IQA-vs-Decision-score AND Decision-score-vs-real-Execution as two separate correlations, never fusing them into one number) is a clean template — our benchmark should likewise report (a) RQ1-judge-score vs. some independent geometric/held-out proxy, and (b) that proxy vs. actual inspection/defect-detection outcome, as two distinct, separately-interpretable numbers.
- **Ensemble-of-judges inter-agreement as a calibration floor for Q17.** 15 diverse ≤8B VLMs/VLAs average only ~0.25–0.31 pairwise SRCC with each other (Figure 3/4, §4) even on a well-defined, narrow task (10-word task description / 7-DoF pose). This sets a realistic expectation for how much agreement to demand from a multi-judge or judge-vs-human-slice control in our RQ1 design (Q17) — near-perfect agreement should not be the bar; the paper's own conclusion ("collect their general preference," §4) argues for ensembling/aggregating multiple judges rather than trusting any single one, directly actionable for our judge design.
- **Severity calibrated by measured effect on the downstream signal, not by naive uniform physical steps (Figure 7, JND framing, §4 Sensitivity).** Directly reusable for Q9: rather than picking pose-error/dropout/offset magnitudes on a linear physical scale, define "mild/medium/severe" by tertiles of their actual measured effect on view-selection or defect-visibility outcomes, the same way this paper defines JND-based severity from the Cognition/Decision score distribution rather than from the corruption parameter itself.
- **Architectural priors tuned for the wrong construct can make a metric worse than a naive zero-shot baseline post-fine-tuning (LPIPS/DISTS/CLIPIQA underperforming Q-Align zero-shot, §5.2).** A caution for RQ1: if our view-quality judge reuses a backbone whose inductive biases were tuned for human-photographic aesthetics (sharpness, centering, exposure) rather than for orthogonality/coverage, fine-tuning it on our task may not fully overcome that prior — worth testing a from-scratch or geometry-only baseline (no human-vision-pretrained backbone) alongside any adapted human-IQA metric, mirroring this paper's zero-shot-vs-fine-tuned comparison.

## What does NOT transfer, and why

- **No view planning, no NBV, no multi-view coverage, no point cloud/mesh geometry anywhere in the paper — checked explicitly across §3–5 and skimmed Appendices B–H.** This paper is entirely orthogonal in *content* to Q1, Q2, Q4–Q16, Q18, Q20–Q22: no coverage/reconstruction-error metric, no dataset of 3D primitives, no sensor-defect injection in 3D (pose error/dropout/fused-vs-single-view offset — their "distortions" are 2D image corruption: blur/noise/compression/brightness/color, a categorically different defect space than ours), no baseline viewsphere of any kind, no view budget or stopping-condition treatment, no symmetry/view-equivalence handling. Do not cite this paper for anything about geometry, coverage, or view selection — it has none.
- **The "unit of evaluation" here (single static image, scored by aggregate divergence of 15 downstream models across 5 unrelated manipulation tasks) is a genuinely different construct from either option Q1 poses.** It is neither "(object, surface region) pair scored by evidence-view quality" nor "(object, question) episode scored end-to-end" — it is closer to "(image, corruption) scored by behavioral drift across many unrelated tasks." What this rejected/different design would have measured, if adopted wholesale for us: whether a *single frame* supports correct downstream behavior across a battery of generic tasks, with no view-selection or orthogonality construct at all — useful as a sensor-robustness check, not as a substitute for either of Q1's actual options.
- **No analytical/geometric ground truth of any kind — GT here is behavioral self-consistency (Cognition/Decision) or real-robot success (Execution), never a closed-form geometric answer.** Directly does not answer Q2 (does an analytical "best view" survive contact with a survey cloud) — this paper has no analogue to a fitted primitive or a face normal; its closest structural cousin is the "metric-based" GT strategy in Q2's own list (score derived from measurable downstream behavior rather than geometry or human label), but even that comparison is loose since the "metric" here is itself another opaque model's output, not a designed measurement.
- **No handling of judge/metric provenance as a circularity risk in the way Q3 frames it.** The paper's IQA metrics (Table 2) and its ground-truth generators (15 VLMs/VLAs) are *disjoint* by construction — the metrics under test never generated their own ground truth — so this paper never actually confronts the "judge scores views generated with knowledge of the judge" scenario Q3 is worried about. It demonstrates the *consequence* of proxy-target divergence (useful for Q3's argument) but does not test or discuss circularity/self-preference itself — that is the self-preference-bias-judge.md paper's job, not this one's. Don't conflate the two: this paper shows "plausible proxy is empirically wrong," not "judge favors familiar/self-like outputs."
- **Statistical treatment does not transfer to our small-N regime (Q12).** Correlations here are computed over tens of thousands of image pairs (29,520/7,380 train/val) and 10 repeated train/test splits with mean reported (§5.1) — no confidence intervals or variance across repeats are reported in the extracted text (checked §5.1–5.2; only point-estimate SRCC/KRCC/PLCC appear in Table 2). Our benchmark, with "few objects and few trials" per Q12, cannot lean on this paper's implicit "large N makes point estimates trustworthy" approach and would need the binomial-CI/median-variance treatment Q12 already specifies instead.
- **Distortions are entirely 2D/photometric, not 3D/geometric — does not inform Q9's actual defect list.** Blur, luminance, chrominance, noise, compression, spatial (7 categories, 30 types) have no correspondence to our stated defects (pose error of a few mm, per-view depth dropout, ~4cm fused-vs-single-view offset, Q10). The *methodology* of severity calibration transfers (see above); the specific defect taxonomy does not.

## Answering the specific "anti-circularity" angle (Q3/Q19) directly

This paper is the branch's designated **empirical-divergence reference**, distinct from the self-preference-bias-judge.md paper's **mechanism reference**. Together they answer different halves of Q3: self-preference-bias-judge.md explains *why* a judge and a metric sharing assumptions will spuriously agree (familiarity/perplexity, not authorship); this paper demonstrates *how large the resulting error can be* when a proxy that looks right by its own native standard (SRCC ≈0.9 on human-oriented IQA) is applied to a superficially similar but actually distinct target (SRCC <0.6–0.65 on embodied-task consistency, dropping further to <0.5/~0.6 against real robot execution). For our benchmark, the actionable takeaway is procedural, not just cautionary: **run this exact experiment shape ourselves** — take whatever view-quality judge RQ1 produces, correlate it against a held-out, differently-sourced outcome (geometric GT per Q2, or downstream defect-detection success per Q4's third exit), and report the SRCC gap explicitly as a benchmark result, the same way this paper reports Table 2 and §5.4 as its two headline findings rather than asserting transferability. A judge that has not been checked this way should be assumed guilty of the same gap this paper measured, by default.
