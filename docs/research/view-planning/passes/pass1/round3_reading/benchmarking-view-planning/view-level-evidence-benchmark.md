- **Authors**: Yimu Wang, Yee Man Choi (equal contribution), Barry Zhang, Mozhgan Nasr Azadani, Sean Sedwards, Krzysztof Czarnecki — University of Waterloo
- **Source used**: full HTML (`arxiv.org/html/2606.09644`), fetched and parsed directly (converted with pandoc; all prose, tables, and equations captured — no abstract-only reading). Section/table/equation numbers below are as in the paper.

## Zeroth thing to get right: this is NOT a robotic view-planning paper

The title mentions "view" and the branch is about NBV/view-planning benchmarks, but **this paper's "view selection" is selection among a fixed set of already-captured, synchronized camera images** — six NuScenes surround-view channels (`CamFront`, `CamFrontLeft`, `CamFrontRight`, `CamBack`, `CamBackLeft`, `CamBackRight`) at one timestamp — not planning where to move a camera to acquire new evidence. There is no action space, no camera pose, no IK, no budget, no stopping rule, no coverage, no reconstruction. "Golden view" here means *which existing camera's field of view contains the pixels that answer the question*, decided from **3D actor-visibility reprojection**, not from any geometric "best angle" criterion.

Why it was still selected for this branch: the **evaluation protocol** — exact-match view-ID scoring against a single golden label, an oracle-given-correct-source condition, a joint condition, and a view×answer 2×2 outcome grid — is a clean, reusable pattern for separating "did the system look at the right evidence" from "did it produce a correct outcome." That protocol transfers; the task semantics (visibility-based golden label, no geometry) do not.

## Core method (§3, Appendix B.1–B.3)

**Task formalization** (Eq. 3–5, §B.1). Given six synchronized images `{I₁,…,I₆}` and question `q`, three prediction functions are evaluated:

- `f_view(I₁,…,I₆,q) → v̂` — view selection only.
- `f_oracle(I_{v*}, q) → â` — answer given only the ground-truth golden-view image (isolates reasoning from source localization).
- `f_joint(I₁,…,I₆,q) → (v̂, â)` — select view and answer in one pass (JSON output with `Golden_view`, `Answer`, `Rationale`).

**Metrics** (Eq. 1/2, §3.2, restated in §B.3 Eq. 6–8 — branch-1 territory, not reused here except to note the structure):
- `Acc_view` = exact-match fraction against the single golden view label `v*` (candidate set 𝒞 = 6 named channels + `None`).
- For MC answers: exact identifier match. For free-form: a separate fixed LLM judge scores semantic equivalence against the reference answer (§C.4) — judge receives `q`, gold answer, model's free-form answer, optional rationale, and (for joint-setting outputs) the *predicted* view's image, or (for oracle-setting outputs) the *gold* view's image.
- `Acc_joint` = strict joint success: **both** `v̂ = v*` **and** `â = a*` must hold (Eq. 2/8). This is the number that gets deflated by shortcut answers.
- `None` is the correct label on unsupported examples (5 of the 122 QA pairs are deliberately unanswerable, to test abstention); selecting `None` on an answerable example is scored wrong.

**Ground truth construction (§3.1, §3.3, §B.4.1–B.4.3, Fig. 2).** A two-stage pipeline:
1. *Automatic proposal*: mine "conflict-centric" events (pedestrian crossing, braking, cut-in, lane change, left/right turn, Car-to-Car Front Turn-Across-Path) from a pre-populated SQLite cache of per-frame kinematic/geometric/HD-map features via SQL queries + geometric post-filters (e.g., cut-in defined by lateral-offset trajectory shape, turn direction by yaw-rate + route shape). For the actor involved, project its 3D bounding box into all six camera image planes per frame; a camera "contains" the actor if the projection overlaps the image rectangle. This produces the *candidate* golden view(s).
2. *Manual verification*: annotators (paid graduate students, trained on written guidelines) confirm (i) the question is answerable, (ii) the proposed golden view actually contains the critical evidence, (iii) **no alternative view offers equally direct evidence**. If no single view dominates, **the sample is excluded from the dataset** — not resolved as a set/equivalence class.

So GT provenance is **reprojection-geometry-proposed, human-verified, single-label-enforced-by-exclusion** — closest to Q2's "human labels" bucket, not an analytical optimum (there is no formula for "best" camera; visibility is necessary but not sufficient, humans break ties/adjudicate ambiguity, and ambiguous cases are thrown out rather than resolved).

## Concrete results (§4.3, Table 2/3; full numbers Table 6–9)

**Headline view-selection accuracy** (Table 2, mean over 5 runs, with SD/95% CI):
| Model | Mean view Acc. (%) | SD | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|
| Claude | 82.62 | 0.69 | [81.77, 83.47] |
| GPT-5.4 | 77.54 | 0.45 | [76.98, 78.10] |
| Gemini | 74.10 | 1.89 | [71.76, 76.44] |
| Qwen3VL-8B | 61.64 | 2.42 | [58.64, 64.64] |
| InternVL3 | 61.48 | 2.09 | [58.88, 64.07] |
| Qwen2.5VL-7B | 12.62 | 1.24 | [11.08, 14.17] — "near or below a naive uniform baseline over the candidate labels" |

**Oracle → Joint drop** (Table 3, MC): Claude 89.3→73.2, GPT-5.4 86.9→66.9, Gemini 89.3→68.8. Because oracle QA is given the *correct* view, this ~15–20pp drop when the model must also find the view itself isolates **view identification as the dominant bottleneck**, not answer reasoning. Free-form is consistently below MC in both oracle and joint settings for every model (§4.3).

**The load-bearing number for Q1/Q15 — shortcut answers inflate answer-only accuracy.** Table 8 (view × MC outcome grid, N=122 per model, all 6 models pooled = 732 trials):
- All models pooled: 409/732 (55.9%) grounded success (view✓/answer✓), 49/732 (6.7%) right-view-wrong-answer, **192/732 (26.2%) wrong-view/right-answer** ("shortcut or ungrounded answer" per the paper's own Table 5 taxonomy), 82/732 (11.2%) total failure.
- Worst case, **Qwen2.5VL-7B**: G✓/MC✓=10, G✓/MC×=3, G×/MC✓=86, G×/MC×=23 (row sums to 122). I recomputed from these raw counts: **raw answer-only accuracy = (10+86)/122 = 78.7%**, but **grounded (strict joint) accuracy = 10/122 = 8.2%**. That is a **~70 percentage-point gap between "the answer was right" and "the answer was right because the model looked at the right evidence."** (Table 3's reported Joint-MC=8.4% for this model is consistent, small discrepancy likely from run-averaging vs single-table counts.) This is the single most quotable number for why our benchmark cannot score end-to-end inspection accuracy alone (Q1, Q15).
- Free-form version (Table 9) shows the same pattern less extremely: 309/732 grounded, 149/732 right-view/wrong-answer, 116/732 wrong-view/right-answer, 158/732 total failure — free generation "exposes both evidence misattribution and downstream reasoning failures" (§B.5 Analysis) rather than hiding them behind option guessing.

**Per-region difficulty** (Table 6 — labeled "per-region"; front/side/rear/none-insufficient columns, View/MC/Free sub-columns): side views are hardest for every model due to "adjacent-camera ambiguity and partial actor visibility" (§B.5). E.g. Claude: Front-view-Acc 96.59%, Side-view-Acc 46.15%, Rear-view-Acc 100.00%, None/insufficient-view-Acc 16.67%. Gemini/GPT-5.4 show the same front≫side pattern (87.5/53.8 and 88.6/53.8 respectively). **None/insufficient-evidence recall is uniformly bad** — "even strong proprietary models rarely abstain correctly" (§B.5) — best case Gemini/GPT-5.4/Qwen2.5-VL at 16.67–33.33% MC accuracy on the abstention slice, i.e., they mostly hallucinate a plausible-looking camera instead of saying "none of these views answer this."

**Per-question-type** (Table 7): view accuracy drops specifically on intent-prediction questions for several models (e.g., Claude 88.46 causality → 65.71 intent-prediction view-Acc) even while oracle-MC answer accuracy stays comparatively high — forecasting-style questions need identifying *subtle* interaction evidence, not just a salient object.

## Named failure modes (§4.4) — a small, explicit taxonomy

1. Correct answer / wrong view = shortcut reasoning or evidence misattribution (quantified above).
2. **Front-camera bias**: models over-select `CAM_FRONT` even when evidence is in a side/rear channel.
3. **Adjacent-view confusion**: `CAM_FRONT` vs `CAM_FRONT_LEFT` when evidence spans a boundary region.
4. **Failure to abstain**: on unsupported (`None`) examples, models pick a visually plausible but non-evidential view rather than saying "insufficient evidence."
5. Free-form answers that are "plausible but weakly grounded," especially on counterfactual/intent questions.

## Statistical treatment (§B.2, Table 2)

122 QA pairs total (52 causality / 35 counterfactual / 35 intent), from 73 scenes — genuinely small-N, much closer to our scale than most benchmarks in this branch. For view selection they report **repeated-run statistics: mean, SD, 95% CI over 5 runs** (Table 2) — the only place variance is quantified; no CI/variance is given for oracle/joint numbers in the main tables (Table 3), and no significance testing between models is reported anywhere.

## What transfers to our setting

- **Q1 / Q15 (unit of evaluation, grounded vs lucky success)** — the strongest transfer. This paper is a working, quantified existence proof that scoring only the end outcome (here: answer correctness) systematically **overstates** system quality when the evidence-selection step is wrong but the outcome happens to be right — Qwen2.5-VL's 78.7% raw-answer accuracy collapsing to 8.2% grounded accuracy is the concrete number to cite. Directly actionable: adopt the **view-correct × outcome-correct 2×2 grid** (their Table 5: grounded success / right-source-failed-outcome / shortcut / full failure) as our own reporting unit whenever we report an end-to-end inspection metric. Any (object, question)-style end-to-end number we report should ship with this breakdown or it is not trustworthy on its own — exactly the branch's stated worry.
- **Q3 (avoiding circularity with the judge)** — clean separation precedent: **view-correctness GT comes from 3D reprojection geometry + human verification (no model in the loop)**; the **LLM judge is scoped only to free-form answer semantic-equivalence**, never to view correctness. This is a genuine construct separation (geometry/human GT for "is this the right evidence" vs. model-judge only for "is this text answer equivalent to the reference") — directly matches the separation Q3 asks us to defend (GT from geometry, judge from image/text), and shows it's workable in practice: MC view-selection scoring never touches a judge at all. **Caveat verified by direct re-read (§3.2, §C.4): the judge model's identity is never named anywhere in the main text or the judge-prompt appendix** — only "an LLM judge with a semantic-equivalence rubric" is stated. Since Claude is one of the six evaluated systems, this is an unquantified, undisclosed self-preference risk (cf. sibling note `self-preference-bias-judge.md`) sitting inside an otherwise clean construct-separation design: the separation Q3 wants (GT-from-geometry vs. judge-from-text) holds for *view* scoring, but the *free-form answer* score's provenance is opaque, so it cannot be claimed as a fully verified separation — only the view-accuracy numbers are judge-free.
- **Q17 (judge controls) — mostly a documented gap, useful as a checklist of what's missing.** The paper uses a single fixed LLM judge, no stated multiple-judge agreement, no position-swapping, no anonymization procedure, and no held-out human-labelled slice checked against judge divergence. The only control mentioned is "keep prompting and judge criteria fixed across models" (consistency, not agreement/bias control). Treat as a negative precedent: if we cite this paper's judge design, flag that it does not implement the Q17 controls — we would need to add them, not copy them.
- **Q16 (named VLM spatial failure-mode test cases)** — reusable taxonomy items with real numbers behind them: front-camera / dominant-view bias, adjacent-region (boundary) confusion, and abstention failure on ambiguous/no-good-evidence cases. The abstention failure mode is a genuinely useful addition beyond the branch's listed set (composition, distance, allocentric, wrong-face, left/right, count) — worth adding "fails to say 'no good view exists'" as its own named test case, directly relevant to our Q11 near-primitive/ambiguous-fit scenario (does the agent correctly signal "fall back to uniform sphere" rather than confidently picking a wrong orthogonal view?).
- **Q19 (report both view quality and outcome without contaminating either)** — the paper's answer is structural, not statistical: report `Acc_view`, `Acc_oracle` (outcome given correct view), and `Acc_joint` (strict, both-correct) as three separate numbers, plus the full 2×2 count table. No blending into one score. Directly reusable as our own three-number-plus-grid reporting template for "was the evidence view good" vs "was the inspection conclusion correct."
- **Q14 (isolating one component's quality by holding the other at oracle)** — the Oracle-QA setting (supply the ground-truth view, measure only downstream reasoning) is the same "hold one stage fixed at ground truth, measure the other" mechanic Q14 asks for, just applied in the opposite direction (they fix view→measure answering; we'd fix action-space/selector→measure the VLM, or vice versa). Structural precedent, not a ready-made oracle-selector ablation for action-space comparison.
- **Q12 (statistics at small N)** — a genuinely small-scale precedent (122 items, categories down to 35): report mean/SD/95% CI over repeated runs (N=5) rather than relying on large-N point estimates. Much closer to our few-objects/few-trials situation than ObjView-Bench's 5,691-object scale (see sibling note `objview-bench.md`).
- **Q13 (negative results/limitations as first-class)** — §6 Limitations is a clean model for honest scoping: explicitly states the benchmark is compact/diagnostic not leaderboard-scale, that the single-golden-view design can't handle real cases needing multi-view evidence, that free-form scoring inherits judge noise, and that NuScenes-derived pretraining contamination could bias absolute numbers — concluding it should be read as "a controlled diagnostic probe rather than evidence of deployment readiness." Good template for stating our own benchmark's honest limits rather than overclaiming.
- **Deliberate no-train-split design**: dataset is test-only "to reduce annotation leakage into evaluation... rather than optimize a model specifically for this label space" (§3.3) — worth considering if we ever face pressure to build a training split from our benchmark objects.

## What does NOT transfer, and why

- **Q2 (analytical GT normal survives a defective survey cloud?)** — no transfer at all. There is no analytical "best view" concept here; GT is visibility-based (does the actor's 3D bbox reproject into this camera's frustum) plus human adjudication of "most direct evidence," not a geometric optimum like a face-normal-through-centroid. Nothing about angular error, primitive fitting, or cloud defects is discussed or discussable in this paper's framework.
- **Q4 / Q9 / Q10 (simulation, sensor defects, fused-vs-single-view offset)** — zero transfer. Images are real, calibrated, production NuScenes camera captures; there is no injected pose error, dropout, or fusion offset, and no simulation layer at all. Cannot be cited as evidence for "does ranking change under defects."
- **Q5 / Q6 (baselines, view budget, stopping)** — not applicable. There is no action space to plan over and no multi-step acquisition process; "view selection" is a one-shot 7-way classification (6 channels + None) over already-captured images, not a sequential planning-and-stopping problem. No view-budget regimes, no baselines to compare against beyond MLLMs themselves (no random/uniform/greedy/oracle-geometric baseline family at all).
- **Q7 (orthogonality-as-score metric family)** — none. "Golden" here means content-visible, not geometrically well-posed (no notion of viewing angle, surface normal, or framing quality anywhere in the metric definitions).
- **Q8 (view equivalence under symmetry)** — the paper's design actively avoids this problem rather than solving it: annotators are instructed to **exclude** any sample where "no single view is dominant" (§3.1, §B.4.3) — i.e., cases where the correct answer is genuinely a *set* of equally-valid views get thrown out of the dataset, not handled with an equivalence relation. §6 Limitations explicitly flags this: "Each question currently has a single golden view, although real scenarios may require multi-view evidence." This is a documented negative precedent directly relevant to our box/cylinder symmetry problem (Q8) — exact-match-against-a-single-label breaks down exactly where we most need it to work (primitives with rotational/reflective symmetry), and this paper's fix was to define the problem away by filtering, which is not available to us since symmetric primitives are the common case, not a rare edge case to exclude.
- **Q11 (primitive object-set stratification)** — not applicable; objects are NuScenes driving-scene actors (vehicles/pedestrians), no primitive-shape framing, no near-primitive-ambiguity concept.
- **Q18 / Q20 / Q21 / Q22** — no relevant content (intake-format design, release strategy, and metrology-standard questions are outside anything this paper discusses).

## Relevance summary

High relevance for the *evaluation-protocol skeleton* (Q1, Q15, Q19: the view×outcome grid and the three-number reporting template are directly adoptable, with a strong quantified argument — the 78.7%→8.2% Qwen2.5-VL gap — for why end-to-end-only scoring is unsafe) and for a small, named VLM spatial-failure-mode taxonomy with real numbers (Q16, plus a useful abstention-failure addition relevant to Q11). Near-zero relevance for anything geometric (Q2, Q4, Q7, Q9, Q10) since the paper has no notion of viewing angle, primitive shapes, point clouds, or sensor defects — its "view" is a discrete pre-existing camera channel selected for content visibility, not a pose chosen from an action space for geometric quality. Q8 gets a genuine negative-precedent contribution: this paper's single-dominant-view-only design, enforced by excluding ambiguous samples, is a documented example of the exact failure mode our symmetric-primitive benchmark cannot afford to inherit.
