## Core method/idea (from the verified abstract — CVGIP 1994, confirmed via IBM Research + ResearchGate + Unpaywall/Crossref metadata, consistent wording across all four)

> "In this paper, the authors analytically characterize the domain of admissible camera locations, orientations, and optical settings for which features of interest in a scene are in focus, inside the field-of-view, and magnified to a certain specification. A general 3D viewing geometry is considered and the camera lens is modeled by a general thick lens model. The analytical relationships describing the complete viewpoint loci that satisfy the above optical feature detectability constraints of resolution, focus, and field-of-view are obtained. These analytical relationships are used as sensor placement constraints in the MVP model-based vision sensor planning system."

Structurally (inferred from this abstract plus consistent secondary characterization, e.g. the arXiv 2306.06969 related-work section describing this exact paper as one of "the first seminal studies that considered the characterization of a continuous solution space in ℝ³... synthesiz[ing] analytical relationships to characterize a handful of constraints geometrically: resolution, focus, field of view, visibility, view angle, occluding regions"):

- Three constraints get **closed-form solved as regions of viewpoint space** (not scored, not sampled — solved): **resolution** (feature must be magnified enough on the sensor for detection), **focus** (feature must fall within depth of field of a general thick-lens model), **field of view** (feature must project inside the sensor's angular extent).
- Each constraint's satisfying set is a closed-form 3D region ("locus") in camera-pose space. The MVP system (companion papers) intersects these loci (plus a separately-solved visibility/occlusion locus, from a different paper — see below) to find admissible viewpoints.
- **Visibility/occlusion is explicitly NOT in this paper's scope** — it's the subject of a separate, later paper: Tarabanis, Tsai & Kaul (1996), *"Computing Occlusion-Free Viewpoints,"* IEEE PAMI 18(3), 279–292, which builds occlusion-free viewpoint loci via boundary representations / CSG over polyhedral occluders. This 1994 paper is specifically the resolution+focus+FOV triad, per its own title.
- **"View angle" / incidence angle**: per the same secondary characterization above, Tarabanis's constraint family lists "view angle" alongside resolution/focus/FOV/visibility/occluding-regions — but it is unclear from any source I could access whether the 1994 CVGIP paper treats incidence angle as its own standalone closed-form constraint, or folds it into the resolution constraint (foreshortening reduces a feature's projected/magnified size, which is exactly what the resolution constraint bounds). **This is an open question I cannot resolve without the actual text** — flagged rather than guessed at.

## Proxy source used for the mathematical machinery (NOT the assigned paper — flagged explicitly)

Magaña, Dirr, Bauer & Reinhart (2023), *"Viewpoint Generation using Feature-Based Constrained Spaces for Robot Vision Systems,"* arXiv:2306.06969 (CC BY 4.0, freely accessible, fetched in full). This paper explicitly positions itself as extending the Tarabanis-era "generalized viewpoint" tradition (citing Tarabanis et al. as introducing "the characterization of a continuous solution space in ℝ³" for resolution/focus/FOV/visibility/view-angle/occluding-regions) into a modern SE(3) formulation with 9 named constraint spaces ("𝒞-spaces"). Content extracted, all attributable to **this 2023 paper, not to Tarabanis 1994**:

- **Incidence-angle constraint** (their Eq. 5, their own re-derivation, cites Tarabanis-tradition precedent): `φ_sf = arccos( n_f · e_sz / (|n_f| |e_sz|) )` — the angle between the feature's surface normal `n_f` and the sensor's optical axis `e_sz`. Constraint: `φ_sf ≤ φ_max_sf`.
- **Incidence-angle threshold values they cite (from other, cited sensor-specific sources, not their own measurement)**: **60°** as a commonly-used general maximum incidence angle [attributed in their text to Biagio 2017]; **45°** as the typical limit specifically for **structured-light sensors** [attributed to Bertagnolli 2006]; **30°–50°** as a tilt-angle range from a separate source [Raffaeli 2013]. **This is the closest thing this reading pass surfaced to a citable "knee near ~60°" for RQ2 (angle penalty: soft weight vs. hard threshold)** — treated by this literature as a **hard feasibility cutoff** (manufacturer-specified max, pass/fail), not a smooth weight, and it is **sensor-type-dependent**: structured-light/projector-based sensors (which the D405 fundamentally is, active-stereo-with-projector) get a *tighter* bound (45°) than generic optical sensors (60°). This is a secondhand citation chain (2023 paper → 2017/2006/2013 sources I have not independently verified) and is about **structured-light signal return geometry, not measured depth-error curves** — it does not answer the branch's "sigma(range, theta) for the D405" question, which needs actual metrology/photogrammetry literature, not this sensor-planning lineage.
- **Frustum/FOV constraint** (their Eq. 4): pose lies in `SE(3)` such that `(d_s, h_near, h_far, θ_x, ψ_y) ∈ I_s` — depth-of-field range and horizontal/vertical FOV angles jointly bound admissible standoff and offset. Concrete numeric example they give for a **ZEISS Comet Pro AE** structured-light scanner: FOV angles θ_x=51.5°, ψ_y=35.5°; working volume 396×266 mm² at 400 mm standoff, 588×392 mm² at 600 mm standoff — i.e., a real, if unrelated-sensor, standoff-vs-coverage-area worked example.
- **C-space intersection formulation** (their Eq. 10–12): overall admissible pose set is the intersection `𝒞 = ⋂ᵢ 𝒞ᵢ(cᵢ)` over all active per-constraint C-spaces — same "solved-region intersection" logic the original Tarabanis MVP system used, just reformulated in SE(3) instead of the original's presumably lower-dimensional parameterization.
- **Reported performance**: average per-C-space characterization time 4.1 ms (30 repetitions) on a 2014-era laptop CPU (i7-4810MQ) — i.e., closed-form region computation is cheap, consistent with the whole point of solving loci analytically instead of sampling.
- **Their own novelty claim relative to the Tarabanis-era baseline**: continuous SE(3) treatment (vs. Tarabanis's presumably more restricted parameterization), 9 named constraints in one consistent framework (vs. "a handful"), and explicit kinematic-error / robot-workspace / multi-sensor constraints that the 1994-era papers did not need to model (robots and calibration were less of a concern in a controlled lab rig).

## A second freely-accessible secondary source, same tradition

Roberts & Marshall (year not confirmed, hosted at Edinburgh CVonline, a long-standing open academic mirror), *"Viewpoint Planning for Complete Three Dimensional Object Surface Coverage with a Variety of Vision System Configurations"* — a survey/review in the same lineage, fetched directly (freely accessible, no paywall). Useful, again explicitly **not** the assigned paper:

- **Depth-of-field constraint, thin-lens form**: `D = f·D_max(a+c) / (af + c·D_max)` and `D = f·D_min(a+c) / (af − c·D_min)`, where `D` = focus distance, `f` = focal length, `a` = aperture diameter, `c` = permissible circle-of-confusion diameter. (This is the standard photographic DOF formula — the Tarabanis 1994 paper's stated contribution over documents like this one is doing the equivalent derivation with a *general thick-lens* model instead of this simplified thin-lens form, per its abstract — I cannot confirm how much the results differ without the source text.)
- **Field-of-view / stereo FOV**: modeled as a right circular cone per camera; stereo FOV = intersection of both cameras' cones. Explicit focal-length-from-target-size formula given: `f_i = targetsize / (2·tan(asin(r·sin(φ_i)/h)))`.
- **Best-view / view-angle definition, stated in plain language**: *"The best view of an object face is obtained when it is viewed head on. This occurs when the viewing direction is the inverse surface normal of the face."* For occluded/oblique cases: *"the best view is the direction that lies within the face's visibility region and has the smallest angular offset from its inverse surface normal."* — **This is a direct, citable, formally-stated answer to the branch's "formal definitions of best view within a neighbourhood" question** (RQ: "argmax over the visibility-constrained region of −|angular offset from inverse normal|" is exactly the "analytical: face normal through region centroid" family the branch brief already names) — though again, sourced from this survey, not from Tarabanis 1994 directly.
- **Resolution**: only briefly noted (minimum camera-pixel dimension folded into DOF calc) — no separate closed form given in what was extracted.

## Load-bearing assumptions (of the Tarabanis-era tradition generally, inferred across all sources above)

- **Known, accurate CAD/geometric model of the scene and object.** This entire tradition is explicitly "model-based" (in the taxonomy sense the sibling branch document, `nbv-taxonomy-survey.md`, already established) — the feature geometry, its normal, and occluder geometry are assumed exactly known in advance, not reconstructed from noisy sensor data. **This does not transfer cleanly to our setting**: our "model" is a fused survey PLY with mm-level pose error, dropout holes, and a measured ~4 cm fused-vs-single-view offset — none of these papers model input-geometry uncertainty propagating into the constraint loci.
- **Single rigid feature at a time**, not a whole object or scene — the loci are computed per named feature (a hole, an edge, a planar patch), then intersected across a feature set for one viewpoint (multi-feature constraint in the 2023 paper's terms). Scaling this to "score every point on a fused PLY" is a nontrivial extension not covered by this literature as accessed.
- **Sensor optics assumed exactly characterized** (thick-lens parameters, aperture, circle of confusion) — reasonable for a fixed lab camera rig with known optics, and roughly reasonable for our fixed D405, but these constraints (resolution/focus/FOV) say nothing about **depth-sensing error**, which is what several branch RQs (D405 sigma(range,theta), specular reflection, defect detectability vs angle) actually need. **This is the paper's most consequential mismatch to the branch's needs**: it is entirely about whether a 2D imaging feature is *legible* (magnified, focused, framed) — not about whether an *active-stereo depth measurement* at that pixel is *accurate*. Those are different physics (geometric optics of image formation vs. structured-light/stereo triangulation error), and this document (even if I could read it) would not answer the depth-accuracy-vs-incidence-angle questions this branch most needs.

## What transfers to our setting

- **Vocabulary and framing**: "solve viewpoint constraints as closed-form regions of pose space, then intersect" is a clean, classical alternative framing to today's sampling/scoring NBV approaches — directly relevant to the branch's RQ about whether view quality can be a smooth, climbable, or even closed-form landscape rather than something requiring sampling. If our per-primitive rules (box face → normal through centroid; cylinder → orbit at fixed normal-standoff) are considered, this is precedent that a **fully analytical, no-search route is legitimate and has 30+ years of prior art**, not a shortcut we're inventing.
- **Best-view-within-a-region definition** (via the Roberts & Marshall proxy): "argmax over the feasible/visible region of −(angular offset from inverse surface normal)" is a clean, formally stated, directly computable definition — this is usable as-is for our RQ on "formal definitions of best view within a neighbourhood," and is compatible with the "analytical: face normal through region centroid" family already named in the branch brief.
- **Incidence-angle-as-hard-cutoff framing** (via the 2023 proxy, secondhand): supports treating orthogonality tolerance as a **feasibility gate with a sensor-type-dependent threshold** (tighter for structured-light/active-stereo sensors like ours: ~45° cited, vs ~60° generic) rather than only a smooth weight — but this is a directly relevant *hypothesis* to chase into the actual metrology/photogrammetry literature (a different branch document), not a number to cite as established for the D405 specifically.
- **FOV cone / DOF closed forms** (via the Roberts & Marshall proxy) are plug-in-usable off-the-shelf if we ever need a closed-form standoff/FOV feasibility check for the D405's known lens parameters, independent of anything PLY-derived.
