## Core method / idea

The paper's selection rationale for this branch — **"joint range × incidence model plus propagation from per-point precision to whole-cloud quality — the template for scoring a surface region rather than a point"** — is delivered by two linked pieces:

1. **A physically-derived, closed-form degradation model** (Ch.4) built from the laser link budget, not just curve-fitted.
2. **A propagation + patch-aggregation methodology** (Ch.5) that turns per-point range residuals into a per-patch (per-region) quality number, validated on two real point clouds.

### 1. Incidence angle and the physical noise model (Ch.4)

- **Incidence angle definition** (Eq. 4.1, Fig. 4.1): for laser-beam vector **P**ᵢ = [xᵢ,yᵢ,zᵢ] (scanner→surface point) and surface normal **N**, `αᵢ = cos⁻¹(Pᵢ·N / |Pᵢ||N|)`, domain `0 < α < π/2`. Identical orthogonality definition to the 2009 precursor — this is the paper's "orthogonality variable," reusable as-is.
- **Full physical derivation of the laser range equation** (Sec. 4.3, Eqs. 4.2–4.16) — this is materially more rigorous than the 2009 paper's shorthand "radar range equation." Starting from the Friis free-space-path transmission equation `P_R(ρ) = P_T G_T G_R η_sys η_atm L_FSP` (Eq. 4.2), the authors derive footprint area, irradiance, a target cross-section `σ = 4πχ·A_eSRx/β_s` (Eq. 4.13), and — assuming Lambertian scattering with outgoing photons scattered over solid angle `β_s = π` and `A_eSRx = A_eTx cos α` — arrive at the final closed form for received power:
  ```
  P_R = (P_T D_R² / 4ρ²) · η_sys · η_atm · χ · cos α        (Eq. 4.16)
  ```
  where `P_T` = transmitted power, `D_R` = receiver aperture diameter, `χ` = surface reflectivity, `η_sys`/`η_atm` = system/atmospheric transmission factors, `ρ` = range, `α` = incidence angle. **This is the paper's actual "joint range × incidence model"**: SNR ∝ `cos(α) / ρ²`.
- **Two independent, separable degradation coefficients** are then derived from Eq. 4.16, each normalized to [0,1]:
  - **Incidence contribution:** `c_I(α) = cos α` (Eq. 4.23), derived via propagation of uncertainty from the along-beam residual `êᵨ` to the orthogonal (surface-normal-direction) residual `ê_d = êᵨ cos α` (Eq. 4.20/4.22). This is the **propagation law** that converts a raw per-point range-noise estimate into a geometry-corrected one.
  - **Range contribution:** `c_R(ρ) = (ρ_max² − ρ²) / (ρ_max² − ρ_min²)` (Eq. 4.24) — a **closed-form, fitted-parameter range degradation model**, quadratic in ρ, bounded between the scanner's manufacturer-specified min/max reliable range. This is new/more explicit than the 2009 precursor, which only asserted the qualitative range trend.
- Both `c_I` and `c_R` are framed explicitly as **reliability coefficients**, 1 = fully reliable, 0 = unreliable — i.e., the authors' own design choice is a **continuous [0,1] weight**, not a binary constraint, at the modeling stage (see "hard threshold" discussion below for how this changes at the *planning* stage).

### 2. Point → patch → whole-cloud quality propagation (Ch.5) — the part the branch selected this paper for

This is the methodological core relevant to "scoring a surface region rather than a point":

- **Segmentation:** point cloud → planar segments via a gradient-based range-image method (Sec. 5.1.1) — requires **locally planar surfaces with a fittable normal**; not mesh-based, not applicable to free-form geometry without a plane-fit step.
- **Two competing patch-subdivision schemes** (Sec. 5.1.3, Fig. 5.3), evaluated head-to-head:
  - **Cartesian patches** (fixed physical area, e.g. 20×20 cm): patch area is constant across the whole point cloud, but **point count per patch varies wildly with range/incidence** (Fig. 5.5: at 5 m a 20×20 cm patch near-normal holds 2601 points; at 20 m only 169; number of points collapses "dramatically" past ~70° orientation). This introduces a *point-density confound* into any per-patch RMSE.
  - **Spherical patches** (fixed angular size, e.g. 5°×5°): patch holds a roughly constant point count (~1200–12000 pts depending on set-up) but its physical area varies with distance/orientation.
  - **Explicit negative finding (Sec. 5.3.2.4–5.3.2.5):** "the quality of the Cartesian patches is dominated by the number of points, which biases the comparison of the patches. **The Cartesian patches are therefore not really suitable when assessing the quality of local patches** of a planar segment with big variations in point density." The authors switch to spherical patches specifically to decouple noise-quality from density.
- **Per-patch aggregation rule:** standard deviation of the plane-fit residual, per patch — `σ_êᵨ = sqrt(êᵨᵀêᵨ / (n−m))` (Eq. 4.21, Least-Squares residual RMS with `m` = # fitted plane parameters) — i.e. **quadratic-mean (RMSE-style) pooling of per-point residuals within a region**, not mean, not worst-point/min pooling.
- **Whole-cloud (whole-view) quality number:** a single scalar per scan, obtained by averaging the per-patch/per-point statistic over the entire captured region — e.g. "the average standard deviation of the room is σ_êᵨ = 3.23 mm... average remaining standard deviation... σ_ê_d = 2.55 mm" (Sec. 5.3.2.5/5.4). This is the explicit **point → patch → whole-view propagation chain** the branch was looking for as a template.

## Concrete results and numbers

**Sensors used** (both single-beam, phase/pulse TLS — *not* stereo/active-stereo cameras):
- Plate experiments: **Leica HDS6000** (phase-based TLS).
- Room experiment: **FARO LS880 HE** (phase-based TLS), scanning resolution set to 1/4 of full resolution.

**Plate Experiment 1** (Sec. 5.2.1, Fig. 5.7–5.8): board fixed at **20 m**, rotated 0°→80° in 10° steps, 9 scans, 4,900–38,500 points/scan depending on angle.
- Raw along-beam noise σ_êᵨ grows with α, consistent with the theoretical `cos α` (equivalently `1/cos α` on the noise side) model.
- After correcting via `ê_d = êᵨ cos α`, the orthogonal noise σ_ê_d is "almost independent of the incidence angle" but shows a residual non-monotonic wiggle (slight rise at 10°, decline to 70°, uptick past 70°) attributed to the board's imperfect Lambertian BRDF.
- **Lichti's independently-published a-priori threshold of 65° max incidence angle** for discarding unreliable TLS points is cited here as corroborating evidence (Sec. 5.2.1, citing Lichti [90]).

**Plate Experiment 2** (Sec. 5.2.2, Fig. 5.9–5.12): board at 5, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 m × 0–80° in 10° steps = 54 attempted scans; **only 45 retained** — 9 discarded outright because, per the text (Sec. 5.2.2): *"at 60° and 50 m distance, only the points on the border of the board are measured, no points on the board are measured because of the high incidence angle and range of the measurements. The return signal strength is too low for the detection unit to be detected."* → **This is a hard sensor-detection cliff layered on top of the smooth cos(α)/ρ² degradation curve** — the model is smooth, but real hardware has a floor below which no return is registered at all (see branch-question mapping below).
- After incidence-angle correction (Eq. 4.20) and further range correction (Eq. 4.24, fitted with `ρ_min = 0 m, ρ_max = 80 m` for the HDS6000), the remaining noise is "almost independent of range," validating both closed-form coefficients jointly (Fig. 5.11–5.12).

**Room Experiment** (Sec. 5.3, Fig. 5.13–5.23): room 6.45 m × 5.43 m, **two scanner viewpoints compared** — Viewpoint A (room center, 1,225,307 pts) vs Viewpoint B (room corner, 1,591,288 pts) — 4 planar segments (floor + 3 wall pieces).
- **Theoretical incidence-angle map** (Fig. 5.16) shows the corner viewpoint produces systematically higher incidence angles at the far sides of segments than the center viewpoint.
- **Quantified contribution:** moving the scanner "roughly 2 m" from center to corner changes the fraction of noise attributable to incidence-angle effects by **~20% on average across the room, ~22% on the floor segment specifically** (Sec. 5.3.2.2, 5.3.2.5).
- **Counter-intuitive headline number:** despite the corner viewpoint having *worse* incidence angles at the far edges, its **average per-patch quality is better overall**: mean Cartesian-patch standard deviation = **0.0023 m (Viewpoint A, center) vs 0.0017 m (Viewpoint B, corner)** — a **~25% improvement** from moving the scanner 2 m to a position that is "maybe not the most trivial position one can think of" (Sec. 5.3.2.4, 5.4). This is because the corner position trades a few high-incidence far patches for many more near-perpendicular near patches — **average-pooled regional quality does not simply track "most globally centered/orthogonal," it depends on the full angle-and-range distribution across the region.**
- **Whole-cloud scalar summary:** average raw σ_êᵨ = **3.23 mm**, average incidence-corrected σ_ê_d = **2.55 mm**, i.e. ≈20% of total per-point range noise in a real indoor room scan is attributable to non-zero incidence angle alone (Sec. 5.3.2.5/5.4).
- **A documented "orthogonality can hurt" observation** (Sec. 5.3.2.2): *"In the corner viewpoint (Viewpoint B) results, a circular pattern of higher noise levels can be observed in segment 2, corresponding to a surface that is oriented nearly perpendicular to the laser beam. A possible explanation for this effect can be found in the saturation of the detection unit, due to an overload of the intensity sensor of the laser beam."* — near-normal incidence produced a **localized halo of degraded points**, hypothesized (not proven) to be **detector saturation from an unusually strong return signal precisely because incidence was near-zero**. The authors flag this as a "possible explanation," not a validated causal claim, and note it recurs in the earlier plate experiment as an off-trend point near 0°/10° (Sec. 5.2.2.1). This is a *second* TLS study by the same group replicating the same near-normal anomaly.

## Load-bearing assumptions

- **Lambertian (or "almost Lambertian") reflectance** for the Eq. 4.16 derivation — explicit in Sec. 4.3 ("assumed to have Lambertian scattering behavior") and re-flagged when applied to the imperfectly-Lambertian test board and room surfaces.
- **Locally planar surfaces with a fittable normal.** The entire pipeline — incidence angle, both coefficients, and the patch-quality propagation — requires a plane-fit per segment/patch (built on Ch.3's least-squares plane-fitting machinery). No treatment of curved/free-form surfaces or of normals derived by any method other than local plane fitting.
- **Monostatic backscatter sensing physics.** The Friis-derived link budget (Sec. 4.3) is fundamentally a *same-optical-path* transmit/receive model — this determines both the `cos α` law and the specular-reflection behavior discussed below. It does not describe a bistatic/multi-camera stereo-triangulation depth pipeline.
- **Range treated as the only noisy observable** — angular (θ,φ) pointing noise is not modeled; only along-beam range residuals are analyzed.
- **Reliability coefficients `c_I`, `c_R` are un-fitted/theoretical multiplicative weights** — the paper validates that the *shape* of empirical noise growth matches the theoretical cos(α) and range-quadratic curves, but does not report a jointly-fitted regression with confidence intervals; `ρ_min`/`ρ_max` in `c_R` are scanner spec values, not fitted from the data.
- **Segmentation and patching require enough points per patch to fit a stable plane** — spherical patches were sized to ~1200–12000 points specifically to keep the plane fit well-conditioned; the method is not demonstrated at sparse, low-point-count regions.

## What transfers to our setting

- **The point→patch→whole-view propagation template is exactly the pattern this branch needs for "how do we aggregate per-point PLY quality to a per-view score."** Concretely: (1) local plane/normal fit over a small neighborhood, (2) per-point incidence angle from that normal, (3) per-point noise proxy, (4) RMSE-pool within a patch, (5) average-pool patches within the region/view. This requires **no mesh** — only local neighborhoods and normal estimates — which matches our fused-PLY-without-mesh constraint directly (relevant to BQ10, BQ2 "unit of scoring").
- **The Cartesian-vs-spherical patch finding is directly reusable as a methodological warning**: if we patch/pool a PLY region by fixed physical area, patches at grazing incidence or far range will be sparse, and any RMSE computed there is confounded by low point count, not just by geometry. A **fixed-point-count (or density-normalized) patching scheme is the more defensible choice** when comparing regions/views of a survey cloud with non-uniform density — directly answers part of BQ9/BQ11 (dropout holes and sparse regions "reading as visible" is exactly this same density-confound problem in a different guise).
- **The counter-intuitive room-experiment finding — average-pooled region quality is not maximized by the most-orthogonal/most-central single viewpoint, but by the position with the best overall angle+range distribution across the whole region** — is a load-bearing caution for RQ1/RQ2: "most orthogonal" and "argmax of a quality score over the region" are not automatically the same answer once you're scoring a *region* rather than a single point, and averaging vs worst-point pooling can flip which viewpoint wins.
- **The near-normal-incidence detector-saturation anomaly, replicated across two independent experiments (plate + room) by the same group**, is a second data point (beyond the 2009 precursor's single speculative mention) that **near-normal incidence is not unconditionally best even for a diffuse-ish surface**, strengthening (not proving) the case for BQ3's "does orthogonality ever hurt" question — though the physical mechanism (backscatter detector overload) differs from the D405's active-stereo/structured-light retroreflection concern; see below.
- **The soft-model/hard-constraint split in the companion planning chapter (Ch.6, part of the same thesis, built directly on this paper's model) is directly on-point for BQ2.** The *quality model itself* (`c_I(α) = cos α`) is continuous/smooth. But when the same authors move from *modeling* to *planning* (Sec. 6.3.1), they explicitly convert it into a **hard binary feasibility cutoff**: *"When adding a maximum incidence angle α_max constraint, the edge is only partially visible... The parts of the edges that are observed with incidence angles greater than the maximum threshold α_max are considered not visible anymore."* — i.e., their own answer to "soft weight or hard constraint" is **both, at different stages**: smooth cost for scoring/ranking, hard threshold for feasibility/visibility gating in the optimization. This is a directly citable precedent for a two-tier design (soft term in the score + hard cutoff in the action-space feasibility filter).
- **Four-factor top-level taxonomy of point-cloud quality** (thesis Sec. 2.3: instrument/calibration, atmospheric conditions, object scattering properties, scanning geometry) is a clean organizing frame for the branch's term table (BQ5): our "orthogonality" and "standoff/range" terms are exactly their "scanning geometry" sub-factors; glossiness/specularity concerns map to their "object scattering properties" bucket, which they explicitly scope *out* of this paper (see below).

## What does NOT transfer, and why

- **Wrong sensor physics for the D405.** The entire quantitative model (Eq. 4.16, the `cos α / ρ²` law, the `c_I`/`c_R` coefficients) is derived from a **monostatic backscatter link budget** — the same optical path emits and receives, and the "target cross-section" formalism (Eq. 4.13) is specific to that geometry. The Intel D405 is an **active-stereo** camera: depth comes from triangulating an IR-projected pattern between two side-by-side IR imagers (a baseline, not backscatter). The error mechanism there is fundamentally different — correspondence/matching precision as a function of pattern deformation, effective baseline-vs-surface-tilt foreshortening, and defocus — not signal-power attenuation on a single return path. **This paper cannot supply the numeric or even the exact functional form of σ(range, θ) for the D405** (branch question 1); it can only supply the qualitative expectation of a smooth, monotonically-worsening-with-angle law and a physically-motivated shape (`~1/cos α`-type growth) as a *prior*, not a citable D405 curve.
- **Wrong range regime, by roughly two orders of magnitude.** Tested ranges are 5–50 m (plate) and 0–6.5 m (room); our operating range is 10–40 cm. None of the absolute noise numbers (mm-level σ at meters range) are usable as D405 numbers — only the qualitative shape of the range-degradation curve (quadratic-in-ρ, bounded by min/max reliable range) transfers as a *template form*, not fitted values.
- **Specular-surface physics point the opposite direction from the branch's D405 worry, for a mechanistic reason worth stating precisely.** Thesis Sec. 2.3.3 (background chapter, not this paper's own experiment, but load-bearing context the paper inherits): for a **monostatic backscatter** TLS, *"in the particular case of specular reflections no light signal is returned back to the scanner if the surface is not perpendicular to the emitted beam. A TLS can therefore not measure mirror-like surfaces if they are not exactly placed perpendicularly to the emitted laser beam."* — i.e., for this sensor class, **near-normal incidence is the *only* geometry that returns any signal at all off a mirror-like surface**, the opposite failure mode from the branch's concern (that near-normal incidence on a glossy surface reflects the projector pattern *back into the imager*, saturating/blinding it). Both can be true simultaneously for different sensor architectures — **this paper's physics does not transfer to justify or refute the D405-specific specular-blowout-at-normal-incidence concern; it describes a different sensor class where the failure mode runs the other way.** The room experiment's own near-normal detector-saturation anomaly (see above) is the closest thing to supporting evidence, but is explicitly speculative in the source and mechanistically distinct (intensity-detector overload from a strong backscatter return, not IR-projector retroreflection into a stereo imager pair).
- **No mesh, but also no treatment of non-planar/curved primitives.** The method requires a *plane* fit; a cylinder or any curved surface would need per-point local tangent-plane fits over small enough neighborhoods that curvature is negligible, which the paper never tests or discusses — an open transfer risk for our box+cylinder primitive regime (their validated cases are flat plate, flat room walls/floor).
- **No normal-estimation error propagation into the quality score at all** (BQ7). Normals here come from least-squares plane fits over thousands to tens of thousands of points on *known-flat, comparatively clean* test surfaces (test board, painted walls) — a far better-conditioned estimate than what a small, sparse, defect-laden neighborhood on our fused PLY (with mm pose error and dropout holes) would produce. The paper's implicit assumption — normals are cheap and accurate given "enough" points on a genuinely flat surface — does not hold under our stated PLY defects; no sensitivity analysis of d(score)/d(normal error) is present anywhere in this material.
- **No propagation of upstream/input geometric uncertainty into the score** (BQ12). The plane/normal, once fit, is treated as ground truth; there is no discussion of how an *already-uncertain* input point cloud (ours has a documented ~4 cm fused-vs-single-view offset) would itself bias the incidence-angle term computed from it. This is a genuine gap the paper does not address — consistent with the branch's suspicion that this is an open contribution opportunity, not a solved problem elsewhere in this line of work either.
- **No defect-detectability, image-space, saliency/entropy, information-gain, or VLM-judging content whatsoever.** This is a pure geometric-metrology paper; zero content for BQ4 ("industrial-inspection answer"), BQ17–21 (image-space judging, VLM-as-judge). Must be sourced elsewhere.
- **No coverage/multi-view set-selection content in this specific paper** (that's Ch.6 of the same thesis, a related-but-distinct 2011 ISPRS Archives conference paper — out of this branch's scope per the framing doc, belongs to "branch 4").

## Answers mapped to specific branch questions

- **BQ1 (D405 σ(range,θ) curve):** Not answerable from this paper — wrong sensor class and wrong range regime (see above). Contributes only the *template functional shape* (`cos α`-type smooth degradation × range-quadratic term) as a hypothesis to test against actual D405 data, not a citable number.
- **BQ2 (smooth vs threshold):** **Both, at different pipeline stages**, per this group's own practice: the *quality model* (Eq. 4.23, `c_I(α)=cos α`) is smooth/continuous; the same authors' *planning* layer (Ch.6, Sec. 6.3.1) explicitly converts it to a **hard `α_max` feasibility cutoff** for visibility/view-point selection. Empirically there is also a genuine **physical hard cliff** layered on top of the smooth curve at combined extreme range+angle (Sec. 5.2.2: total signal-detection failure at 60°/50 m) — so "smooth penalty + hard feasibility backstop" is directly precedented, not just a design compromise.
- **BQ3 (does orthogonality ever hurt):** Two independent within-group observations (plate exp. off-trend point near 0°, and room exp.'s localized noise halo at near-normal incidence in the corner-viewpoint scan) both point at **detector-saturation-from-strong-return as a candidate near-normal failure mode**, but both are explicitly presented as speculative/unconfirmed by the authors, not validated. Mechanistically distinct from the D405/structured-light retroreflection concern (see "what does not transfer"). Net: **weak circumstantial support that near-normal incidence is not unconditionally safe, but no transferable mechanism or threshold for the D405 case.**
- **BQ5 (term table):** Contributes two clean, formula-complete rows — *incidence/cosine term* (`α = cos⁻¹(P·N/|P||N|)`, PLY-space, inputs: view-ray direction + local surface normal) and *range/standoff term* (`c_R(ρ) = (ρ_max²−ρ²)/(ρ_max²−ρ_min²)`, PLY-space, inputs: range + scanner/sensor-specific min/max reliable range) — plus the physically-grounded combined form `quality ∝ cos(α)/ρ²` as the joint interaction term the branch is looking for (functional form only, not D405-fitted).
- **BQ6 (redundant terms):** The paper's own **separability result is directly relevant**: it demonstrates empirically that after regressing out the incidence-angle contribution (`ê_d = êᵨ cos α`), the *remaining* noise is close to independent of angle and instead explained by range — i.e. incidence and range are **not** redundant with each other in their model (each explains a distinct share of variance: ~20-22% incidence, separate range contribution). This is evidence *against* two specific candidate terms being redundant, useful as a counter-example when auditing the branch's term table for collapsible terms.
- **BQ9 (unit of scoring — point/patch/face/view):** Direct, citable precedent: **patch-level RMSE of per-point residuals (Eq. 4.21), average-pooled across patches to a whole-view scalar** — explicitly *not* worst-point pooling, and explicitly shown to require *density-normalized* (fixed-point-count / spherical) patches rather than fixed-area (Cartesian) patches to avoid a point-count confound (Sec. 5.3.2.4–5.3.2.5, a real negative finding about the wrong choice).
- **BQ10 (survives a defective fused PLY):** The incidence-angle term is **computable today** given only a local normal (no mesh required) — but the paper's own accurate-normal assumption rests on dense, genuinely-planar, low-noise neighborhoods; our fused cloud's dropout holes, mm pose error, and curved (not flat) primitives put this in **"computable today, but normal-estimate quality is contingent, not free"** — reinforcing that Branch 3's cleanup/normal-estimation quality is a hard dependency, not a nice-to-have (also the paper's answer to BQ7: it never measures this sensitivity, so we cannot borrow a d(score)/d(normal-error) number from it — that has to be established independently).
- **BQ11 (visibility/self-occlusion without a surface):** Not addressed — segmentation here presupposes a *scanned* point cloud with real backscatter dropout patterns, not a synthetic visibility computation from a hypothetical viewpoint; no hidden-point-removal discussion. No transfer.
- **BQ13 (4 cm fused-vs-single-view offset / judging in the wrong frame):** Not addressed directly, but the propagation methodology (fit locally, don't trust the input cloud as ground truth beyond the neighborhood you fit) is at least *compatible* with a design where the incidence-angle term is computed from a **local, re-fit normal per candidate view/region** rather than from a globally-fixed fused-cloud normal — which would reduce (not eliminate) sensitivity to a systematic fused-vs-single-view offset. This is an inference from the method's structure, not a stated finding in the paper.
