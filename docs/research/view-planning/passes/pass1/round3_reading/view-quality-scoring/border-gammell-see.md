# Border, Gammell — "The Surface Edge Explorer (SEE): A Measurement-Direct Approach to Next Best View Planning"

Full text read via the arXiv HTML rendering (`arxiv.org/html/2207.13684v4`, fetched to a local file, converted to plain text with pandoc and read section-by-section, not just the abstract) — all of Sections 1–6, all algorithms (1–12), all equations, Tables 1–5, Figs 1–17, and the full Related Work (Section 2) were read in full.

## Core method/idea

SEE proposes and scores views **directly from raw sensor measurements**, never building a voxel grid or mesh (Section 3, Fig. 3). Its pipeline (Algorithm 1) each iteration:

1. **Classify** new measurements by local density into `core` / `frontier` / `outlier` (Section 3.2, DBSCAN-inspired). A point `p` has neighbourhood `N_p := {q ∈ P | ||q-p|| ≤ r}`. `core` if `|N_p| ≥ k_min`; `frontier` if `|N_p| < k_min` but touches a core point; `outlier` otherwise. `k_min = ⌈(4/3)π ρ r³⌉` (Alg. 2, line 15) from a target density `ρ` and resolution radius `r`. **Frontier points are literally the definition of "surface edge"** — the boundary between sufficiently- and insufficiently-observed surface.
2. **Propose a view per frontier point** (Section 3.3, Alg. 4): local surface geometry is estimated by eigendecomposition of the covariance of neighbours within `r` (Alg. 5) — normal `e_n` = min-eigenvalue eigenvector, frontier direction `e_f` = eigenvector most aligned with the mean-neighbour direction, boundary tangent `e_b = e_n × e_f`. The view is placed **exactly** at `x = f + d·e_n`, oriented `φ = -e_n` (Alg. 4, line 4) — i.e. every proposed view is orthogonal to the locally-estimated surface by construction, at a fixed standoff `d`.
3. **Resolve which normal sign is "outward"** (Section 3.3.2, Alg. 6): a naive "point toward the current view" heuristic is explicitly said to "fail when [the current view] observes the surface at an acute angle" because "the sightline and surface normal are nearly perpendicular and measurement noise can corrupt the identification of the outwards facing normal direction" (Section 3.3.2, para. 2). Their fix is a Hidden-Point-Removal-inspired (Katz, 2015) spherical projection: sample points outward along `+e_n`/`-e_n`, project nearby measurements onto a unit sphere centred on the current view, and pick the direction whose projected neighbourhood is empty first (Alg. 6).
4. **Refine occluded proposals** (Section 3.4): occlusion is tested by walking sample points along the sightline and checking, at each, whether any measurement exists within a `υ`-radius (Alg. 8, `IsOccluded`) — a pure point-proximity test, no ray/mesh intersection. If occluded, an alternative unoccluded view direction is found by a **maximin optimisation on the same HPR-style spherical projection**: project nearby occluders onto a unit sphere and solve for the direction of maximum angular separation from all of them (the antipole of the smallest containing spherical cap; Drezner & Wesolowsky 1983 / Patel & Chidambaram 2002) — Alg. 10.
5. **Score views** (Section 3.5, Alg. 11 `GraphViews`): builds a directed **frontier visibility graph** `𝒢=(M,K)` where vertex `m=(f,v)` pairs a frontier with its proposed view, and an edge `(m_i,m_j)` exists if view `v_i` can see frontier `f_j` (occlusion-free per Alg. 8). **A view's value is simply the count of frontier points it can see (its graph out-degree) — an unweighted, unbounded integer.** Quote: "SEE quantifies the predicted coverage from a proposed view as the number of visible frontier points" (Section 3.5).
6. **Select next-best-view** (Section 3.6, Alg. 12 `SelectNBV`): restrict candidates to views that see at least as many frontiers as the closest proposed view, then `m* = argmax_{m∈M'} (deg⁺(m) / ||x - x_c||)` — out-degree divided by Euclidean distance from the current sensor pose (Alg. 12, line 4). Fall back to the closest view if no such `m*` exists.

The paper also states the generic formal NBV objective it instantiates (Section 2.1, unnumbered display equation): `v' = nbv(V,Y) = argmax_{v∈V} metrics(v,Y)`. SEE's `metrics()` is the graph-restricted greedy ratio in step 6 above — a third concrete "best view in a neighbourhood" family (alongside constraint-satisfaction-with-margin and mutual-information viewpoint stability) worth logging: **restrict-then-ratio-maximize on a local visibility graph.**

Parameter linkage (Alg. 2) ties target density to sensor FOV/resolution and standoff distance under a **fronto-parallel assumption**:
`d ← sqrt( ω_x ω_y / (12 ρ tan(0.5θ_x) tan(0.5θ_y)) − 2r²/3 )` and its inverse `ρ ← ω_x ω_y / (4 tan(0.5θ_x) tan(0.5θ_y)(3d² + 2r²))` (Alg. 2, lines 6–11). This is a clean GSD↔standoff↔density relation but it is derived assuming the view is normal to the surface — there is no `cos(θ)` incidence term anywhere because incidence is never anything but 0° by construction.

## Concrete results and numbers

**Simulation (Section 4, Figs. 14–16, Table 2/3):** compared against 7 volumetric NBV baselines — Average Entropy (AE), Area Factor (AF), Occlusion Aware (OA), Proximity Count (PC), Rear Side Entropy (RSE), Rear Side Voxel (RSV), Unobserved Voxel (UV) — using implementations from Delmerico et al. (2018). 100 independent runs per model.

- *Small models* (UR10 + simulated RGB-D, 70°×43° FOV, 848×480, Gaussian depth noise σ=0.01 m; Table 2), final coverage/distance/time at max views taken:
  - Newell Teapot (23 views): SEE 97.6% / 28 m / 99 s vs. best-coverage baseline AE 98.5% / 71 m / 224 s. SEE is the *only* model where a baseline (AE) beats SEE's final coverage, "because sensor noise causes the intersections between the teapot body and the handle to be prematurely classified as fully observed" (Section 4.3.1).
  - Stanford Bunny (27 views): SEE 99.7% / 36 m / 127 s — best on every metric vs. all baselines (next best UV/OA ≈92% at ~79m/238s).
  - Stanford Dragon (40 views): SEE 98.4% / 38 m / 145 s, best on every metric.
  - Stanford Armadillo (37 views): SEE 99.9% / 38 m / 150 s (best baseline RSV 98.4% / 51m / 170s).
  - Happy Buddha (25 views): SEE 99.6% / 26 m / 101 s (best baseline RSV 95.7% / 34m/117s).
  - Helix (44 views): SEE 99.4% / 47 m / 194 s (best baseline AE 96.3%/121m/378s).
- *Large models* (simulated quadrotor+2-axis-gimbal LiDAR, 60°×40° FOV, 1200×800, σ=0.01m; 40×40×40 m scenes):
  - Statue of Liberty (31 views): SEE 96.9% / 504 m / 431 s vs. best baseline AE 84.7% / 2007 m / 1754 s.
  - Radcliffe Camera (40 views): SEE 97.6% / 692 m / 636 s vs. best baseline AE 95.4% / 3009 m / 2683 s.
  - Notre-Dame de Paris (83 views): SEE 91.0% / 1000 m / 1008 s — here SEE is "marginally worse" on final coverage than all baselines except PC (≈93% best), because "it took SEE more observation time to plan and capture views of the flying buttresses without successfully increasing surface coverage underneath these uniquely challenging features" (Section 4.3.2) — but still at far lower cost (1008 s vs. AE's 4922 s).
- Overall pattern stated in Discussion (Section 4.3): "SEE achieves similar or better surface coverage than the volumetric approaches on every tested model while using shorter travel distances and less observation time... For the maximum number of views taken, SEE captures equivalent or greater surface coverage per view, unit of travel distance and unit of observation time than the volumetric approaches on every model" (Section 4.3, para. 1). At 50%/75% of max views, the ratio-based volumetric baselines (RSE, RSV) sometimes win on early-stage coverage-per-distance because SEE's later views chase hard-to-reach surfaces (dragon folds, helix interior, building balconies/buttresses) that cost disproportionate travel (Section 4.3.1–4.3.3).

**Real-world (Section 5, Fig. 17, Tables 4–5):** UR10 arm + Intel RealSense L515 (70°×43° FOV, 640×480) on a turntable, observing the "Oxford Deer" statue, 20 independent runs. Real-world SEE parameters (Table 5) reuse the small-model config except target density `ρ` raised an order of magnitude to 5,000,000 pts/m³ and minimum separation `ε` lowered similarly, "in order to capture sufficient measurements on the deer antlers after noise filtering" (Section 5, para. 4); standoff `d=0.5 m`, `r=0.03 m` — unchanged from simulation. A radius outlier filter (drop points with <170 neighbours in 3 cm) plus ICP alignment was added to handle real sensor noise (Section 5, para. 3). **No ground truth exists for this object, so coverage is reported "as a percentage of the final SEE pointcloud" (Section 5, para. 5) — i.e. the completeness metric is self-referential against SEE's own output**, not an independent reference. Qualitatively: "high-quality observations... except for some noise around the face, antlers and hind legs" (Section 5, para. 7). The exact per-metric table (views/distance/times) rendered corrupted through HTML→text conversion in my extraction and I do not have confidently-parsed numbers for it; the reliable, clearly-stated facts are the ones above (parameters, 20 runs, self-referential coverage metric, qualitative noise regions).

## Load-bearing assumptions (and where they conflict with our setting)

- **Views are orthogonal by construction, never scored on-axis, never traded off.** There is no incidence-angle term anywhere in the scoring or selection math (Sections 3.5, 3.6) — not a soft weight, not a hard threshold. The paper sidesteps the "is the angle penalty smooth or a knee" question entirely by generating only normal-aligned proposals. It gives **no evidence either way** on whether off-normal views are ever better (glare/specular) or on reachability-constrained "within tolerance" views — those cases are outside its scope. This is a genuine gap, not a hidden answer.
- **No incidence-angle-dependent noise model, anywhere, including in their own simulation.** Simulated depth noise is isotropic Gaussian, `σ=0.01 m` regardless of view angle relative to surface (Section 4, para. 4, "Gaussian noise (μ=0m, σ=0.01m) to the ray intersections... representative of... an Intel RealSense L515... and a Hesai XT32"). Since only normal views are ever taken, this simplification costs them nothing — but it means **SEE provides no incidence-vs-error curve or model at all**; it is silent on the exact question Branch's Q1 (D405 incidence-vs-depth-error curve) needs, and its own architecture never needed to answer it.
- **Normal-estimation error is never quantified or ablated.** Section 3.1 only qualitatively requires that "measurement noise should be encompassed within the resolution radius `r`" and that `r` be "large enough to handle measurement noise robustly... while still retaining surface features" — no `d(score)/d(normal-error)` relationship, no sensitivity sweep on eigendecomposition stability under sparse/noisy neighbourhoods is reported anywhere in the paper. This is a first-class negative finding for the branch's top risk item: **this paper cannot supply the normal-accuracy tolerance number Branch 3 needs.**
- **Score is a raw, unbounded, unnormalized integer count** — out-degree in the frontier visibility graph (Section 3.5) — with **no cross-object or cross-scene comparability intended**: it is only ever used to rank candidate views *within one ongoing observation of one object*, and scales with how many frontier points exist, which itself scales with model complexity/size. This directly instantiates the branch's concern: **an unbounded utility like this is not usable as a comparable, VLM-facing per-view score.** It's a clean negative exemplar, not a solution to borrow.
- **Aggregation unit is the single point, and the aggregation operator is unweighted cardinality (count).** No area-weighting, no worst-point/min pooling, no propagated measurement-precision weighting is applied when going from point-level visibility to view-level score (Alg. 11). This is the simplest possible answer to the "unit of scoring / aggregation" question and a useful contrast baseline — but it is not designed for inspection-grade defect judgement, only reconstruction completeness.
- **No uncertainty propagation from input geometry into the view score.** Point positions are treated as ground truth for classification and occlusion purposes; there is no pose-uncertainty or per-point-confidence term feeding `metrics(v,Y)` anywhere in Sections 3.5–3.6. Supports the branch's "if nobody does this, it's a contribution claim" framing — SEE is one more paper in the "nobody" column.
- **No handling of a separately-fused reference cloud vs. live sensor coordinates.** SEE operates incrementally on its own single accumulating point set in one consistent frame (robot FK + fixed extrinsics; ICP-aligned once per capture in the real-world case, Section 5, para. 3) — it never has two disagreeing geometric sources the way our fused-PLY-vs-live-D405-depth setup does. **This paper is silent on the ~4 cm fused-vs-single-view offset question**; it has no analogous failure mode to report on.
- **Dropout holes / non-uniform density are foundational, not tested as a failure mode.** Density-based classification is exactly the mechanism that turns "sparse/no measurements" into frontier points to re-observe — but a true dropout hole (a region with literally zero neighbouring measurements anywhere, isolated from any core point) would never satisfy the frontier condition `N_p ∩ P_c ≠ ∅` (Section 3.2) and so could sit unclassified/unaddressed rather than being flagged for observation. The paper does not discuss or test this edge case at all.

## What transfers to our setting

- **The permission-slip claim itself is real and specific**: view scoring, view proposal, occlusion/visibility testing, and normal-sign disambiguation are all done via local point-neighbourhood queries and covariance eigendecomposition on the raw cloud (Sections 3.2, 3.3, 3.4) — no mesh, no voxel grid, at any stage. This validates that scoring/optimizing directly on a defective fused PLY without reconstructing a surface is methodologically established practice, not a novel risk we're inventing.
- **The mesh-free visibility recipe is directly reusable** for our "how is visibility/self-occlusion computed without a surface" question: (a) a cheap point-proximity-along-sightline test for binary occluded/unoccluded (Alg. 8), and (b) an HPR-inspired spherical projection + maximin optimisation for finding the *least occluded direction* when the naive proposal fails (Alg. 6, Alg. 10). Both are concrete, implementable, mesh-free algorithms with pseudocode, not just a citation to Katz (2015) HPR in the abstract.
- **A clean, closed-form GSD/standoff/density relation** (Alg. 2, lines 6–11) is directly transferable as the skeleton of our resolution/standoff scoring term — with the caveat that it must be re-derived with an explicit `cos(θ)` incidence correction before use off-normal, since SEE's version silently assumes `θ=0`.
- **A concrete, citable "restrict-then-ratio-maximize" formal definition of best-view-in-a-neighbourhood** (Alg. 12: candidates restricted to those visibility-dominating the nearest proposal, then argmax value/distance) — a computable third option alongside MVP's constraint-satisfaction-with-margin and MI-framework viewpoint stability, worth carrying into the "formal definitions of best view" comparison.
- **Refinement is solved once, at proposal/construction time, via constrained optimisation — not sampling, not gradient descent, not online at query time.** Alg. 10's maximin antipole solve is a one-shot correction applied when a candidate view is first proposed and found occluded, never revisited interactively. This is direct precedent for doing local refinement offline, at action-space construction time, rather than in response to a live VLM request.

## What does NOT transfer, and why (negative findings)

- **SEE does not score "view quality for inspection" at all — it scores "how much new coverage will this reveal."** Its unit of value is frontier-point count for reconstruction completeness; it has zero notion of incidence-angle penalty, defect detectability, saliency, image-space quality (exposure, blur, specular blowout), or GSD trade-off *beyond* the fronto-parallel density relation above. It cannot answer, and does not attempt, any of the defect-detectability, image-space, or VLM-judge branch questions. It was correctly scoped by the branch owner as a *methodology* source (mesh-free scoring), not a *view-quality-criteria* source.
- **No incidence-angle-vs-error curve, no glossy/specular off-normal discussion, no defect-detectability-vs-angle discussion** — these branch questions get zero evidence from this paper; its own simulated noise model is isotropic and angle-independent by design, and its real-world sensor (L515) is only ever used at normal incidence.
- **No bounded/normalized score precedent to imitate** — the opposite: its raw, unbounded count is a documented failure mode to avoid if the goal is a comparable per-view number.
- **No sensitivity analysis of `ψ`/`υ` (occlusion/visibility search distances) under non-uniform density or dropout** — these are user-tuned per scene (Section 4.2.1–4.2.2, Table 3; e.g. `ψ` halved for large models purely to bound compute) with no reported ablation.
- **No treatment of a two-source geometry problem** (fused survey cloud vs. live single-view depth) — not applicable to SEE's single-accumulating-cloud architecture, so it cannot inform the calibration-vs-modelling-requirement question about our 4 cm offset.
- **The real-world coverage metric is self-referential** (computed against SEE's own final cloud, Section 5, para. 5, because no ground truth exists for the Oxford Deer) — a mild instance of the same GT-circularity risk the branch flags for metric-based "best view" ground truth, worth citing as a precedent that even a well-regarded IJRR paper accepts this circularity for real-world validation when no better GT is available.

## Assessment for the branch

High relevance as the methodological anchor the branch selected it for: it is a fully worked, pseudocode-level demonstration that view proposal, view scoring, and mesh-free visibility/occlusion can all be computed directly on a raw, noisy, non-uniform point cloud with no mesh or voxel grid — directly licensing our own PLY-space scoring work. Its concrete, reusable content is the point-neighbourhood occlusion test (Alg. 8) and the HPR-style maximin unoccluded-view search (Alg. 6/10), plus the fronto-parallel GSD/standoff relation (Alg. 2) as a starting skeleton. It is near-zero yield, however, for every branch question about *what makes a view good for inspection specifically* — incidence-angle-vs-error curves, angle thresholds, off-normal/specular tradeoffs, defect detectability, image-space judgment, bounded/normalized scoring, and normal-error sensitivity are all either explicitly absent or actively contradicted (its own score is unbounded and its own architecture assumes exact-normal viewing is always both desirable and achievable, which our reachability-constrained, defect-inspection setting cannot assume).
