## Core method / idea

The paper isolates **incidence angle** as one of four factors degrading terrestrial laser scanner (TLS) point precision (the other three: instrument calibration, atmospheric conditions, object properties — Sec 1.2), and builds a physically-motivated correction model:

1. **Incidence angle definition** (Eq. 3, Fig. 1a): `α = cos⁻¹(P·N / |P||N|)`, angle between the laser-beam vector **P** (scanner→surface) and the surface normal **N**. This is *exactly* our orthogonality variable — α=0 is normal incidence / most orthogonal.
2. **Physical noise mechanism** (Sec 2.3, radar range equation under Lambertian assumption, Eq. 4): received power `Pr = κ·Pt·cos(α)`. SNR degrades with cos(α) because the laser footprint elongates at grazing angles (Fig. 1b) — weaker, temporally-smeared return pulse → worse range-determination precision. Fig. 2 plots the theoretical "signal deterioration" as `1/cos(α)`, a steep curve that is near-flat until ~50-60° then blows up.
3. **Geometric incidence coefficient** `cα = cos(α)` expressed directly from point-cloud spherical angles (θ,φ) and local plane normal **N** = [u,v,w] (Eq. 8) — computable per-point from angular info + one fitted plane, no external calibration needed.
4. **Empirical correction**: fit a plane to a segment (planar patch) by least squares, take the per-point residual `ê_ρ = P_M·N^T` (Eq. 9) as raw noise, then "remove" the incidence contribution via `ê_d = ê_ρ · cα` (Eq. 10) to get a *remaining* noise term attributed to other factors (surface property, range, calibration).
5. **Aggregation to patch-level quality**: per-segment standard error `σ = sqrt(ê·êᵀ / n)` (Eq. 11) — i.e. **RMSE of per-point plane-fit residuals, pooled over all n points in a planar segment**. This is a quadratic-mean point→patch aggregation, not a mean or a worst-point/min pooling.

Validated on two experiments: (A) a reference plate rotated 0–80° at a fixed 20 m range, and (B) the same rotations repeated at 5/10/20/30/40/50 m; then applied to (C) a real room scan (>20M points, 4 planar segments: floor + 3 wall pieces).

## Concrete results and numbers

- **Sensor:** FARO LS880-HE80 TLS — a single-beam scanning laser rangefinder (mirror-scanned, spherical θ/φ/ρ acquisition). **Not** a stereo/RGB-D camera.
- **Target:** 1×1 m white-coated plywood board, "almost Lambertian" but explicitly not perfectly isotropic; goniometer rotation precision 2°.
- **Exp. 1 (Sec 3.1, fixed 20 m range):** 9 scans, α = 0°→80° in 10° steps. Point count per scan dropped from **38,500 (near 0°) to 4,900 (at 80°)** — foreshortening reduces the number of points landing on a fixed-size target as incidence angle grows (an empirical illustration of resolution/GSD loss with angle, orthogonal to the noise story).
  - Fig. 4: raw σ_ρ (blue) tracks the theoretical `1/cos(α)` curve (green) closely; after subtracting the incidence term, remaining σ_d (red) is "almost independent of incidence angle" but shows a residual structure (rise at 10°, slow decline to 70°, uptick at 80°) attributed to non-Lambertian BRDF and possible detector-saturation effects near normal incidence.
- **Exp. 2 (Sec 3.2, range × angle grid):** distances 5, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 m; angles 0–70° in 10° steps. 54 scans attempted, **only 45 retained** — 9 discarded because "the standard error obtained was higher than 5 mm" at combinations of high range *and* high incidence angle. Point counts: 4,500–150,500 at low range, 3,100–5,500 at high range. Fig. 6 shows σ_ρ increasing monotonically with both range and incidence angle; corrected σ_d still rises with range (footprint grows with distance too) but the angle-dependence collapses.
- **Headline finding (Abstract, Sec 3.1 discussion):** "**above 60° the incidence angle dominates the scan point precision**." A cited companion result: (Lichti, 2007) independently recommends **an a-priori hard threshold of 65° incidence angle** for discarding unreliable TLS points.
- **Room experiment (Sec 4, Fig. 7–8):** room 6.45 m × 5.43 m, TLS scanning from room center, >20M points, segmented into floor + 3 wall pieces (4 segments total). Average raw standard error **σ_ρ = 3.225 mm**; average remaining (incidence-corrected) **σ_d = 2.550 mm** → **≈20% of total measurement noise in a typical room scan is attributable to incidence angle alone**. Per-segment breakdown (Fig. 8b/c, mm): raw noise {4.6, 2.8, 3.0, 2.5}; corrected {3.2, 2.3, 2.8, 1.9}.
- Near-0° anomaly (Sec 3.1 discussion): the lowest-incidence-angle scan's noise sits off-trend from the rest; authors speculate the detector may rescale/adapt near saturation "because it is more likely to saturate the detection system when the laser beam hits a surface in a nearly perpendicular way" — a hedge, not a validated finding.

## Load-bearing assumptions

- **Lambertian / near-Lambertian reflectance** assumed for the radar-range-equation derivation (Eq. 4); the authors flag their own test board and the room's white paint/linoleum as "almost" but not perfectly Lambertian, and attribute residual structure in σ_d to this violation.
- **Planar surfaces with a fittable normal** — the whole pipeline (α, cα, ê_ρ) requires a least-squares plane fit per segment; there is no free-form/curved-surface treatment.
- **Angular noise (δθ, δφ) treated as negligible**, only range noise ê_ρ studied (Sec 2.3). Biases are explicitly out of scope — only noise/precision, not systematic error, is modeled.
- **Single-beam, backscatter TLS physics**: the derivation depends on transmitted/received power on the *same* optical path (Eq. 4, "backscatter" note under Eq. 3) — this is fundamentally a monostatic active-sensing argument, not a stereo-triangulation one.
- Eq. 10's "removal" of the incidence effect is an ad hoc empirical correction (multiply raw noise by cα) validated only by checking that the resulting curve looks flatter, not a fitted parametric model with reported goodness-of-fit statistics or numeric coefficients.

## What transfers to our setting

- **The orthogonality/incidence-angle formalism itself** (Eq. 3, α between view ray and local surface normal) is exactly our "orthogonality variable" — clean, reusable definition and the natural PLY-space computation (needs a local normal, obtainable from a neighborhood plane fit or PCA on the survey cloud).
- **Qualitative angle-error law**: noise grows slowly with α up to roughly 50–60°, then accelerates sharply — a physically motivated case for a **smooth-but-convex penalty function that steepens near a knee around 60°**, not a linear one. Directly informs RQ1 ("how orthogonal it is" scoring) as a shape prior even though the sensor differs.
- **60–65° as a practically-validated feasibility threshold** — two independent TLS studies (this paper's own 20 m/60° finding, and Lichti 2007's 65° recommendation) converge on a hard cutoff in that band. Useful as a starting anchor for "soft weight below knee, treat past it as infeasible" — answers the branch's "smooth vs threshold" question with "both: smooth penalty with a real, literature-supported feasibility knee near 60–65°," at least for laser-based active ranging.
- **Point→patch aggregation precedent** (Eq. 11, RMSE/quadratic-mean pooling of per-point residuals over a planar segment) is a directly reusable, defensible answer to "what is the unit of scoring" (Q9) for the PLY-space track: aggregate per-point depth-quality proxies to a per-region score via RMSE, not mean or worst-point.
- **Point density/coverage also degrades with incidence angle on a fixed-size target** (Exp. 1's 38,500→4,900 point-count drop) — an empirical example that a single 1/cos(θ)-type foreshortening term can simultaneously explain both resolution loss (GSD) and, in this sensor's case, noise growth; but note our GSD/resolution term and our depth-noise term should probably still be tracked separately since they arise from different mechanisms in our sensor (see below).
- **Four-factor decomposition of point-cloud quality** (Sec 1.2: calibration / atmosphere / object properties / scan geometry, with scan geometry further split into incidence angle, range, local point density) is a clean top-level taxonomy that could organize the branch's view-quality term table (Q5): our "orthogonality," "standoff/GSD," and "sampling density" terms map directly onto their "scan geometry" sub-factors; material/glossiness maps to "object properties."

## What does NOT transfer, and why

- **Wrong sensor class entirely.** FARO LS880-HE80 is a single-beam, mirror-scanned, monostatic time-of-flight/phase laser rangefinder. The D405 is an **active-stereo camera** (projected IR pattern + stereo correspondence matching between two IR imagers). The noise mechanisms are physically distinct: TLS noise here comes from *return-pulse timing/phase precision degrading as the reflected signal weakens and smears in time* (footprint elongation); active-stereo depth noise instead comes from *correspondence-matching precision degrading as the projected pattern deforms, defocuses, or is seen at a shrinking effective baseline-vs-surface-tilt geometry*, plus IR dropout on the second/third imager view at grazing angles. **The `1/cos(α)` signal-power law and the 60–65° knee are not derivable from this paper's physics for the D405** — they'd need to come from an active-stereo-specific study (this paper cannot be the source for RQ-branch-question-1's D405 curve; it can only supply the orthogonality *definition* and a plausibility prior for the smooth-then-knee *shape*).
- **Wrong range regime by 1–2 orders of magnitude.** Closest tested range is 5 m (Sec 3.2); the main experiment is at 20 m. Our operating range is 10–40 cm. Any numeric noise-vs-angle values (mm-level σ at 20–50 m) are not usable as citable numbers for D405 at cm range — only the qualitative angle-dependence shape transfers, not the magnitude or the fitted curve.
- **No closed-form σ(range, θ) equation with fitted numeric parameters is reported.** Despite the framing-doc's hope for "a citable curve or closed form," this paper gives a *theoretical* proportionality (`noise ∝ 1/cos α`, Fig. 2) and an *empirical decomposition trick* (Eq. 10), but never fits and reports actual regression coefficients relating σ to range and θ jointly — Fig. 6 is a qualitative multi-line plot, not a stated formula. This paper alone cannot fully satisfy branch question 1's request for a citable closed form.
- **No treatment of specular/glossy materials or projector-retroreflection risk** (branch question 3). Both test surfaces (matte plywood, painted room walls/linoleum) are diffuse. The single relevant hint is the speculative near-0° detector-saturation remark (Sec 3.1) — worth flagging as circumstantial support for "near-normal incidence can misbehave," but it is explicitly presented by the authors as an unconfirmed post-hoc explanation, not a validated finding. This paper does **not** model or recommend off-normal viewing for glossy surfaces.
- **No defect-detectability / image-space content at all.** The entire paper is about geometric point-precision (mm-level noise in XYZ), never about detecting scratches, dents, or coating flaws, and never touches raking-light visibility. Zero transfer to branch question 4 — must be sourced from industrial-inspection literature instead.
- **No visibility/self-occlusion, viewpoint entropy, saliency, information-gain, or VLM/image-judging content.** Out of scope for this single-topic metrology paper; irrelevant to branch questions 5(partial only), 11, 17–21.
- **No sensitivity analysis of normal-estimation error on the resulting score** (branch question 7). Normals here come from a least-squares plane fit over a full, comparatively clean, high-point-count planar segment (thousands to tens of thousands of points on a *known-planar* test board or wall) — a much better-conditioned normal estimate than our sparse, defective, non-planar-primitive survey cloud will produce. The paper's implicit assumption (normals are cheap and accurate given enough points on a real plane) does not hold under our stated PLY defects (mm pose error, dropout holes, curved/primitive-but-not-flat surfaces).
- **No propagation of upstream geometry uncertainty into the view score** (branch question 12) — the plane/normal is treated as ground truth once fit; no discussion of how an already-uncertain input point cloud (as ours is, with a ~4 cm fused-vs-single-view offset) would propagate into the incidence-angle term itself.

## Answers mapped to specific branch questions

- **Q ("smooth or threshold?")** — Best available answer from this document: **smooth convex growth (~1/cos α) up to ~50–60°, then a real, cross-validated knee around 60–65°** where two independent TLS studies recommend hard exclusion. Supports a hybrid: soft penalty as the primary term, with a hard feasibility cutoff near 60–65° as a backstop — though this is a laser-TLS number, not a D405-validated one.
- **Q ("does orthogonality ever hurt?")** — No confirmed answer; only a speculative, unvalidated remark about detector saturation near 0° incidence on a TLS. Not strong enough to cite as evidence either way for the D405/specular question; treat as a "worth checking, not yet shown" pointer.
- **Q ("defect detectability")** — Not addressed; zero content.
- **Q (term table, Q5)** — Contributes one clean row: *incidence/cosine term*, PLY-space, formula `α = cos⁻¹(P·N/|PN|)`, inputs = view ray direction + local surface normal (from plane/PCA fit over a neighborhood).
- **Q ("unit of scoring", Q9)** — Contributes a concrete, citable point→patch aggregation rule: RMSE (quadratic mean) of per-point residuals over a planar segment (Eq. 11), used precisely for this kind of geometric quality score.
- **Q ("survive a defective fused PLY", Q10)** — The incidence term itself is cheap and computable-today given any local normal estimate; but the paper's own accuracy for that normal estimate depends on dense, genuinely-planar, low-noise input that our fused cloud (dropout holes, mm pose error, primitive-but-curved surfaces) won't always provide — so classify as "computable today, but normal-estimation quality here is contingent on Branch 3's cleanup, not free."
