# Zeng, Zhang, Zhu, Wei, Zhu, Su et al. — "GenNBV: Generalizable Next-Best-View Policy for Active 3D Reconstruction"

CVPR 2024. arXiv:2402.16174v3. Full HTML text obtained (`arxiv.org/html/2402.16174v3`) and cross-checked against raw HTML source (not just the WebFetch summary) for every quote/number below. Read in full including all appendices (A.1 Occupancy Grid Mapping, A.2 Network and Training, B Data Preprocessing, C Additional Experiments).

## Core idea, in one paragraph

GenNBV formulates Next-Best-View selection for active 3D reconstruction as an MDP solved end-to-end with RL (PPO), where the action **is** the next camera pose itself — a continuous 5D vector `(x, y, z, yaw, pitch)`, roll excluded because "it is not supported in all drone platforms" (§3.1). There is no discrete candidate set, no tessellation, no node ID anywhere in the pipeline: the policy network outputs the parameters of a Gaussian over this 5D space and the next viewpoint is sampled from it, every step (§3.2, "the policy network is a 3-layer MLP... used to parameterize a normal distribution over action space," `a ~ π(·|o_t)`). The paper's stated target is explicitly the elimination of hand-crafted/limited action spaces: "typical rule-based NBV policies... heavily rely on empirically designed view-selection criteria and hand-crafted action space, such as a hemisphere... which limit their generalizability to unseen scenes" (§1). To make this large space learnable, GenNBV pairs it with a "multi-source state embedding" (probabilistic-occupancy geometric grid + grayscale-image semantic CNN + action-history embedding, §3.2) trained across 256 procedurally-varied building meshes (Houses3K) in Isaac Gym, then evaluated for cross-dataset generalization on OmniObject3D and Replica.

## Formal setup (checkable against equations)

- **Agent/platform**: simulated CrazyFlie drone, RGB-D + IMU, free-flying in open 3D space, no arm, no ground contact, no kinematic chain (§3.1, §4.1).
- **Observation** `o_t`: RGB `I_t`, depth `D_t`, state vector (yaw, pitch, x, y, z), plus history of prior captures/actions (§3.1).
- **Action** `a_t`: 5D continuous `(x, y, z, yaw, pitch)` — "camera location and camera angle," restricted to a workspace "approximately 20m × 20m × 10m" (§1, §3.1). No discretization anywhere in this space; confirmed no binning at inference or training.
- **Reward** (Eq. 2–3): `CR_t = (Ñ_t / N*) · 100%` (coverage ratio: occupied voxels detected so far over ground-truth occupied voxels `N*`, obtained from Poisson-disk-sampled, voxelized ground-truth meshes, Appendix B); main reward `r^CR_{t+1} = CR_{t+1} − CR_t`. Plus a negative reward + episode termination on collision, and a negative reward once captured-keyframe count exceeds an empirical threshold (§3.3). **No orthogonality term, no surface-normal alignment term, no framing/composition term of any kind appears in the reward.** Coverage is defined purely by whether a camera ray's back-projected depth point lands in a not-yet-occupied ground-truth voxel (Bresenham ray-casting into a log-odds occupancy grid, Eq. 1, §3.2/Appendix A.1) — a grazing/oblique view that happens to touch new voxels scores identically to an orthogonal one.
- **Policy optimization**: PPO (Schulman et al.), clipped surrogate objective (Eq. 4–5), 256 parallel Isaac Gym environments (each one building object), 32M training iterations, ~24h on one V100 (§3.3, Appendix A.2). Episode ends at 99% coverage, on collision, or after 100 steps; evaluation caps views at 30 (Houses3K) or 20 (OmniObject3D) (Appendix A.2, Table 1 caption).
- **Geometric state**: probabilistic 3D occupancy grid, shape `(N=256 envs, 20, 20, 20, 4)` — i.e., a **20×20×20 voxel grid over a ~15m×15m×8m building**, so each voxel is roughly 0.75m×0.75m×0.4m (Appendix A.2, Appendix B). This resolution is set by the building-scale task; nowhere near mm-scale defect resolution.

## Concrete results (Table 1, Houses3K test / OmniObject3D house category — exact numbers, verified against raw table)

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Random | free 5D, random | 48.53% | 58.24% | 60.13% | 73.73% |
| Random Hemisphere | fixed discrete hemisphere, random draw | 71.19% | 79.72% | 74.03% | 84.74% |
| **Uniform Hemisphere** | fixed discrete hemisphere, evenly spaced, non-learned | **82.91%** | **89.71%** | **83.09%** | **92.90%** |
| Uncertainty-Guided [Lee et al.] | 100-candidate discrete set, NeRF-driven | 83.13% | 89.31% | 69.08% | 92.85% |
| ActiveRMap [Zhan et al.] | discrete hemisphere candidate set | 84.86% | 90.77% | 69.47% | 93.15% |
| Scan-RL [Peralta et al., ECCVW'20] | 3D hemisphere, RL-learned | 84.49% | 91.61% | 82.17% | 92.53% |
| Scan-RL w/ GenNBV's state repr. | 3D hemisphere, RL-learned | 86.48% | 92.20% | 86.14% | 93.73% |
| GenNBV's free action w/ Scan-RL's repr. | free 5D, RL-learned | 87.39% | 95.63% | 86.81% | 93.64% |
| **GenNBV (full)** | **free 5D, RL-learned** | **91.19%** | **98.26%** | **88.63%** | **97.12%** |

Non-house cross-category generalization (Table 2, 20 views, trained only on houses): Animals 84.28%/94.07% AUC/FCR (vs. Scan-RL 76.43%/84.98%), Trucks 75.54%/83.47% (vs. 69.58%/76.37%), Dinosaurs 84.24%/94.03% (vs. 78.47%/86.89%), Replica Room 0 88.53%/99.16% (vs. 69.21%/80.75%). GenNBV beats Scan-RL everywhere but coverage on non-house categories (esp. Trucks, 83.47% FCR) is markedly below the in-domain house numbers (97–98%) — cross-category generalization is real but not saturated even for the winning method.

Ablations (Table 3, Table 4, Table 6 — Houses3K/OmniObject3D):
- Multi-source state ablation (uni-source): action/pose-history alone ("5-DoF Pose" baseline, no perception at all) = **69.53% AUC / 76.61% FCR** — the weakest of all uni-source variants; probabilistic-3D-grid alone = 81.06%/84.56%; semantic-2D alone = 81.24%/87.90%. All-three-combined = 91.19%/98.26%.
- Depth representation ablation (Table 4): 2D geometric map 71.86%/74.88% < binary 3D grid 77.28%/80.85% < probabilistic 3D grid 81.06%/84.56%.
- Training-object-count ablation (Table 6, on unseen OmniObject3D houses): 1 object → 61.76%/67.32%; 32 → 82.39%/92.41%; 64 → 82.09%/93.70%; 128 → 87.21%/96.75%; 256 → 88.63%/97.12%. Generalization scales with training diversity, not saturated even at 256.

**Decomposition of the free-action-space vs. state-representation contributions** (computed by me from Table 1's two "half-swap" rows, Houses3K): holding the hemisphere action space fixed and only swapping in GenNBV's representation gives +1.99pp AUC / +0.59pp FCR over base Scan-RL; holding Scan-RL's representation fixed and only swapping in the free 5D action space gives +2.90pp AUC / +4.02pp FCR. The full combination gives +6.70pp AUC / +6.65pp FCR — clearly super-additive (synergy), but action-space widening alone is not dominant: it buys a real but bounded few points, comparable in magnitude to the representation change, not an order-of-magnitude effect.

## Load-bearing assumptions

- **Free-flying rigid camera with no kinematic chain, no IK, no arm-collision geometry.** The only physical constraint modeled is drone-vs-scene collision, added as a negative reward + episode termination (§3.3) — precisely because, as the paper itself notes, "previous limited-space agents [Uncertainty-Guided, ActiveRMap, SCONE] do not consider collision avoidance since their search space, like hemisphere, is by-design safe" (§3.3, line ~225). I.e., **the paper's own text identifies discrete/bounded action spaces as a mechanism for guaranteeing feasibility by construction** — the thing GenNBV gives up by going free, and has to re-earn via a learned collision penalty.
- **Reward = coverage of ground-truth occupied voxels only; requires a known ground-truth mesh per training/eval object** to compute `N*` (Eq. 2) via Poisson-disk sampling (Appendix B). This is a simulation-and-mesh-native setup: there is no notion of "surface region currently under inspection" or per-surface quality — coverage is a single scalar over the whole object.
- **Object class is architectural/building-scale exteriors** (Houses3K: 3,000 building meshes, 256 for training after filtering out "complex internal structures" and "redundant bases," §4.1/Appendix B), rescaled to ~15m×15m×8m. Generalization tests (OmniObject3D house category, then non-house animals/trucks/dinosaurs, then Replica indoor) are all evaluated with the same house-trained policy, at 20×20×20 voxel resolution.
- **Training cost is amortized over the whole object class, not per-object**: 32M PPO iterations, ~24h on one V100, 256 parallel Isaac Gym envs (Appendix A.2). This "build cost" model — heavy one-time RL training, cheap per-episode inference — is structurally different from a per-object discrete-structure compilation (e.g., fitting primitives to one surveyed cloud): GenNBV needs no per-object build step but needed a large labeled-mesh training corpus and a physics simulator up front.

## What transfers to our setting

- **The critique's target is real and citable, and matters for RQ12/RQ18 (variable arity / cross-shape addressing).** GenNBV's stated failure mode of hand-crafted candidate sets is specifically about *scale*: "many hand-crafted designs [Uncertainty-Guided, ActiveNeRF] limit the available viewpoints to a small predefined set, and thus cannot generalize if there is a drastic change in scales" (§1). This is a genuine, load-bearing risk for any fixed global tessellation ({h,v} bins, HEALPix cell count) applied across our box/cylinder/assembly object classes at different sizes — it is direct support for choosing an addressing scheme whose *meaning* (not just count) is stable across shape and scale (face-orthogonal, orbit-normal), which primitive-anchored addressing already targets by construction.
- **Table 1 is a genuine, directly-comparable existence proof for the branch-2 benchmark-tag question** ("does any found work compare a geometry-conditioned view space against a uniform sphere on the same task with a metric?"). GenNBV's own "Uniform Hemisphere" row is exactly that comparison, run by the paper's own authors as a baseline, with AUC/FCR/Accuracy on the same task as their free-space policy. Record this as a positive hit, with the caveat that the "geometry" being conditioned on there is a fixed hemisphere sized to bound the object set, not a per-object primitive decomposition — it's a uniform-sphere-vs-free-space comparison, not a uniform-sphere-vs-geometry-conditioned-discrete-space comparison. Nobody in this paper compares a *geometry-conditioned discrete* space against either baseline.
- **"By-design safe" (§3.3) is a directly reusable argument for Branch 4/our safety constraints.** A bounded/discrete action space is a feasibility guarantee, for free — no collision-avoidance machinery is needed if the address space is pre-constrained to reachable, collision-free poses. This is the strongest pro-discreteness argument the paper itself supplies, stated as the reason *prior* work didn't need what GenNBV had to add back in (a learned collision penalty). It directly informs the branch's infeasibility question: rather than "does the address persist with a degraded pose, vanish, or get flagged," GenNBV shows the alternative failure mode of *not* having bounded addresses — you must instead learn to avoid collision from scratch, with a nonzero training-time collision rate.
- **The uni-source ablation (action-history alone = weakest signal, 69.53% AUC) is evidence against "the action space size is what matters most."** Raw freedom of action, without informative geometric/semantic state to aim it, is the worst-performing input in Table 3 — reinforces that *what the agent knows about the surface* dominates over *how large its action space is*, which favors our approach of deriving addresses from actual surveyed geometry (our RQ1/RQ2) over simply widening a generic action space.

## What does NOT transfer, and why (negative findings)

- **No orthogonality requirement anywhere, at the level of the reward function itself (Eq. 2–3) or the coverage metric.** This is the single largest transfer gap for our setting, where "evidence views must be orthogonal to the inspected surface" is a hard constraint (framing doc). GenNBV's coverage ratio rewards *any* ray that hits a not-yet-seen voxel; an oblique grazing view scores identically to a face-on view as long as it touches new occupied voxels. The free 5D space earns its coverage numbers precisely because it is not constrained to be surface-normal anywhere — it is optimizing a different objective than ours. Nothing in this paper argues discretization is a "bottleneck" *for orthogonality* — orthogonality is simply outside its scope.
- **No addressing/naming layer of any kind exists to borrow.** GenNBV is the zero-address extreme of the design space: there is no node ID, no symbol, no discrete anything that an orchestrator (VLM or otherwise) could refer to, predict the outcome of, or reason about ("go to view 7" is meaningless here — the action is a raw 5-vector sampled from a learned Gaussian). This paper cannot inform "what is an address, formally" beyond providing the null case; it offers no PAMDP-style discrete-`k`/continuous-`x_k` split, no evidence for or against a middle path of "discrete address naming a region + continuous refinement inside it" — that split is simply not tested here in either direction.
- **No manipulator, no IK, no reachability shell, no gripper/camera hull collision geometry.** The platform is a free-flying drone in open air; the only "reachability" constraint is a soft workspace bound (~20m×20m×10m) and a learned collision penalty against the scene mesh. None of this transfers to a UR5e eye-in-hand arm with joint limits, self-collision, minimum shell radius, and a fixed base — the paper never has to reason about kinematic infeasibility at all, which is a central constraint in our setting (per framing doc: "not every desired view is reachable").
- **Trained policy, not a deterministic geometric procedure — requires a full RL training pipeline, a physics simulator (Isaac Gym), and a corpus of many ground-truth 3D meshes with known occupancy** to even produce a usable policy (32M iterations / ~24h / 256 parallel envs / 256 distinct training objects, Appendix A.2/B). This is not liftable into our single-session, survey-then-inspect loop on a single real, noisy fused point cloud with no ground-truth mesh and no simulator in the loop — there is no equivalent of "fit primitives to this one PLY and derive an address space deterministically." GenNBV's method requires amortizing cost over a large offline-trained object class; our setting requires per-object, per-session, cheap construction.
- **Coarse voxel resolution (20×20×20 over a ~15m building, ≈0.4–0.75m voxels) is architecturally mismatched to our defect-level inspection task**, which the framing doc describes as needing to detect real, fine-grained surface defects on a tabletop object. Nothing in GenNBV's representation or evaluation addresses fine-surface-detail coverage; "coverage" here means gross building-shell occupancy, not defect-resolution surface sampling.
- **Cross-category generalization, while real, is not saturated even for GenNBV itself** (Trucks: 83.47% FCR vs. 97–99% for in-domain houses/Replica room, Table 2) — the paper's own headline generalization claim should not be read as "the free action space solves generalization"; it solves it partially, and worse on shapes further from the building-exterior training distribution. This tempers any claim that a free continuous space is a drop-in fix for cross-shape consistency (our RQ18) — GenNBV's own numbers show meaningful residual shape-dependence.
- **No discussion of adjacency, ordering, seams, or bounded-cardinality guarantees** — there is no structure to have these properties. This paper is silent on (and cannot be cited for) RQ4/RQ5/RQ7/RQ9/RQ14's adjacency/seam/cardinality questions; it is the boundary case where these questions do not apply because there is no address space, only a dense continuous one.

## Reading the headline claim against its own numbers

The abstract's framing ("existing NBV policies... limited action space... limits cross-dataset generalizability") reads, from the abstract alone, as a strong case that discretization itself is the dominant bottleneck. Table 1 does not fully support that strength of claim: the non-learned, hand-crafted **Uniform Hemisphere** baseline — a fixed discrete set, evenly spaced, no learning at all — already reaches 89.71% FCR (Houses3K) / 92.90% FCR (OmniObject3D), within 8.5pp / 4.2pp of GenNBV's full free-space learned result (98.26% / 97.12%). And the decomposition above shows the free-action-space contribution in isolation (holding representation fixed) is +2.90pp AUC / +4.02pp FCR — real, but comparable in size to the state-representation contribution (+1.99pp / +0.59pp), not dominant over it. For our branch's question of "what discrete selection costs us," GenNBV's own data suggests: on a *coverage-only* objective with *no orthogonality/quality requirement*, a well-chosen fixed discrete set gets most of the way there, and the residual gap that free continuous control closes is real but modest — this is evidence for, not against, the branch's premise that a geometry-conditioned *discrete* space (rather than fully free continuous control) can be sufficient for a bounded, well-understood object class like ours.

## Assessment for the branch

GenNBV is genuinely the strongest available counter-position — it is the zero-address extreme (dense continuous ℝ^5 action, no discrete anything) and its own text explicitly frames discretization as the thing to escape. But read closely, three things blunt its force as a counter-argument to *our* branch specifically: (1) its own Table 1 shows a hand-crafted, non-learned, fixed discrete hemisphere gets within single digits of percentage points of the full free-space result on its own coverage-only metric; (2) its reward has no orthogonality or surface-quality term at all, so it never tests the thing our branch actually cares about; (3) it explicitly identifies bounded/discrete action spaces as a free feasibility guarantee ("by-design safe," §3.3) — exactly the property our reachability/collision constraints need and that GenNBV had to re-earn through learned collision avoidance. Net effect: this paper is best cited as evidence that *free continuous control has a real but bounded upside on a coverage-only, unconstrained-platform task*, not as evidence that discrete addressing is unfit for an orthogonality-constrained, kinematically-constrained tabletop task. It supplies zero material for the addressing-mechanics questions (RQ pertaining to address format, adjacency, ordering, seams, cardinality) since it has no address space to describe.
